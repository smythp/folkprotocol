# Licorice — Preparations

*Glycyrrhiza glabra L. / G. uralensis Fisch. / G. inflata Bat. | Evidence as of 2026-02-28*

---

## Overview: Two Preparation Classes

All licorice preparations divide into two classes with fundamentally different safety and efficacy profiles:

1. **GA-containing preparations** — whole root, tinctures, standardized extracts retaining glycyrrhizic acid; carry mineralocorticoid risk; WHO/EMA 4-week duration limit
2. **DGL (deglycyrrhizinated licorice)** — GA removed; suitable for prolonged GI use; lacks pseudoaldosteronism risk; most clinical GI trial evidence uses DGL

---

## Dried Root / Herbal Tea

**WHO-Referenced Standard Dosing:**
- 1.0–5.0 g dried root (or equivalent decoction), three times daily
- Source: WHO Monographs on Selected Medicinal Plants, Volume 1 (1999)

**German Commission E Dosing:**
- Average daily dose: 5–15 g root or equivalent preparations
- Duration: Not recommended beyond 4–6 weeks without medical supervision

**Preparation:**
- Standard decoction: 1–5 g cut/sifted root per cup (250 mL); simmer 10–15 minutes; strain; drink up to 3× daily
- Traditional tea (infusion): steeping less effective than decoction for saponin extraction; root should be simmered
- GA yield from decoction: variable (4–24% GA in root; actual extraction yield depends on root quality, grind, preparation)

**GA Exposure Estimate:**
- At 5 g root (minimum Commission E dose) with 4% GA content: ~200 mg GA/day — above WHO 100 mg/day limit
- At 1 g root (minimum WHO dose) with 4% GA: ~40 mg GA/day — within WHO limit
- Note: actual GA content varies substantially; caution warranted with higher doses

---

## Tinctures and Fluid Extracts

**Typical Ratios:**
- Fluid extract (1:1): 1 mL = 1 g herb equivalent; dose 2–4 mL three times daily
- Tincture (1:5, ethanol-water): 5 mL = 1 g herb equivalent; dose 5–15 mL three times daily

**Practical Notes:**
- Glycyrrhizin is water-soluble; ethanol-water menstruums (20–40% ethanol) extract effectively
- Higher ethanol ratios may preferentially extract flavonoids over saponins
- Tinctures retain full GA content; mineralocorticoid risk applies; 4-week duration limit

---

## Standardized Extracts (GA-Containing)

**Regulatory-Referenced Range:**
- European Pharmacopoeia standard: ≥4.0% glycyrrhizic acid (dry basis) — minimum quality bar for the root
- Clinical use extracts typically standardized to known GA% or to GA dose per capsule/tablet

**Dosing from Clinical Trials:**
- Hot flashes (Nahidi et al. 2012): 330 mg licorice root extract × 3 capsules/day = 990 mg/day; 8 weeks
- COVID-19 trial (Ameri et al. 2023): 760 mg licorice extract × 3× daily = 2280 mg/day; 7 days (no benefit on primary outcomes; GA dose not specified)
- Testosterone suppression (Armanini et al. 2004): 3.5 g/day commercial preparation at 7.6% GA = 266 mg GA/day; 2 menstrual cycles (mineralocorticoid effects expected and observed at this dose)

**Blood Pressure Benchmark:**
- 100 mg GA/day for 2 weeks measurably raises systolic BP by 3.1 mmHg and suppresses renin/aldosterone by 30–45% in healthy adults (Af Geijerstam et al. 2024)

---

## DGL (Deglycyrrhizinated Licorice) Preparations

**Definition:** Licorice root extract from which glycyrrhizic acid (GA) has been removed by industrial processing; GA content reduced to <2–3% (vs 4–24% in whole root).

**Mechanism shift:** DGL acts locally in the GI tract; retains flavonoid bioactivity (glabridin, liquiritigenin, isoliquiritigenin); lacks systemic mineralocorticoid effects.

### GutGard — Clinically Validated DGL Extract

Three Phase II/III RCTs have been published on GutGard, a standardized DGL extract from G. glabra:

**Standardization (GutGard®):**
- Glabridin ≥3.5% w/w
- Total flavonoids ≥10% w/w
- GA content: deglycyrrhizinated (specific % not disclosed in trials; registered DGL product)

**Clinical Doses and Outcomes:**

| Indication | Dose | Duration | N | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Functional dyspepsia | 75 mg twice daily (150 mg/day) | 30 days | 50 | 56% marked improvement vs 0% placebo |
| GERD (Phase III) | DGL GutGard® (dose not specified per publication) | 28 days | 200 | Significant heartburn (p=0.005) and regurgitation improvement |
| H. pylori eradication | 150 mg once daily | 60 days | 100 | 56% stool antigen negative vs 4% placebo |

**Safety across all three GutGard trials:** Well tolerated; safety profile comparable to placebo; no blood pressure or electrolyte adverse events.

### Commercial DGL Tablets (Chewable)

- Typical product format: 400–500 mg DGL per chewable tablet; 2–4 tablets 15 minutes before meals
- Chewing / direct mucosal contact proposed to enhance local GI effect
- Not standardized in the same way as GutGard; quality varies by manufacturer
- No large RCTs for generic DGL tablets; evidence base derives from GutGard trials

## H. pylori Add-on Protocol

**Hajiaghamohammadi et al. 2016 (PMID 27614124):**
- Preparation: Licorice supplement 380 mg twice daily (species not fully specified)
- Combined with: Clarithromycin-based triple therapy (clarithromycin + amoxicillin + omeprazole) for 2 weeks
- Eradication rate: 83.3% (licorice + triple therapy) vs 62.5% (triple therapy alone)
- Note: 380 mg twice daily = 760 mg/day total; GA content not reported; full-root preparation assumed given non-DGL characterization

## IV Glycyrrhizin (Clinical/Hospital Use)

Not a consumer preparation; documented in clinical literature for liver disease:
- Stronger Neo-Minophagen C (SNMC): 80–240 mg IV glycyrrhizin given 3× weekly
- Used in Japan for chronic hepatitis and liver enzyme normalization
- ALT reduction: 23–29% depending on dose (van Rossum et al. 1999)
- 18α-glycyrrhizinate (magnesium isoglycyrrhizinate): preferred epimer for IV use; 74% lower ADR rate than 18β compound

---

## Topical Preparations (Oral Mucosa)

**Aphthous ulcer treatment:**
- 1–5% licorice patch, paste, or mouthwash
- Evidence base: 6 clinical trials, N = 314 (Dorsareh et al. 2023 systematic review)
- Outcomes: Significant pain reduction within 4 days; ulcer size significantly reduced by day 8
- Mechanism: Local anti-inflammatory, antibacterial (S. mutans, P. gingivalis), EGF elevation
- Commercial formats: Dissolving oral patches (Martin et al. 2008 used OTC intraoral adhesive patch)

## Preparation Selection Guide

| Clinical Goal | Recommended Preparation | GA Exposure | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| GI (dyspepsia, GERD, H. pylori) | DGL GutGard 150 mg/day or equivalent | Negligible | 28–60 days in trials; extended use possible |
| Liver support | Whole-root extract or IV glycyrrhizin (clinical) | Relevant; monitor | ≤4 weeks oral; IV under supervision |
| Aphthous ulcers | 1–5% topical patch, paste, or mouthwash | Topical only | Days to 2 weeks |
| Hot flashes | Licorice root extract 990 mg/day (Nahidi 2012) | Present; monitor BP/K⁺ | ≤8 weeks |
| Respiratory (traditional) | Dried root decoction 1–5 g 3× daily (WHO) | Present; monitor | ≤4 weeks |
| H. pylori (adjunct) | 380 mg licorice 2× daily + triple therapy | Present; 2-week course | 14 days |
| PCOS metabolic support | 1.5 g/day licorice extract + dietary modification | Present; monitor | ≤8 weeks |
