# Licorice — Clinical Uses

*Glycyrrhiza glabra L. / G. uralensis Fisch. / G. inflata Bat. | Evidence as of 2026-02-28*

---

## Regulatory Approvals

**WHO Monograph (1999) — Radix Glycyrrhizae**
- Approved: Demulcent for sore throats; expectorant for coughs and bronchial catarrh; adjuvant in gastric and duodenal ulcers
- Dosing: 1.0–5.0 g dried root three times daily
- Safety limit: ≤100 mg glycyrrhizin (GA) per day

**EMA/HMPC Monograph (2013) — EMA/HMPC/571121/2010**
- Well-established use: Adjuvant in gastric and duodenal ulcers; catarrh of upper respiratory tract
- Traditional use: Relief of digestive disorder symptoms; expectorant in productive cough
- Maximum duration: 4 weeks without medical supervision

**German Commission E (positive monograph)**
- Approved: Catarrh of upper respiratory tract; gastric and duodenal ulcers
- Dose: 5–15 g root/day or equivalent preparations
- Duration: Not recommended beyond 4–6 weeks without supervision

## Randomized Controlled Trials

### Functional Dyspepsia

**Raveendra et al. 2012 (PMID 21747893)**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- N: 50 (25 GutGard, 25 placebo)
- Intervention: GutGard standardized G. glabra extract 75 mg twice daily (150 mg/day)
- Duration: 30 days
- Results:
  - Total symptom score: significantly decreased vs placebo (p ≤ 0.05) at days 15 and 30
  - Global efficacy: 56% marked improvement with GutGard vs 0% placebo
  - Nepean dyspepsia index (QoL): significant improvement at both time points
  - Upper abdominal fullness and epigastric pain significantly improved
  - Early satiety: not significantly different
- Safety: No treatment-related adverse effects

### Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)

**Raj et al. 2025 (PMID 39929150) — Phase III RCT**
- Design: Phase III, double-blind, parallel-group, randomized, placebo-controlled
- N: 200 (296 screened)
- Intervention: GutGard® DGL extract standardized to glabridin ≥3.5% w/w and total flavonoids ≥10% w/w
- Duration: 28 days
- Results:
  - Quality of life: significantly better at day 28 (p = 0.014)
  - Heartburn resolution: significant at day 14 (p = 0.017) and day 28 (p = 0.005)
  - Regurgitation improvement: significant on day 7 (p = 0.025), day 14 (p = 0.029), and day 28 (p = 0.022)
- Safety: Well tolerated; safety profile comparable to placebo

### Helicobacter pylori — Standalone

**Puram et al. 2013 (PMID 23606875)**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- N: 100 (50 GutGard analyzed, 50 placebo analyzed)
- Intervention: GutGard standardized G. glabra extract 150 mg once daily
- Duration: 60 days
- Results:
  - Stool antigen day 60: 56% negative in GutGard vs 4% placebo (p < 0.05)
  - 13C-urea breath test day 60: 48% negative in GutGard group (statistically significant vs placebo)
  - GutGard 3.73× more effective than placebo at reducing H. pylori gastric load
- Safety: Well tolerated; no safety concerns

### Helicobacter pylori — Add-on to Triple Therapy

**Hajiaghamohammadi et al. 2016 (PMID 27614124)**
- Design: Randomized controlled trial
- N: 120 (H. pylori-positive by rapid urease test)
- Intervention: Licorice 380 mg twice daily + clarithromycin-based triple therapy (clarithromycin + amoxicillin + omeprazole) for 2 weeks
- Comparator: Triple therapy alone
- Results:
  - Eradication rate: 83.3% licorice + triple therapy vs 62.5% triple therapy alone (statistically significant)
- Conclusion: Licorice addition significantly increases H. pylori eradication, especially in peptic ulcer disease

### Aphthous Ulcers (Oral Cavity)

**Martin et al. 2008 (PMID 18348383)**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind controlled trial (3 arms)
- N: 69 (23 active, 23 placebo patch, 23 no-treatment)
- Intervention: Intraoral adhesive dissolving patch containing glycyrrhiza root extract
- Results:
  - Day 4: 81% active group reported no pain vs 63% placebo vs 40% no-treatment (p < 0.01 pre-stimulus pain)
  - Day 8: Significantly lower ulcer size in active group vs controls (p < 0.05)
- Safety: Well tolerated; no adverse effects

### Menopausal Hot Flashes

**Nahidi et al. 2012 (PMID 24250477)**
- Design: Double-blind, randomized controlled clinical trial
- N: 90 menopausal women
- Intervention: 3 capsules/day containing 330 mg licorice root extract (990 mg/day total)
- Duration: 8 weeks intervention + 4 weeks follow-up
- Results:
  - Hot flash frequency: significantly decreased in licorice vs placebo
  - Hot flash severity: significantly decreased in licorice vs placebo
  - Effects persisted 2 weeks post-treatment before recurrence
  - Placebo group: significant improvement only in week 1
- Safety: No adverse effects reported
- Note: Effect did not persist; hot flashes returned within 2 weeks of stopping

### PCOS — Metabolic Parameters

**Hooshmandi et al. 2024 (PMID 39080737)**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- N: 66 (33 licorice, 33 placebo); overweight/obese women with PCOS (BMI 25–35, age 18–45, Rotterdam criteria)
- Intervention: 1.5 g/day licorice extract + low-calorie diet
- Comparator: Placebo + low-calorie diet
- Duration: 8 weeks
- Results:
  - Significant differences in body weight, BMI, and body fat favoring licorice
  - Significant improvements in TG, TC, LDL-C, and HDL-C with licorice vs placebo
  - Significant improvements in FBS, insulin, HOMA-IR, and HOMA-B with licorice vs placebo
- Conclusion: Licorice as adjunct to low-calorie diet improves metabolic parameters in PCOS more than diet alone

### Testosterone Suppression in Women

**Armanini et al. 2004 (PMID 15579328)**
- Design: Clinical pharmacodynamic study
- N: 9 healthy premenopausal women (age 22–26)
- Intervention: 3.5 g/day commercial licorice preparation containing 7.6% glycyrrhizic acid
- Duration: 2 menstrual cycles
- Results:
  - Month 1: testosterone decreased from 27.8 ± 8.2 to 19.0 ± 9.4 ng/dL (p < 0.05)
  - Month 2: further decrease to 17.5 ± 6.4 ng/dL (p < 0.05)
  - Returned to baseline after discontinuation
  - Androstenedione, 17OH-progesterone, LH, blood pressure, cortisol: unchanged
  - Renin and aldosterone: both decreased (mineralocorticoid-like effect)
- Proposed use: Potential adjuvant therapy for hirsutism and PCOS

### Cortisol Augmentation (Addison's Disease)

**Methlie et al. 2011 (PMID 21896619)**
- Design: Comparative study, two 3-day intervention periods
- N: 17 Addison's disease patients on stable cortisone acetate replacement
- Intervention: Licorice co-administered with cortisone acetate for 3 days
- Results:
  - Cortisol AUC: increased from 50,882 to 53,783 (p < 0.05)
  - Cortisol at 2–6h: elevated to 223 nmol/L vs baseline 186 nmol/L (p < 0.05)
  - Urinary cortisol/cortisone ratio: increased from 0.21 to 0.43 (p < 0.00001)
- Mechanism: 11β-HSD2 inhibition by glycyrrhetinic acid increases cortisol availability
- Clinical implication: Licorice may reduce cortisone acetate dose requirements; also raises drug interaction concern

### Hepatoprotection — Alcohol Exposure

**Chigurupati et al. 2016 (PMID 27539273)**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover
- N: 12 (6 male, 6 female); 12 days per arm with nightly vodka consumption achieving BAC 0.12%
- Intervention: Proprietary glycyrrhizin product concurrent with alcohol
- Results:
  - Alcohol control: significant AST, ALT, and GGT elevations from baseline to day 12
  - Glycyrrhizin group: no statistically significant enzyme elevations; alkaline phosphatase significantly decreased vs alcohol control; plasma glutathione decreased relative to alcohol control (protective effect)
- Conclusion: Glycyrrhizin product may support liver health vs alcohol alone

### Chronic Hepatitis C — IV Glycyrrhizin

**van Rossum et al. 1999 (PMID 10574137)**
- Design: Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled Phase I/II trial
- N: 57 (chronic HCV, interferon non-responders)
- Intervention: IV glycyrrhizin (Stronger Neo-Minophagen C) 3× weekly for 4 weeks
  - Arms: 240 mg, 160 mg, 80 mg, or placebo
- Results:
  - ALT within 2 days: dropped 15% below baseline in all 3 glycyrrhizin groups (p < 0.02)
  - ALT at end: mean decrease 26% with glycyrrhizin vs 6% placebo (significant)
  - Dose response: 240 mg → 29%, 160 mg → 26%, 80 mg → 23% ALT decrease
  - HCV-RNA: mean decrease 4.1 × 10⁶ genome equivalents/mL (not statistically significant; no viral clearance)
- Safety: No major adverse effects; all patients completed study
- Conclusion: IV glycyrrhizin reduces ALT significantly but has no effect on HCV viral load

### COVID-19

**Ameri et al. 2023 (PMID 37847472)**
- Design: Randomized controlled trial
- N: 60 confirmed COVID-19 patients (moderate illness)
- Intervention: Licorice extract 760 mg three times daily (2280 mg/day) for 7 days
- Results:
  - No significant difference in oxygen saturation, body temperature, or respiratory rate
  - Mortality, hospital stay, adverse events, disease progression: comparable to control
  - CRP and ALT showed improvement relative to baseline in licorice group
- Safety: Safe; adverse event profile comparable to control
- Conclusion: Licorice had no beneficial effect on COVID-19 clinical symptoms

### Blood Pressure Effect — Safety-Relevant RCT

**Af Geijerstam et al. 2024 (PMID 38246526)**
- Design: Randomized controlled crossover (nonblinded, 2×2 design); 2-week treatment periods
- N: 28 healthy adults (median age 24.0 years); no dropouts
- Intervention: Daily licorice intake containing exactly 100 mg glycyrrhizic acid
- Results:
  - Systolic home BP: increased by 3.1 mmHg (95% CI: 0.8, 5.4 mmHg)
  - Renin: suppressed by mean −30.0% (95% CI: −56.7%, −3.3%)
  - Aldosterone: suppressed by mean −45.1% (95% CI: −61.5%, −28.7%)
- Significance: Demonstrates the WHO 100 mg GA/day threshold produces measurable cardiovascular effects; suggests the threshold may warrant reconsideration

## Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

### Liver Function in Primary Liver Disease

**Giangrandi et al. 2024 (PMID 39079711)**
- 15 RCTs; N = 1,367 patients with primary liver disease
- ALT: decreased by 15.63 U/L vs control (pooled)
- AST: decreased by 7.37 U/L vs control (pooled)
- Purified glycyrrhizic acid compounds showed greater ALT/AST reductions with minimal heterogeneity
- No significant change in GGT or total bilirubin overall

### Metabolic Effects

**Luís et al. 2018 (PMID 29433679)**
- 26 trials; N = 985
- Body weight: WMD −0.433 kg (95% CI: −0.683 to −0.183); significant
- BMI: WMD −0.150 kg/m² (95% CI: −0.241 to −0.058); significant
- Diastolic BP: increased by 1.737 mmHg (attributed to glycyrrhizin-induced hypernatremia)

### Blood Pressure by Component Type

**Wu et al. 2024 (PMID 39519602)**
- 8 RCTs; N = 541
- Glycyrrhizic acid: SBP +3.48 mmHg (95% CI: 2.74–4.21, p < 0.001); DBP +1.27 mmHg (p < 0.001)
- Licorice flavonoid fraction: SBP change 0.58 mmHg (NS); DBP change 0.17 mmHg (NS)
- Conclusion: Glycyrrhizic acid raises blood pressure; flavonoid fraction does not

### Aphthous Ulcers — Topical

**Dorsareh et al. 2023 (PMID 37786470)**
- 6 clinical trials; N = 314
- Formulations: 1% or 5% paste, patch, mouthwash
- Significant reduction in aphthous pain and ulcer diameter
- Expected healing within 4–8 days
- Licorice elevated EGF levels vs control
- No adverse effects in any intervention group

### Peptic Ulcer Disease

**Zaeif Khorasani et al. 2025 (DOI: 10.1016/j.aimed.2025.100485)**
- 9 studies; N = 618
- Pain improvement: OR 0.52 (95% CI: 0.42–1.45); trend toward improvement, not statistically significant
- Complete healing: OR 0.60 (95% CI: 0.24–1.43); trend toward improved healing, not statistically significant
- High heterogeneity; further high-quality RCTs needed

### Pediatric Hepatitis — IV Glycyrrhizin

**Liang et al. 2022 (PMID 34522606)**
- 6 RCTs; N = 608 children with acute icteric hepatitis
- Compound glycyrrhizin injection (CGI) + conventional western medicine vs western medicine alone
- Total bilirubin: −8.19 mmol/L (MD; 95% CI −9.86 to −6.53)
- ALT: −24.09 U/L (MD; 95% CI −30.83 to −17.34)
- AST: −18.67 U/L (MD; 95% CI −21.88 to −15.45)
- Evidence certainty: low or very low (GRADE); no adverse events reported

---

## Evidence Gaps

- No long-duration RCTs (>8 weeks) for oral licorice alone in GI indications
- No dose-finding studies establishing minimum effective doses per indication
- No direct head-to-head comparison of DGL vs whole-root preparations for GI efficacy
- No definitive RCTs for menopausal hot flashes with adequate power
- Species-specific clinical profiles (G. glabra vs G. uralensis) not directly compared in trials
