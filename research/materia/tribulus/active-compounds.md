# Tribulus terrestris - Active Compounds

## Primary Bioactive Classes

### Steroidal Saponins — Primary Active Compounds

Steroidal saponins are the dominant pharmacologically active constituents of Tribulus terrestris and serve as the standardization markers in commercial preparations and clinical trials.

**Two structural subclasses**:
1. **Furostanol saponins** (furostane-type) — considered the primary pharmacologically active fraction
2. **Spirostanol saponins** (spirostane-type)

**Total saponins characterized**: 108 distinct steroidal saponins isolated from TT (58 spirostane + 50 furostane types), per Štefănescu et al. 2020

---

## Key Individual Compounds

### Protodioscin (Dominant Furostanol Saponin)

**Status**: Primary pharmacologically active compound and principal standardization marker

**Chemical classification**: Furostanol saponin; steroidal glycoside

**Concentration range in plant material**:
- **Range across geographic origins**: 1–1,530 mg/100g dry weight
- **Variation within same country**: Remarkable variability even between samples from identical geographic origin
- **Key implication**: A 1,530-fold concentration range means that unstandardized products cannot be assumed to contain therapeutic levels of protodioscin

**Proposed mechanisms**:
1. Conversion to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) — Salgado 2017 demonstrated significant DHT elevation with protodioscin-containing extract; proposed to facilitate testosterone-to-DHT conversion affecting spermatogenesis
2. Increase in free and bioavailable testosterone (mechanism proposed in De Souza 2016, Vale 2018)
3. Possible nitric oxide (NO) pathway effects — proposed peripheral mechanism for erectile function independent of testosterone (not yet clearly established)

**Bioavailability**: See dedicated section below

---

### Other Furostanol Saponins

Protodioscin is the dominant furostanol saponin but co-occurs with:
- Prototribestin
- Dioscin (from intestinal hydrolysis of protodioscin)
- Furostanol glycosides with varied sugar attachments

---

### Spirostanol Saponins

- Diosgenin (sapogenin backbone)
- Tigogenin
- Ruscogenin
- Gitogenin
- Gracillin

**Note**: Less studied for pharmacological activity than furostanols; the relative contribution of spirostanol saponins to clinical effects remains unclear

---

### Flavonoids

Secondary bioactive class identified in TT:
- Kaempferol
- Quercetin
- Rutin
- Isorhamnetin

**Proposed roles**: Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant; may contribute to lipid-improving effects observed in athletic populations (Fernández-Lázaro 2022)

## Standardization

### Commercial Standardization Markers

**Primary marker**: Furostanol saponins (typically as protodioscin equivalent)

**Tribestan® (Sopharma, Bulgaria) — Best-Characterized Commercial Extract**:
- **Tablet content**: 250 mg TT dry extract
- **Standardization**: ≥ 112.5 mg furostanol saponins per tablet (≥ 45%)
- **Clinical trial dose**: 6 tablets/day = 1,500 mg extract; ~675 mg furostanol saponins/day
- **Used in**: Kamenov 2017 (n=172; IIEF improvement p < 0.0001); cited in Vilar Neto 2025 systematic review

**Fernández-Lázaro 2021 extract (CrossFit RCT)**:
- **Dose**: 770 mg/day
- **Standardization**: 72.64% active principles; 40% saponins (385 mg saponins/day)

**Androsten® (Salgado 2017 — fertility RCT)**:
- 250 mg dried TT extract per capsule
- Active compound specified as protodioscin
- Used in n=65 men; demonstrated sperm parameter improvements and DHT elevation

### Typical Commercial Range

- **Standardized extracts**: 40–45% furostanol saponins is the common commercial range
- **Unstandardized preparations** (water extracts, traditional decoctions): Protodioscin content entirely unpredictable due to extreme geographic variation

## Bioavailability and Pharmacokinetics

### Protodioscin Oral Bioavailability

**Key study**: Zhang et al. (Steroids, 2016) — rat pharmacokinetic study using HPLC-ESI-MS/MS
- **Oral bioavailability of protodioscin**: ~5.7% (low)
- **Analytical method**: HPLC-ESI-MS/MS in rat plasma

**Contradictory finding**: A separate study using protodioscin from Dioscorea extract demonstrated good bioavailability — suggesting the formulation matrix significantly affects absorption

**Human pharmacokinetic data**: **None** — all PK data is animal-derived

**Clinical implications of low bioavailability**:
- Low oral bioavailability (~5.7%) may explain the relatively high doses required in clinical trials (750–1,500 mg extract/day)
- Formulation and manufacturing process likely have a major impact on effective dose
- Comparison across different commercial products is inherently unreliable without standardization to the same marker at the same dose

### Intestinal Metabolism

- Furostanol saponins undergo intestinal hydrolysis by gut microbiota
- Protodioscin is hydrolyzed to dioscin and further to the sapogenin diosgenin
- Intestinal microbiome composition may modulate effective bioavailability
- Membrane permeabilizing properties of saponins may enhance absorption of co-administered drugs (drug interaction mechanism — see Safety)

## Geographic Variability and Adulteration

### Geographic Influence on Saponin Content

- Climate, soil type, altitude, and developmental stage at harvest all affect saponin concentrations
- **Plant parts**: Roots, fruits, leaves, and stems have different saponin profiles; most clinical evidence uses fruit extracts
- **Fruits**: Highest protodioscin content; standardized pharmaceutical preparations use fruits
- **Aerial parts (herba)**: Lower and more variable saponin content; EMA could not establish traditional-use monograph for herba

### Quality Concerns

- **Adulteration risk**: Štefănescu 2020 flags ongoing adulteration risk in commercial TT products
- The extreme concentration variability (1–1,530 mg protodioscin/100g dry weight) makes quality assurance critical
- Products not standardized to furostanol saponin content may contain negligible active compounds

## Structure-Activity Considerations

### Saponin Membrane Effects

- Saponins possess membrane-permeabilizing properties (detergent-like action on phospholipid bilayers)
- This property is relevant to:
  1. Enhanced absorption at high doses (potential for toxicity)
  2. Interaction with drug membrane transporters
  3. The CYP3A4 inhibition mechanism (possibly mediated by saponin effects on hepatic metabolism)

### DHT vs Testosterone Mechanism

- **Salgado 2017**: TT extract standardized to protodioscin raised DHT significantly in infertile men; testosterone-to-DHT conversion proposed
- **GamalEl Din 2019**: Total testosterone raised in late-onset hypogonadism men (2.2 → 2.7 ng/dL)
- **Neychev 2005 and Santos 2014**: No testosterone effect in healthy eugonadal young men
- **De Souza 2016 / Vale 2018**: Free and bioavailable testosterone increased in women
- **Synthesis**: The mechanism likely involves modulation of androgen binding and metabolism rather than direct LH-mediated testosterone synthesis (LH was unchanged in Neychev 2005)

## Summary: Active Compounds at a Glance

| Compound Class | Key Member | Standardization Role | Evidence Link |
|---------------|-----------|---------------------|--------------|
| Furostanol saponins | Protodioscin | Primary marker (40–45%) | DHT increase (Salgado 2017); testosterone support (GamalEl Din 2019) |
| Spirostanol saponins | Diosgenin | Not standardized | Unknown clinical contribution |
| Flavonoids | Kaempferol, quercetin, rutin | Not standardized | Anti-inflammatory, lipid effects |

**Key research gap**: No human pharmacokinetic data on any constituent; all PK data from animal models. No comparative studies of furostanol-standardized vs spirostanol-standardized extracts.
