# Tribulus terrestris - Clinical Uses

## Evidence-Based Clinical Applications

### 1. Erectile Dysfunction (Male)

**Evidence Quality: Probable Benefit — Meta-Analysis Support**

#### Standard Dosing Protocols

**Kamenov 2017 (Tribestan® — Best-Characterized Preparation)**
- **Preparation**: Tribestan® film-coated tablets (Sopharma, Bulgaria)
- **Dose**: 1,500 mg/day standardized extract (6 × 250 mg tablets)
- **Furostanol saponins**: ~675 mg/day (≥ 45% per tablet)
- **Frequency**: 3 × 2 tablets daily after meals
- **Duration**: 12 weeks
- **Sample size**: 172 completers (86 per arm); enrolled 180

**Results (Kamenov 2017)**:
- IIEF score: significantly improved vs placebo (difference 2.70, p < 0.0001)
- Significant improvements in: intercourse satisfaction, orgasmic function, sexual desire, overall satisfaction
- Well tolerated; no drug-related serious adverse events

**Santos 2014 — Negative Trial (800 mg/day, 30 days)**
- Dose: 800 mg/day (2 divided doses); n = 30 (15/15 split)
- Duration: 30 days
- Results: No significant difference vs placebo
  - TT IIEF-5: 13.2 → 15.3; Placebo IIEF-5: 11.6 → 13.7 (p not significant)
- **Interpretation**: Short duration (30 days) and small sample may explain negative result

#### Meta-Analysis Results

**Suharyani 2025 Meta-Analysis (8 RCTs)**:
- IIEF-5 improved from baseline: MD 4.21 (p < 0.00001)
- IIEF-15 improved from baseline: MD 15.88 (p = 0.0004)
- TT outperformed placebo on IIEF-5: MD 3.23
- TT outperformed placebo on IIEF-15: MD 14.44
- No significant difference in total testosterone vs placebo
- Adverse event rate similar to placebo

**Vilar Neto 2025 Systematic Review (10 trials; 483 participants)**:
- 3 of 5 studies measuring erectile function showed improvements
- Range of doses studied: 400–750 mg/day; duration 1–3 months
- Evidence quality limited by low Jadad scores in 50% of included studies

#### Clinical Effect Sizes — Erectile Dysfunction
| Metric | Finding | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| IIEF-5 vs placebo | MD 3.23 | Suharyani 2025 |
| IIEF-15 vs placebo | MD 14.44 | Suharyani 2025 |
| IIEF vs placebo | difference 2.70, p < 0.0001 | Kamenov 2017 (n=172) |
| IIEF: 10.7 → 16.1 | p < 0.001 | GamalEl Din 2019 (n=70, hypogonadal) |

### 2. Female Sexual Dysfunction (Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder)

**Evidence Quality: Likely Beneficial — Multiple RCTs with Positive Outcomes**

#### Postmenopausal Women

**De Souza 2016 (n=36 completers; enrolled 45)**:
- **Dose**: 750 mg/day TT
- **Duration**: 120 days (4 months)
- **Population**: Postmenopausal women with HSDD

**Results (QS-F questionnaire)**:
- Sexual desire: significant improvement (P < 0.01)
- Arousal/lubrication: significant improvement (P = 0.02)
- Pain: significant reduction (P = 0.02)
- Anorgasmia: significant improvement (P < 0.01)

**Results (FSFI)**:
- Improvement across all domains except lubrication
- Free and bioavailable testosterone levels significantly increased (P < 0.05)

#### Premenopausal Women

**Vale 2018 (n=40 premenopausal women with diminished libido)**:
- **Dose**: Not specified; study protocol consistent with 250–750 mg range
- **Duration**: Study duration consistent with 1–3 months

**Results**:
- Total FSFI score: significant improvement vs placebo (p < .001)
- Desire domain: significant improvement (p < .001)
- Arousal domain: significant improvement (p < .001)
- Satisfaction domain: significant improvement (p < .001)
- Placebo group: no comparable improvements
- Free and bioavailable testosterone: increased in TT group

#### Systematic Review — Female Sexual Dysfunction

**Martimbianco 2020 (5 RCTs; 279 participants)**:
- Women receiving TT showed significant increase in sexual function scores after 1–3 months
- 3 months of treatment produced significant increase in serum testosterone in premenopausal women
- No serious adverse events reported across included trials
- Evidence certainty: **very low** (clinical heterogeneity prevented meta-analysis)

### 3. Male Infertility / Sperm Quality

**Evidence Quality: Probable Benefit — Limited by Methodological Quality**

#### Semen Parameter Trials

**Salgado 2017 (n=65 men with abnormal semen evaluation)**:
- **Preparation**: Androsten® (250 mg dried TT extract per capsule; active compound: protodioscin)
- **Outcome**: Significant enhancement of sperm concentration, motility, and liquefaction time
- Secondary findings: Significant decrease in body fat percentage; significant increase in lean mass; significant increase in dihydrotestosterone levels

**Sellandi 2012 (Ayurvedic RCT — Oligozoospermia; n=60 day study)**:
- **Preparation**: Gokshura (TT) granules, 60-day treatment
- **Population**: Males aged 21–50 with oligozoospermia
- TT group: 78.11% improvement in clinical parameters (vs placebo 70.95%)
- Significant improvement in total sperm count and semenogram analysis parameters

#### Systematic Review — Male Fertility

**Sanagoo 2019 (7 articles; from 5,775 initial records)**:
- 6 of 7 studies showed positive effects on sperm parameters
- Improvements documented in sperm number, motility, and morphology
- Only 1 study (quasi-experimental, no control arm) reported ineffectiveness
- **Caution**: Small sample sizes and methodological heterogeneity across studies

### 4. Late-Onset Hypogonadism / Testosterone Support

**Evidence Quality: Modest Effect — Restricted to Hypogonadal Men**

**GamalEl Din 2019 (n=70; 35 per arm)**:
- **Dose**: Three times daily (specific mg not reported in abstract)
- **Duration**: 3 months
- **Population**: Men with late-onset hypogonadism

**Results**:
- Arabic IIEF score: 10.7 → 16.1 (p < 0.001)
- Total testosterone: 2.2 → 2.7 ng/dL (p < 0.001)
- IPSS (lower urinary tract symptoms): 14.4 → 14.6 (p = 0.67) — no improvement
- AST modestly elevated: 26.5 → 27.8 (p = 0.03)
- Total PSA increased: 1.4 → 1.7 (p = 0.007)

**Systematic Review Context**:
- Vilar Neto 2025: Only 2 of 10 studies showed meaningful testosterone increases (~60–70 ng/dL)
- Testosterone increases occurred exclusively in hypogonadal subjects
- 8 of 10 studies showed no significant hormonal changes overall

**Neychev 2005 — Negative for Eugonadal Young Men (n=21)**:
- Doses: 20 mg/kg/day (n=7) and 10 mg/kg/day (n=7) vs placebo (n=7); 4 weeks
- Testosterone: TT1 15.75±1.75, TT2 16.32±1.57, controls 17.74±1.09 nmol/L (p > 0.05)
- Androstenedione: No significant differences (p > 0.05)
- Luteinizing hormone: No significant differences (p > 0.05)
- **Conclusion**: No androgen-increasing properties in young eugonadal men

### 5. Athletic Performance and Sports Biomarkers

**Evidence Quality: No Clear Benefit — Possible Testosterone Maintenance Effect**

**Fernández-Lázaro 2021 RCT (n=30 CrossFit-trained males; 15/15 split)**:
- **Dose**: 770 mg/day TT extract (empty stomach); 40% saponins (385 mg saponins/day)
- **Duration**: 6 weeks
- **Testosterone finding**: Intervention group maintained levels (−0.20% change) vs controls declined (−21.85%); p < 0.05, η²p = 0.495
- **Bench press**: 69.17±11.58 kg → 80.00±11.40 kg (+19.22% vs placebo +8.52%)
- **Body composition**: No significant differences between groups
- **Other lifts** (squat, deadlift): No significant differences between groups
- **Interpretation**: TT may attenuate exercise-induced testosterone decline rather than increase baseline testosterone

**Fernández-Lázaro 2022 Systematic Review (7 studies)**:
- Significant improvements in lipid profile in TT groups
- Moderate beneficial effects on inflammatory and hematological markers
- No meaningful changes to kidney function
- No clear evidence of beneficial effects on muscle damage indicators
- No clear evidence of beneficial hormonal responses in athletes

## Minimum Effective Dose and Duration

- **Minimum effective trial**: 4–8 weeks at standard doses to assess response
- **Most RCTs**: 30 days to 12 weeks; longest female sexual dysfunction trial: 120 days
- **Critical observation**: Santos 2014 (30 days, 800 mg/day) was negative; 12-week Kamenov 2017 trial was positive — duration appears to matter for erectile dysfunction outcomes

---

## Mechanism of Action (Clinical Inference)

- **Saponin-mediated**: Furostanol saponins (particularly protodioscin) are the proposed active fraction
- **DHT pathway**: Salgado 2017 showed significant DHT increases; protodioscin may facilitate conversion of testosterone to DHT
- **Free testosterone increase**: Both De Souza 2016 and Vale 2018 documented increases in free and bioavailable testosterone in women
- **Testosterone maintenance (athletes)**: Fernández-Lázaro 2021 suggests an attenuating effect on exercise-induced testosterone decline rather than anabolic action
- **Non-androgenic pathways**: Suharyani 2025 found no testosterone change vs placebo in erectile dysfunction trials, suggesting additional peripheral mechanisms (nitric oxide, smooth muscle, psychogenic)
