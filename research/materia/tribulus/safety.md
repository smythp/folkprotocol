# Tribulus terrestris - Safety Profile

## Overall Safety Assessment

**Summary**: At standard clinical doses (250–750 mg/day), TT is generally well tolerated with mild, infrequent adverse effects similar to placebo in controlled trials. However, serious adverse events have been documented in case reports involving high doses, extended use, specific preparations (water extracts), and drug interactions. The LiverTox database rates TT as **E*** — unproven but possible rare cause of clinically apparent liver injury at high doses or in patients with pre-existing liver disease.

**Safety Landscape**:
- Controlled trials (12 weeks): No serious adverse events in major RCTs; adverse event rates similar to placebo
- Case reports: Multi-organ toxicity documented including hepatorenal injury, rhabdomyolysis, priapism
- Drug interactions: CYP3A4 moderate inhibition confirmed (atorvastatin interaction case)
- Regulatory: No serious safety signals at standard doses; high-dose/long-term safety data lacking

---

## Common Adverse Effects at Standard Doses

**Sources**: Major RCTs (Kamenov 2017, GamalEl Din 2019, Santos 2014, De Souza 2016)

**Mild, transient adverse effects**:
- Gastrointestinal discomfort (nausea, dyspepsia, bloating)
- GI discomfort more likely on empty stomach
- No serious adverse events reported in any major RCT

**From Suharyani 2025 meta-analysis (8 RCTs)**:
- Adverse event incidence: No significant difference vs placebo

**From LiverTox database**:
- At typical doses (250–750 mg/day): not convincingly linked to liver damage
- One documented case at typical dose showed injury more likely attributable to concurrent anabolic steroid use (confounded)

## Serious Adverse Event Case Reports

### 1. Hepatorenal Injury (Mohy-Ud-Din 2024)

**Source**: PMID 38328764 — ACG Case Reports Journal

**Case**: 46-year-old male; TT supplements (marketed as testosterone booster) taken daily for 2 months

**Laboratory values**:
- Peak total bilirubin: 48 mg/dL
- Peak creatinine: 7.1 mg/dL
- Follow-up bilirubin after treatment: 1.1 mg/dL

**Pathology**: Liver biopsy — marked cholestasis

**Treatment**: 3 plasmapheresis sessions; bilirubin decreased after each session

**Outcome**: Full recovery; bilirubin normalized at follow-up

**Clinical significance**: High-dose or prolonged use may cause severe cholestatic hepatitis and acute kidney injury. Dose and preparation details not specified — "testosterone booster" products often contain unstandardized extracts at high doses.

---

### 2. Multi-Organ Toxicity — Nephrotoxicity, Hepatotoxicity, Neurotoxicity (Talasaz 2010)

**Source**: PMID 20667992 — Nephrology Dialysis Transplantation

**Case**: Iranian male; consumed herbal water extract reportedly for kidney stone prevention; duration: 2 days; presented with seizure

**Manifestations**:
- Nephrotoxicity
- Hepatotoxicity (very high serum aminotransferases)
- Neurotoxicity (seizure)

**Outcome**: Improvement and normalization of liver enzymes after discontinuation

**Clinical significance**: Multi-organ toxicity possible even with short-term use. Water extracts may deliver saponins in different concentrations/formulations than standardized extracts. Safety is NOT established across all preparations. The kidney stone prevention indication (a traditional use) was associated with serious toxicity.

---

### 3. Rhabdomyolysis via CYP3A4 Drug Interaction (Huff 2024)

**Source**: PMID 39012853 — American Journal of Case Reports

**Case**: 71-year-old male on long-term atorvastatin; developed rhabdomyolysis after adding TT supplementation

**Mechanism**: TT is a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor; inhibition of atorvastatin metabolism → elevated statin plasma levels → statin-induced muscle toxicity (rhabdomyolysis)

**Treatment**: Discontinuation of both substances plus aggressive hydration; lab markers peaked day 4 then improved

**Clinical significance**:
- Drug interaction with statins (atorvastatin, simvastatin, lovastatin) is a confirmed, serious risk
- Patients on any CYP3A4-metabolized medication should be screened before initiating TT
- This interaction applies to a large class of commonly prescribed drugs

---

### 4. Priapism (Štefănescu 2020 Review)

**Source**: Documented in Štefănescu et al. 2020 (Biomolecules); original case report

**Case**: Priapism lasting 72 hours attributed to TT use

**Clinical significance**: Rare but serious sexual adverse event. Relevant given TT's use for erectile dysfunction — paradoxical prolonged erection is a known risk category for phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor drugs and some herbal aphrodisiacs.

---

### 5. LiverTox Reference Case

**Source**: NIH LiverTox database; LiverTox rating: E*

**Documented case**: 30-year-old bodybuilder; bilirubin 36 mg/dL, ALT 90 U/L, creatinine 3.1 mg/dL; bland cholestasis pattern

**Confound**: Case authors noted injury was more likely due to unacknowledged anabolic steroid use (common in bodybuilder population)

**Rating explanation**: E* = Unproven but possible rare cause of clinically apparent liver injury at high doses or in those with pre-existing liver disease

## Drug Interactions

### Confirmed Interaction

**CYP3A4 Moderate Inhibition**:
- **Mechanism confirmed** in Huff 2024 rhabdomyolysis case (atorvastatin)
- **Theoretical basis**: Saponin effects on hepatic enzyme activity
- **Risk level**: Serious potential interaction requiring clinical screening

**High-risk co-medications (CYP3A4 substrates)**:
- Statins: Atorvastatin, simvastatin, lovastatin (statin toxicity risk)
- Immunosuppressants: Cyclosporine, tacrolimus (narrow therapeutic window)
- Benzodiazepines: Midazolam, triazolam
- Calcium channel blockers: Amlodipine, diltiazem
- Antifungals, antiretrovirals, and many others

### Membrane Permeabilization — General Interaction Mechanism

- Saponins possess membrane-permeabilizing properties
- May enhance absorption of co-administered drugs across gastrointestinal membranes
- Effect direction and magnitude are preparation-dependent and not well characterized
- **Clinical implication**: Avoid combining TT with narrow-therapeutic-index drugs

### Androgenic Medications (Theoretical)

- Additive androgenic effects when combined with testosterone replacement therapy or DHEA
- Could elevate DHT beyond desired ranges (given proposed testosterone-to-DHT conversion mechanism)
- **No clinical data available**; theoretical concern

## Laboratory Findings — Monitoring Considerations

### PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen)

**GamalEl Din 2019** (n=70 men with late-onset hypogonadism):
- PSA: 1.4 → 1.7 (p = 0.007) after 3 months TT treatment
- **Clinical significance**: Statistically significant increase; modest absolute change
- **Monitoring recommendation**: Baseline and follow-up PSA measurements recommended in men using TT, particularly those with BPH or prostate cancer history

### Liver Enzymes

**GamalEl Din 2019**:
- AST: 26.5 → 27.8 (p = 0.03) — modest, statistically significant elevation
- All values within normal range; no clinical symptoms
- **Monitoring recommendation**: Baseline liver function and follow-up in patients with pre-existing hepatic disease or prolonged use

### Livestock Toxicology (Context for Human Safety Interpretation)

- In grazing livestock: steroidal sapogenins (from TT) form crystals in bile ducts and renal tubules causing hepatogenous photosensitivity and locomotor disorders
- **Human relevance**: Uncertain; livestock consume large quantities from extensive grazing; human exposure via standardized extracts is different in magnitude and preparation
- The crystalline biliary deposition mechanism may be relevant to the cholestatic injury pattern seen in human case reports

## Contraindications

### Evidence-Based Contraindications

**1. Pre-existing liver disease**
- LiverTox E* rating specifically flags this population
- Mohy-Ud-Din 2024 case of severe cholestatic hepatitis supports caution
- **Recommendation**: Avoid use; if clinical benefit considered essential, hepatic monitoring required

**2. Pre-existing kidney disease**
- Talasaz 2010 (nephrotoxicity + hepatotoxicity + seizure after 2 days of water extract)
- Mohy-Ud-Din 2024 (creatinine 7.1 mg/dL at peak)
- **Recommendation**: Avoid in any degree of renal impairment

**3. Concurrent statin therapy (CYP3A4 interaction)**
- Huff 2024: Confirmed rhabdomyolysis in patient on atorvastatin
- **Recommendation**: Screen all patients for statin use before initiating TT; if on statins, avoid TT or reduce statin dose under medical supervision

**4. Concurrent CYP3A4-metabolized medications**
- Any co-medication dependent on CYP3A4 for metabolism represents a potential interaction
- **Recommendation**: Review full medication list before initiating TT

### Precautionary Contraindications

**5. Pregnancy**
- No clinical data available; androgenic mechanism raises theoretical concerns for fetal development
- **Recommendation**: Avoid during pregnancy (precautionary)

**6. Breastfeeding**
- No data on excretion in breast milk
- **Recommendation**: Avoid during lactation (precautionary)

**7. Prostate cancer history or elevated PSA**
- PSA elevation documented in GamalEl Din 2019; proposed androgenic mechanism (DHT increase)
- **Recommendation**: Avoid in men with prostate cancer or significant BPH; monitor PSA if used

**8. History of hormone-sensitive conditions**
- Potential androgenic effects (free testosterone, DHT elevation)
- **Relevant conditions**: Testosterone-sensitive tumors (some breast cancers), polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS) in women seeking low-androgen state

## Special Populations

### Elderly (>65 years)
- Huff 2024 case was 71-year-old on statins — heightened drug interaction risk in polypharmacy patients
- CYP3A4 inhibition more clinically significant when co-medications are present
- **Recommendation**: Screen for polypharmacy; avoid if on any CYP3A4 substrate

### Young Healthy Males
- Clinical trials show good tolerability (Neychev 2005, Santos 2014)
- No serious adverse events in young healthy male populations at standard doses
- Prostate cancer and statin concerns not typically relevant

### Women (Reproductive Age)
- De Souza 2016 and Vale 2018 trials: Well tolerated; no serious adverse events in premenopausal and postmenopausal women
- Theoretical androgenic effects — avoid if androgenic state is clinically undesirable
- Avoid during pregnancy and lactation

### Athletes
- Fernández-Lázaro 2021 (n=30 CrossFit athletes, 6 weeks): No adverse events; well tolerated
- No TT-induced toxicity in Fernández-Lázaro 2022 systematic review of athlete studies

---

## Quality and Adulteration Risks

- **Extreme protodioscin variability**: 1–1,530 mg/100g dry weight by geographic origin creates inherent unpredictability in unstandardized products
- **Adulteration**: Unscrupulous products may contain anabolic steroids or other adulterants (flagged in Štefănescu 2020; liver cases are harder to attribute when products may be adulterated)
- **Water extracts**: Associated with the most severe toxicity case (Talasaz 2010 — multi-organ failure after 2 days); unstandardized water preparations should be considered higher risk than pharmaceutical-grade standardized extracts
- **Recommendation**: Use only standardized preparations from reputable manufacturers; avoid traditional water extracts for therapeutic use

## Summary: Safety at a Glance

| Domain | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| Common adverse effects | Mild GI (nausea, dyspepsia); similar to placebo |
| Serious adverse events in RCTs | None documented |
| Serious adverse events (case reports) | Hepatorenal injury, rhabdomyolysis (CYP3A4), multi-organ toxicity, priapism |
| LiverTox rating | E* (possible rare liver injury, high dose/pre-existing disease) |
| Drug interactions | CYP3A4 moderate inhibitor — confirmed serious risk with statins |
| PSA monitoring | Recommended in men (significant increase documented) |
| Contraindications | Liver/kidney disease, statin therapy, CYP3A4 medications, pregnancy |
| Population safety | Well tolerated in healthy adults at standard doses in controlled trials |
| Duration safety data | Most trials ≤12 weeks; no long-term safety data (>3 months) available |
