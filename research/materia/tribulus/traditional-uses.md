# Tribulus terrestris - Traditional Uses

## Overview

Tribulus terrestris has been used as a medicinal plant for thousands of years across multiple traditions in China, India, Sudan, and Pakistan. It is known by different names in each tradition and has distinct, regionally specific applications. It received official pharmacopoeia status in China, Japan, Korea, and Siddha India, but has no WHO monograph, no Commission E assessment, and failed to achieve an EMA traditional-use monograph.

---

## Ayurvedic Medicine (India)

**Traditional Name**: *Gokshura* (गोक्षुर) — from Sanskrit for "cow's hoof," referring to the shape of the spines

**History**: Used for millennia in Ayurvedic practice; documented in classical Sanskrit medical texts

**Traditional Applications**:
- Infertility (male and female)
- Impotence / erectile dysfunction
- Low libido
- General vitality and strength
- Urinary tract disorders
- Kidney stones (diuretic use)
- Edema

**Traditional Preparations in Ayurveda**:
- **Gokshura granules** (choorna): Dried fruit powder; used in clinical study (Sellandi 2012) for oligozoospermia treatment
- **Decoctions**: Fruit boiled in water
- **Gokshuradi Guggulu**: Compound formulation combining Gokshura with other herbs; used for urinary complaints and kidney stones
- **Kashaya**: Aqueous extracts of fruit

**Pharmacopoeia Status**: Included in the Siddha Pharmacopoeia of India, Volume 1 (2008)

**Evidence Bridge to Modern Use**: The Sellandi 2012 double-blind RCT was conducted within the Ayurvedic framework using Gokshura granules for oligozoospermia, providing a modern evidence bridge for this traditional application.

## Traditional Chinese Medicine (China)

**Traditional Name**: *Ci Ji Li* (刺蒺藜) — "puncture vine fruit"; also *Bai Ji Li* (白蒺藜)

**Historical Use**: Documented in traditional Chinese pharmacopoeias and folk medicine for thousands of years

**Traditional Indications** (as documented in Chinese Pharmacopoeia and referenced in Zhu et al. 2017):
- Tonifying the kidneys (*Bu Shen*)
- Diuretic use
- Cough and expectoration
- Improving eyesight / treating eye conditions
- Skin pruritus (itching)
- Headache and vertigo
- Mammary duct blockage / insufficient lactation
- Liver *qi* stagnation (headache, dizziness from this pattern)

**Part Used**: Fruits (*Tribuli Fructus*)

**Pharmacopoeia Status**:
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2005 edition — monograph included
- Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2015 edition — monograph included (referenced in Zhu et al. BMC Chemistry 2017)

**Traditional Preparation**:
- Typically decocted in water with other herbs as part of compound formulations
- Dry fruit preparation; standardized to fruit material in pharmacopoeia

**Note on Indication Differences**: TCM applications for Ci Ji Li focus predominantly on liver and kidney tonification, eye conditions, and skin disorders — notably different from Western and Ayurvedic emphasis on sexual function and fertility.

## Other Asian Traditions

### Japanese Pharmacopoeia

- **Edition**: 16th Edition (2012)
- **Status**: Official pharmacopoeia monograph
- **Plant part**: Fruits
- Used as a traditional crude drug (*Shitsu Rei Ko* — Japanese name)

### Korean Pharmacopoeia

- **Edition**: 9th Edition (2007)
- **Status**: Official pharmacopoeia monograph
- **Plant part**: Fruits

## Traditional Use in Sudan, Pakistan, and North Africa

**Historical Records** (referenced in Zhu et al. 2017):
- Used as folk medicine for thousands of years across the Indian subcontinent, Middle East, and North Africa
- Sudan: Traditional use as an aphrodisiac and vitality tonic
- Pakistan: Use in Unani medicine for sexual debility, urinary conditions, and general tonic
- The geographic breadth of traditional use reflects the plant's wide natural distribution (weedy annual found globally in tropical and subtropical zones)

---

## European Traditional Use — Absence of Documentation

**EMA Assessment**:
- The European Medicines Agency conducted a formal assessment report on *Tribulus terrestris* L. herba (aerial parts)
- **Finding**: No information on traditional use of TT herb found in searched literature
- **Outcome**: A traditional-use EU monograph could not be established
- **Regulatory implication**: TT does not meet EMA criteria for "traditional herbal medicinal product" status in the EU

**Commission E (Germany)**:
- Not included in German Commission E monographs (active period 1984–1994)
- TT did not receive assessment; evidence base was not established for European use during this period

**Interpretation**: Despite broad traditional use in Asia and South Asia, TT has no documented traditional use in Europe and did not enter the European herbal tradition. This contrasts sharply with its long-standing status in Ayurvedic and Chinese pharmacopoeias.

---

## WHO Status

- No WHO monograph exists for Tribulus terrestris
- This absence is significant: plants with extensive international traditional use often receive WHO monographs as a reference standard. The absence for TT likely reflects the regionally concentrated nature of its use and the limited pre-2000 Western pharmacological literature.

## Traditional Energetics and Characterizations

**Ayurveda**:
- Rasa (taste): Sweet (madhura)
- Guna (quality): Heavy (guru), unctuous (snigdha)
- Virya (potency): Cooling (shita)
- Vipaka (post-digestive taste): Sweet (madhura)
- Dosha effect: Pacifies Vata and Pitta; may increase Kapha in excess
- Actions: *Vrishya* (aphrodisiac), *Mutrala* (diuretic), *Balya* (strengthening), *Rasayana* (rejuvenating)

**TCM**:
- Nature: Warm; pungent, bitter
- Meridians: Liver, lung
- Actions: Disperses liver *qi* stagnation, tonifies liver and kidney, dispels wind-dampness

## Relationship Between Traditional Use and Modern Evidence

| Traditional Use | Modern Evidence Status |
|----------------|----------------------|
| Aphrodisiac/male virility (Ayurveda, Unani) | Supported: erectile dysfunction RCTs positive |
| Male infertility treatment (Ayurveda) | Supported: sperm parameter improvements in 6/7 studies |
| Female libido (Ayurveda) | Supported: FSFI improvements in premenopausal and postmenopausal women |
| Kidney tonic, diuretic (TCM, Ayurveda) | Not well studied in modern trials |
| Eye conditions (TCM) | No modern clinical evidence |
| Skin pruritus (TCM) | No modern clinical evidence |
| Headache/vertigo (TCM) | No modern clinical evidence |

The convergence between Ayurvedic/Unani sexual function indications and positive modern clinical evidence is notable. The TCM indications (eye, skin, headache) have not been explored in modern pharmacological research.
