# White Willow Bark — Clinical Uses

*Salix alba / Salicis cortex*

## Low Back Pain

**Strongest evidence base.** Two landmark RCTs and one active-comparator trial establish dose-dependent efficacy for acute exacerbations of chronic low back pain.

### Chrubasik 2000 (n=210, 4 weeks)
Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, three-arm trial. Acute exacerbation of chronic low back pain (VAS ≥5/10).

| Group | Dose | Pain-free at 4 weeks |
|-------|------|---------------------|
| High dose | 240 mg salicin/day | **39%** (27/65), p<0.001 vs placebo |
| Low dose | 120 mg salicin/day | 21% (15/67), p<0.001 vs placebo |
| Placebo | — | 6% (4/59) |

- Dose-dependent effect confirmed
- High-dose group showed measurable improvements **within one week**
- Placebo group required significantly more rescue tramadol throughout
- One severe allergic reaction; otherwise well-tolerated

### Chrubasik 2001 — vs. Rofecoxib (n=228, 4 weeks)
Randomized controlled comparative trial. Chronic low back pain.

| Outcome | Willow Bark (240 mg salicin) | Rofecoxib |
|---------|------------------------------|-----------|
| Response rate | ~60% | ~60% |
| Pain-free (VAS <2) | ~20 patients | ~20 patients |
| Aarhus Index improvement | ~20% | ~20% |
| Pain component improvement | ~30% | ~30% |
| Total pain index improvement | ~35% | ~35% |

- No statistically significant difference between willow bark and rofecoxib
- Willow bark (Assalix) was **approximately 40% less expensive** than rofecoxib
- Non-inferiority to a COX-2 inhibitor at lower cost

### Economic Study — Chrubasik 2001 (n=451, postmarketing)
Open non-randomized study across three groups (W120, W240, conventional treatment).

- W240 group: ~40% pain-free at 4 weeks vs ~18% conventional treatment
- Cost reduction: 14–40% less than conventional treatment (W240); 35–50% less (W120)
- Multivariable modelling identified W240 as independently associated with better outcomes

## Osteoarthritis

**Moderate evidence — mixed RCT results, positive observational data.**

### Schmid 2001 (n=78, 2 weeks)
Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind. Osteoarthritis patients.

- **Dose:** 240 mg salicin/day (standardized extract)
- **WOMAC pain:** 14% reduction from baseline (treatment) vs 2% **increase** in placebo (p=0.047)
- Patient assessment: willow bark superior (p=0.0002)
- Investigator assessment: willow bark superior (p=0.0073)
- Well-tolerated; moderate analgesic effect

### Biegert 2004 — vs. Diclofenac (n=127, OA arm)
Randomized, double-blind, three-arm trial (willow bark 240 mg salicin / diclofenac 100 mg / placebo).

| Group | Pain Reduction (VAS) |
|-------|---------------------|
| Willow bark | 8 mm (17%) |
| Diclofenac | 23 mm (47%) |
| Placebo | 5 mm (10%) |

- Willow bark vs placebo: **not statistically significant** (negative trial)
- Diclofenac substantially outperformed both
- Provides important context: willow bark is not comparable to full-dose NSAIDs in OA

### Beer 2008 — Gonarthrosis/Coxarthrosis (n=139, 6 weeks)
Controlled cohort study. Knee and hip osteoarthritis.

- Willow bark extract comparable to standard therapies in effectiveness by week 6
- Trend toward better outcomes in chronic cases (>3 months duration)
- **0 adverse drug effects** in willow bark group vs reflux symptoms in conventional group
- Willow bark rated better tolerability than conventional therapy

### Uehleke 2013 — Long-term (n=436, 6 months)
Multicenter observational study. Mixed population: 56.2% osteoarthritis, 59.9% back pain.

- Baseline pain VAS: 58.4 ± 22.6 mm
- Pain at 24 weeks: 31.8 ± 22.5 mm
- Week 3 reduction: ~26 mm (45.6% improvement from baseline)
- Relative reduction maintained at 33–44% throughout study period
- Extract: STW 33-I (Proaktiv®), drug-extract-ratio 16–23:1; concurrent NSAIDs permitted when necessary

## Rheumatoid Arthritis

**Insufficient evidence.**

- Biegert 2004 RA arm (n=26): 8 mm vs 2 mm pain reduction — not statistically significant
- Critically underpowered; no firm conclusions possible
- Vlachojannis 2009 systematic review: no significant effect demonstrated, attributed to insufficient study power

---

## Meta-Analysis Summary

**Lin 2023** (6 RCTs, n=329, arthritis patients):
- Pain relief: SMD −0.31 (95% CI −0.53 to −0.08; p=0.007) — significant vs placebo
- Physical function: WOMAC SMD −0.80 (95% CI −1.08 to −0.53) — significant
- Safety: No significant difference in adverse event risk vs analgesics or placebo (OR 1.37; p=0.26)
- Certainty: Very low — potential bias, small sample sizes; additional trials needed

## Dosing by Indication

| Indication | Dose | Duration | Source |
|-----------|------|----------|--------|
| Acute low back pain | 240 mg salicin/day | 4 weeks | Chrubasik 2000, 2001 |
| Chronic low back pain | 240 mg salicin/day | 4+ weeks | Chrubasik 2001 |
| Osteoarthritis | 240 mg salicin/day | 2–6 weeks minimum | Schmid 2001; Beer 2008 |
| Long-term rheumatic pain | 240 mg salicin/day | Up to 6 months | Uehleke 2013 |

**Dose-response confirmed:** 240 mg salicin significantly more effective than 120 mg (Chrubasik 2000).

## Regulatory Indications

| Body | Established Indication |
|------|----------------------|
| EMA/HMPC (2017) | Low back pain (temporary relief), symptomatic relief of mild fever, headache, minor joint pain |
| ESCOP (2017) | Low back pain, mild rheumatic conditions, fever associated with common cold, headache |
| German Commission E | Febrile illnesses, rheumatic complaints, headaches |
| WHO | Febrile conditions, mild rheumatic complaints, back pain, headache |

*Note: Fever and headache indications are historically established but lack RCT evidence.*
