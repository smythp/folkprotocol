# White Willow Bark — Preparations and Dosing

*Salix alba / Salicis cortex*

## Overview

All dosing for therapeutic use is standardized to **salicin content**. The clinically effective dose established in RCTs is **240 mg salicin/day** (effective for low back pain and moderately for osteoarthritis). A lower dose of **120 mg salicin/day** shows less pronounced but still statistically significant benefit.

---

## Standardized Extracts (Primary Evidence-Based Form)

### Clinical Standard Dose
| Dose | Evidence Level | Effect |
|------|---------------|--------|
| **240 mg salicin/day** | Strong (2 RCTs, 1 active comparator trial) | 39% pain-free vs 6% placebo at 4 weeks (low back pain) |
| **120 mg salicin/day** | Moderate (1 RCT) | 21% pain-free vs 6% placebo at 4 weeks (low back pain) |

**Dose-response confirmed** (Chrubasik 2000): 240 mg significantly outperforms 120 mg.

### Commercial Extracts Used in Research
| Product | Standardization | Daily Dose | Studies |
|--------|----------------|------------|---------|
| **Assalix** | 15.2% salicin | 1,572 mg extract = 240 mg salicin | Chrubasik 2000, 2001; März 2002 |
| **STW 33-I (Proaktiv®)** | Drug-extract-ratio 16–23:1 | Not specified in mg | Uehleke 2013 |

### General Dosing Formula
- To achieve 240 mg salicin/day: extract standardized to 15% salicin → ~1,600 mg extract
- Extract doses without standardization are not equivalent and should not be substituted

---

## Decoction (Traditional Form)

**WHO Monograph standard:** 6–12 g powdered bark daily, in two divided doses

| Parameter | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Bark amount | 1–2 teaspoons (approximately 2–4 g) per cup |
| Water | 240 ml (8 oz) cold water |
| Method | Bring to boil, simmer 10–15 minutes, strain |
| Frequency | 2–3 times daily to reach 6–12 g total |
| Equivalent | Aims to provide 120–240 mg salicin/day (variable by bark quality) |

**Important caveat:** Salicin content in raw bark varies by species, part of bark, harvest time, and processing. Decoctions cannot guarantee standardized salicin delivery. Clinical efficacy data is based on standardized extracts, not raw bark preparations.

---

## Tea / Infusion

- **Less effective than decoction** for bark material; boiling is required to adequately extract phenolic glycosides from bark
- Simple hot water infusion (steeping) extracts less salicin than a proper decoction
- Not a recommended primary preparation if therapeutic dosing is the goal

---

## Tincture

| Parameter | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Ratio | Typically 1:5 (herb:solvent) |
| Solvent | 25–40% ethanol (ethanolic extracts used in most studies) |
| Dose | 5–8 ml three times daily (traditional herbalist dosing; not standardized to salicin) |
| Salicin delivery | Variable; standardization essential for therapeutic equivalence |

*Note: Tinctures appear in traditional pharmacy but are not the preparation used in RCTs. Ethanol extract studies use defined extract preparations, not artisan tinctures.*

---

## Fluid Extract

| Parameter | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Ratio | 1:1 (herb:solvent) |
| Use | Historical pharmacy; declined with standardized extract development |
| Dosing | 1–2 ml three times daily (traditional; not evidence-based) |

---

## Duration Guidelines

| Indication | Duration | Evidence Source |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| Acute low back pain flare | **4 weeks** | Chrubasik 2000, 2001 |
| Osteoarthritis | **2–6 weeks minimum** | Schmid 2001; Beer 2008 |
| Chronic rheumatic pain | **Up to 6 months** (with monitoring) | Uehleke 2013 (observational) |
| Traditional fever/headache | Short-term (days) | WHO, Commission E; no RCT data |

---

## Pharmacopoeial Dosing (Regulatory Standards)

| Body | Oral Dose | Form |
|------|----------|------|
| WHO | 120–240 mg salicin/day | Extracts, tinctures, fluidextracts, or powdered drug |
| WHO (decoction equivalent) | 6–12 g powdered bark/day | Two divided doses |
| EMA/HMPC | Well-established use: standardized extracts to 120–240 mg salicin | |
| ESCOP | Equivalent to 120–240 mg salicin/day | |
| German Commission E | Not specified in mg; traditional forms referenced | |

---

## Notes on Preparation Selection

1. **For evidence-based therapeutic use:** Standardized extract (240 mg salicin/day) is the only preparation with RCT support
2. **For traditional/tonic use:** Decoction of 6–12 g bark/day follows WHO guidance; salicin content variable
3. **Do not assume equivalence** between raw bark, tinctures, and standardized extracts — the clinical evidence base is specific to standardized preparations
4. **Quality marker:** Always check that products specify salicin content, not just extract weight, when purchasing commercial preparations
