# White Willow Bark — Active Compounds

*Salix alba / Salicis cortex*

## Primary Salicylate Constituents

The phenolic glycosides are the primary regulated constituents and serve as quality control markers.

| Compound | Type | Role |
|---------|------|------|
| **Salicin** | Phenolic glycoside (prodrug) | Primary quality marker; converted to salicylic acid in vivo |
| **Salicortin** | Acylated phenolic glycoside | Additional salicylate precursor |
| **Acetyl-salicortin** | Acylated phenolic glycoside | Additional salicylate precursor |
| **Tremulacin** | Acylated phenolic glycoside | Additional salicylate precursor |
| **Salicyl alcohol (saligenin)** | Phenolic alcohol | Intermediate metabolite |

### Salicin as Prodrug
Salicin is metabolized to salicylic acid in the body via two sequential steps:
1. **Intestinal bacteria:** Cleave glucose from salicin → saligenin (salicyl alcohol)
2. **Hepatic oxidation:** Saligenin → salicylic acid (the active metabolite)

This two-step conversion distinguishes willow bark from aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid), which is absorbed intact and rapidly hydrolyzed.

## Secondary Actives (Polyphenols and Flavonoids)

**Critical insight from pharmacokinetic research (Schmid 2001; Vlachojannis 2011):** The salicylate fraction alone is insufficient to explain willow bark's full clinical effect. At 240 mg salicin/day, serum salicylate levels are equivalent to only ~87 mg acetylsalicylic acid — far below analgesic doses of synthetic salicylates (typically 500–1000 mg). The polyphenol and flavonoid fractions contribute independently.

| Compound Class | Contribution |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Polyphenols** | Anti-inflammatory activity independent of salicylate pathway; TNF-α and NF-kB modulation |
| **Flavonoids** | Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects; multiple pathway action |
| **Tannins** | Astringent; minor anti-inflammatory contribution |

Shara & Stohs 2015 (literature review) specifically identifies polyphenols and flavonoids as co-contributors to therapeutic effect alongside salicin.

## Standardization

### Primary Quality Marker
**Salicin** is the pharmacopoeial standardization marker across all major regulatory frameworks:

| Standard | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| European Pharmacopoeia | Minimum salicin content; identification tests; chromatographic fingerprinting |
| EMA/HMPC (2017) | Active constituents: salicin, salicortin, acetyl-salicortin, tremulacin |
| WHO Monograph | Daily dose expressed as 120–240 mg total salicin |
| USP Safety Review | Doses in studies: 120–240 mg salicin/day |

### Commercial Extract Standardization
- **Assalix:** 1,572 mg/day extract standardized to 15.2% salicin = **240 mg salicin/day** (used in Chrubasik 2000, 2001; März & Kemper 2002)
- **STW 33-I (Proaktiv®):** Drug-extract-ratio 16–23:1 (used in Uehleke 2013)
- Note: Standardization captures only the salicin fraction; polyphenol/flavonoid content varies by preparation and is not routinely controlled

## Pharmacokinetics

### Source: Schmid, Kötter & Heide 2001
*Clinical pharmacokinetic study; n=10 healthy volunteers; protocol: 240 mg salicin (1,360 mg total extract) in two doses 3 hours apart*

| Parameter | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Oral bioavailability** | **43.3%** |
| Peak serum salicylic acid | 1.2 mg/L |
| **Time to peak** | **<2 hours** |
| AUC equivalent | ~87 mg acetylsalicylic acid |

### Serum Metabolite Profile
| Metabolite | % of serum salicylates |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Salicylic acid | 86% |
| Salicyluric acid | 10% |
| Gentisic acid | 4% |

### Urinary Elimination
| Metabolite | % of urinary salicylates |
|-----------|------------------------|
| Salicyluric acid | 71% |
| Salicylic acid | 15% |
| Gentisic acid | 14% |

### Clinical Implication
Serum salicylate levels from therapeutic willow bark doses (240 mg salicin) are far below analgesic doses of synthetic salicylates. This confirms that **salicylic acid formation alone cannot explain clinical efficacy** — polyphenols and flavonoids must contribute to therapeutic effect.

## Mechanism of Action

### Confirmed Pathways
1. **Salicin → salicylic acid conversion:** Anti-inflammatory via COX pathway (minor contribution at therapeutic doses)
2. **TNF-α downregulation:** Anti-inflammatory cytokine modulation (Shara & Stohs 2015)
3. **NF-kB inhibition:** Master inflammatory pathway suppression (Shara & Stohs 2015)
4. **Flavonoid/polyphenol activity:** Independent anti-inflammatory effect; broader than aspirin mechanism (Vlachojannis 2011)

### Distinctions from Aspirin
| Property | White Willow Bark | Aspirin |
|---------|-------------------|---------|
| Mechanism | Multifactorial (salicylates + polyphenols + flavonoids) | Primarily COX-1/COX-2 inhibition |
| Antiplatelet effect | **Minimal** at standard doses (240 mg salicin) | Significant; used therapeutically for anticoagulation |
| GI mucosal damage | **Does not damage gastric lining** | Direct gastric irritant |
| Speed of action | Slower (requires metabolic conversion) | Faster |
| Serum equivalent | ~87 mg aspirin from 240 mg salicin | Direct dose |

*Source: Vlachojannis 2011 (mechanism letter); März & Kemper 2002 (review)*
