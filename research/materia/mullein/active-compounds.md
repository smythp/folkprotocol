# Mullein — Active Compounds

*Verbascum thapsus L. | Evidence as of 2026-02-28*

---

## Key Marker Compounds

### Verbascoside (Acteoside)
- **Class:** Phenylethanoid glycoside
- **Typical content:** Up to 254.8 mg/mL in leaf aqueous extract (Calabrese et al. 2021, HPLC-DAD)
- **Status:** Predominant polyphenol; most studied constituent
- **Pharmacological roles:**
  - Anti-inflammatory: inhibits ICAM-1 expression, reduces nitric oxide (NO•), inhibits COX-2 and iNOS
  - Antioxidant
  - Antimicrobial
- **Bioavailability:** Undergoes significant reduction in antioxidant activity after simulated in vitro digestion; phenolic acids more stable than intact glycosides; no human PK data

### Aucubin
- **Class:** Iridoid glycoside
- **Pharmacological roles:** Anti-inflammatory
- **Safety note:** Aglycone form (liberated from aucubin by bacterial hydrolysis) has DNA-binding potential and inhibits replication/transcription enzymes — EFSA 2021 genotoxic concern flag at chronic high doses
- **Content in tincture:** ~0.004% average (EFSA 2021 analysis)

### Saponins
- **Class:** Triterpenoid glycosides
- **Pharmacological roles:**
  - Expectorant via saponin-mediated mucociliary stimulation (proposed mechanism for traditional cough use)
  - Antiviral via membrane disruption

### Hesperidin
- **Class:** Flavanone glycoside
- **Pharmacological roles:** Antioxidant; anti-inflammatory; antiviral (demonstrated in related Verbascum species)

### Quercetin
- **Class:** Flavonol
- **Typical content:** 17.29 mg/g (Grigore et al. 2013, V. phlomoides)
- **Pharmacological roles:** Anti-inflammatory; antioxidant; in silico COX/inflammatory pathway inhibition
- **Note:** In silico molecular docking (Blanco-Salas 2021) supports quercetin as key mechanistic compound explaining traditional anti-inflammatory folk uses

## Full Phytochemical Profile (Calabrese 2021 — Leaf Extract)

Quantified by HPLC-DAD in aqueous leaf extract:
- Verbascoside: 254.8 mg/mL
- Iso-verbascoside: 142.9 mg/mL
- Forsythoside B: 108.1 mg/mL
- Martynoside: 118.4 mg/mL
- Echinacoside: 42.1 mg/mL
- Samioside: 52.3 mg/mL
- Total phenolic content: 124.0 ± 0.7 mg gallic acid equivalent (GAE)/g extract

**V. phlomoides flower polyphenol profile (Grigore 2013):**
- Total polyphenols: 4.18% expressed as gallic acid
- Rosmarinic acid: 14.93 mg/g
- Caffeic acid: 39.96 mg/g
- Ferulic acid: 29.61 mg/g
- Quercetin: 17.29 mg/g

**Additional constituents (Gupta 2022 review):**
- Coumarin
- Iridoid glycosides (beyond aucubin)
- Vitamin C
- Minerals
- GC-MS identified: 1-hexanol (2.11%) and 2-hexene (1.95%) in ethanolic extract (potential anticarcinogenic components, Mahdavi 2020)

---

## Standardization Status

**No standardized extract definition exists for commercial oral mullein preparations as of 2026.** Unlike Echinacea (standardized to alkylamides or caffeic acid derivatives) or Ginkgo (standardized to flavone glycosides and terpene lactones), mullein has no pharmacopoeial or industry consensus standardization marker.

The EMA monograph authorizes traditional preparations (infusion, tincture) without requiring standardized extract quantification.

---

## Bioavailability Notes

- No human pharmacokinetic studies for any V. thapsus extract as of 2026
- Verbascoside: antioxidant activity significantly reduced after simulated in vitro digestion; phenolic acids more stable than intact glycosides
- Aucubin pharmacokinetics characterized in animal models (Zeng et al. 2020, cited in EFSA 2021) — no published human PK data
- Aqueous/traditional preparations likely carry lower aucubin aglycone risk than concentrated ethanolic tinctures (based on extraction differences)

## Antiviral Activity (In Vitro)

Source: Zgórniak-Nowosielska et al. 1991 (PMID 1666504), lyophilized flower infusion of V. thapsiforme:
- Influenza A and B: 1–3 log unit reduction in virus titers
- Herpes simplex virus (HSV): 2.3 log unit titer reduction
- At 300 µg/mL: direct virucidal inactivation of HSV (did not directly inactivate influenza)
- Active constituents: flavonoids, iridoids, phenolic acids, saponins, amino acids, free sugars

---

## Anti-inflammatory Mechanistic Data (In Vitro)

Source: Calabrese et al. 2021 (PMCID PMC8434610), V. thapsus leaf extract:
- 50–100 µg/mL in LPS-stimulated RAW 264.7 macrophages (6 days): significantly reduced NO• levels; superior to Harpagophytum procumbens positive control at 50 µg/mL
- 100 µg/mL in IL-1β-stimulated human chondrocytes (6 days): significant inhibition of IL-6, COX-2, iNOS; significant reduction of MMP-1, MMP-8, MMP-9 expression

Source: Grigore et al. 2013 (PMID 23627472), V. phlomoides:
- Significantly inhibited TNF-α-induced ICAM-1 expression by 55–58.8% in human endothelial cells at higher concentrations
- Failed to reduce egg-white-induced rat paw edema in vivo
