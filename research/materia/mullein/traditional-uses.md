# Mullein — Traditional Uses

*Verbascum thapsus L. | Evidence as of 2026-02-28*

---

## Overview

Mullein has at least 30 years documented traditional use, with at least 15 years within the EU — sufficient for EMA traditional-use registration. Primary traditional applications center on the respiratory tract, skin, and ear. Traditional use of root extracts differs from flower/leaf use.

---

## European / Western Herbal Tradition

**Respiratory applications (most documented):**
- Sore throat relief
- Dry cough, irritant cough
- Bronchitis and pulmonary congestion
- Asthma and spasmodic coughs
- Used as both expectorant and demulcent/soothing agent

**Source:** EMA monograph 2018; Turker & Gurel 2005 (Phytotherapy Research); Turker & Camper 2002 (J. Ethnopharmacol.)

**Preparations in European tradition:**
- Dried flower infusion: steeped 10–15 minutes, strained through fine cloth to remove leaf hairs
- Tincture: 1:5 in 25% ethanol
- Poultices of leaves for skin conditions
- Flower oil infusions for ear complaints

**Other traditional European indications:**
- Diarrhoea
- Migraine headaches
- Inflammatory diseases (general)
- Wound healing and skin conditions

---

## Spanish Folk Medicine

**Source:** Blanco-Salas et al. 2021 (Biology, PMID 34356473) — review of 10 Verbascum species documented in Spain's Traditional Knowledge inventory

Traditional indications documented in Spanish folk medicine:
- Respiratory diseases (primary use)
- Otitis (ear inflammation)
- Arthrosis and rheumatism
- General inflammatory conditions

In silico molecular docking analysis suggests anti-inflammatory action of constituents (particularly quercetin) provides mechanistic explanation for these traditionally documented uses.

---

## North American Traditional Use

**Source:** Turker & Gurel 2005; Gupta et al. 2022

- Dried leaves, flowers, capsules, and alcohol extracts widely available in US health stores by 2005
- Used for pulmonary problems, inflammatory diseases, asthma, spasmodic coughs
- Root extracts used historically for toothache, stiffness, and seizures

## Traditional Preparations

| Preparation | Method | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flower infusion (tea) | 1.5–2 g dried flower per 150 mL boiling water, steep 10–15 min | Must strain through fine cloth to remove irritant hairs |
| Leaf infusion | 1–2 tsp (approx. 1.5–4 g) per 150–250 mL boiling water | Same straining requirement |
| Tincture | 1:5 in 25% ethanol | EMA-approved preparation |
| Dried herb powder | Mixed with honey for palatability | |
| Flower oil | Flower-infused olive oil | Traditional ear drops base |
| Leaf poultice | External application | Skin and wound use |

## Scope Notes

- EMA monograph covers four species: V. thapsus L., V. densiflorum Bertol., V. thapsiforme Schrad., V. phlomoides L. — all with similar constituent profiles, interchangeable in traditional preparations
- Common names include: Common mullein, Great mullein, Verbascum, Torchweed, Flannel leaf, Mullein flower (Verbasci flos)
- Traditional use basis acknowledged as valid by EMA for cough/throat indication; clinical trial evidence not required for this approval category
