# Mullein — Preparations and Dosing

*Verbascum thapsus L. | Evidence as of 2026-02-28*

---

## Oral Preparations

### Herbal Tea / Infusion (Flower)
- **EMA-approved preparation**
- Dose: 1.5–2 g dried flower per 150 mL boiling water
- Steep: 10–15 minutes
- Frequency: 3–4 times daily
- Duration: Maximum 1 week without medical supervision (EMA recommendation)
- **Critical:** Must strain through fine cloth to remove irritant leaf/flower hairs
- Source: EMA/HMPC monograph 2018 (EMA/HMPC/611537/2016)

### Herbal Tea / Infusion (Leaf)
- Dose: 1–2 teaspoons (approx. 1.5–4 g) dried leaf per 150–250 mL boiling water
- Steep: 10–15 minutes
- Frequency: 3–4 times daily
- **Critical:** Must strain through fine cloth — leaf hairs are particularly irritating
- Source: Traditional pharmacopoeia convention; Gupta et al. 2022 review

### Tincture
- **EMA-approved preparation**
- Ratio: 1:5 in 25% ethanol
- Daily dose: 15–40 mL per day in divided doses
- Source: EMA/HMPC monograph 2018

### Dried Herb Powder / Capsule
- Dose: 1–3 g per dose, 2–3× daily
- Can be mixed with honey for palatability when taken directly
- Source: Traditional pharmacopoeia convention; Turker & Gurel 2005 (commercial forms noted by 2005)

## Topical Preparations

### Wound Cream (V. thapsus extract)
- **Source:** Taleb & Saeedi 2021 RCT (PMID 34103042)
- Dose: 2 cm of V. thapsus cream applied to episiotomy wound twice daily × 10 days
- Outcome: Significantly improved REEDA score at day 10 vs. placebo (p = 0.01)
- Note: Only RCT data available for topical V. thapsus; wound-healing context only

### Ear Drops (Multi-herb formulation)
- **Source:** Sarrell et al. 2001 (PMID 11434846) and 2003 (PMID 12728112)
- Preparation: Naturopathic herbal extract ear drops (Otikon/NHED) — mullein combined with Allium sativum, Calendula flores, Hypericum perforatum (±lavender, vitamin E) in olive oil base
- Dose: 5 drops instilled in affected ear, 3× daily
- Note: Mullein is 1 of 4–6 botanical components; individual contribution indeterminate. Traditional ear oil preparations use flower-infused olive oil as the base.

## Commercial Availability (as of 2005, Turker & Gurel)

Commercially available forms in US health stores by 2005:
- Dried leaves and flowers
- Capsules
- Alcohol (tincture) extracts
- Flower oil

---

## Species Interchangeability

EMA monograph covers four species under "Verbasci flos" — all considered equivalent for the approved indication:
- Verbascum thapsus L.
- V. densiflorum Bertol.
- V. thapsiforme Schrad.
- V. phlomoides L.

---

## Standardization Notes

No standardized extract definition exists for commercial mullein preparations. No extract is standardized to a defined percentage of verbascoside, aucubin, or any other marker compound. Product quality and consistency therefore vary between manufacturers. This is a significant evidence gap compared to herbs like Echinacea or Ginkgo.
