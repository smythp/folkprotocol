# Mullein — Safety

*Verbascum thapsus L. | Evidence as of 2026-02-28*

---

## Overall Safety Profile

At therapeutic doses, V. thapsus has a favorable safety record within documented traditional use. The EMA 2018 monograph notes no adverse effects reported at therapeutic doses as of assessment date. Long-term safety data are absent; a genotoxicity concern exists for the aucubin aglycone at high/chronic concentrations, particularly in concentrated tinctures.

---

## Contraindications

- **Known hypersensitivity to Verbascum species or Scrophulariaceae (figwort family)**
- Source: EMA/HMPC monograph 2018

---

## Age Restrictions

- **Children < 12 years:** Not recommended due to lack of safety data
- Source: EMA 2018 monograph

---

## Duration Limits

- Treatment should not exceed **1 week without medical supervision** (oral preparations for cough/throat)
- Source: EMA 2018 monograph

---

## Pregnancy and Lactation

- No data identified in the bibliography
- EMA monograph does not list specific pregnancy/lactation guidance, which typically implies insufficient data to confirm safety — standard practice is to avoid unsupervised use in these populations

---

## Drug Interactions

- No drug interactions documented in the bibliography sources reviewed
- Gap: no formal drug-drug interaction studies identified

---

## Genotoxicity — Critical Analysis

**EFSA 2021 Genotoxicity Concern (Animal Feed Context):**
- Source: EFSA Journal 2021, 19(7):e06711 (PMCID PMC8317052)
- Concern: Aucubin aglycone (liberated from aucubin glycoside) can bind DNA and inhibit replication/transcription enzymes
- Aucubin content in tincture: ~0.004% average
- EFSA conclusions:
  - Tincture safe at maximum 50 mg/kg complete feed for short-lived fattening animals
  - Maximum 600 mg/kg complete feed for horses
  - No conclusions possible for long-lived animals (e.g., cattle, pigs used as breeders) due to genotoxic concerns
  - No consumer safety concerns at approved use levels for food-producing species

**Murine Micronucleus Study (Escobar et al. 2011, PMID 21834240):**
- Design: In vivo genotoxicity, mouse bone marrow micronucleus assay
- Extract type: Methanolic extract of V. thapsus
- Doses: 100, 300, 500 mg/kg
- Results: **No genotoxic effects detected at any dose**
  - No significant increase in micronucleated polychromatic erythrocytes (MNPCE)
  - PCE/NCE ratios not significantly affected vs. controls
  - No bone marrow toxicity
- Authors' conclusion: Results "provide security in its use as herbal medicine"

**Reconciling the two findings:**
- The methanolic extract study (Escobar 2011) showed no genotoxicity at up to 500 mg/kg in mice
- The EFSA concern is specific to the aucubin aglycone in concentrated ethanolic tinctures in animal feed (very different exposure scenario — chronic, high-dose, concentrated)
- Methanolic and aqueous preparations may extract lower concentrations of aucubin and are less likely to produce free aglycone than concentrated ethanolic tinctures
- Traditional aqueous infusion preparations likely carry lower genotoxic risk than concentrated tinctures at high chronic doses

**Practical implication:** At standard therapeutic doses (EMA-approved infusion or tincture), short-term use (≤1 week) appears safe based on traditional use record and available safety data. Caution warranted for high-dose, long-term, or concentrated tincture use — particularly in populations with compromised DNA repair capacity.

---

## Leaf Hair Safety Note

- Mullein leaves are covered in dense hairs (trichomes) that can be irritating to mucous membranes
- All infusions/teas made from mullein leaf or flower must be strained through a fine cloth or cheesecloth before drinking
- This is standard practice in all traditional preparations — failure to strain properly is an avoidable irritation risk

---

## Antimicrobial Activity and Oral Microbiome

- In vitro: Gram-positive bacteria (e.g., Bacillus cereus) more sensitive to V. thapsus extract than Gram-negative strains (Mahdavi 2020)
- Water extracts inhibited Klebsiella pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus, Staphylococcus epidermidis, and E. coli in bioassay (Turker & Camper 2002)
- Clinical significance of these concentrations at standard oral doses is unclear

---

## Evidence Gaps in Safety

- No long-term (>1 week) human safety data
- No human pharmacokinetic/bioavailability studies
- No formal drug interaction studies
- No pregnancy/lactation safety data
- Genotoxic potential of aucubin aglycone at chronic high doses not fully characterized in humans
