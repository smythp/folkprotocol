# Pau d'Arco (Handroanthus impetiginosus) - Preparations and Dosing

## Overview

No validated dosing standard exists for pau d'arco from any major regulatory body (WHO, Commission E, EMA/HMPC, ESCOP, AHP, USP). Dosing guidance derives from traditional use patterns and the single published clinical safety trial. Preparation type critically determines the phytochemical profile and likely bioactivity.

## Medicinal Part

**Inner bark** of trunk or branches (*cortex*), not the outer bark.
- Inner bark comprises ~88% of traditional preparations documented across Brazil, Bolivia, and Argentina (Bezerra 2022)
- The inner bark contains the naphthoquinone-rich phloem layer
- Commercial products should specify inner bark; outer bark products are less desirable

## Traditional Decoction (Primary Traditional Preparation)

**Standard preparation**:
- 2–4 g dried inner bark per 250 mL (one cup) water
- Simmer (not boil) for 15–20 minutes; strain

**Dosing**:
- 1–3 cups per day (traditional South American practice)
- Duration: typically 2–4 weeks for acute conditions; longer for chronic conditions in traditional practice

**Bioavailability notes**:
- Lapachol is poorly water-soluble; aqueous decoctions deliver very low naphthoquinone content relative to ethanol or encapsulated preparations
- Heat during decoction may degrade some phenolic constituents (Pires 2015, PMID 26703544)
- The immunosuppressive activity demonstrated in Böhler 2008 used an aqueous extract; only the aqueous extract showed activity (not ethanol extract), suggesting the responsible polar constituent is water-extractable and different from lapachol/beta-lapachone
- Traditional efficacy, if real, may be more related to flavonoids, phenolic glycosides, and other polar constituents than to naphthoquinones

## Encapsulated Dried Bark / Standardized Extract (Clinical Trial Dose)

**Only human clinical trial dose** (McClure et al. 2022, PMID 36960315):
- **Dose**: 1050 mg/day *Tabebuia avellanedae* inner bark powder
- **Frequency**: Divided doses (specific split not detailed in publication)
- **Duration**: 8 weeks (safety trial)
- **Standardization**: The encapsulated preparation was used but naphthoquinone content was not reported or standardized against lapachol/beta-lapachone
- **Outcome**: Generally safe in n=9 completers; significant pain reduction (no control group); mild AEs in 75%

**Commercial encapsulated products**:
- Typically 300–500 mg per capsule; 1–3 capsules 2–3 times daily
- Standard range approximates 600–1500 mg/day total daily dose
- NOTE: Commercial pills and syrups show dramatically reduced in vitro bioactivity vs. laboratory methanolic extracts (GI50 >400 μg/mL for commercial pills vs. 76.67–110.76 μg/mL for methanolic extract; Pires 2015)

## Tincture

**No validated tincture ratio or dosing standard from any regulatory monograph exists** (no Commission E, ESCOP, or AHP monograph published).

**General guidance based on botanical medicine conventions** (not validated in clinical trials):
- **Ratio**: 1:4 or 1:5 (dried bark : menstruum)
- **Menstruum**: 40–60% ethanol (higher ethanol improves naphthoquinone extraction; lapachol has poor water solubility)
- **Typical dose**: 2–4 mL three times daily (6–12 mL/day total)
- **Duration**: Standard herbalism guidance suggests 4–6 weeks with breaks for chronic conditions

**Bioavailability note**: Ethanol-based tinctures are expected to deliver higher naphthoquinone content than aqueous preparations, but this has not been measured in clinical pharmacokinetic studies.

## Standardization Markers and Quality Considerations

### Marker Compounds
- **Lapachol**: Primary naphthoquinone marker; no quantitative standard established for commercial products
- **Beta-lapachone**: Secondary naphthoquinone marker; not standardized in commercial products

### Quality Concerns
- **High inter-product variability**: Commercial products show markedly variable naphthoquinone content (Gómez Castellanos 2009, PMID 18992801)
- **Species mislabeling**: *T. impetiginosa*, *T. avellanedae*, and *H. impetiginosus* used interchangeably on labels; other *Handroanthus* species may also be sold as pau d'arco
- **Commercial supplement bioactivity**: Pills and syrups tested in vitro showed no cytotoxic activity at concentrations up to 400 μg/mL, while methanolic laboratory extracts were active at 76.67–110.76 μg/mL (Pires 2015)

### Selecting a Quality Product
Given the absence of official standards, quality indicators to look for:
1. Species clearly identified as *Handroanthus impetiginosus* or *Tabebuia impetiginosa*
2. Inner bark specified (not outer bark)
3. Ethanol-extracted or standardized (methanolic/ethanolic extraction preferred for naphthoquinone content)
4. Third-party testing for identity and lapachol content
5. Certificate of Analysis provided by manufacturer

## No Human Pharmacokinetic Data

No human PK studies exist for:
- Lapachol from whole-bark extract in humans
- Beta-lapachone from whole-bark extract in humans
- Any pau d'arco constituent in botanical context

**Only available PK data** are from isolated/synthetic compound Phase I trials:
- Lapachol (NCI Phase I, 1974): oral; therapeutic plasma concentrations caused unacceptable toxicity
- ARQ 761 (Gerber 2018, PMID 30318513): synthetic beta-lapachone analogue, IV only; 2-hour infusion; MTD 390 mg/m² q2wks; dose-dependent PK characterized — not applicable to oral botanical

## Comparison of Preparation Types

| Preparation | Naphthoquinone Yield | Traditional Use | Clinical Data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bark decoction (aqueous) | Low (poor solubility) | Primary traditional | Only polar-constituent activity studied |
| Ethanol tincture | Moderate-high | Modern supplement | None |
| Encapsulated bark powder | Variable (depends on standardization) | Modern supplement | 1050 mg/day (McClure 2022); not standardized |
| Commercial pills/syrups | Low in vitro activity | Modern supplement | None; reduced bioactivity vs. extracts |
| Methanolic extract (lab) | High | Research use only | In vitro studies only |
