# Pau d'Arco (Handroanthus impetiginosus) - Safety

## Human Clinical Safety Data

### Single Clinical Trial (Botanical)
**McClure et al. 2022 (PMID 36960315)** — only published human safety data for pau d'arco:
- Population: n=12 enrolled; n=9 completers; healthy women ages 18–45
- Dose: 1050 mg/day *Tabebuia avellanedae* encapsulated extract for 8 weeks
- Adverse event rate: 75% (9/12 participants) experienced at least one adverse event
- Serious adverse events: None (0%)
- Severity: Most AEs were mild
- Laboratory monitoring: Prothrombin time, CBC, hepatic and renal panels — most remained within normal range; few mild clinically insignificant abnormalities noted; no clinically significant coagulation changes
- Limitations: Very small sample (n=9 completers); only 8 weeks; preparation not standardized for naphthoquinone content; no control arm; long-term safety completely unknown

## Drug Interactions

### Anticoagulant / Antiplatelet Drugs — PRIMARY INTERACTION CONCERN

**Mechanism**: Lapachol inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase and vitamin K quinone reductase — the same enzymatic mechanism as warfarin (coumarins). This was demonstrated in human subjects in the NCI Phase I trial (Block 1974, PMID 4614904): anticoagulant toxicity at doses required for antitumor efficacy, ultimately leading to trial termination.

**Clinical relevance at botanical doses**:
- The anticoagulant risk at traditional decoction doses is uncharacterized in humans
- Gómez Castellanos 2009 (PMID 18992801) notes that whole-bark tea may have this effect partially offset by co-occurring pro-vitamin K compounds; however, this has not been studied clinically
- **Practical guidance**: Avoid pau d'arco with warfarin, heparin, aspirin, clopidogrel, and other anticoagulant/antiplatelet agents until drug interaction studies exist

**Warfarin interaction risk**: HIGH theoretical risk; clinical magnitude unknown at botanical doses

### Immunosuppressants — Clinically Relevant Concern

**Mechanism**: Aqueous extract of *T. avellanedae* inner bark produces dose-dependent, IL-2-independent inhibition of T-lymphocyte proliferation and CD25/CD71 expression in human cells (Böhler 2008, PMID 18158117). This immunosuppressive activity may:
- Augment immunosuppressant drugs (tacrolimus, cyclosporine, mycophenolate) → excess immunosuppression
- Interact with immune checkpoint inhibitors used in oncology

**Context**: Böhler study was conducted in transplant immunology context; the mechanism and magnitude at botanical doses in vivo are not established.

### Other Potential Interactions
- **NSAIDs**: Additive anti-inflammatory effects possible; no human data
- **Chemotherapy**: Beta-lapachone mechanism involves topoisomerase I/II inhibition; theoretical pharmacodynamic interaction with topoisomerase-targeting drugs (e.g., irinotecan, topotecan, etoposide); no human data for the botanical

## Contraindications

### Pregnancy
- **Status**: Contraindicated
- **Basis**: Fetotoxic effects reported in animal studies (specific study details not captured in bibliography)
- **Evidence level**: Animal data; no human pregnancy studies exist
- **Recommendation**: Avoid use during pregnancy

### Anticoagulant Therapy
- **Status**: Contraindicated (or requires close monitoring with INR tracking)
- **Basis**: Vitamin K antagonist mechanism of lapachol (Block 1974); risk of additive anticoagulation with warfarin and other anticoagulants
- **Recommendation**: Do not use with warfarin or other anticoagulants without close medical supervision and INR monitoring

### Pre-Surgical Period
- **Recommendation**: Discontinue pau d'arco at least 2 weeks before surgery due to anticoagulant risk (standard herbalmedicine guidance for vitamin K antagonist herbs)

### Lactation
- No human safety data exist for use during lactation; use not recommended

## Genotoxicity

**Lemos et al. 2012 (PMID 22888300)** — in vivo rat genotoxicity study (alkaline comet assay):

| Dose (oral, Wistar rats) | DNA Damage Index (liver) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Negative control | 46.3 | No damage |
| 100 mg/kg | Not significantly different from control | No significant genotoxicity |
| 300 mg/kg | Significantly elevated | Significant genotoxic effect |
| 500 mg/kg | 230.6 | High genotoxicity |

- Liver cells showed substantially higher DNA damage than blood cells at genotoxic doses
- Dose-dependent relationship confirmed
- The 100 mg/kg dose in rats (approximately the allometric equivalent of traditional human doses) showed no significant genotoxicity
- The 1050 mg/day human trial dose falls below the threshold associated with genotoxicity in the allometric comparison
- **Human genotoxicity data are entirely absent**: these are animal data only

## Adverse Effects Profile

### At Botanical Doses (Traditional/Supplement Use)
- Mild adverse events in 75% of participants in the only human trial (8-week, 1050 mg/day, n=9 completers)
- Nature of adverse events not detailed in available summary data
- No serious adverse events at this dose and duration

### At High Lapachol Doses (NCI Phase I, not botanical)
- Nausea and vomiting (dose-dependent GI toxicity)
- Anticoagulant effect (vitamin K antagonism) — dose-limiting
- These data apply to isolated lapachol, not whole-herb preparations

### At Beta-Lapachone Analogue Doses (Phase I IV drug, not botanical)
- Anemia: 79% (most common; dose-limiting at MTD)
- Fatigue: 45%
- Hypoxia: 33% (Grade 3 hypoxia in 26%; possibly methemoglobinemia-related via NQO1 mechanism)
- Nausea: 17%
- Vomiting: 17%
- These are class-effect toxicities of the NQO1-mediated mechanism at IV pharmacological doses; not applicable to whole-herb botanical preparations

### Theoretical Concerns from Preclinical Data
- Hemolytic anemia: reported as a limiting toxicity in animal studies; not observed in the single small human pilot trial at 1050 mg/day

## Regulatory Safety Assessment

No major regulatory body (WHO, EMA/HMPC, Commission E, ESCOP, AHP) has published a safety assessment for pau d'arco. The absence of official monographs means:
- No established maximum safe dose
- No defined contraindication list from a regulatory authority
- No pregnancy/lactation classification from any herbal regulatory body
- Safety guidance available only from integrative medicine databases (e.g., MSKCC) and the single published human trial

## Summary of Key Safety Signals

| Risk | Evidence Basis | Clinical Magnitude at Botanical Doses |
|---|---|---|
| Anticoagulant interaction | Lapachol mechanism (human, NCI Phase I) | Unknown; potentially significant |
| Immunosuppressive interaction | Human cell data (Böhler 2008) | Unknown |
| Genotoxicity | Rat study ≥300 mg/kg (Lemos 2012) | Below threshold at standard doses; no human data |
| Fetotoxicity/pregnancy | Animal studies | Unknown in humans; avoid |
| Hemolytic anemia | Animal studies | Not observed in human pilot |
| Mild GI/other AEs | Human pilot trial (McClure 2022) | Mild; 75% rate at 1050 mg/day, 8 weeks |
