# Yarrow — Active Compounds

*Achillea millefolium L.*

---

## Overview

Yarrow contains a complex phytochemical profile across five major compound classes. Significant **chemotype variation** exists — different populations of *A. millefolium* can have substantially different essential oil compositions, which affects both pharmacological activity and quality standardization. No single universal marker captures the full therapeutic potential of the plant.

---

## 1. Sesquiterpene Lactones (Guaianolide Type)

**Primary compounds:** Achillin, achillicin, achillifolin, alpha-peroxyachifolid

| Compound | Activity | Notes |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Achillin | Anti-inflammatory | Guaianolide skeleton |
| Achillicin | Anti-inflammatory, antispasmodic | Guaianolide |
| Achillifolin | Anti-inflammatory | Guaianolide |
| **Alpha-peroxyachifolid** | **Primary contact sensitizer** | Principal allergen; causes contact dermatitis |

**Pharmacological roles:**
- Anti-inflammatory (COX-independent pathway via HNE inhibition — Benedek & Kopp 2007)
- Antispasmodic (smooth muscle relaxation)
- Contact allergenicity (cross-reactive with other Asteraceae sesquiterpene lactones)

**Note on chamazulene:** Chamazulene is a sesquiterpene (not a lactone) formed as an artifact during steam distillation from the proazulene achillin. It is the primary essential oil marker for flos quality (≥0.02% required by European Pharmacopoeia) and is a potent CYP450 inhibitor.

---

## 2. Flavonoids

**Primary compounds:** Apigenin, luteolin, rutin, casticin, quercetin, kaempferol

| Compound | Activity | Key PK Data |
|----------|----------|-------------|
| **Apigenin** | Anti-inflammatory, antispasmodic, antioxidant | Half-life: **91.8 h** (accumulates); <40% excreted in 5 days |
| Luteolin | Anti-inflammatory, antispasmodic | Closely related to apigenin |
| Rutin | Antioxidant, vascular-protective | Standard flavonoid glycoside |
| Casticin | Anti-inflammatory, immunomodulatory | More specific to yarrow vs. other Asteraceae |
| **Quercetin** | Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, **P-gp inhibitor** | **Clinically significant DDI:** 413% ↑digoxin AUC |
| Kaempferol | Antioxidant | Half-life: 2.93–3.79 min (very rapid elimination) |

**Pharmacological roles:**
- Antispasmodic on smooth muscle (mediates GI and menstrual antispasmodic effects — Benedek & Kopp 2007)
- Anti-inflammatory
- Antioxidant
- P-glycoprotein inhibition (quercetin — drug interaction significance)

**Standardization status:** European Pharmacopoeia does not currently require flavonoid standardization for either herba or flos monographs. Flavonoid profile is a secondary quality marker in research contexts.

## 3. Essential Oil Constituents

**Monoterpenes constitute approximately 90% of essential oil by weight.**

### Key Compounds and PK Data

| Compound | Typical % EO | Key Activity | Pharmacokinetics |
|----------|-------------|-------------|------------------|
| **Camphor** | Variable (major) | Antimicrobial, analgesic, counterirritant | t½ 93–167 min; hepatic CYP450 metabolism; Tmax 1–3h |
| **1,8-Cineole** | Variable (major) | Antimicrobial, expectorant, anti-inflammatory | t½ **6.7 min** (extremely rapid); complete oral absorption |
| **Alpha-thujone** | Variable | Antimicrobial; **neurotoxic at excess** | LD50 45 mg/kg (mice); optimal intake 3–7 mg/day |
| **Chamazulene** | ≥0.02% (flos standard) | Anti-inflammatory; **CYP1A2/2C9/2D6/3A4 inhibitor** | Formed during distillation from proazulene achillin |
| Borneol | Minor | Antimicrobial, analgesic | — |
| **Camphene** | Variable | Antimicrobial | t½ **9.12 days** (very long accumulation risk) |
| **Limonene** | Variable | Antimicrobial, antioxidant | t½ 12–24 h; low carcinogenic/mutagenic potential |
| **Linalool** | Variable | Sedative, anti-inflammatory | t½ 3.2 h; ~10% renal excretion |

### Chemotype Variation

Yarrow displays pronounced chemotype diversity across its geographic range. Major chemotype categories:

| Chemotype | Dominant EO Component | Geographic Association |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Camphor type | Camphor dominant | Common in many European populations |
| Chamazulene type | High chamazulene, blue oil | More anti-inflammatory profile |
| Thujone type | Alpha-thujone dominant | Some European/Asian populations |
| Chrysanthenyl acetate type | Chrysanthenyl acetate dominant | Some populations |

**Clinical implication:** Chemotype significantly affects both therapeutic activity and drug interaction risk. Commercial preparations may not specify chemotype; chamazulene-poor preparations will have reduced CYP450 DDI risk but also reduced anti-inflammatory potency.

## 4. Caffeic Acid Derivatives

**Primary compounds:** Chlorogenic acid, dicaffeoylquinic acids (3,5-diCQA, 4,5-diCQA)

| Compound | Activity |
|----------|----------|
| Chlorogenic acid | Antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, hepatoprotective |
| Dicaffeoylquinic acids | **Choleretic (bile-stimulating)** — primary mediators of traditional GI/biliary use |

**Pharmacological role:** Benedek & Kopp (2007) specifically attributed the choleretic (bile secretion-stimulating) properties of yarrow to dicaffeoylquinic acids. This supports the traditional European use for liver and biliary complaints.

---

## 5. Alkaloid

**Primary compound:** Achilleine (betonicine)

| Compound | Activity |
|----------|----------|
| Achilleine | **Hemostatic** — reduces bleeding time; platelet-activating properties |

Achilleine is the primary pharmacological basis for yarrow's traditional hemostatic applications (wound healing, nosebleed arrest). Paradoxically, flavonoid and caffeic acid components may mildly inhibit platelet aggregation — the net hemostatic effect in therapeutic use is likely achilleine-dominant.

## Standardization Markers

### European Pharmacopoeia Standards

| Preparation | Required Marker | Minimum Specification |
|-------------|----------------|----------------------|
| **Millefolii herba** | Essential oil content | ≥2 mL/kg (dried drug basis) |
| **Millefolii flos** | Proazulenes (as chamazulene) | ≥0.02% (dried drug basis) |

### Research/Quality Context

| Marker | Relevance | Commercial Standardization? |
|--------|-----------|----------------------------|
| Essential oil ≥2 mL/kg (herba) | GC-MS identity, antimicrobial potency | Ph. Eur. required |
| Chamazulene ≥0.02% (flos) | Anti-inflammatory, DDI risk marker | Ph. Eur. required |
| Flavonoid profile | Antispasmodic, antioxidant | Not Ph. Eur. required |
| Sesquiterpene lactone profile | Anti-inflammatory, allergen content | Not standardized |
| Achilleine content | Hemostatic activity | Not standardized |
| Caffeic acid derivatives | Choleretic activity | Not standardized |

**Key gap:** No single universal marker captures the full therapeutic profile. Clinical trial preparations frequently lack standardization disclosure, preventing inter-study comparisons.

## Bioavailability Summary

| Compound | Tmax | Half-life | Route | Key Note |
|----------|------|-----------|-------|----------|
| Camphor | 1–3 h | 93–167 min | Oral | With solvent: Tmax 1h; alone: 3h |
| 1,8-Cineole | Rapid | **6.7 min** | Oral | Complete absorption; extremely rapid clearance |
| Apigenin | — | **91.8 h** | Oral | Long accumulation; <40% excreted in 5 days |
| Quercetin | — | — | Oral | P-gp inhibitor; 413% ↑digoxin AUC |
| Kaempferol | — | 2.93–3.79 min | Oral | Very rapid elimination |
| Camphene | — | **9.12 days** | Oral | Longest t½; accumulates significantly |
| Limonene | — | 12–24 h | Oral | Low toxicological concern |
| Linalool | — | 3.2 h | Oral | ~10% renal excretion |

**Data source:** Far et al. 2023 (PMID 38076118) — constituent-level PK review.

**Whole-herb PK gap:** No human in vivo pharmacokinetic study with standardized whole *A. millefolium* preparations measuring plasma concentrations exists. All PK data is constituent-level.

**Accumulation concern:** Apigenin (t½ 91.8 h) and camphene (t½ 9.12 days) both accumulate with daily dosing. Clinical significance at therapeutic doses not established.

## Drug Interaction Mechanisms (Phytochemistry Basis)

| Compound | Mechanism | Clinical Consequence |
|----------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Quercetin** | P-glycoprotein (P-gp/ABCB1) inhibition | **413% ↑digoxin AUC** (human PK study) |
| **Chamazulene** | CYP1A2 inhibition | ↑ levels of CYP1A2 substrates (e.g., theophylline, caffeine) |
| **Chamazulene** | CYP2C9 inhibition | ↑ warfarin, phenytoin levels |
| **Chamazulene** | CYP2D6 inhibition | ↑ codeine metabolites, antidepressants |
| **Chamazulene** | CYP3A4 inhibition | ↑ erythromycin, diazepam, cyclosporine levels |
