# Yarrow — Preparations

*Achillea millefolium L.*

---

## Overview

Yarrow is prepared from two official pharmacopoeial materials:
- **Millefolii herba** — dried aerial parts (stems, leaves, flowers)
- **Millefolii flos** — dried flowers only

Both are official in the European Pharmacopoeia with distinct quality standards. Commission E approved both forms; EMA has separate monographs for each. Flos preparations are considered more standardized and are often preferred for modern products.

---

## Regulatory Quality Standards

| Material | European Pharmacopoeia Minimum |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| **Millefolii herba** | Essential oil content ≥ **2 mL/kg** (dried drug) |
| **Millefolii flos** | Proazulenes expressed as chamazulene ≥ **0.02%** (dried drug) |

**Chemotype consideration:** Yarrow shows multiple essential oil chemotypes (camphor, chamazulene, thujone, chrysanthenyl acetate types). Chamazulene content is chemotype-dependent; the Ph. Eur. minimum of 0.02% for flos selects for proazulene-containing material. Commercial herba may be any chemotype meeting the 2 mL/kg essential oil threshold.

## 1. Infusion (Tea)

### Standard Infusion

**Material:** Dried *Millefolii herba* or *Millefolii flos*
**Preparation:** Steep 1–2 teaspoons (2–4 g) dried herb or flower in 1 cup (250 mL) freshly boiled water for 10–15 minutes; cover while steeping to retain volatile oils; strain.

**Regulatory dose (Commission E, 1990):**
- **2–4 g dried herb/flower, 3 times daily**
- Total daily dose: 6–12 g

**EMA duration limits:**
- GI complaints / appetite: maximum 2 weeks without physician consultation
- Menstrual cramps / wounds: maximum 1 week without physician consultation

**Notes on preparation:**
- Covering the cup during steeping retains aromatic essential oil constituents (camphor, 1,8-cineole)
- Hot infusion (covered) is the traditional diaphoretic preparation; drink hot for fever management
- Cold infusion (herb steeped in cold water overnight) is the traditional bitter preparation for appetite stimulation; drink before meals

**Traditional dysmenorrhea use:** Jenabi 2015 trial used teabag form for 3 days at onset of menstruation, per cycle × 2 cycles. Exact gram dose not quantified in the trial.

## 2. Tincture

### Standard Tincture

**Preparation:** 1:5 tincture in 25–40% ethanol (v/v)

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Drug:menstruum ratio | 1:5 |
| Alcohol strength | 25–40% (v/v) |
| Typical adult dose | 2–4 mL, 3× daily |
| Total daily dose | 6–12 mL |

**Higher-strength tincture:**
Some preparations use 1:2 or 1:3 ratios in 45–60% ethanol; dose adjusted proportionally downward.

**Notes:** Tinctures preserve both hydrophilic (flavonoids, caffeic acid derivatives, achilleine) and some lipophilic (sesquiterpene lactones) constituents. Essential oil constituents are partially captured in alcohol extracts. Tinctures are the preferred form for dysmenorrhea and digestive bitter use in contemporary Western herbal practice.

## 3. Encapsulated Dry Extract / Capsules

### As Used in Clinical Trials

| Trial | Dose | Duration | Outcome |
|-------|------|----------|---------|
| Daneshvar-Ghahfarokhi 2025 (T2D) | **500 mg/day** (encapsulated dried herb) | 90 days | ↓ ALT, AST, LDL, TG, total cholesterol |
| Ayoobi 2019 (MS) | **250 mg/day** (aqueous extract) | 1 year | ↓ relapse rate, improved cognitive function |
| Ayoobi 2019 (MS) | **500 mg/day** (aqueous extract) | 1 year | As 250mg + ↓ lesion volume on MRI |

**Standardization gap:** Neither trial provided full standardization data (phytochemical quantification) on the study preparation. Inter-study comparisons are therefore limited.

### Commercial Standardized Extracts

Commercial preparations vary; no universal standard applies outside Ph. Eur.:
- **Dried herb powder capsules:** 250–500 mg per capsule; 1–2 capsules × 1–3 daily
- **Standardized dry extract (flavonoids):** Some products standardize to total flavonoids (as apigenin or luteolin); dose equivalents vary by ratio
- **Standardized dry extract (essential oil/chamazulene):** Less common commercially

**Typical commercial extract dose range:** 250–500 mg encapsulated extract, 1–3 times daily.

## 4. Topical Preparations

### Ointment / Salve

**As used in clinical trial (Mahmoudi 2023 — Hemorrhoids):**
- **5% hydroalcoholic extract** in ointment base
- Applied **3 times daily** for **10 days**
- Effective for: pain, defecation discomfort, bleeding
- Not effective for: itching

**Traditional salve:** Infused oil (dried yarrow in olive oil, 4–6 weeks) combined with beeswax to form a salve. Standard formulation: 1 oz beeswax per 1 cup infused oil.

### Fresh Herb Poultice

**Traditional wound/hemostatic application:**
- Crush fresh aerial parts (leaves preferred)
- Apply directly to wound, puncture, or nosebleed site
- Secure with cloth if needed
- Change every few hours

*The alkaloid achilleine is the primary hemostatic constituent; water-soluble and likely present in both fresh juice and strong aqueous extract applications.*

### Distillate Mouthwash

**As used in clinical trial (Miranzadeh 2015 — Oral Mucositis):**
- *A. millefolium* aqueous distillate added to routine oral solution
- **15 mL**, gargled **4 times daily**, for **14 days**
- Reduced oral mucositis severity from 2.39 to 0.32 (vs. control: worsened)

**Preparation note:** Distillation captures volatile essential oil constituents; the mouthwash preparation likely delivers antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory essential oil components to the oral mucosa.

### Compress

- Prepare strong infusion (double standard tea strength: 4–8 g per cup)
- Soak clean cloth in warm infusion
- Apply to affected area (bruise, sprain, wound, inflammatory skin lesion)

### Sitz Bath

**Traditional European use:** For perineal healing, postpartum recovery, vaginal complaints
- Prepare: 50–100 g dried herb in 2–3 liters hot water, steep covered 15–20 min
- Add strained liquid to shallow sitz bath at comfortable temperature
- Sit for 10–20 minutes

## Quality Selection Criteria

When selecting yarrow preparations:

1. **Specify material:** Millefolii flos (flowers) vs. herba (aerial parts). Flos is preferred for standardized chamazulene content.
2. **Check essential oil content:** For herba, Ph. Eur. minimum ≥2 mL/kg; verify certificate of analysis.
3. **Chamazulene content (flos):** Ph. Eur. minimum ≥0.02%; higher chamazulene content indicates stronger anti-inflammatory and CYP450 DDI potential.
4. **Chemotype disclosure:** Reputable suppliers will specify botanical source/region; chamazulene-high preparations differ substantially from camphor or thujone chemotypes.
5. **Organic certification:** Reduces pesticide contamination risk (yarrow grown at field margins may have higher pesticide exposure).
6. **Absence of adulterants:** *A. millefolium* is sometimes adulterated with other *Achillea* species; botanical authentication (TLC, HPLC fingerprint) ensures correct species.
