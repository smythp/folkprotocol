# Oil of Oregano - Clinical Uses

**Species note:** Most therapeutic use targets *Origanum vulgare* subsp. *hirtum* (Greek/Mediterranean oregano), prized for high carvacrol content (≥70%). Common oregano (*O. vulgare* subsp. *vulgare*) often contains <20% carvacrol; not suitable for therapeutic use without carvacrol standardization. Products must specify subspecies and carvacrol percentage for clinical relevance.

**Carvacrol note:** Several human RCTs use purified carvacrol rather than whole oregano oil. Since carvacrol is the primary bioactive constituent of high-carvacrol oregano oil (typically 70–86%), carvacrol trial data is included here as directly applicable constituent evidence — clearly flagged as carvacrol-specific below.

---

## Rhinosinusitis (Chronic — CRS without Nasal Polyps)

### Qaraaty & Kamaneh 2020 — CRS Nasal Spray (RCT)
- **Sample:** 75 adults with chronic rhinosinusitis without nasal polyps (CRSsNP); 3 parallel groups
- **Dose:** Nasal spray containing oregano oil (*O. vulgare* L.); standardization not specified
- **Comparator:** Fluticasone nasal spray (active pharmacotherapy control) or sesame oil (placebo)
- **Duration:** 4 weeks
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, three-arm controlled clinical trial
- **Publication:** Indian Journal of Traditional Knowledge; Cochrane CENTRAL CN-02183755

**Primary Outcome — SNOT-22 (Sinonasal Outcome Test) Score Reduction:**
- **Oregano group:** Mean SNOT-22 reduction of **51.52 points** (95% CI: −55.79 to −47.24)
- **Fluticasone group:** Mean SNOT-22 reduction of **21.60 points** (95% CI: −25.48 to −17.71)
- **Sesame oil (placebo) group:** Mean SNOT-22 reduction of **11.84 points** (95% CI: −13.18 to −10.51)
- Oregano oil superior to both fluticasone and placebo

**Safety:** Not detailed in available abstract.

**Limitations:** Published in regional journal without PubMed indexing; independent replication needed. Short duration (4 weeks) with no long-term follow-up. Oregano oil preparation not standardized. Oregano outperforming fluticasone is an unusual finding requiring confirmation.

**Clinical Significance:** Striking effect size (51.52-point SNOT-22 reduction vs 21.60 for fluticasone; vs 11.84 for placebo) in the only direct RCT using oregano oil nasal spray for CRS. Single study from a single center; cannot be relied upon without replication.

---

### Hoang et al. 2023 — Rhinosinusitis Network Meta-Analysis (47 RCTs)
- **Design:** Systematic review and network meta-analysis
- **Scope:** 47 RCTs examining 18 herbal medicines across 6 rhinosinusitis clinical populations
- **Publication:** PMID 36609950

**Key Findings for Oregano:**
- For **chronic rhinosinusitis without nasal polyps (CRSsNP):** *Origanum vulgare* ranked **#1** among 18 herbal medicines for improving symptoms and health-related quality of life (HRQoL)
- Evidence certainty: **Low** (per GRADE criteria)
- Conclusion: *Origanum vulgare* is the most beneficial herbal treatment for CRSsNP; larger trials with extended follow-up needed

**Clinical Significance:** Independent corroboration from a network meta-analysis, consistent with Qaraaty 2020. Low certainty rating reflects limited primary studies. Together these provide the strongest clinical evidence available for any oil of oregano indication.

## Antioxidant and Exercise Recovery

### Shirvani et al. 2022 — Exercise-Induced Oxidative Stress (RCT)
- **Sample:** 24 healthy male soldiers, mean age 24±3 years; oregano n=12, placebo n=12
- **Dose:** 500 mg single capsule *Origanum vulgare* powder, consumed immediately post-exercise
- **Comparator:** Placebo capsule
- **Duration:** Single acute intervention; measurements at 4 timepoints (pre, immediately post, 60 min, 120 min)
- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial, parallel-group
- **PMID:** 36615794

**Primary Outcomes:**
- **Creatine kinase (CK):** Significantly lower in oregano group at 60 and 120 minutes post-exercise (p<0.0001)
- **Malondialdehyde (MDA):** Significantly reduced oxidative stress marker in oregano group vs placebo (p<0.0001)
- **Total antioxidant capacity (TAC):** Significantly increased in oregano group (p<0.0001)
- **Superoxide dismutase (SOD):** Elevated post-intervention in oregano group
- **Glutathione peroxidase (GPX):** Elevated post-intervention in oregano group

**Safety:** No adverse effects reported.

**Limitations:** Single acute dose; no multi-dose or long-term data; small n=24, exclusively healthy young males; no blinding or allocation concealment details provided; crude powder capsule with no standardization described.

**Clinical Significance:** Suggests 500 mg oral oregano powder attenuates acute exercise-induced oxidative stress and muscle damage markers. Cannot be generalized to chronic dosing or clinical populations.

---

### Nurmi et al. 2006 — Antioxidant / Absorption Confirmation (RCT)
- **Sample:** 45 healthy nonsmoking men; 3 parallel groups (placebo, 300 mg/day, 600 mg/day)
- **Dose:** 300 mg/day or 600 mg/day total phenolic compounds from oregano extract (in mango-orange juice)
- **Comparator:** Plain mango-orange juice
- **Duration:** 4 weeks
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, parallel-group controlled trial
- **PMID:** 16881679

**Primary Outcomes:**
- **Urinary phenolic acid excretion:** Markedly increased in the 600 mg/day group vs placebo (confirms absorption; specific fold-change not reported in abstract)
- **Serum lipid peroxidation (MDA/TBARS):** No significant changes in either oregano group vs placebo
- **Serum lipids:** No significant changes
- **Safety biomarkers (liver enzymes, renal markers):** No significant changes at either dose

**Safety:** No adverse effects; no changes in safety parameters at 300 or 600 mg/day for 4 weeks.

**Extract type note:** Aqueous/ethanolic phenolic extract standardized to total phenolics — not an essential oil preparation; applies to phenolic fraction specifically.

**Clinical Significance:** Confirms oral absorption of oregano phenolics via increased urinary excretion. However, does not support lipid peroxidation reduction in healthy men at these doses. Primary importance: safety confirmation and absorption validation.

## Respiratory — Carvacrol Constituent Studies

*Carvacrol is the primary bioactive constituent of high-carvacrol oregano oil preparations (typically 70–86% of oil by mass). The following RCTs used purified carvacrol capsules. Dosing synthesis: 1.2 mg/kg/day carvacrol ≈ 84 mg/day for a 70 kg adult ≈ 112–168 mg/day of oregano oil standardized to 50–75% carvacrol.*

### Ghorani et al. 2018 (pub. 2021) — Phase I Safety Study (Carvacrol)
- **Sample:** Healthy subjects (exact n not stated in abstract); two parallel groups
- **Dose:** 1 mg/kg/day or 2 mg/kg/day carvacrol for 1 month
- **Design:** Phase I clinical trial, randomized, parallel-group
- **PMID:** 30486682 | Phytotherapy Research / Drug and Chemical Toxicology

**Key Safety Findings:**
- No critical adverse reactions at either dose
- No clinically significant changes in biochemical, hematological, endocrine, renal, or hepatic function tests
- At 1 mg/kg/day: some changes in TG, phosphorus, LDH, PT, MCH, MCHC — all within normal range
- At 2 mg/kg/day: FEV1 (lung function) increased significantly (p<0.05), still within normal limits
- **Conclusion:** Clinical safety and tolerability established for carvacrol at 1–2 mg/kg/day × 1 month

**Dosing equivalence (70 kg adult):** 1 mg/kg/day = ~70 mg carvacrol = ~93 mg oregano oil at 75% carvacrol; 2 mg/kg/day = ~140 mg carvacrol = ~187 mg oregano oil

**Clinical Significance:** First human Phase I safety data for carvacrol; establishes tolerability at doses used in subsequent efficacy trials.

---

### Alavinezhad et al. 2018 — Phase II Asthma (Carvacrol, RCT)
- **Sample:** 23 asthmatic patients; carvacrol n=12, placebo n=11
- **Dose:** Carvacrol capsule 1.2 mg/kg/day (≈84 mg/day for 70 kg adult) for 2 months
- **Comparator:** Placebo capsule
- **Duration:** 2 months
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase II clinical trial
- **PMID:** 29193478 | Phytotherapy Research

**Key Outcomes:**
- Pulmonary function tests (FEV1, FVC, PEF): Significantly increased in carvacrol group (p<0.05 to p<0.001)
- Most respiratory symptoms significantly reduced after 2 months
- Total WBC and differential WBC counts significantly reduced (p<0.05 to p<0.001)
- Serum hs-CRP significantly reduced in carvacrol group
- No significant changes in placebo group across any measured parameter

**Safety:** No adverse events reported.

**Clinical Significance:** First Phase II efficacy data for carvacrol in asthma. Significant pulmonary function, symptom, and inflammatory marker improvements with 1.2 mg/kg/day × 2 months.

---

### Ghorani et al. 2021 — Asthma Adjunct Therapy (Carvacrol, RCT)
- **Sample:** 33 moderate asthmatic patients; carvacrol n=17, placebo n=16
- **Dose:** Carvacrol 1.2 mg/kg/day in capsules taken three times daily, alongside routine asthma medications
- **Comparator:** Placebo (alongside routine medications)
- **Duration:** 2 months
- **Design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind clinical trial
- **PMID:** 33773189 | Phytomedicine

**Key Outcomes:**
- Respiratory symptoms significantly decreased at 1 month and 2 months (p<0.05 to p<0.001)
- Pulmonary function tests improved significantly at 1 and 2 months vs. baseline
- Most oxidative stress markers improved following treatment
- Serum and PBMC cytokine levels improved significantly
- No significant changes in placebo group

**Safety:** No adverse events reported.

**Clinical Significance:** Confirms Alavinezhad 2018 findings in a larger sample. Demonstrates carvacrol benefit as adjunct to standard asthma therapy with consistent 1.2 mg/kg/day dose.

---

### Khazdair & Boskabady 2018 — Sulfur Mustard Lung Injury (Carvacrol, RCT)
- **Sample:** 20 veterans with sulphur mustard-induced chronic lung disorders; 10 per group
- **Dose:** Carvacrol 1.2 mg/kg/day for 2 months
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind clinical trial
- **PMID:** 29574804 | Journal of Clinical Pharmacy and Therapeutics

**Key Outcomes:**
- Peak expiratory flow (PEF) significantly increased at month 2 (p<0.01)
- Total WBC and neutrophil counts decreased significantly at 1 and 2 months
- Thiol, superoxide dismutase, and catalase increased significantly (p<0.05 to p<0.001)
- Malondialdehyde (oxidative stress marker) decreased significantly by month 2

**Safety:** No adverse events reported.

---

### Khazdair & Boskabady 2019a — Sulfur Mustard Cytokines (Carvacrol, RCT)
- **Sample:** 20 patients with sulphur mustard-induced lung injury; 10 per group
- **Dose:** Carvacrol 1.2 mg/kg/day for 2 months
- **Design:** Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial
- **PMID:** 30075886 | Cytokine

**Key Outcomes:**
- Inflammatory cytokines IL-2, IL-4, IL-6, IL-8 significantly reduced in carvacrol group
- Anti-inflammatory markers IL-10 and IFN-γ increased
- IFN-γ/IL-4 ratio enhanced substantially by study end
- Maximum mid-expiratory flow and other pulmonary function metrics improved vs. baseline

**Safety:** No adverse events reported.

---

### Khazdair & Boskabady 2019b — Sulfur Mustard Inflammatory Mediators (Carvacrol, RCT)
- **Sample:** 21 veterans with chronic respiratory symptoms from sulfur mustard exposure >20 years prior
- **Dose:** Carvacrol 1.2 mg/kg/day for 2 months
- **Design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial
- **PMID:** 30961947 | Respiratory Medicine

**Key Outcomes:**
- FEV1 improved significantly in carvacrol group by month 2
- Chest wheeze and nighttime wheeze decreased substantially within 1 month
- Nighttime cough and exercise-induced coughing improved by month 2
- Inflammatory markers TNF-α, EGF, VEGF decreased in treatment group
- Serum MCP-1 decreased in carvacrol group at month 2
- Anti-inflammatory cytokines increased

**Safety:** No adverse events reported.

**Clinical Significance (Sulfur Mustard Series):** Three RCTs (n=20, 20, 21) from the same Iranian research group consistently show carvacrol 1.2 mg/kg/day × 2 months improves pulmonary function, reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines, and increases antioxidant markers in chronic chemical-induced lung injury. Results are internally consistent; independent replication from another group is needed.

---

### Carvalho et al. 2020 — Carvacrol Respiratory Systematic Review/Meta-Analysis
- **Design:** Systematic review and meta-analysis
- **Scope:** 5 human studies + 12 animal studies on carvacrol anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects in respiratory conditions
- **PMID:** 32249518

**Key Meta-Analytic Findings:**
- Significant reductions in IL-1β, IL-4, IL-8, and MDA in human studies
- Anti-inflammatory and antioxidant effects consistently demonstrated
- Conclusion: Current evidence supports carvacrol's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects in respiratory conditions; human evidence limited; further research recommended

**Clinical Significance:** Provides systematic synthesis of carvacrol respiratory evidence. Validates the individual RCT findings above.

## Antiparasitic (Enteric Infections)

### Force et al. 2000 — Enteric Parasites (Uncontrolled Study)
- **Sample:** 14 adult patients with stool-confirmed enteric parasitic infection
- **Dose:** 600 mg/day emulsified oil of Mediterranean oregano
- **Comparator:** None (uncontrolled)
- **Duration:** 6 weeks
- **Design:** Open-label uncontrolled clinical study
- **PMID:** 10815019

**Key Outcomes:**
- **Entamoeba hartmanni:** Complete disappearance in 4/4 cases (100%)
- **Endolimax nana:** Complete disappearance in 1/1 case (100%)
- **Blastocystis hominis:** Complete disappearance in 8/11 infected cases (73%); remaining 3 cases showed score decline
- **GI symptom improvement:** 7/11 (64%) Blastocystis-positive patients

**Safety:** No adverse effects reported.

**Limitations:** No control group, no randomization, no blinding; n=14; single publication site (Biotics Research Corporation) — potential conflict of interest; emulsified oil carvacrol content not specified.

**Clinical Significance:** First published clinical study using oil of oregano. Noteworthy antiparasitic signal; 600 mg/day × 6 weeks is the only human dosing reference for antiparasitic use. Requires confirmation in controlled trials — no follow-up RCT identified.

## Antimicrobial — Metabolic Syndrome (Enteric Bacteria)

### Ghitea et al. 2021 — Metabolic Syndrome / Enteric Bacteria (Clinical Study)
- **Sample:** 106 patients with metabolic syndrome and confirmed enteric bacterial infection; 3 treatment groups + 20 control patients
  - L1: 32 patients with staphylococcal infection, treated with oregano oil
  - L2: 51 patients with *E. coli* infection, treated with oregano oil
  - L3: 2 patients with streptococcal infection (oregano treatment)
  - CG: 20 control patients (no infection, no treatment)
- **Intervention:** Essential oil of *Origanum vulgare* L. at 0.2, 0.4, and 0.6 μL concentrations (route not fully specified in abstract)
- **Duration:** 10 days
- **Design:** Clinical trial with multi-group bacteriological assessment; not randomized placebo-controlled
- **PMID:** 33429991 | Molecules

**Key Outcomes:**
- Antibacterial activity against *Staphylococcus*, *E. coli*, and *Streptococcus* comparable to antibiotic treatment
- After 10 days, gastrointestinal side effects were absent
- Oregano oil improved phase angle (a cellular health and hydration marker)
- No significant disruption of the gut microbiome observed (unlike standard antibiotics)

**Safety:** No adverse events; intestinal side effects absent post-treatment.

**Limitations:** Not randomized or placebo-controlled. Very small streptococcal subgroup (n=2). Oregano oil preparation not standardized — concentrations appear to be in vitro test doses applied to an in vivo setting; intervention route unclear. Short duration (10 days); no long-term follow-up.

**Clinical Significance:** Provides preliminary clinical evidence that oregano oil may match antibiotic efficacy for enteric bacterial infections in metabolic syndrome patients while preserving gut microbiome integrity. No RCT confirmation; doses and route of administration need clarification.

## Antimicrobial — H. pylori

### Nikolić et al. 2023 — H. pylori (Customer Case Study + In Vitro)
- **Sample:** 15 H. pylori-positive individuals using HerbELICO® dietary supplement (non-randomized, no placebo)
- **Intervention:** HerbELICO® liquid/solid (savory + oregano + thyme essential oil mixture); exact oregano dose not specified
- **Duration:** 8 weeks (outcome assessment post-treatment)
- **Design:** In vitro + uncontrolled customer case study (NOT an RCT)
- **PMID:** 36903396

**Clinical Signal:** 14/15 users (93%) tested H. pylori negative at 8 weeks post-treatment; no side effects reported.

**In Vitro Data:** GC-MS characterization confirmed carvacrol and thymol as major compounds; tested against 20 H. pylori clinical strains including antibiotic-resistant isolates; MIC range 36.6–45.7 g/L; 10 minutes exposure sufficient to kill H. pylori strains; penetrated artificial mucin barrier.

**Limitations:** Customer case study; no randomization, no placebo, no blinding; n=15; cannot distinguish herb contribution from spontaneous resolution; authors explicitly state "cannot be used to make clinical conclusions directly."

**Clinical Significance:** In vitro evidence for anti-H. pylori activity is strong. Clinical signal (93% eradication) is noteworthy but evidence grade is insufficient for clinical use; awaits RCT confirmation.

## SIBO / Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth (Multi-Herb Context)

### Chedid et al. 2014 — Herbal Therapy vs Rifaximin (Comparative Effectiveness)
- **Sample:** 104 adults with confirmed SIBO by lactulose breath testing; 67 rifaximin, 37 herbal
- **Intervention:** Multi-ingredient herbal formulas (Dysbiocide and FC-Cidal by Biotics Research — containing berberine, oregano, thyme, and other botanicals)
- **Comparator:** Rifaximin 1200 mg/day
- **Duration:** 4 weeks treatment + follow-up breath testing
- **Design:** Comparative effectiveness trial with optional crossover for non-responders
- **PMID:** 24891990

**Key Outcomes:**
- **Herbal therapy:** 46% negative follow-up breath test (17/37 patients)
- **Rifaximin:** 34% negative follow-up breath test (23/67 patients)
- **Adjusted odds ratio:** 1.85 favoring herbs (p=0.17, non-significant)
- **Rifaximin non-responders given herbal rescue:** 57% response rate (8/14)

**Safety (herbal arm):** Diarrhea in 1 case; p=0.22 vs rifaximin arm (non-significant difference).

**Limitations:** Not an RCT; non-random treatment assignment; multi-ingredient products — oregano contribution cannot be isolated; non-significant primary endpoint.

**Clinical Significance:** Herbal multi-ingredient therapy (containing oregano among other botanicals) was non-inferior to rifaximin for SIBO clearance. Oregano's individual contribution is indeterminate; result applies to the multi-herb formula as a whole.

## Overall Clinical Evidence Assessment

### Updated Evidence Count

**Oregano oil/extract RCTs (whole preparation):**
- Rhinosinusitis: Qaraaty 2020 (n=75) — strong signal, needs replication
- Antioxidant/safety: Nurmi 2006 (n=45) — absorption confirmed, no systemic antioxidant effect
- Exercise recovery: Shirvani 2022 (n=24) — acute oxidative stress reduction

**Oregano oil clinical studies (non-RCT):**
- Metabolic syndrome / antimicrobial: Ghitea 2021 (n=106)
- Antiparasitic: Force 2000 (n=14)
- H. pylori (multi-herb): Nikolić 2023 (n=15)

**Carvacrol constituent RCTs (directly applicable to high-carvacrol products):**
- Phase I safety: Ghorani 2018 (~20 subjects, 1–2 mg/kg/day × 1 month)
- Phase II asthma: Alavinezhad 2018 (n=23, 1.2 mg/kg/day × 2 months)
- Asthma adjunct: Ghorani 2021 (n=33, 1.2 mg/kg/day × 2 months)
- Sulfur mustard lung × 3: Khazdair & Boskabady 2018/2019 (n=20, 20, 21; 1.2 mg/kg/day × 2 months)

**Systematic reviews:**
- Rhinosinusitis network meta-analysis: Hoang 2023 (47 RCTs, *O. vulgare* ranked #1 for CRSsNP)
- Carvacrol respiratory SR/MA: Carvalho 2020 (5 human studies)

### Clinical Bottom Line

**Rhinosinusitis (CRS):** Strongest emerging indication. Qaraaty 2020 (n=75 RCT, oregano nasal spray) and Hoang 2023 (network meta-analysis ranking *O. vulgare* #1 for CRSsNP) converge on a promising but low-certainty signal. Independent replication required before clinical recommendation.

**Respiratory (carvacrol):** Consistent across 5 RCTs from the same Iranian group — carvacrol 1.2 mg/kg/day × 2 months improves pulmonary function, inflammatory cytokines, and oxidative markers in asthmatic patients and those with sulfur mustard-induced lung injury. Phase I safety confirmed at 1–2 mg/kg/day × 1 month. Extrapolation to whole oregano oil products requires dose conversion (see Preparations file).

**Antioxidant / Exercise Recovery:** 500 mg oral oregano powder post-exercise shows acute attenuation of oxidative stress markers (n=24, single dose); 300–600 mg/day phenolic extract for 4 weeks confirms absorption but no systemic antioxidant benefit in healthy men (n=45).

**Antiparasitic:** 600 mg/day emulsified oil × 6 weeks cleared Entamoeba (100%), Blastocystis (73%) in uncontrolled study (n=14). No RCT confirmation exists.

**H. pylori:** Strong in vitro evidence; single uncontrolled case series (n=15, 93% clearance) with multi-herb product; insufficient clinical evidence.

**SIBO:** Multi-herb preparation (containing oregano) non-inferior to rifaximin in non-randomized trial; oregano's individual contribution unknown.

**Evidence grade:** Overall, insufficient for clinical recommendations in any indication. Rhinosinusitis and respiratory/asthma (via carvacrol) are the most clinically promising directions, warranting confirmatory Phase III trials.

### Remaining Evidence Gaps
- No human pharmacokinetic studies measuring plasma carvacrol concentrations after commercial oregano oil supplementation
- No RCTs using whole oregano oil preparations (not pure carvacrol) for respiratory indications
- No independent replication of the Iranian carvacrol respiratory RCT series
- No long-term safety data (>2 months) in humans
- No dose-finding RCTs comparing carvacrol concentrations in oregano oil for respiratory effects
- No RCTs for H. pylori, antifungal use, or SIBO (isolated to oregano)
