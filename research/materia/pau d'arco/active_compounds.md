# Pau d'Arco (Handroanthus impetiginosus) - Active Compounds and Bioavailability

## Primary Bioactive Class: Naphthoquinones

### Lapachol
- **IUPAC name**: 2-hydroxy-3-(3-methyl-2-butenyl)-1,4-naphthoquinone
- **Chemical class**: Naphthoquinone
- **Role**: Historical antitumor interest; primary constituent studied in NCI program; anticoagulant risk at high doses
- **Mechanism of action**:
  - Antitumor: inhibits pyruvate kinase M2; active against Walker 256 intramuscular tumor in preclinical models (Rao et al. 1968)
  - Anticoagulant: inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase and vitamin K quinone reductase (mechanism analogous to warfarin); this mechanism led to Phase I trial termination
- **Solubility**: Poorly water-soluble; traditional aqueous decoctions deliver substantially lower doses than ethanol extracts or encapsulated preparations
- **Clinical development**: NCI Phase I (Block 1974, PMID 4614904) — terminated; therapeutic plasma concentrations caused unacceptable anticoagulant and GI toxicity; IND closed by NCI 1970
- **Standardization**: No standardization benchmarks established for commercial botanical preparations

### Beta-Lapachone
- **Chemical class**: Naphthoquinone (cyclized lapachol analogue)
- **Role**: Primary anti-tumor naphthoquinone; identified as the principal compound with pro-apoptotic effects in vitro
- **Mechanism of action**:
  - NQO1 (NAD(P)H quinone oxidoreductase 1)-bioactivatable: undergoes NQO1-dependent futile redox cycling leading to tumor-selective programmed necrosis (necroptosis)
  - Inhibits topoisomerase I and II
  - Induces apoptosis via Bax/Bcl-2 regulation and caspase activation (MCF-7, NCI-H460, HeLa, HepG2 lines)
  - Tumor selectivity linked to NQO1 overexpression in many cancers vs. normal tissue
- **Clinical development (synthetic analogues only)**:
  - ARQ 501 (Eder 2005): Phase I; 18 patients; IV weekly 10–550 mg/m²; 1 partial response; manageable toxicity at tested doses
  - ARQ 761 (Gerber 2018, PMID 30318513): Phase I; 42 patients; MTD 390 mg/m² IV q2wks; DLT anemia; trend toward efficacy in NQO1-high tumors (p=0.06)
- **Important caveat**: Synthetic ARQ 501 and ARQ 761 are not equivalent to the botanical; these clinical data do not apply to whole-herb preparations
- **Standardization**: Not standardized in commercial botanical preparations; content highly variable

## Secondary Compounds

### Anthraquinones
- **Key compound**: Anthraquinone-2-carboxylic acid
- **Role**: Identified as a constituent class; specific pharmacological contribution in the whole herb not fully characterized

### Flavonoids
- **Content**: 71.12 mg CE/g (catechin equivalents) in methanolic extract of bark (Pires 2015, PMID 26703544)
- **Function**: Anti-inflammatory, antioxidant; may contribute to the IL-2-independent immunosuppressive activity of the aqueous extract (Böhler 2008, PMID 18158117) — that activity was confirmed to be beta-lapachone-independent, suggesting a polar constituent (possibly flavonoid or phenolic glycoside)

### Phenolic Compounds
- **Total phenolics (methanolic extract)**: 247.50 mg GAE/g (gallic acid equivalents) (Pires 2015)
- **Alpha-tocopherol**: 2.07 mg/100g dry weight
- **Phenolic glycosides**: 19 identified in comprehensive phytochemical analysis (Zhang 2020, PMID 32962180)

### Fatty Acids
- **Oleic acid**: 32.51% of total fatty acid content (predominant; Zhang 2020, Pires 2015)
- **Palmitic acid**: Present
- **Linoleic acid**: Present

### Cyclopentene Derivatives
- **Compounds**: Avallaneine A–H
- **Significance**: Novel structural class; pharmacological role not fully characterized

### Volatile Constituents
- **4-Methoxybenzaldehyde**: Present in volatile fraction
- **4-Methoxyphenol**: Present in volatile fraction

## Standardization Status

**Marker compounds**: Lapachol and beta-lapachone are the accepted phytochemical markers for pau d'arco, but:
- No regulatory authority (WHO, Commission E, EMA/HMPC, ESCOP, AHP, USP) has established standardization benchmarks
- No official monograph exists for pau d'arco from any major regulatory body as of 2026-02-27
- Commercial products show high inter-product variability in naphthoquinone content (Gómez Castellanos 2009)
- Species identity is frequently mislabeled in commerce; *T. impetiginosa*, *T. avellanedae*, and *H. impetiginosus* used interchangeably on labels

## Bioavailability

**Human pharmacokinetic data**: None exist for whole-bark extract or any of its constituents delivered in botanical context.

**Key bioavailability considerations**:
- Lapachol is poorly water-soluble → traditional aqueous decoctions yield very low naphthoquinone exposure compared to ethanol extracts or encapsulated powders
- Commercial supplement forms (pills, syrup) show dramatically reduced in vitro bioactivity vs. methanolic laboratory extracts: GI50 of pills/syrups >400 μg/mL vs. 76.67–110.76 μg/mL for methanolic extract against tumor cell lines (Pires 2015, PMID 26703544)
- Infusion preparations show reduced bioactivity vs. cold extracts, attributed to heat-induced phenolic degradation (Pires 2015)
- Co-occurring pro-vitamin K compounds in whole bark may partially offset lapachol's anticoagulant effect at decoction doses (Gómez Castellanos 2009)

**Mechanistic bioavailability insight** (Böhler 2008):
- The immunosuppressive activity of the aqueous extract in human T-lymphocytes is NOT attributable to beta-lapachone, implying an unknown polar constituent is responsible — this constituent has not been identified or characterized
