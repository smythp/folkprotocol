# Pau d'Arco (Handroanthus impetiginosus) - Clinical Uses

## Evidence Overview

Clinical evidence for pau d'arco is extremely limited. No randomized controlled trials of the botanical have been published as of 2026-02-27. The human evidence base consists of two single-arm uncontrolled studies: one pilot safety trial for dysmenorrhea (n=12, 2022) and one Phase II trial of a lapacho-based multi-ingredient product for oral mucositis (n=40, 2015). Isolated constituent trials (lapachol, ARQ 501/761) involve synthetic or purified compounds and are not directly applicable to the botanical.

## Primary Dysmenorrhea

**Evidence Level**: Very low (single-arm pilot, n=12; no control group)

**Key Trial** (McClure et al. 2022, PMID 36960315):
- Study type: Single-arm open-label pilot (uncontrolled)
- Population: 12 healthy women ages 18–45 with primary dysmenorrhea; 9 completers (75% completion)
- Intervention: Encapsulated *Tabebuia avellanedae* 1050 mg/day for 8 weeks
- Pain outcomes (significant; p<0.01 at all time points):
  - Significant pain intensity decrease at first dose (p<0.01)
  - Significant pain intensity decrease at 4 weeks (p<0.01)
  - Significant pain intensity decrease at 8 weeks (p<0.01)
- Pain interference, quality of life, sexual function: nonsignificant trend toward improvement
- No control arm; effect size vs. placebo unknown
- This is the only published human trial of pau d'arco for any primary traditional indication

**Clinical Note**: Authors noted no prior clinical studies on pau d'arco for dysmenorrhea existed at the time of publication. Results are hypothesis-generating only; placebo-controlled trials required before clinical conclusions can be drawn.

## Oral Mucositis Prevention (Head and Neck Radiotherapy)

**Evidence Level**: Very low (Phase II single-arm, n=40; no control; multi-ingredient product)

**Key Trial** (Giacomelli et al. 2015, PMID 26451712):
- Study type: Single-arm Phase II (uncontrolled prospective; January–June 2014)
- Population: 40 adults with head and neck cancer undergoing radiotherapy
- Intervention: Orasol Plus (swallowable lapacho-based nutritional solution) three times daily from radiotherapy start through completion; lapacho content and standardization not specified
- Primary outcome: Safety and tolerance — well tolerated; nearly all patients completed without interruption
- Mucositis outcomes compared against historical controls (Grade 3–4 mucositis reported in 70–80% of IMRT patients historically):
  - Grade 2 mucositis: 27.5% (11/40)
  - Grade 3 mucositis: 10% (4/40)
  - Grade 4 mucositis: 0% (0/40)
  - No treatment discontinuations due to mucositis

**Clinical Note**: Orasol Plus is a multi-ingredient product; lapacho's individual contribution cannot be isolated. No randomization or control arm. Authors recommend Phase III RCT confirmation before clinical adoption.

## Anticancer Activity

**Evidence Level**: Preclinical only for the botanical; Phase I/II data exist only for isolated synthetic analogues

- No human clinical trials testing pau d'arco botanical preparations for cancer indications
- Beta-lapachone (synthetic analogues ARQ 501 and ARQ 761) advanced to Phase I/II in refractory solid tumors:
  - ARQ 501 (Eder et al. 2005): 18 patients, doses 10–550 mg/m² IV weekly; 1 partial response (uterine leiomyosarcoma, >56 weeks on study); stable disease in 2 patients
  - ARQ 761 (Gerber et al. 2018, PMID 30318513): 42 patients; MTD 390 mg/m² IV q2wks; best response stable disease (12/32 evaluable); tumor shrinkage in 6 patients; trend toward improved efficacy in NQO1-high tumors (p=0.06)
- Lapachol (Block et al. 1974, PMID 4614904): NCI Phase I terminated — anticoagulant toxicity prevented achievement of therapeutic concentrations; no antitumor response demonstrated
- In vitro cytotoxicity documented against 15 tumor cell lines (HL-60, MDA-MB-435, MDA-MB-231, MCF-7, HT-29, HCT-8, HCT-116, HEp-2, HepG2, CACO-2, SF-295, NCI-H292, NCI-H460, HeLa, OVCAR-8)

**Clinical Note**: ARQ 501/761 are synthetic compounds not equivalent to the botanical. No anticancer indication is supported for whole-herb pau d'arco preparations.

## Anti-Inflammatory / Immunomodulatory Activity

**Evidence Level**: Ex vivo human cell data only; no in vivo clinical trials

- Ryan et al. 2021 (PMID 33750911): Primary human PBMCs — all five solvent extracts nontoxic; multiple extracts produced potent suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IFN-γ, IL-1β, IL-2, IL-6, IL-8, IL-10, MCP-1, TNF); some extracts outperformed positive clinical control
- Böhler et al. 2008 (PMID 18158117): Primary human T-lymphocytes — aqueous extract produced dose-dependent, IL-2-independent inhibition of T-cell proliferation and CD25/CD71 expression; mechanism not attributable to beta-lapachone; ethanol extract inactive

**Clinical Note**: These are ex vivo cell studies, not clinical trials. They provide mechanistic support for anti-inflammatory traditional use but do not establish clinical efficacy.

## Antimicrobial / Antifungal

**Evidence Level**: No human clinical data

- Traditional use for bacterial and fungal infections (syphilis, trypanosomiasis, Candida) is not supported by any published human clinical trial
- Preclinical antimicrobial and antifungal activity has been reported but no human studies exist
