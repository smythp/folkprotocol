# Cramp Bark - Active Compounds

## Overview

The medicinal activity of Viburnum opulus is attributed primarily to three constituent classes: coumarins (scopoletin), iridoids (viopudial), and hydroxycinnamic acids (chlorogenic acid family). The bark and fruit have distinct phytochemical profiles. No standardized commercial extract with defined marker compound percentages has been validated in clinical studies.

---

## Primary Active Constituents

### 1. Scopoletin (7-hydroxy-6-methoxycoumarin)

**Source**: Jarboe CH, Zirvi KA, Nicholson JA, Schmidt CM. "Scopoletin, an Antispasmodic Component of Viburnum opulus and prunifolium." J Med Chem. 1967;10(3):488–489. PMID 22185161.

- **Class**: Coumarin
- **Part of plant**: Bark (cortex)
- **Mechanism**: Direct smooth muscle relaxation; antispasmodic activity on uterine and other smooth muscle
- **Significance**: Primary constituent responsible for smooth muscle relaxant properties of cramp bark; foundational identification paper (1967)
- **Standardization**: Not standardized in commercial preparations; content varies by plant part and extraction method
- **Human PK data**: None identified

---

### 2. Viopudial

**Source**: Nicholson JA, Darby TD, Jarboe CH. "Viopudial, a hypotensive and smooth muscle antispasmodic from Viburnum opulus." Proc Soc Exp Biol Med. 1972;140(2):457–461. PMID 5037580.

- **Class**: Iridoid
- **Part of plant**: Bark
- **Mechanism**: Hypotensive, smooth muscle antispasmodic; cholinesterase inhibitory, parasympathomimetic activities
- **Models**: In vitro + in vivo (rats, cats, dogs)
- **Significance**: Second major active constituent; established alongside scopoletin as dual antispasmodic mechanism
- **Standardization**: No commercial standardization benchmarks established
- **Human PK data**: None identified

---

### 3. Chlorogenic Acid (and Isomers)

**Sources**: Multiple (Kajszczak 2020 PMID 33167421; Pietrzyk 2021 PMID 34279426; Ilhan 2014 PMID 25165481; Erdogan-Orhan 2011 PMID 21186982)

- **Class**: Hydroxycinnamic acid (phenolic)
- **Part of plant**: Fruit (primarily); also present in bark and leaves
- **Related compounds**: Neochlorogenic acid, cryptochlorogenic acid (all increase via isomerization during GI digestion)
- **Measured concentrations**:
  - 0.3227 mg/mL in fresh fruit juice (HPLC, Ilhan 2014)
  - 0.51–1.71 mg/100 mg dry weight in ethyl acetate/methanol extracts (Saltan 2016)
  - Total phenolics in fruit: 325.4–1460 mg/100 g fresh weight (range across cultivars)
- **Mechanisms**:
  - Anticholinesterase (AChE) inhibition: chlorogenic acid is one of three major bioactive compounds; leaf methanol extract shows 93.19±0.87% AChE inhibition at 200 μg/mL (Erdogan-Orhan 2011)
  - Antioxidant (DPPH, ABTS, FRAP assays)
  - Anti-inflammatory: reduced TNF-α, VEGF, IL-6 in endometriosis rat model (Saltan 2016)
- **Proposed role in stone expulsion**: AChE inhibition → cholinergic smooth muscle activation → ureteral peristalsis enhancement
- **Human PK data**: None for bark extract; in vitro bioavailability data from fruit only

---

### 4. Amentoflavone

- **Class**: Biflavonoid
- **Source**: Erdogan-Orhan 2011 (PMID 21186982); AHP 2000 monograph
- **Activity**: Identified as one of three major bioactive compounds with AChE inhibitory activity
- **Notes**: Inhibitory activity partially attributed to this compound alongside chlorogenic acid and salicin

---

### 5. Salicin

- **Class**: Phenolic glycoside
- **Source**: Erdogan-Orhan 2011 (PMID 21186982); AHP 2000 monograph
- **Activity**: One of three major bioactive compounds studied for AChE inhibition and antioxidant properties

## Bioavailability

**Source**: Pietrzyk N, Zakłos-Szyda M, Redzynia M, Podsędek A. "The Effect of Simulated In Vitro Digestion on Biological Activity of Viburnum opulus Fruit Juices." Molecules. 2021;26(13):4086. PMID 34279426.

**Methods**: Three-stage simulated GI digestion (mouth, stomach, small intestine); UPLC–MS quantification; dialysis membrane separation for bioavailable fractions. Fresh juice (FJ) and phenolic-rich juice (PJ).

**Key findings:**
- Chlorogenic acid decreased ~7-fold (FJ) and ~30-fold (PJ) after full digestion
- Total hydroxycinnamic acids decreased 31.65% (FJ) and 87.60% (PJ) post-digestion
- **Bioavailable hydroxycinnamic acid fraction** (dialyzed): 42.28% (FJ) and 10.17% (PJ) of initial content
- Neochlorogenic and cryptochlorogenic acids increased via isomerization — partially compensate for chlorogenic acid losses
- Post-digestion activity retained: 10–15% lipid accumulation reduction (hepatocytes), 10–30% enhanced glucose uptake (myoblasts), 40–70% reduction in IL-6 and TNF-α from adipocytes

**Critical limitation**: Data apply to fruit juice only. No human PK studies exist for V. opulus bark extract or for isolated scopoletin/viopudial from any V. opulus preparation.

## Acetylcholinesterase Inhibition Data

**Source**: Erdogan-Orhan I, et al. J Med Food. 2011;14(4):434–440. PMID 21186982.

Leaf methanol extract AChE inhibition (dose-response):
- 50 μg/mL → 57.63±1.23%
- 100 μg/mL → 87.41±0.99%
- 200 μg/mL → 93.19±0.87%

Note: These are in vitro values. Relevance to oral dosing at therapeutic doses depends on bioavailability and tissue concentrations not yet studied in humans.

---

## Standardization Status

**No standardized V. opulus bark extract with validated marker compound content thresholds has been established in clinical studies.** The Turkish clinical trials used 1000 mg oral extract without specifying:
- Extraction solvent or method
- Scopoletin content
- Chlorogenic acid content
- Viopudial content

The AHP 2000 monograph specifies scopoletin and viopudial as markers and provides identity/quality tests, but commercial standardization to these markers is not routinely reported.

## Bark vs. Fruit: Different Phytochemical Profiles

| Feature | Bark (Western tradition) | Fruit (Turkish tradition) |
|---------|-------------------------|--------------------------|
| Primary antispasmodic compounds | Scopoletin, viopudial | Not primary source |
| Primary phenolics | Tannins, coumarins, biflavonoids | Chlorogenic acid, anthocyanins, vitamin C |
| Clinical data part used | No RCTs | All published human trials use fruit extract |
| Traditional use | Dysmenorrhea, muscle cramps, threatened miscarriage | Kidney stones, hypertension, diabetes |

**Important caveat**: The two published human clinical trials (Gok 2021, Kızılay 2019) used fruit-based preparations marketed as "gilaburu." These findings may not be directly applicable to bark (cortex) preparations used in Western herbalism.
