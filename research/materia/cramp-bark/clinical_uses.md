# Cramp Bark - Clinical Uses

## Evidence Summary

The clinical evidence base for Viburnum opulus (cramp bark) is thin. No RCTs exist for its primary traditional indications (dysmenorrhea, muscle cramps). The only published human clinical trials are two Turkish urology studies on ureteral stone expulsion (2019–2021). One registered trial for dysmenorrhea (NCT02467543) uses a homeopathic 3X dilution, not a botanical extract, and has not produced a peer-reviewed publication.

---

## Ureteral Stone Expulsion

### Gok et al. 2021 — RCT (PMID 34610178)

**Gok B, Atik YT, Uysal B, et al. "Gilaburu extract (Viburnum opulus Linnaeus) is as effective as Tamsulosin in medical expulsive therapy of distal ureteral calculi." Int J Clin Pract. 2021;75(12):e14950.**

- **Design**: Prospective, parallel-group RCT
- **Sample size**: 86 participants (n=43 per group)
- **Population**: Adults with distal ureteral stones ≤10 mm
- **Intervention**: V. opulus extract 1000 mg orally three times daily (3000 mg/day) + diclofenac 50 mg as needed
- **Comparator**: Tamsulosin 0.4 mg once daily + diclofenac 50 mg as needed
- **Duration**: Until stone expulsion or 4-week maximum

**Outcomes:**
- **Stone expulsion rate**: No significant difference between groups overall
- **Additional analgesic requirement**: 37.2% (V. opulus) vs 65.1% (tamsulosin), P=.017
- **Emergency admission rates**: 11.6% (V. opulus) vs 34.8% (tamsulosin), P=.02
- **Time to expulsion (5–10 mm stones)**: 7.1±4.2 days (V. opulus) vs 11.8±5.2 days (tamsulosin), P=.005
- **Safety**: No serious adverse events reported in either group

**Conclusion**: V. opulus extract non-inferior to tamsulosin for stone expulsion; significantly reduced analgesic need and emergency visits. Only published RCT; not conducted for traditional indications.

---

### Kızılay et al. 2019 — Retrospective Comparative Study (PMID 30978165)

**Kızılay F, Ülker V, Çelik O, et al. "The evaluation of the effectiveness of Gilaburu (Viburnum opulus L.) extract in the medical expulsive treatment of distal ureteral stones." Turk J Urol. 2019;45(Suppl 1):S63–S69.**

- **Design**: Retrospective cohort, two urology clinics, January 2017–June 2018
- **Sample size**: 103 participants (n=53 treatment, n=50 control)
- **Population**: Adults with distal ureteral stones <10 mm
- **Intervention**: V. opulus extract 1000 mg orally 3×2 daily + diclofenac 50 mg on-demand
- **Comparator**: Diclofenac sodium 50 mg on-demand only

**Outcomes:**
- **Stone expulsion rate**: 82% (V. opulus) vs 66% (control), P=0.026 — **16 percentage point improvement**
- **Time to expulsion**: 9±1.8 days (V. opulus) vs 14±2.3 days (control), P=0.018 — **5-day reduction**
- **Additional treatment need (URS/ESWL)**: 9.4% vs 20%, P=0.038
- **Analgesic requirement**: 24.5% vs 44%, P=0.042

**Limitations**: Retrospective design; no randomization; not double-blinded; extract preparation and standardization not specified.

## Dysmenorrhea and Muscle Cramps

**No RCTs have been published for these primary traditional indications.** One registered trial exists:

- **NCT02467543** — "The Efficacy of Viburnum Opulus 3X in the Treatment of Dysmenorrhea"
  - Intervention: Viburnum opulus 3X (homeopathic dilution, 1:1000)
  - Design: Randomized controlled trial (registered)
  - Status: No peer-reviewed publication identified as of 2026-02-27
  - **Note**: A homeopathic 3X preparation cannot be used as evidence for botanical extract efficacy

## Clinical Dosing Used in Trials

| Study | Dose | Frequency | Total Daily Dose | Duration |
|-------|------|-----------|-----------------|----------|
| Gok 2021 (RCT) | 1000 mg oral extract | 3× daily | 3000 mg/day | Until expulsion or 4 weeks max |
| Kızılay 2019 (retrospective) | 1000 mg oral extract | 3×2 daily (6 doses/day?) | 6000 mg/day* | Not specified |

*Note: "3×2 daily" in Kızılay 2019 likely means 1000 mg three times per day on 2 periods, or the reporting is ambiguous. Standardization and extract preparation not specified in either study.

## Effect Size Summary

### Ureteral Stone Expulsion
- **Expulsion rate improvement**: +16 percentage points vs diclofenac alone (82% vs 66%, Kızılay 2019)
- **Non-inferiority to tamsulosin**: Confirmed for overall expulsion rate (Gok 2021)
- **Faster expulsion (5–10 mm stones)**: 7.1 vs 11.8 days vs tamsulosin (Gok 2021)
- **Analgesic reduction**: ~40% fewer patients needing additional pain relief vs tamsulosin (Gok 2021)
- **Emergency visit reduction**: 11.6% vs 34.8% vs tamsulosin (Gok 2021)

### Dysmenorrhea / Cramps
- **No human clinical data available**

## Preclinical Support for Traditional Indications

While not clinical evidence, preclinical studies provide mechanistic support:

**Antinociceptive activity (Altun et al. 2009)**
- Water extract of leaf, oral administration in mice/rats
- 100 mg/kg: 56.59% inhibition of acetic acid-induced abdominal stretching
- 200 mg/kg: 63.21% inhibition
- Comparable to morphine at 90 min, aspirin at 150 min time points
- No significant anti-inflammatory effect in carrageenan model

**Endometriosis model (Saltan et al. 2016, PMID 27647013)**
- Surgically-induced endometriosis rat model
- Ethyl acetate extract (100 mg/kg): reduced implant volume, no adhesion
- Reduced TNF-α, VEGF, IL-6 cytokine levels
- Relevant to dysmenorrhea pathophysiology, not direct evidence

**Uterine relaxant properties (Jarboe et al. 1966, PMID 5988219)**
- Classical animal/in vitro study establishing uterine smooth muscle relaxant activity
- Scientific basis for traditional use in dysmenorrhea and threatened miscarriage
