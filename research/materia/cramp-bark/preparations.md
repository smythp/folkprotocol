# Cramp Bark - Preparations

## Source Standards

Primary dosing authority: **American Herbal Pharmacopoeia (AHP) 2000** — Roy Upton, ed. *Cramp Bark, Viburnum Opulus: Analytical, Quality Control, and Therapeutic Monograph.* American Herbal Pharmacopoeia, Scotts Valley, CA. ISBN 1929425082. (17 pp.)

Secondary: British Herbal Pharmacopoeia 1983; German phytotherapy tradition (Weiss)

---

## Standard Preparations (AHP 2000)

### Dried Bark Decoction
- **Part used**: Dried stem bark (cortex)
- **Dose**: 2–4 g
- **Frequency**: Three times daily
- **Preparation**: Simmer in water for 10–15 minutes; strain
- **Notes**: Traditional preparation; concentration varies with bark quality

### Tincture (1:5, 45% Ethanol)
- **Dose**: 5–10 mL
- **Frequency**: Three times daily
- **Total daily dose range**: 15–30 mL
- **Notes**: Most common commercial preparation; use higher end of range at acute onset; 45% ethanol optimizes extraction of coumarins and phenolics

### Fluid Extract (1:1)
- **Dose**: 2–4 mL
- **Frequency**: Three times daily
- **Notes**: More concentrated than tincture; adjust down from tincture doses

---

## German Phytotherapy Tradition (Weiss)

For dysmenorrhea specifically:
- **Preparation**: Tincture (1:5)
- **Dose**: 4–8 mL
- **Frequency**: Three to four times daily
- **Notes**: Higher-end dosing compared to AHP; consistent with acute symptom management

## Clinical Trial Doses (Human Evidence)

The only human clinical data come from Turkish urology studies using fruit extract:

| Study | Preparation | Dose | Frequency | Total/Day |
|-------|-------------|------|-----------|-----------|
| Gok 2021 (RCT, n=86) | Oral extract (unspecified) | 1000 mg | 3× daily | 3000 mg/day |
| Kızılay 2019 (retrospective, n=103) | Oral extract (unspecified) | 1000 mg | 3×2 daily* | — |

*Standardization not reported in either study. "3×2 daily" notation in Kızılay 2019 is ambiguous in the published text.

**Important**: These doses are for fruit-based "gilaburu" extract used for ureteral stone expulsion. Bark extract and fruit extract have different phytochemical profiles (see active_compounds.md). Direct translation of these doses to bark preparations is not established.

## Standardization Markers

| Marker Compound | Class | Role | Commercial Status |
|----------------|-------|------|-------------------|
| Scopoletin | Coumarin | Primary antispasmodic | Not standardized in commercial products |
| Viopudial | Iridoid | Antispasmodic, hypotensive | Not standardized in commercial products |
| Chlorogenic acid | Hydroxycinnamic acid | Antioxidant, AChE inhibitor | Measured in research; not a commercial standard |

**No standardized extract definition exists** for V. opulus bark with defined percentage of any marker compound. This is a significant quality gap: two commercial products labeled "cramp bark extract" may have substantially different potency.

The AHP 2000 monograph provides identity/quality standards (botanical identity, microscopy, TLC/HPLC authentication) but does not mandate therapeutic compound standardization.

## Part Used

| Tradition | Part | Preparation |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| Western (British, North American, German) | Bark (cortex) of stems and branches | Decoction, tincture, fluid extract |
| Turkish (gilaburu) | Fruit (berries/juice) | Fresh juice, fermented extract, concentrated oral extract |

The bark and fruit have different phytochemical profiles and different evidence bases. Most traditional Western use references bark. Published clinical data (Gok 2021, Kızılay 2019) used fruit preparations.

---

## Quality Considerations

### Harvest and Processing
- Bark is harvested in spring/early summer when scopoletin and viopudial content is highest (traditional guidance)
- AHP 2000 provides botanical identity standards, TLC/HPLC identification methods
- Fresh vs dried bark: volatile oil content differs; tinctures made from fresh bark may have different profile

### Commercial Products
- Tinctures (1:5, 40–60% ethanol) are the most common commercial form
- Standardized extract tablets/capsules exist but lack published clinical validation
- No third-party verified standardization to scopoletin or viopudial content is routinely available
- Quality varies significantly between suppliers
