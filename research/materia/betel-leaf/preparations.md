# Betel Leaf (Piper betle L.) - Preparations

## Overview

> **Note**: No standardized clinical dosing is established for internal/systemic use of isolated Piper betle leaf extract as of reviewed literature (2022). Clinical dosing data exists only for topical/oral-cavity preparations (mouthwash, toothpaste) and one combined-extract weight management product. Preparations below are organized by evidence quality.

---

## Clinically Studied Preparations

### Mouthwash / Oral Rinse

**Formulation**: 2% Piper betle aqueous extract mouthwash
**Dose**: 5 mL
**Frequency**: Twice daily, 30 minutes after brushing
**Duration**: 30 days (RCT duration)
**Indication**: Periodontal disease (Stage II periodontitis; post-scaling and root planing)
**Evidence**: RCT (n=60); non-inferior to 0.2% chlorhexidine for P. gingivalis reduction
**Reference**: Sundaram et al. 2021 (PMC8452170)

**Preparation notes**:
- Hot aqueous extraction used for standardized mouthwash formulations
- Antimicrobial activity maintained in aqueous medium

---

### Herbal Toothpaste / Dentifrice

**Formulation**: Betel leaf herbal toothpaste (exact extract concentration not specified in reviewed study)
**Dose**: Standard toothpaste application
**Frequency**: Twice daily
**Duration**: 20 days per treatment arm (crossover design with 2-week washout)
**Indication**: Mild gingivitis; plaque control
**Evidence**: Pilot RCT crossover (n=60); superior gingival bleeding reduction vs. miswak, significant plaque reduction
**Reference**: Ali et al. 2022 (PMID 36135162)

---

### Combined Herbal Extract Capsule (Weight Management)

**Product**: LI10903F (proprietary combined extract)
**Formulation**: Standardized extracts of Dolichos biflorus + Piper betle
**Dose**: 300 mg combined extract per capsule
**Frequency**: Three times daily (900 mg/day total)
**Duration**: 8 weeks
**Indication**: Weight management (BMI 30–40 kg/m²)
**Evidence**: RCT (n=50); 2.49 kg weight reduction (p=0.00005)

**Critical caveat**: Combined product — contribution of Piper betle alone cannot be determined
**Reference**: Sengupta et al. 2012 (PMID 23270333)

## Preclinical / Research Preparations

### Standardized Spray-Dried Powder (Arawwawala 2011)

Quality parameters established for pharmaceutical-grade spray-dried hot water extract:

**Preparation method**: Hot water extraction → spray-drying
**Appearance**: Powder

**Quality control markers** (established batch parameters):

| Parameter | Specification |
|-----------|--------------|
| Moisture content | 2.2–2.5% |
| Total ash | 6.8–7.0% |
| Acid insoluble ash | 0.003–0.005% |
| Water-soluble ash | 4.1–4.3% |
| Ethanol extractable matter | 15.8–16.2% |
| Heavy metals | Below WHO acceptable limits |
| Microbial pathogens | Absent (E. coli, Salmonella, S. aureus, P. aeruginosa) |

**Authentication**: HPLC fingerprint and densitogram
**Clinical use**: Being evaluated in an antidiabetic clinical trial (as of Arawwawala 2011 publication); results not available in reviewed sources
**Reference**: PMID 21716924 (PMC3113356)

---

### 5% Topical Gel (Preclinical Wound Healing)

**Formulation**: 5% w/w methanol leaf extract gel
**Application**: Topical, directly to wound site
**Evidence**: Swiss mouse burn and excision wound models
- Burn wound healing improvement: p < 0.01
- Excision wound healing improvement: p < 0.001
**Translational status**: Preclinical only; no human wound-healing trials
**Reference**: Lien et al. 2015 (PMC4964315)

---

### Gastroprotective Animal Doses (Not for Human Use)

**Extracts tested**: Hot aqueous and cold ethanolic
**Doses studied in rats**: 200, 300, and 500 mg/kg body weight (oral)
**Efficacy**: Dose-dependent gastroprotection; 500 mg/kg exceeded misoprostol
**Note**: These are rat doses — direct extrapolation to human dosing is not valid without pharmacokinetic bridging studies
**Reference**: Arawwawala et al. 2014 (PMC4012360)

## Traditional Preparations (Ethnopharmacological)

### Fresh Leaf — Chewing

**Description**: 1–2 whole fresh leaves chewed, typically after meals
**Traditional indication**: Oral hygiene, digestion, mouth freshening
**Population**: Indian, Southeast Asian traditional use
**Clinical dose equivalent**: Not validated
**Safety note**: Chewing the leaf alone (without areca nut or lime) differs from betel quid

### Leaf Juice

**Description**: Fresh leaf juice expressed and consumed
**Traditional indication**: Internal use (Ayurvedic vehicle / *anupana* for other medicines)
**Preparation**: Fresh leaves pressed or ground; juice strained
**Clinical dose equivalent**: Not established

### Decoction (Boiled Leaf Water)

**Description**: Leaves boiled in water; liquid used as topical wash or rinse
**Traditional indication**: Wound care, vaginal health, oral rinse, skin infections
**Preparation**: Standard decoction (leaves boiled in 2–3 parts water for 15–20 minutes, strained)
**Clinical dose equivalent**: Not established

### Leaf Poultice

**Description**: Fresh, warmed, or crushed leaves applied directly to skin
**Traditional indication**: Wound healing, skin lesions, inflammation, bruising
**Application**: Leaves heated gently or bruised; applied topically and bandaged in place
**Clinical dose equivalent**: Not established

## Extraction Methods and Their Significance

Different extraction methods yield different compound profiles:

| Extraction Method | Key Compounds Extracted | Studied Applications |
|-------------------|------------------------|---------------------|
| Hot aqueous (water, boiled) | Phenolics, tannins, flavonoids | Gastroprotection, mouthwash, spray-dried powder |
| Cold ethanolic | Phenolics, terpenes, alkaloids | Gastroprotection, antimicrobial |
| Methanol | Broad-spectrum phenolics, hydroxychavicol | Wound healing gel |
| Essential oil / steam distillation | Volatile terpenoids, phenylpropanoids | Antimicrobial (in vitro) |

**Research gap**: No comparative bioavailability or clinical efficacy data across extraction methods. Zamri 2023 explicitly identifies "best extraction method" as a key unknown requiring further research.

## Formulation Considerations

**Perishable raw material**: Fresh betel leaf is highly perishable, limiting standardized manufacturing
**Stability**: Spray-drying addresses stability; hot-water extract spray-dried powder meets microbial safety thresholds
**No standardized extract**: Unlike many herbal medicines, no pharmacopeial monograph or standardized extract specification exists for Piper betle
**Antimicrobial synergy**: In vitro combination with streptomycin, chloramphenicol, and gentamicin enhances antimicrobial activity — relevant for potential combination preparations
