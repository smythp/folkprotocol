# Betel Leaf (Piper betle L.) - Clinical Uses

## Evidence-Based Clinical Applications

> **Evidence grade: LIMITED.** Only 3 RCTs involving human subjects identified. Most evidence is preclinical (in vitro and animal models). Bioavailability and pharmacokinetic data in humans absent. No meta-analyses of clinical trials exist.

---

### Oral Health — Periodontal Disease

#### Piper Extract Mouthwash vs. Chlorhexidine (Sundaram 2021)

**Study Design**: Randomized, single-blind, controlled trial
- Participants: 60 enrolled (screened 85), Stage II periodontitis patients
- Groups: Piper betle extract mouthwash vs. 0.2% chlorhexidine mouthwash
- Treatment duration: 30 days
- Post-procedure context: Post-scaling and root planing

**Dosing**: 2% Piper betle extract mouthwash, 5 mL twice daily, 30 minutes after brushing

**Key Results**:
- Intragroup: Both groups showed significant reduction in Porphyromonas gingivalis levels and clinical parameters (p < 0.0001)
- Intergroup: No statistically significant difference between Piper betle and chlorhexidine groups
- Clinical significance: Non-inferior to chlorhexidine gold standard at equivalent dosing

**Conclusion**: 2% Piper betle mouthwash demonstrated equivalent antimicrobial activity to 0.2% chlorhexidine against P. gingivalis over 30 days
- Reference: PMC8452170

### Oral Health — Gingivitis / Plaque

#### Betel Leaf Dentifrice Crossover Trial (Ali 2022)

**Study Design**: Randomized crossover pilot study
- Participants: 60 individuals with mild gingivitis
- Washout period: 2 weeks between treatment arms
- Treatment duration: 20 days per arm
- Comparators: Miswak (Salvadora persica) herbal toothpaste; conventional chemical toothpaste

**Dosing**: Betel leaf herbal toothpaste, standard application twice daily for 20 days

**Key Results**:
- Plaque index: Significantly decreased with betel leaf toothpaste
- Gingival bleeding score: p < 0.001 (betel leaf) vs p = 0.007 (miswak)
- Betel leaf produced superior reduction in gingival bleeding compared to miswak
- No meaningful improvement when participants returned to conventional toothpaste

**Conclusion**: Betel leaf toothpaste outperformed miswak for gingival bleeding reduction; significant plaque-reducing activity
- Reference: PMID 36135162

### Weight Management

#### LI10903F Combined Herbal Formulation (Sengupta 2012)

**Study Design**: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
- Participants: 50 adults with BMI 30–40 kg/m²
- Treatment duration: 8 weeks
- Lifestyle context: 2,000 kcal/day diet + 30-minute walks 5 days/week

**Intervention**: LI10903F — combined standardized extracts of Dolichos biflorus + Piper betle
**Dosing**: 300 mg of combined herbal formulation three times daily (900 mg/day total)

**Key Results**:
- Body weight reduction: 2.49 kg (p = 0.00005) vs placebo
- BMI reduction: 0.96 kg/m² (p = 0.00004)
- Serum adiponectin: Increased significantly (p = 0.0076)
- Serum ghrelin: Decreased significantly (p = 0.0066)
- Safety: Favorable; mild adverse events equally distributed between groups

**Critical Limitation**: Combined extract formulation — effects cannot be attributed to Piper betle alone; relatively small sample size (n=50)
- Reference: PMID 23270333

### Gastroprotection (Preclinical Animal Evidence)

#### Gastroprotective Effect in Rats (Arawwawala 2014)

**Study Design**: Preclinical animal study
- Model: Wistar rats, ethanol-induced gastric ulcer model
- Groups: 9 animals per group
- Extracts tested: Hot aqueous and cold ethanolic extracts

**Doses Tested**: 200, 300, and 500 mg/kg body weight

**Key Results**:
- Marked dose-dependent gastroprotection (r² = 0.97 hot aqueous; r² = 0.96 cold ethanolic)
- Highest dose (500 mg/kg) of both extracts exceeded misoprostol reference drug efficacy
- Hot aqueous extract increased mucus content on gastric walls
- Reduced gastric acid volume vs control
- Acidity and pH unchanged across groups

**Translational Note**: Consistent with traditional use for stomachache and gastric complaints; no human dose established
- Reference: PMC4012360

### Wound Healing (Preclinical Evidence)

#### Piper Betle Leaf Extract Gel in Wound Models (Lien 2015)

**Study Design**: In vitro and in vivo preclinical study
- In vitro: NIH3T3 fibroblast cells at 0.25–20.0 μg/mL concentrations
- In vivo: Swiss mice with burn and excision wound models
- Formulation tested: 5% w/w gel

**Key Results**:
- Fibroblast proliferation: Significantly enhanced across 0.25–20.0 μg/mL range (in vitro)
- Burn wound healing: Statistically significant improvement with 5% gel (p < 0.01)
- Excision wound healing: Statistically significant improvement with 5% gel (p < 0.001)
- Liver malondialdehyde levels: Substantially decreased in treated mice vs controls (antioxidant effect)

**Translational Note**: Supports wound-healing properties consistent with traditional topical use; no human clinical trial conducted
- Reference: PMC4964315

### Anticancer / Chemopreventive (Preclinical Evidence Only)

**Mechanism (Biswas 2022; Gundala 2014; Rai 2011)**:
- Hydroxychavicol and related compounds show dual action:
  - Normal cells: Chemopreventive antioxidant responses; protective against chemical carcinogens
  - Cancer cells: Pro-apoptotic via ROS amplification and selective apoptosis
- NF-κB pathway inhibition documented in vitro (Ou 2025)
- IC50 cytotoxicity values: 7.13–163.30 μg/mL across different cancer cell lines

**Evidence Level**: Preclinical only; no human clinical trials on anticancer effects of isolated Piper betle leaf

---

### Anti-inflammatory (Preclinical/Molecular Evidence)

**Mechanism (Ou 2025)**:
- 23 hydroxychavicol derivatives isolated
- 11 compounds showed PDE4 inhibitory activity with IC50 values 1.8–10 μM
- Compound 7: Most potent PDE4 inhibitor at IC50 = 1.8 μM (comparable to rolipram)
- Compounds 5, 7, 9, 16: Decreased inflammatory mediators (TNF-α, IL-6) in LPS-stimulated macrophages
- Mechanism: PDE4 inhibition + NF-κB pathway suppression (IκB-α and p65 phosphorylation)

**Evidence Level**: In vitro molecular pharmacology; no human trials
- Reference: PMID 40007350

## Research Gaps

- No standardized clinical dosing established for systemic/internal use
- No meta-analyses or systematic reviews of clinical trials
- Bioavailability and pharmacokinetics in humans: absent (explicitly identified as research gap, Biswas 2022)
- No human trials for anti-inflammatory, anticancer, gastroprotective, or wound-healing effects
- Drug interactions: not systematically studied
- Optimal extraction method and dosage for treatment: explicitly identified as unknown (Zamri 2023)
