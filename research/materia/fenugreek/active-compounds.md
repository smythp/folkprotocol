# Fenugreek (Trigonella foenum-graecum) — Active Compounds

## Primary Active Compounds

### Furostanol Saponins (Steroidal Saponins)

The furostanol saponins are considered the primary bioactive class responsible for fenugreek's hormonal and anabolic effects.

**Protodioscin** is the most studied individual saponin:
- A furostanol-type steroidal saponin
- Believed to be the principal compound responsible for testosterone-related effects
- The Furosap extract is standardized to **20% protodioscin** (Maheshwari 2017)
- Proposed mechanisms include stimulation of luteinizing hormone (LH) release and inhibition of aromatase and 5-alpha-reductase, though direct evidence in humans is limited

**Fenugreek glycosides** (broader class):
- The Albaker 2023 systematic review specifically attributes anabolic and androgenic activity to fenugreek glycoside supplementation
- Wankhede 2016 used a fenugreek extract at 600 mg/day and observed significant body composition changes (body fat decrease p=0.01, lean mass increase p=0.004) — attributed to the glycoside fraction

### 4-Hydroxyisoleucine

- An unusual amino acid found almost exclusively in fenugreek seeds
- **Mechanism for blood sugar effects:** Stimulates glucose-dependent insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells — the "glucose-dependent" part is key, meaning it potentiates insulin release when glucose is elevated rather than causing hypoglycemia independently
- This compound is considered the primary driver of fenugreek's glycemic effects documented in Neelakantan 2014 (fasting glucose -0.96 mmol/L, 2h postload -2.19 mmol/L, HbA1c -0.85%)
- Also contributes to the antidiabetic drug interaction risk noted in EMA safety assessments

### Soluble Fiber (Galactomannan)

- Fenugreek seeds are approximately 50% fiber by weight, primarily galactomannan
- Contributes to glycemic control through delayed gastric emptying and reduced glucose absorption
- Also responsible for the mucilaginous quality of fenugreek preparations
- Relevant to the digestive and appetite effects recognized by Commission E and EMA

## Standardized Extracts

Three proprietary standardized extracts appear in the clinical literature:

### Testofen
- **Used in:** Rao 2016 (n=120, healthy aging males 43–70)
- **Dose tested:** 600 mg/day for 12 weeks
- **Standardization:** Proprietary extract standardized to furostanol saponins (specific percentage not publicly disclosed)
- **Results:** Significant increases in total and free testosterone, improved sexual function (p<0.001)
- **Also used in:** Rao 2015 as "Libifem" (same research group, women's formulation at 600 mg/day)

### Furosap
- **Used in:** Maheshwari 2017 (n=100, males 35–60)
- **Dose tested:** 500 mg/day for 12 weeks
- **Standardization:** 20% protodioscin (explicitly stated)
- **Results:** Free testosterone reportedly improved in 90% of participants (open-label, no control — interpret cautiously)
- **Note:** The explicit protodioscin standardization makes this extract's composition more transparent than Testofen

### Libifem
- **Used in:** Rao 2015 (n=80, healthy menstruating women)
- **Dose tested:** 600 mg/day for 2 menstrual cycles
- **Standardization:** Proprietary (same base as Testofen, marketed for women)
- **Results:** Significant increases in free testosterone, estradiol, sexual desire and arousal (p<0.05)

## Compound-to-Effect Mapping

| Compound | Primary Effects | Key Evidence |
|----------|----------------|--------------|
| Furostanol saponins / protodioscin | Testosterone modulation, anabolic activity | Mansoori 2020 (WMD 0.89 nmol/L), Rao 2016, Albaker 2023 |
| 4-Hydroxyisoleucine | Blood glucose reduction, insulin potentiation | Neelakantan 2014 (fasting glucose -0.96 mmol/L) |
| Galactomannan fiber | Glycemic control, digestive effects, appetite | Commission E (6g/day), EMA traditional recognition |
| Steroidal saponins (broad) | Libido, sexual function | Rao 2015 (women), Rao 2016 (men) |

## Extract vs Whole Seed

The clinical trials on testosterone and libido exclusively use standardized extracts (Testofen, Furosap, Libifem) at concentrated doses. The traditional pharmacopoeia (Commission E, EMA, Ayurvedic) reference the whole seed.

The saponin content of whole fenugreek seed is substantially lower than in standardized extracts — the clinical hormonal effects should not be assumed to transfer directly to culinary use of the seed. The glycemic and digestive effects may be more achievable with whole seed due to the contribution of fiber and 4-hydroxyisoleucine at dietary doses.
