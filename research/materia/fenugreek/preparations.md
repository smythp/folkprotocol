# Fenugreek (Trigonella foenum-graecum) — Preparations

## Whole Seed (Culinary)

### Commission E Dosing
- **Daily dose:** 6 grams of drug (dried seed), or equivalent preparations
- Approved for: loss of appetite (internal), local inflammation (external poultice)

### Culinary Forms
- **Whole seeds:** Used in Indian, Ethiopian, Middle Eastern, and North African cooking. Toasted, tempered in oil, or soaked.
- **Ground seed (powder):** Incorporated into spice blends (e.g., panch phoron, berbere), curries, breads
- **Sprouted seeds:** Used fresh in salads and dishes
- **Leaves (Methi / Kasuri Methi):** Fresh or dried leaves used as a culinary herb — the leaf is a different preparation from the seed and has a different compound profile
- **Soaked/mucilaginous preparation:** Seeds soaked overnight produce a mucilaginous liquid consumed as a traditional digestive and blood sugar remedy

### Culinary Dose vs Clinical Extract Dose
This is a critical distinction. A typical culinary serving might include 1–3 grams of fenugreek seed in a dish. The Commission E dose is 6 grams of whole seed per day.

The clinical trials on testosterone and sexual function used **standardized concentrated extracts** at 500–600 mg/day, which represent a substantially higher concentration of furostanol saponins than would be present in 6g of whole seed.

**The hormonal effects demonstrated in clinical trials should not be assumed from culinary use.** The glycemic and digestive effects may be more achievable at dietary doses due to the contribution of fiber (galactomannan) and 4-hydroxyisoleucine, which are present in meaningful amounts in whole seed.

## Standardized Extracts (Clinical)

### Testofen
- **Dose used in trials:** 600 mg/day
- **Duration tested:** 12 weeks (Rao 2016, n=120)
- **Standardization:** Proprietary, standardized to furostanol saponins (exact percentage not publicly disclosed)
- **Clinical context:** Healthy aging males 43–70 years. Significant improvements in total and free testosterone, sexual function (morning erections, sexual activity frequency, p<0.001).
- **Also marketed as Libifem** for women's formulations

### Furosap
- **Dose used in trials:** 500 mg/day
- **Duration tested:** 12 weeks (Maheshwari 2017, n=100)
- **Standardization:** 20% protodioscin (explicitly stated)
- **Clinical context:** Healthy males 35–60 years. Open-label study — free testosterone reportedly improved in 90% of participants, but no placebo control.
- **Note:** Lower dose than Testofen (500 vs 600 mg/day) but with explicit protodioscin standardization

### Libifem
- **Dose used in trials:** 600 mg/day
- **Duration tested:** 2 menstrual cycles (Rao 2015, n=80)
- **Standardization:** Proprietary (same base as Testofen)
- **Clinical context:** Healthy menstruating women with low sexual drive. Significant increases in free testosterone, estradiol, desire and arousal (p<0.05).

## Traditional Ayurvedic Preparations

As **Methi** in the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India:
- **Churna (powder):** Ground seed taken with warm water, milk, or ghee
- **Kwatha (decoction):** Seeds boiled and the liquid consumed
- **Incorporated into formulations:** Used as an ingredient in polyherbal Rasayana (rejuvenative) and Vajikarana (aphrodisiac) preparations
- **With ghee or oil:** Traditional vehicle for enhancing absorption and palatability
- Traditional applications span digestive tonic (Deepana, Pachana), rejuvenative (Rasayana), galactagogue, blood sugar support, and reproductive tonic

## External Preparations

### Poultice (Commission E & EMA approved)
- Ground seed mixed with hot water to form a paste
- Applied topically for minor skin inflammations
- One of only two uses formally approved by Commission E
- The mucilaginous and anti-inflammatory properties of the seed make this effective

## The Culinary-Clinical Gap

The gap between traditional culinary use and clinical extract dosing is significant for hormonal claims:

- **Culinary fenugreek** provides fiber, 4-hydroxyisoleucine, and small amounts of saponins — supports the traditional digestive, glycemic, and appetite uses
- **Standardized extracts** concentrate the furostanol saponins to levels far above what dietary intake provides — the hormonal and sexual function data comes exclusively from these concentrated forms
- **No clinical trials** have tested whole fenugreek seed for testosterone or libido outcomes
- This does not invalidate traditional aphrodisiac use — it means the mechanism and magnitude may differ between the traditional preparation and the studied extract
