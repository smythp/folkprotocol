# Fenugreek (Trigonella foenum-graecum) — Safety Profile

## Regulatory Safety Assessment

### EMA Public Statement (2020)
The European Medicines Agency issued a public statement on Trigonella foenum-graecum L., semen, identifying the following safety concerns:

## Contraindications

### Pregnancy
- **Should not be used during pregnancy** due to reports of uterine stimulant effects in animal studies
- This is a firm contraindication, not merely a precaution
- Note the tension: fenugreek is traditionally used as a galactagogue (postpartum, for milk production) — the contraindication is specifically for pregnancy, not postpartum/breastfeeding use

### Peanut/Soya Allergy Cross-Reactivity
- **Potential for allergic reactions, including cross-reactivity in people with peanut and soya allergies**
- Fenugreek is a legume (Fabaceae family), the same botanical family as peanuts, soybeans, chickpeas, and lentils
- This is an important and often overlooked risk — people with peanut or soy allergies should be warned before using fenugreek
- Reactions can include typical allergic symptoms up to and including anaphylaxis in sensitized individuals

## Drug Interactions

### Anticoagulant Drugs
- **May enhance the effect of anticoagulant drugs** (e.g., warfarin)
- EMA advises caution with concurrent use
- Mechanism: fenugreek contains coumarin compounds that may have additive anticoagulant effects
- Relevant for anyone on warfarin, heparin, or other blood thinners

### Antidiabetic Drugs
- **May lower blood sugar** — diabetics should monitor blood glucose levels closely
- Supported by clinical evidence: Neelakantan 2014 meta-analysis showed fasting blood glucose reduction of -0.96 mmol/L and HbA1c reduction of -0.85%
- The 4-hydroxyisoleucine in fenugreek potentiates insulin secretion in a glucose-dependent manner
- Risk of hypoglycemia when combined with insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas, or other glucose-lowering agents
- Not a reason to avoid fenugreek in diabetics, but monitoring and potential dose adjustment of medications may be needed

## Side Effects

### Gastrointestinal
- **Common side effects include diarrhea and flatulence**
- Consistent with the high fiber content of fenugreek seed (approximately 50% galactomannan)
- Generally mild and dose-related
- More common with whole seed than with standardized extracts

### Body Odor Effect
- Fenugreek consumption commonly produces a distinctive **"maple syrup" body odor** in sweat and urine
- Caused by sotolone (3-hydroxy-4,5-dimethyl-2(5H)-furanone), also the compound responsible for the smell of maple syrup and fenugreek itself
- Not harmful but can be socially noticeable and surprising
- Has been mistaken for maple syrup urine disease in clinical settings (a serious metabolic disorder) — worth noting if undergoing medical testing

## Supplement Quality Concerns

### Adulteration Risk
- Fenugreek supplements are at risk of adulteration with cheaper fillers (e.g., rice flour)
- Contamination with heavy metals, pesticides, or mycotoxins is a potential concern
- **Recommendation:** Choose products with third-party testing and clear standardization (e.g., stated percentage of furostanol saponins or protodioscin)
- The Furosap extract explicitly states 20% protodioscin standardization; Testofen's exact standardization is proprietary

## Clinical Trial Safety Data

Across the clinical trials in the bibliography:
- **Rao 2016** (Testofen, n=120, 600 mg/day, 12 weeks): Serum prolactin and estradiol unchanged — no estrogenic or prolactin-related adverse signal
- **Maheshwari 2017** (Furosap, n=100, 500 mg/day, 12 weeks): No significant adverse events reported
- **Rao 2015** (Libifem, n=80, 600 mg/day, 2 menstrual cycles): No adverse effects reported
- **Wankhede 2016** (600 mg/day, 8 weeks, n=60): No significant adverse events noted

The clinical trial durations (8–12 weeks) limit what can be said about long-term safety.

## Safety Summary

| Concern | Severity | Population |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Pregnancy (uterine stimulant) | Contraindicated | Pregnant women |
| Peanut/soya cross-reactivity | Potentially severe | People with legume allergies |
| Anticoagulant interaction | Moderate | Warfarin/blood thinner users |
| Antidiabetic interaction | Moderate | Diabetics on medication |
| GI effects (diarrhea, gas) | Mild | General |
| Maple syrup body odor | Cosmetic | General |
| Supplement adulteration | Variable | All supplement users |
