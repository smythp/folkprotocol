# Marshmallow Root (Althaea officinalis) - Clinical Uses

## Evidence Overview

Clinical evidence for marshmallow root is limited and primarily supports traditional-use classification under European regulatory frameworks. The EMA/HMPC (2016) classifies marshmallow root as a "traditional herbal medicinal product" — not well-established use — for respiratory and GI indications. Five RCTs have been identified covering dry cough, atopic dermatitis/eczema, vulvovaginal candidiasis, and pediatric fever. No systematic reviews or meta-analyses have been published. The largest clinical dataset (n=822) is an uncontrolled consumer observational survey.

## Dry/Irritative Cough

**Evidence Level**: Traditional use (EMA); limited RCT data

**ACE Inhibitor-Induced Cough (Rouhi & Ganji 2007)**
- Design: Double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT
- Participants: 60 hypertensive patients with ACE inhibitor-induced cough
- Intervention: Althaea officinalis extract drops, 20 drops every 8 hours (three times daily)
- Duration: 4 weeks
- Outcomes:
  - Cough severity reduced from 2.66±0.958 to 1.23±1.006 (significant vs. placebo)
  - 8 of 30 treatment patients showed nearly complete abolition of cough
  - Spirometry: no significant change in either group

**Consumer Survey (Fink et al. 2018, n=822)**
- Design: Two prospective, non-interventional pharmacy-recruited consumer surveys
- Participants: 822 adults purchasing STW42 marshmallow root product (lozenges or syrup)
- Intervention: Aqueous marshmallow root extract STW42
- Duration: 7 days
- Outcomes:
  - Both lozenges and syrup rated as having "good effect" for oral/pharyngeal irritation and dry cough
  - Onset of symptom relief within 10 minutes in the majority of cases
  - Very good tolerability; only 3 minor adverse events (syrup arm), zero adverse events (lozenge arm)
- Note: Uncontrolled design; subject to placebo effects and recall bias. Largest marshmallow root dataset to date.

**Narrative Review Data (Mahboubi 2020)**
- Animal models: marshmallow reduced cough episodes by ~50% vs. codeine (~61%)
- Expectorant activity: improved sputum liquidity by ~90% vs. guaifenesin as standard
- Combination with Zataria multiflora, Zingiber officinale, or Hedera helix increased efficacy

**EMA Clinical Evidence Cited**
- Two uncontrolled studies in ~900 children (cited in EMA 2016 monograph) showed cough reduction without comparator groups
- One adult study (n=63) showed effectiveness vs. placebo (Rouhi & Ganji 2007)

## Atopic Dermatitis / Eczema (Topical)

**Evidence Level**: Small pilot RCTs; active-controlled

**Naseri et al. 2021 (Children, Pilot RCT)**
- Design: Double-blind randomized active-controlled pilot trial with in-silico molecular docking
- Participants: 22 children with atopic dermatitis (completed)
- Intervention: Althaea officinalis 1% topical ointment
- Comparator: Hydrocortisone 1% ointment
- Duration: 4 weeks (twice daily × 1 week, then three times weekly × 3 weeks)
- Outcomes:
  - SCORAD index: significantly greater improvement vs. baseline (p=0.015) and vs. week 1 (p=0.018)
  - Authors describe marshmallow 1% as more effective than hydrocortisone 1% for disease severity
- Note: Pilot study; small sample limits conclusions; authors recommend large-scale RCTs

**Khalighi et al. 2021 (Adults, Phase II RCT)**
- Design: Phase II double-blind RCT; split-body design
- Participants: 40 adults with moderate-to-severe allergic atopic eczema (confirmed by skin prick test)
- Intervention: Topical Althaea officinalis extract (right body side) vs. triamcinolone corticosteroid (left side)
- Duration: 4 weeks; SCORAD assessed at baseline, week 2, week 4
- Outcomes:
  - SCORAD decreased on both sides
  - No significant difference between herb and steroid sides (p=0.716)
  - Conclusion: Althaea officinalis comparable to topical triamcinolone for atopic eczema
- Note: Split-body design limits blinding validity; herb formulation concentration not specified

## Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (Adjunct, Topical)

**Evidence Level**: Single RCT

**Amini et al. 2023 (n=100)**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, controlled clinical trial
- Participants: 100 women with confirmed VVC (n=50 per arm; 139 assessed, 107 randomized, 100 analyzed)
- Intervention: Marshmallow aqueous extract 4% vaginal cream + clotrimazole 1% vaginal cream
- Comparator: Clotrimazole 1% vaginal cream alone
- Duration: 7 consecutive nights treatment; 30-day follow-up
- Outcomes:
  - Itching: significant decrease at 7 days (p=0.001) and 30 days (p=0.001)
  - Dyspareunia: significant improvement at 7 days (p=0.001) and 30 days (p=0.04)
  - Dysuria: significant improvement at 7 days (p=0.001)
  - No significant adverse events from either cream
- Background: Based on Persian traditional medicine use; marshmallow as adjunct to standard antifungal

## Pediatric Fever (Topical/External)

**Evidence Level**: Single RCT; adjunct use

**Goodarzi et al. 2022 (n=92 children)**
- Design: Parallel-group RCT
- Participants: 92 children aged 6 months to 10 years with tympanic temperature >38.3°C
- Intervention: Body wash with white marshmallow extract + acetaminophen
- Comparator: Body wash with lukewarm water + acetaminophen
- Duration: Single session; temperature monitored at 15-min intervals × 1 hour, then at 4h and 6h
- Outcomes:
  - Time to fever resolution: significantly shorter in marshmallow group (B=8.181, 95% CI 3.778–12.584, p<0.001)
  - Frequency of afebrile children: higher in marshmallow group at all time points (p<0.001)
  - Mean temperature reduction: −0.27°C greater in marshmallow group (p<0.001 unadjusted; p=0.127 after confounder adjustment)
  - Heart rate, fever recurrence timing, need for additional acetaminophen: no significant differences
- Note: Topical/external use; not the traditional oral mucilage indication

## Gastrointestinal Indications

**Evidence Level**: Regulatory/traditional only; no dedicated RCTs identified

- German Commission E (1989) and EMA/HMPC (2016) approve use for mild gastric mucosal irritation and mild GI discomfort
- EMA duration guidance: reassess if no improvement within 2 weeks
- Mechanistic basis: bioadhesive mucilage provides protective coating to gastric mucosa

## Critical Notes

1. **No systematic reviews or meta-analyses** on marshmallow root clinical evidence have been published.
2. **EMA classification is traditional use**, not well-established use, reflecting the limited RCT base.
3. **Most rigorous RCT evidence** is for ACE inhibitor-induced dry cough (Rouhi & Ganji 2007) and VVC adjunct treatment (Amini et al. 2023).
4. **Combination product contamination**: many positive studies use marshmallow in combination formulas; isolated marshmallow RCTs are rare.
5. **Small samples** across dermatological trials (n=22 and n=40) limit generalizability.
