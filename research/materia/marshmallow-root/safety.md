# Marshmallow Root (Althaea officinalis) - Safety

## Overall Assessment

Marshmallow root has one of the best safety profiles among medicinal herbs. The EMA/HMPC (2016) found **no adverse effects reported** at the time of their assessment. The U.S. FDA recognizes marshmallow as **GRAS** (Generally Recognized As Safe) for food amounts. No hepatotoxicity signals have been identified across any clinical or surveillance literature. The primary clinical safety concern is a theoretical pharmacokinetic drug interaction due to GI mucilage coating.

## Adverse Events

### From Clinical Trials
- **ACE inhibitor cough RCT** (Rouhi & Ganji 2007, n=60): No adverse effects mentioned
- **Atopic dermatitis pilot** (Naseri et al. 2021, n=22): No specific adverse events reported
- **Atopic eczema Phase II** (Khalighi et al. 2021, n=40): No side effects or reactions to either treatment
- **VVC adjunct RCT** (Amini et al. 2023, n=100): Neither cream caused any significant adverse events
- **Pediatric fever RCT** (Goodarzi et al. 2022, n=92): No adverse effects reported

### From Observational Surveys
- **Fink et al. 2018** (n=822 consumers, 7 days):
  - Syrup formulation: **3 minor adverse events** (nature not specified as serious)
  - Lozenge formulation: **zero adverse events**
  - Tolerability rated as "very good"

### Regulatory Assessment
- **EMA/HMPC 2016**: "No adverse effects reported at time of assessment"
- **German Commission E 1989**: "Adverse effects: None known"

## Drug Interactions

### Primary Mechanism
**Mucilaginous GI coating** — the same mechanism that makes marshmallow therapeutically effective can form a physical barrier over GI epithelium that may delay or reduce absorption of concurrently administered oral medications.

**Clinical significance**: Theoretical and unquantified; no formal clinical drug interaction studies have been conducted.

### Specific Drug Classes

| Drug Class | Concern | Evidence Level |
|------------|---------|---------------|
| **Lithium** | Reduced lithium absorption possible; narrow therapeutic index drug — monitor serum levels | Theoretical |
| **Antidiabetic agents** (oral) | Potential additive hypoglycemic effect via direct polysaccharide activity + delayed drug absorption; blood glucose monitoring advisable | Theoretical |
| **Diuretics / antihypertensives** | Theoretical absorption alteration; no clinical documentation | Theoretical |
| **Any oral medications** | Non-specific absorption delay from mucilage coating | Theoretical |

### Practical Recommendation
**Take marshmallow root preparations at least 1–2 hours apart from other oral medications.**
- Commission E (1989): acknowledged mucilage absorption interaction but listed "drug interactions: none known"
- ESCOP and EMA (2016): advise spacing from other medications

## Pregnancy and Lactation

### Pregnancy
- **Status**: Insufficient reliable data; absence of safety evidence means standard precautionary approach applies
- **Recommendation**: Not recommended during pregnancy unless clinically indicated and supervised by a healthcare provider
- **Teratogenicity data**: No teratogenicity studies identified in any tradition or modern literature
- EMA/HMPC: did not establish safety in pregnancy

### Lactation
- **LactMed (NBK501839)**: No data on excretion of any marshmallow components into breastmilk
- No data on safety or efficacy in nursing mothers or infants
- **Food amounts** (e.g., marshmallow candy): unlikely to harm breastfeeding infants based on GRAS status
- **Galactogogue use**: Some proprietary galactogogue teas (e.g., Mother's Milk tea) contain marshmallow; however, no valid clinical trials support galactogogue efficacy for marshmallow
  - One small manufacturer-sponsored study of Mother's Milk tea showed no differences in infant safety outcomes vs. lemon verbena tea (but this was a multi-ingredient product)
- **Recommendation**: Medicinal amounts during lactation not supported by evidence; avoid unless supervised

## Contraindications

| Contraindication | Evidence Basis |
|-----------------|---------------|
| No absolute contraindications established | Commission E, EMA, ESCOP |
| Pregnancy (theoretical, insufficient data) | Standard precaution; no safety studies |
| Caution with multiple oral medications (timing) | Absorption interaction concern |

## Special Populations

### Children
- **EMA/HMPC (2016)**: Approves use for oral/pharyngeal irritation and dry cough in children **over 3 years**
- For GI indications: approved for adolescents over 12 years; ethanol-based preparations adults only
- Goodarzi et al. 2022 topical study: used in children aged 6 months to 10 years with no adverse events

### Elderly
- No specific safety concerns identified; expected to be well-tolerated given overall safety profile

### Hepatic/Renal Impairment
- No specific studies; mucilage acts locally and is not absorbed systemically, suggesting hepatic metabolism is not a concern for the primary active fraction
- No hepatotoxicity signals identified

## Allergic Reactions

- Theoretically possible (cross-reactivity within Malvaceae family)
- Not documented in clinical literature
- Pollen allergy to Althaea officinalis could theoretically produce cross-reactivity in atopic individuals

## Key Safety Limitations

1. **No long-term safety studies** beyond 4 weeks in any indication
2. **No quantified drug interaction data**: mucilage effect on oral drug absorption is theoretical and uncharacterized
3. **Pregnancy safety completely absent**: no teratogenicity studies; precautionary avoidance warranted
4. **Combination product dominance**: most consumer exposure is in multi-ingredient products making attribution of any adverse effects difficult
