# Marshmallow Root (Althaea officinalis) - Preparations & Dosing

## Critical Preparation Note

**Cold maceration is the preferred extraction method** for oral preparations. Hot water extraction and boiling degrade the high-molecular-weight polysaccharides that provide the primary therapeutic (bioadhesive mucilaginous) effect. Alcoholic preparations (tinctures) have reduced mucilage content because alcohol precipitates polysaccharides. When therapeutic benefit depends on the mucilage fraction, cold-water preparations consistently outperform other methods.

## Standard Oral Preparations

### Dried Root — Cold Macerate (Tea/Infusion)

**Method**: Soak 2–3 g crushed/sliced dried root in 150–200 mL room-temperature water for 6–8 hours (or overnight). Strain. Warm gently if desired — do not boil.

**Dose**: 6 g dried root per day total
**Frequency**: Divided across 3–4 doses daily (approximately 1.5–2 g per dose)
**Form**: Cold macerate/infusion; crushed drug; liquid extract; dry extract; syrup
**Source**: German Commission E (1989); Expanded Commission E (Blumenthal 2000)
**Notes**: 6 g/day is the traditional and regulatory consensus adult dose. Cold maceration maximizes polysaccharide yield. The slippery, viscous quality of the prepared tea is the expected indication of adequate mucilage extraction.

### Syrup (Standardized; e.g., Phytohustil®, STW42-based)

**Single dose**: 10 g
**Frequency**: Several times daily as needed (Commission E)
**Products**: Phytohustil® (Germany; studied in Bonaterra et al. 2022); STW42 syrup (studied in Fink et al. 2018, n=822)
**Onset**: Relief within 10 minutes in majority of cases (Fink et al. 2018 consumer survey)
**Source**: German Commission E (1989)
**Notes**: Syrups use cold-extracted aqueous marshmallow root extract as base; commercially standardized products have most consistent clinical data. Larger clinical dataset (n=822) comes from STW42 syrup/lozenge formulation.

### Lozenges (Standardized; e.g., STW42)

**Dose**: As directed on product label (typically 1 lozenge several times daily)
**Source**: Fink et al. 2018 (consumer surveys); EMA/HMPC 2016
**Notes**: Zero adverse events in 822-consumer lozenge arm (Fink et al. 2018); rated "good effect" for dry cough and pharyngeal irritation; onset within 10 minutes in most cases. Convenient form for throat/cough indication.

### Tincture (1:5 in 25% ethanol)

**Dose**: 5–15 mL
**Frequency**: Three times daily
**Source**: Traditional/practitioner reference (BHMA — British Herbal Medical Association)
**Notes**: 25% ethanol significantly reduces polysaccharide content versus cold aqueous extraction. Tinctures deliver flavonoids, phenolics, and minor constituents more efficiently than water extracts, but provide less mucilage. Use when a convenient liquid formulation is needed and mucilage benefit is secondary. Higher end of dose range (15 mL TID) attempts to compensate for lower polysaccharide density.

### Fluid Extract (1:1)

**Dose**: 2–5 mL
**Frequency**: Three times daily
**Source**: Traditional/practitioner reference
**Notes**: More concentrated than tincture. Alcoholic fraction still limits polysaccharide yield relative to cold water extract. Common in professional herbal dispensing.

## Topical Preparations (From Clinical Trials)

### Topical Ointment for Atopic Dermatitis

**Concentration**: 1% Althaea officinalis ointment
**Application**: Twice daily × 1 week, then three times weekly × 3 weeks
**Duration**: 4 weeks total
**Evidence**: Naseri et al. 2021 (n=22 children); SCORAD improvement; may outperform hydrocortisone 1%
**Note**: Pilot study; formulation details not fully disclosed; further study needed

### Topical Extract for Atopic Eczema

**Concentration**: Not specified in Khalighi et al. 2021
**Duration**: 4 weeks
**Evidence**: Khalighi et al. 2021 (n=40 adults); SCORAD reduction comparable to topical triamcinolone (p=0.716, non-significant difference)

### Vaginal Cream for VVC (Adjunct)

**Concentration**: 4% aqueous extract vaginal cream
**Application**: Nightly, 7 consecutive nights (adjunct to clotrimazole 1% vaginal cream)
**Evidence**: Amini et al. 2023 (n=100); significant improvement in itching (p=0.001), dyspareunia (p=0.001), dysuria (p=0.001) vs. clotrimazole alone at 7 and 30 days

## Duration Guidelines (Regulatory)

| Indication | Recommended Duration Before Reassessment |
|-----------|----------------------------------------|
| Oral/pharyngeal irritation; dry cough | Consult physician if no improvement within **1 week** (EMA) |
| Mild GI discomfort; gastric mucosal irritation | Consult physician if no improvement within **2 weeks** (EMA) |

## Standardization Marker

**Quality marker**: Mucilage content (polysaccharide fraction), defined by European Pharmacopoeia minimum specification for *Althaeae radix*

**Preferred extraction for maximum mucilage**:
1. Cold maceration (room temperature water, 6–8 hours) — **highest yield**
2. Hot water infusion (10 min) — moderate yield
3. Hot decoction — reduced high-MW polysaccharide
4. Alcoholic tincture (25% ethanol) — significantly reduced polysaccharide

**Drug timing**: Separate all marshmallow root preparations from other oral medications by at least 1–2 hours to avoid potential absorption interference from GI mucilage coating.
