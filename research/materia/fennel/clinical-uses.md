# Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare) — Clinical Uses

## Primary Dysmenorrhea

**Evidence level:** Multiple RCTs and 2 meta-analyses. Strongest evidence base of any indication.

### Meta-analyses

- **Lee et al. 2020** (Nutrients, PMID 33182553): 12 trials included
  - vs. placebo (468 participants): SMD −3.27 (95% CI −5.28 to −1.26, p=0.001) — significantly superior
  - vs. conventional drugs (502 participants): SMD 0.07 (95% CI −0.08 to 0.21) — equivalent to drug therapy
  - Conclusion: Fennel is as effective as conventional (NSAID/antispasmodic) drugs for dysmenorrhea

- **Shahrahmani et al. 2021** (J Complement Integr Med, PMID 34187122): 12 trials
  - vs. placebo: SMD −0.632 (95% CI −0.827 to −0.436, p<0.001)
  - vs. mefenamic acid: SMD −0.214 (95% CI −0.446 to 0.017, p=0.07) — no significant difference

### Key RCTs

| Study | N | Dose | Duration | Finding |
|-------|---|------|----------|---------|
| Namavar Jahromi 2003 (PMID 12566188) | 30 women (ages 15–24) | 2% fennel essence, 25 drops every 4 hours orally | Per menstrual cycle (crossover) | Both fennel and mefenamic acid reduced pain vs. control (P<0.001); mefenamic acid more potent on days 2–3 |
| Omidvar 2012 (PMID 23559811) | 50 participants (60 enrolled, 10 dropped) | 30 mg extract capsule 4x daily | 3 days starting at menstrual onset | Significantly more effective than placebo (P<0.05) |
| Ghodsi & Asltoghiri 2014 (PMID 25085020) | 80 female students (40 intervention, 40 control) | 30 mg soft capsule every 4 hours | 3 days before onset through day 5, for 3 months | Significant reductions in nausea, weakness, menstrual duration, and stress at 2–3 months |

**Standard dysmenorrhea dose across RCTs:** 30 mg soft capsule every 4 hours for 3–5 days at menstrual onset.

## Infantile Colic

**Evidence level:** One well-designed RCT (NNT=2). Clinically impressive but specific formulation concentration not published.

- **Alexandrovich et al. 2003** (PMID 12868253): Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter
  - N = 125 infants aged 2–12 weeks meeting Wessel colic criteria
  - Intervention: Fennel seed oil emulsion (specific mg/mL concentration not published in abstract)
  - **Colic elimination rate:** 65% fennel (40/62) vs. 23.7% placebo (14/59)
  - Absolute risk reduction: 41% (95% CI: 25–57%)
  - **NNT = 2** (95% CI: 2–4), p < 0.01
  - No side effects reported in either group

## Menopausal Symptoms

**Evidence level:** 7 RCTs; 2 included in meta-analysis. Mixed results; strong placebo response.

### Meta-analysis

- **Lee et al. 2021** (Complement Ther Clin Pract, PMID 33725577): 7 RCTs, 2 in meta-analysis (N=145 total)
  - Menopausal symptoms: SMD −1.32 (95% CI −1.76 to −0.87, p<0.00001) vs. placebo
  - Unclear benefits for sexual function, QoL, and psychological health

### Key RCTs

**Rahimikian et al. 2017** (Menopause, PMID 28509813):
- N = 90 postmenopausal women (45 treatment, 45 placebo), ages 45–60
- Dose: 100 mg soft fennel extract capsule × 2 daily = **200 mg/day**
- Duration: 8 weeks + 2-week follow-up
- Menopause Rating Scale (MRS) score decreased significantly (P<0.001) in fennel group; no change in placebo
- Between-group differences significant at weeks 4, 8, and 10

**Ghazanfarpour et al. 2018** (PMID 29755029):
- N = 50 postmenopausal women (25 fennel, 25 placebo)
- Dose: 100 mg capsule (30% standardized extract = 21–27 mg anethole) × 3 daily = **300 mg/day**
- Duration: 3 months
- Urinary symptoms: Fennel superior (P=0.03)
- Sleep disorders: 57% improvement fennel vs. 22% placebo
- Memory: 43% decrease fennel vs. 17% worsening placebo
- Hot flushes: Both groups improved equally (high placebo response)
- Note: Authors recommend placebo run-in designs for future menopausal trials

**Ghazanfarpour et al. 2017** (Post Reprod Health, PMID 28990439) — Vaginal Atrophy:
- N = 60 postmenopausal women, 3 months
- Dose: 100 mg capsule × 3 daily
- Vaginal maturation index: No significant between-group differences on any measure (P=0.19–0.82)
- Conclusion: Oral fennel had **no significant positive effects on vaginal atrophy**

## Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

**Evidence level:** One multicenter RCT; combination product (cannot isolate fennel-specific effect).

- **Portincasa et al. 2016** (J Gastrointest Liver Dis, PMID 27308645):
  - N = 121 patients with mild-to-moderate IBS
  - Intervention: Curcumin + fennel essential oil (CU-FEO) capsules
  - Dose: 2 capsules twice daily × 30 days
  - **IBS-SSS reduction:** 50.05 ± 28.85% treatment vs. 26.12 ± 30.62% placebo (P<0.001)
  - Symptom-free rate: 25.9% treatment vs. 6.8% placebo
  - All QoL domains improved in treatment group

**Traditional use (Commission E / EMA):** 1.5 g crushed fruits per cup, 3–4 cups/day (4.5–6 g/day), up to 2 weeks.

## PCOS and Hormonal Conditions

**Evidence level:** Two combination RCTs with positive but limited results.

**Aliakbari et al. 2022** (PMID 36393822) — Fennel + Bunium persicum:
- N = 70 women with PCOS (35 per group, ages 16–40)
- Dose: 25 mg F. vulgare capsule twice daily (+ 60 mg B. persicum)
- Duration: 4 months
- LH: Significant decrease (P<0.001)
- DHEAS: Significant decrease (P=0.021)
- Hirsutism score: Significant decrease (P=0.002)
- Menstrual duration: Normalized (P=0.004)
- BMI: Significant reduction (P<0.001)
- Testosterone, FSH: No significant change
- Caveat: Combination with Bunium — cannot isolate fennel effect

**Ghavi et al. 2023** (PMID 37655000) — vs. Ferula assa-foetida:
- N = 43 completers (3-group trial), 3 months
- Dose: 46 mg twice daily (92 mg/day)
- Ovarian follicle count: Significant decrease vs. placebo (P<0.01)
- Hormones (FSH, LH, testosterone): No significant between-group differences
- Ferula showed greater right ovary follicle reduction than fennel

## Hirsutism (Topical)

**Evidence level:** One small RCT.

- **Javidnia et al. 2003** (Phytomedicine, PMID 13678227):
  - N = 38 patients
  - Topical cream formulations: 1% vs. 2% vs. placebo
  - Hair diameter reduction: 7.8% (1%) and **18.3% (2%)** vs. −0.5% placebo (increase)
  - Dose-dependent response
