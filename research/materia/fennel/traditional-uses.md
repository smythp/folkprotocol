# Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare) — Traditional Uses

## Overview

Fennel has one of the broadest documented traditional use profiles of any European herb. It spans Mediterranean, Ayurvedic, Unani, Chinese, and Middle Eastern medical traditions, with continuous documented use exceeding 2,000 years.

The EMA/HMPC classification as a "traditional herbal medicine" requires ≥30 years documented use, with ≥15 years in the EU — a threshold fennel comfortably exceeds.

---

## European Traditions

**Ancient Greece and Rome:**
- Used as a carminative (gas-relieving) and digestive herb
- Hippocrates and Dioscorides documented fennel for promoting milk production in nursing mothers (galactagogue use)
- Roman soldiers ate fennel seeds to maintain stamina; associated with strength and longevity

**Medieval Europe:**
- One of the nine sacred herbs of the Anglo-Saxon *Lacnunga* manuscript (9th–10th century)
- Widely used for dyspepsia, flatulence, and as a breath freshener
- Fennel water (distilled) used as a digestive aid for infants

**Commission E (Germany) — Formal Recognition:**
- Bitter fennel: dyspeptic complaints, GI spasm/repletion/flatulence, catarrh of upper respiratory tract, mild cramp-like GI complaints, menstrual cramp relief
- Sweet fennel: dyspeptic complaints, flatulence, respiratory catarrh
- Dose: 5–7 g crushed fruits daily as infusion

**EMA/HMPC (European Union) — Traditional Use:**
- Symptomatic treatment of mild, spasmodic gastro-intestinal complaints including bloating and flatulence
- Symptomatic treatment of minor cramps associated with menstrual periods
- Expectorant in cough associated with colds
- Recognized based on ≥30 years of documented traditional use

## Ayurvedic Tradition (India)

- Sanskrit name: *Shatapushpa* ("hundred flowers")
- Rasa (taste): Sweet, pungent
- Properties: Cooling, light, unctuous
- Doshic action: Vata and pitta pacifying
- **Classic uses:**
  - Digestive aid for bloating, gas, abdominal pain (*amadosha*)
  - Carminative for infant colic — fennel water given to infants
  - Menstrual regulator (*artava kara*): promotes menstrual flow, relieves dysmenorrhea
  - Galactagogue: promotes lactation in nursing mothers
  - Eye tonic: used in eyewash preparations
  - Diuretic: promotes urine flow in urinary complaints
- **Traditional preparations:** Decoction, seed infusion (fennel water), churna (powder), oil massage for infant colic

---

## Unani/Islamic Medical Tradition

- Arabic name: *Razianaj* (bitter fennel) and *Badiyan* (sweet fennel)
- Ibn Sina (Avicenna, 11th century) documented fennel in *Canon of Medicine*:
  - Promotes digestion and relieves flatulence
  - Diuretic and menstrual emmenagogue
  - Eye strengthening (fennel juice for cataracts and visual weakness)
  - Galactagogue properties noted
- Used in compound formulations throughout the Islamic world
- Continues in contemporary Unani (Tibb) practice

---

## Chinese Traditional Medicine (TCM)

- Chinese name: *Xiao Hui Xiang* (小茴香)
- **Classification:** Warm, pungent; enters liver, kidney, spleen, stomach meridians
- **Classic actions:**
  - Warms kidney yang (*wen shen yang*)
  - Disperses cold, relieves pain
  - Regulates *qi* and relieves pain (*li qi zhi tong*)
  - Harmonizes stomach
- **Traditional indications:**
  - Cold hernial pain, testicular pain (cold invading liver channel)
  - Abdominal pain from cold-deficiency type conditions
  - Gastric pain with vomiting
  - Dysmenorrhea from cold-stagnation pattern
- **Typical dose:** 3–6 g per decoction formula
- Used primarily in combination prescriptions, rarely as single herb

---

## Middle Eastern and North African Traditions

- Widely used throughout Persia, Egypt, North Africa as digestive herb and spice
- Fennel seeds chewed after meals across the region as carminative and breath freshener
- Used in traditional remedies for infant colic, flatulence, and urinary complaints
- Fennel-infused oil applied topically for abdominal massage in colic

---

## Traditional Preparations

| Preparation | Method | Traditional Use |
|-------------|--------|-----------------|
| Tea/infusion | 1.5 g crushed fruits steeped 15 min in 250 mL hot water | GI complaints, flatulence, cough |
| Fennel water | Distillation of seeds; or cold infusion | Infant colic, eye wash |
| Seed oil | Pressed or steam-distilled | External abdominal massage (colic), internal (respiratory, GI) |
| Powder (churna) | Dried ground seeds | Ayurvedic digestive formulas |
| Tincture | Alcohol extraction | Carminative, antispasmodic |
| Topical poultice | Crushed seeds applied externally | Local analgesia, skin conditions |
| Eye drops | Fennel decoction or juice | Traditional eye tonics (not evidence-based) |
| Chewing seeds | Raw seeds chewed directly | Breath freshener, digestive stimulant |

---

## Cross-Traditional Consensus Uses

The following uses appear across multiple independent traditions (European, Ayurvedic, Unani, TCM, and North African):
1. **Carminative / flatulence relief** — universal across all traditions
2. **Digestive antispasmodic** — for abdominal cramping and colic
3. **Galactagogue** — promoting milk production in nursing mothers
4. **Menstrual regulator** — promoting flow or relieving dysmenorrhea
5. **Infant colic** — fennel water or seed oil traditionally given to infants
6. **Respiratory expectorant** — for cough, catarrh, and bronchial complaints

The clinical evidence base most directly validates the dysmenorrhea and infantile colic uses, both of which correspond to ancient traditional applications.
