# Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare) — Active Compounds

## Plant Part and Varieties

**Used plant part:** Dry fruits (cremocarps/mericarps, commonly called "seeds")

Two primary varieties in clinical and pharmacopoeial use:
- **Bitter fennel** (*var. vulgare*): Higher volatile oil content; more pungent taste; higher fenchone
- **Sweet fennel** (*var. dulce*): Lower fenchone, milder/sweeter taste; higher anethole fraction; preferred for children and culinary use

---

## Volatile Oil (Primary Active Fraction)

### Pharmacopoeial Minimum Standards

| Variety | Minimum Volatile Oil | Standard |
|---------|---------------------|----------|
| Bitter fennel | ≥4 mL/100 g (≥4% v/m) | European Pharmacopoeia; WHO Vol. 3 |
| Sweet fennel | ≥2 mL/100 g (≥2% v/m) | European Pharmacopoeia; WHO Vol. 3 |

### Composition by Variety

**Bitter fennel (*var. vulgare*):**
- trans-Anethole: 60–75% of volatile oil
- Fenchone: 15–22%
- Estragole (methyl chavicol): 3–5%
- Limonene: 1–5%

**Sweet fennel (*var. dulce*):**
- trans-Anethole: 80–90% of volatile oil
- Fenchone: <1%
- Estragole: 4–7%
- Limonene: 1–5%

### Cultivar Variation (Rafieian et al. 2024)

Across cultivars worldwide, wide variation is documented:
- trans-Anethole: 15.23–90.11%
- Estragole: 4.00–63.72%
- Fenchone: 0.03–12.62%

This variation has important implications for standardization and estragole safety assessment.

## Key Constituent Profiles

### trans-Anethole

**Primary active constituent**; responsible for the characteristic anise-like odor.

- **Mechanism of action:**
  - Structurally similar to dopamine and catecholamines (phytoestrogen-like activity)
  - Relaxes intestinal smooth muscle via anethole-dependent mechanism (antispasmodic)
  - Estrogenic effects via β-estrogen receptor modulation (proposed for menopausal and hormonal effects)
  - Inhibits CYP3A4 liver enzyme

- **Population intake data:** Average 1.15 mg/day from fennel-containing supplements among Finnish users (Uusitalo et al. 2016)

- **Standardization target:** Capsule preparations standardized to 30% fennel extract typically contain 21–27 mg anethole per 100 mg capsule

### Fenchone

- Contributes to bitter taste in bitter fennel
- Antispasmodic activity; also carminative
- Much lower in sweet fennel (<1%), which is why sweet fennel is preferred for children

### Estragole (Methyl Chavicol)

- Genotoxic carcinogen in pure form (animal data)
- Risk substantially reduced in whole plant matrix — see Safety file for details
- Higher in sweet fennel (4–7%) than bitter (3–5%) on a relative basis, though bitter fennel has more total oil

### Flavonoids

- **Quercetin** — anti-inflammatory, antioxidant
- **Kaempferol** — antioxidant, some estrogenic activity
- **Rutin** — vascular protective, antioxidant
- **Eriocitrin** — citrus-type flavonoid

These flavonoids also appear to play a protective role against estragole genotoxicity (see Safety file).

### Phenolic Acids

- **Rosmarinic acid** — anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antimicrobial
- **p-Coumaric acid** — antioxidant; monitored in dietary exposure studies
- **Chlorogenic acid** — antioxidant; inhibits glucose absorption

### Fatty Acids (Seed Fixed Oil)

- Petroselinic acid (predominant)
- Oleic acid
- Linoleic acid

### Other Documented Compounds

- Nevadensin (flavone) — key compound inhibiting estragole bioactivation
- Epigallocatechin — catechin-type polyphenol; co-protective against estragole

## Standardization in Clinical Products

### Commercial Capsule Standardization

| Form | Description |
|------|-------------|
| 30% fennel extract | ~21–27 mg anethole per 100 mg capsule |
| Soft capsules | Most studied for dysmenorrhea; 30 mg per capsule |
| Tea/infusion | 1.5 g crushed fruits per cup; volatile oil content variable |

### Preparation Effects on Phytochemical Profile

- **Whole vs. cut fruits:** Whole fruits produce approximately 3-fold less estragole than finely cut material in infusions (van den Berg et al. 2014) — pharmacopoeial preparations specify crushed/bruised (not powdered) fruits
- **Extraction solvent:** Ethanol extracts capture more volatile fraction than aqueous infusions
- **Standardization note:** Volatile oil content and anethole percentage are the primary quality markers

## Pharmacological Activities of Constituents

| Activity | Primary Responsible Constituent(s) |
|----------|-----------------------------------|
| Antispasmodic (GI) | trans-Anethole, fenchone |
| Carminative | trans-Anethole, fenchone |
| Estrogenic/hormonal | trans-Anethole (phytoestrogen) |
| Anti-inflammatory | Rosmarinic acid, quercetin, kaempferol |
| Antimicrobial | trans-Anethole, fenchone, estragole |
| Antioxidant | Quercetin, rutin, rosmarinic acid, chlorogenic acid |
| Galactagogue | trans-Anethole (proposed; mechanism unclear) |
| Hepatoprotective | Flavonoids, rosmarinic acid |
| Memory enhancing | Unclear; possibly trans-anethole |

---

## Bioavailability Notes

- No dedicated human ADME (absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion) pharmacokinetic studies have been published for standardized fennel extracts
- trans-Anethole is excreted in breast milk after maternal consumption
- CYP3A4 inhibition by trans-anethole suggests potential for pharmacokinetic herb-drug interactions
- Further human pharmacokinetic research is needed before definitive bioavailability statements can be made
