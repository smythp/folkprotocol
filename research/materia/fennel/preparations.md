# Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare) — Preparations and Dosing

## Pharmacopoeial Preparations

### Tea / Infusion (Traditional, Commission E / EMA Approved)

**Standard preparation:**
- 1.5 g crushed (not powdered) fennel fruits per cup (250 mL)
- Steep in hot (not boiling) water for 15 minutes
- Cover while steeping to reduce volatile oil loss
- Strain and drink warm

**Dosing:**
| Population | Dose | Frequency | Maximum Duration |
|-----------|------|-----------|-----------------|
| Adults | 1 cup (1.5 g fruits) | 3 times daily (4.5 g/day total) | 2 weeks |
| Children 4–12 years | 1 cup (same preparation) | 3 times daily (~3 g/day effective) | 1 week |
| Children under 4 | Not recommended (EMA) | — | — |

**Source:** EMA/HMPC Sweet Fennel Fruit monograph (2007, revised 2024); German Commission E (5–7 g/day range)

**Notes:**
- Use crushed or bruised fruits, not finely powdered — whole fruit infusions produce ~3-fold less estragole
- Both bitter (var. vulgare) and sweet (var. dulce) fruits are used; sweet is preferred for children due to lower fenchone and milder taste
- Volatile oil minimum: bitter fennel ≥4% v/m; sweet fennel ≥2% v/m (European Pharmacopoeia)

## Standardized Extracts (Capsules)

### 30% Standardized Extract — Menopausal / Hormonal Use

The most frequently studied format in clinical trials for menopausal symptoms, PCOS, and hormonal conditions.

**Standardization:** 30% fennel extract = approximately 21–27 mg trans-anethole per 100 mg capsule

**Doses from clinical trials:**
| Trial | Dose | Frequency | Total/Day | Duration | Indication |
|-------|------|-----------|----------|---------|-----------|
| Rahimikian 2017 | 100 mg | 2x daily | 200 mg | 8 weeks | Menopausal symptoms |
| Ghazanfarpour 2018 | 100 mg | 3x daily | 300 mg | 3 months | Menopausal symptoms |
| Ghazanfarpour 2017 | 100 mg | 3x daily | 300 mg | 3 months | Vaginal atrophy (negative) |

**Practical range:** 200–300 mg/day standardized extract (30%) for hormonal/menopausal indications, for 8–12 weeks.

## Soft Capsules — Dysmenorrhea Protocol

The 30 mg soft capsule at 4-hourly intervals is the most replicated dose format for primary dysmenorrhea.

**Dosing:**
- **Amount:** 30 mg fennel soft capsule
- **Frequency:** Every 4 hours
- **Timing:** Starting 3 days before menstruation (Ghodsi 2014) or at onset of symptoms (Omidvar 2012)
- **Duration:** 3–5 days at menstrual onset; trials extended to 3 menstrual cycles

**Trials using this format:**
- Omidvar et al. 2012 (PMID 23559811): 30 mg × 4 daily, 3 days at onset — significant pain relief vs. placebo
- Ghodsi & Asltoghiri 2014 (PMID 25085020): 30 mg every 4 hours, 3 days before through day 5, 3 months — reduced nausea, weakness, menstrual duration

## Liquid Preparations — Dysmenorrhea

**2% Fennel Essence (Drops):**
- Dose: 25 drops every 4 hours orally
- Source: Namavar Jahromi et al. 2003 (PMID 12566188)
- Equivalent analgesic effect to mefenamic acid 250 mg every 6 hours
- Mean time to effect: ~67–75 minutes
- Note: 16.6% withdrawal rate due to strong odor — practical compliance concern

---

## Seed Oil Emulsion — Infantile Colic

- **Formulation:** Fennel seed oil emulsion (specific concentration not published in the primary Alexandrovich 2003 trial)
- **Route:** Oral
- **Result:** 65% colic elimination vs. 23.7% placebo; NNT=2
- **Practical use:** Commercially available fennel water products for infants; concentration and safety data variable by brand
- **Regulatory caution:** EMA advises against use in children under 4 years despite traditional and RCT evidence; clinicians should weigh on a case-by-case basis

## Tincture

**Standard preparation (traditional):**
- Ratio: 1:5 tincture (1 part fennel : 5 parts 45–60% ethanol)
- Dose: 0.5–2 mL (1:5 tincture) three times daily
- Equivalent to: approximately 0.1–0.4 g dried fruit per dose

**Notes:**
- Less commonly used than tea or standardized extracts for clinical indications
- Alcohol extraction captures more volatile oil fraction than aqueous infusion
- No specific clinical trials using tincture format identified in this bibliography

---

## Topical Preparations — Hirsutism

**Formulation:**
- 1% or 2% fennel extract cream
- Applied to affected skin area

**Clinical data (Javidnia et al. 2003, PMID 13678227):**
- 1% cream: 7.8% reduction in hair diameter
- 2% cream: 18.3% reduction in hair diameter
- Placebo: −0.5% (slight increase)
- Dose-dependent response demonstrated

**Preparation notes:**
- Fennel extract incorporated into a standard topical cream base
- Specific extraction method and carrier not published in detail

---

## Vaginal/Topical Application (Women's Health)

Referenced in Mahboubi 2019 review (PMID 31080784):
- 5% fennel extract has been studied for vaginal application for sexual function, vaginal atrophy, and hirsutism
- Oral fennel extract (100–300 mg/day capsule) did NOT demonstrate benefit for vaginal atrophy in the Ghazanfarpour 2017 RCT
- Topical route may be more relevant for local genitourinary effects

---

## Combination Products

**Curcumin + Fennel Essential Oil (CU-FEO) — IBS:**
- 2 capsules twice daily × 30 days
- Source: Portincasa et al. 2016 (PMID 27308645)
- IBS-SSS reduction: 50.05% vs. 26.12% placebo (P<0.001)
- Caveat: Fennel-specific contribution cannot be isolated from curcumin effect

## Preparation Selection Guide

| Indication | Preferred Preparation | Notes |
|-----------|----------------------|-------|
| GI complaints / flatulence | Tea infusion | Commission E / EMA approved; use crushed fruits |
| Primary dysmenorrhea | 30 mg soft capsule every 4 hours | Best-studied format; 3 days at onset |
| Dysmenorrhea (acute) | 2% essence, 25 drops every 4 hours | Fast-acting; compliance issues with odor |
| Menopausal symptoms | 100–200 mg standardized extract (30%) 2–3x daily | 8–12 week minimum trial |
| Infantile colic | Seed oil emulsion | Use EMA-compliant product; consider age under 4 caution |
| Hirsutism (facial) | 2% topical cream | Applied locally |
| Respiratory/expectorant | Tea infusion | 3 cups/day, max 2 weeks |
| PCOS / hormonal | 25–92 mg/day in capsule form | Limited evidence; adjunctive use |

---

## Quality Markers to Verify in Products

- **Volatile oil content:** Minimum 2% (sweet) or 4% (bitter) per European Pharmacopoeia
- **trans-Anethole percentage:** Primary active marker; standardization target
- **Estragole content:** Should be from whole or crushed fruit preparations, not powdered
- **Variety:** Specify bitter vs. sweet fennel — different fenchone content, different taste profiles and safety margins for children
