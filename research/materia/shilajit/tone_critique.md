# Shilajit Materia Page — Tone Critique

**Reviewed against:** ashwagandha.md (gold standard)
**Date:** 2026-03-29

---

## 1. OVERALL ASSESSMENT: 6/10

The page is well-researched and structurally sound, but it *narrates* where it should *state*. The ashwagandha page is a reference document that respects the reader's time. The shilajit page is a feature article that wants to impress you. It over-explains, over-sells the traditional reverence, and breaks the gold standard voice in several specific ways. The evidence handling is the strongest section; the traditional use section is the weakest. The quality section has the right instincts but reaches for drama instead of letting the facts do the work.

---

## 2. SPECIFIC PASSAGES TO FIX

### 2a. Reverence creep / selling language

**"Few substances in Ayurveda carry the reverence that shilajit does."** (line 136)
- Ashwagandha never tells you how revered it is. It shows you via classifications and etymology. This sentence is marketing copy. Cut it entirely.

**"You can take that as hyperbole, or you can hear in it the weight of what generations of practitioners observed this substance doing for their patients."** (line 137)
- This is editorializing. The Charaka Samhita quote speaks for itself. Adding interpretive framing ("hear the weight") is the page trying to tell you how to feel. Cut.

**"This origin story isn't marketing — it's genuinely what the substance is."** (line 139)
- Protesting that something isn't marketing is marketing. If you describe the geological origin clearly, the reader understands it's real. The defensiveness undermines credibility.

**"Part mineral, part organic, produced on timescales that dwarf human history."** (line 139)
- Purple. Ashwagandha never reaches for grandeur. Replace with the factual geological description without the poetry.

**"...one of Ayurveda's most revered substances, and the early clinical evidence suggests that reverence may be well-placed"** (line 265)
- "Reverence may be well-placed" is the page congratulating itself. State the evidence and let the reader decide.

### 2b. Self-conscious narration

**"The honest caveat"** (line 129, section header)
- Ashwagandha doesn't announce its honesty. It just IS honest. This header says "I'm about to be real with you" — which is what someone says when they've been selling you something. Rename to something neutral like "Limitations" or fold into the evidence summary.

**"Here's what makes this unusual:"** (line 115)
- Conversational bridge that adds nothing. State the fact: "These were healthy men with normally functioning hormonal axes — most adaptogens show effects only in stressed or deficient populations."

**"File this under 'mechanistically plausible, clinically unproven.'"** (line 127)
- Cute. Too cute. Ashwagandha uses "Moderate evidence" as a header. Use the same tier framing.

**"This is the section that matters most."** (line 225)
- Ashwagandha's quality section is titled "Quality Matters (Non-Negotiable)" and then just delivers the information. It doesn't tell you which section to care about. Cut this sentence.

**"Full stop."** (line 229)
- Unnecessary rhetorical emphasis. The statement "Raw, unpurified shilajit is unsafe for human consumption" is already absolute. The "Full stop" is theatrical.

### 2c. Over-long passages

**Traditional Use section** (lines 135-145): ~350 words vs ashwagandha's ~180 words. The geological origin description, the classification of metal types from the Sushruta Samhita, and the purification foreshadowing all bloat this section. Cut by 40%. The traditional section should be: classifications, etymology, key indications, convergence note. Done.

**The Bottom Line** (lines 263-275): Four paragraphs vs ashwagandha's structured three-scenario format (works/doesn't/problems). The shilajit version recapitulates evidence, repeats the quality warning, and adds a "What makes it worth considering" vs "What keeps it tentative" structure that reads like a pros/cons essay. Tighten to match the gold standard's format.

**References section at the end** (lines 279-286): Ashwagandha doesn't have this. The sources are already in the frontmatter. This is redundant. Cut entirely.

## 3. STAT NOTATION AUDIT

Ashwagandha standard format: effect size with units, p-value in parentheses, study context.
Example: `Cortisol reduction -2.36 µg/dL (p<0.0001)`

### Issues in shilajit:

| Location | Issue |
|----------|-------|
| Line 115: "total testosterone up 20.45%" | Percentages without baseline values. Ashwagandha gives absolute values (+96.2 ng/dL). Percentage changes on small baselines can be misleading. Add baseline T levels if available from Pandit 2016. |
| Line 115: "DHEA up 31.35%" | Same issue — percentage without baseline. |
| Line 119: "Sperm count increased 61.4%, motility improved 12-17%" | Motility as a range (12-17%) is sloppy. What's the range? Different motility measures? Specify. |
| Line 119: "testosterone rose 23.5%" | Again percentage only. |
| Line 119: "MDA...suggests the mechanism" | The mechanistic interpretation doesn't belong in the stat line. State the MDA drop as data, interpret later. |
| Line 123: "preserved muscle strength after a fatiguing protocol" | Vague. What was the measured outcome? The frontmatter says "maximal voluntary isometric contraction strength" — use that in the body text too. |
| Line 33 (frontmatter) vs line 115 (body): | Body text says "total testosterone up 20.45%, free testosterone up 19.14%." Frontmatter says "total testosterone +20.45%, free testosterone +19.14%." Consistent, but neither gives absolute values. |
| Missing throughout | Confidence intervals. Ashwagandha doesn't always include them either, but when SMDs are cited, CIs help. |

### Notation inconsistencies:

- Frontmatter uses `+20.45%` format. Body text uses "up 20.45%" prose format. Pick one.
- Some findings include "(all p<0.05)" but the exercise finding uses "(p=0.024)" — the specificity is good but inconsistent with the other entries which lump everything as "p<0.05."
- n= values: frontmatter includes them in evidence.summary (n=75, n=60) but body text doesn't always repeat them. Be consistent.

## 4. VOICE AUDIT

**Gold standard voice (ashwagandha):**
- Declarative. States facts.
- Uses "you" for practical guidance, not for rhetorical questions.
- Bold for emphasis on categories, not for hype.
- Short sentences for key points. Longer sentences only when unpacking nuance.
- Tables for structured comparisons.
- Never explains why it's being honest. Just is.
- Warns bluntly: "FATAL liver injury documented in 23 cases."

**Shilajit departures:**

| Gold standard pattern | Shilajit violation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| States facts | Narrates a journey | "Here's what makes this unusual:" / "File this under..." |
| Never sells | Sells the reverence | "Few substances carry the reverence" / "weight of what generations observed" |
| Bold for categorization | Bold for emphasis/hype | "**Raw, unpurified shilajit is unsafe for human consumption.** Full stop." — the bold is fine, "Full stop" is hype |
| Direct warnings | Dramatized warnings | Ashwagandha: "FATAL liver injury documented in 23 cases." Shilajit: builds a three-paragraph case about why you should be scared |
| Brevity in traditional section | Feature-article treatment | 350 words of geological poetry vs 180 words of classification + etymology |
| Structured Bottom Line | Essay-style Bottom Line | Pros/cons paragraphs vs three clean scenarios |
| No redundancy | References section duplicates frontmatter | Lines 279-286 |

**Voice consistency within the shilajit page itself:**

The evidence section is the tightest — it mostly hits the right register. The traditional section is the loosest — it reads like longform journalism. The quality section splits the difference — good instincts, bad execution. The page would improve dramatically if the whole thing matched the evidence section's register.

Target: Match ashwagandha's three-part structure:

- Standardize n= citation in body text

### Priority 5: Delete References section

Lines 279-286. Redundant with frontmatter sources. Ashwagandha doesn't have one. Cut.

### Not broken (leave alone):

- How To Try It: Clean, practical, matches gold standard register
- What To Track: Good structure, appropriate detail
- Who This Is/Isn't For: Solid, well-matched to gold standard

## Summary

The shilajit page knows what it wants to say. The problem is how it says it. It reaches for reverence where the gold standard reaches for clarity. It narrates where it should state. It dramatizes where it should warn. The underlying research work is solid — this is a rewrite problem, not a research problem. Bring the traditional section, bottom line, and quality section into line with the evidence section's register, and this page jumps from a 6 to an 8.
