# Shilajit - Preparations

## Traditional Preparation: Shodhana (Purification)

Shodhana is the classical Ayurvedic purification process and is **not optional** — it is an integral part of shilajit preparation mandated by the foundational texts.

### Process

1. **Dissolution**: Raw shilajit is dissolved in water or a Triphala decoction (Triphala = combination of three fruits: Amalaki, Bibhitaki, Haritaki)
2. **Filtration**: The solution is filtered multiple times to remove soil, rock particles, and insoluble impurities
3. **Sun drying**: The filtered solution is slowly dried under the sun (**Surya Tapi**) to produce purified resin
4. Repeat cycles may be performed for higher purity

### Why Triphala

Triphala is not arbitrary — it is itself a Rasayana combination with antioxidant properties. The decoction likely serves both as a solvent and as a complexing agent that helps separate heavy metals from the desired organic-mineral matrix.

### Limitations of Traditional Shodhana

Traditional Shodhana reduces impurities but may not meet modern heavy metal safety thresholds (ppb-level limits). The process was developed before analytical chemistry could measure trace metals. Modern purification adds standardized extraction and lab-verified heavy metal testing on top of the traditional approach.

## Modern Purified Extracts

### PrimaVie

PrimaVie is the most clinically studied purified shilajit extract and was used in the key clinical trials:

- Used in Pandit 2016 (testosterone in healthy men)
- Used in Keller 2019 (exercise performance)
- Standardized purification process with documented heavy metal testing
- Standardized to fulvic acid content
- Available as a branded ingredient supplied to supplement manufacturers

### Quality Parameters for Any Extract

- **Fulvic acid content**: Standardized to 20-60% (the primary quality marker)
- **Heavy metal testing**: Batch-specific, third-party COA required — must test Lead, Arsenic, Cadmium, Mercury at minimum
- **Identity testing**: Authentic shilajit vs. adulterants (ozokerite, coal tar, synthetic fulvic acid)

## Dosing from Clinical Trials

### For Testosterone Effects (Healthy Men)

- **Dose**: 250 mg twice daily = 500 mg/day total
- **Form**: Purified extract (PrimaVie)
- **Duration**: 90 days
- **Source**: Pandit 2016 (n=75)
- **Outcome**: +20.45% total T, +19.14% free T, +31.35% DHEA

### For Male Fertility

- **Dose**: 100 mg twice daily = 200 mg/day total
- **Form**: Processed Shilajit capsules
- **Duration**: 90 days
- **Source**: Biswas 2010 (n=60)
- **Outcome**: +23.5% T, +61.4% sperm count, +18.9% morphology

### For Exercise Performance

- **Dose**: 500 mg/day (effective) vs. 250 mg/day (NOT effective)
- **Form**: Purified extract (PrimaVie)
- **Duration**: 8 weeks
- **Source**: Keller 2019 (n=63)
- **Key finding**: Clear dose threshold — 500 mg works, 250 mg does not for exercise outcomes

### Dosing Summary

The effective dose across trials is **500 mg/day** (250 mg twice daily) for general use. The fertility study used a lower dose (200 mg/day) but in a deficient population where there may be more room for improvement. For exercise-specific effects, 500 mg/day is the minimum effective dose based on the Keller crossover design.

## Available Forms

### Resin

- Traditional form — purified shilajit in its natural tar-like consistency
- Dissolved in warm water or milk for consumption
- Harder to dose precisely
- More susceptible to adulteration (visual inspection alone cannot confirm authenticity)
- Requires trust in the supplier's purification and testing

### Powder (Capsules/Tablets)

- Dried and powdered purified extract in capsule or tablet form
- Easier to dose precisely
- Most clinical trials used capsule form
- Standardization to fulvic acid content is more feasible
- More practical for consistent daily dosing

### Liquid/Tincture

- Less common; shilajit dissolved in liquid carrier
- Dosing can be variable
- Less studied in clinical trials

## Quality: Non-Negotiable Requirements

Given the severity of the contamination and adulteration risks (see safety.md), the following are not optional quality preferences — they are requirements:

1. **Third-party Certificate of Analysis (COA)** for heavy metals — batch-specific, not generic
2. **Heavy metal testing** must include: Lead (Pb), Arsenic (As), Cadmium (Cd), Mercury (Hg)
3. **Fulvic acid standardization** — claims should be in the 20-60% range; higher claims warrant suspicion of synthetic fulvic acid adulteration
4. **Reputable manufacturer** with transparent sourcing and purification documentation
5. **Avoid raw/unpurified shilajit** entirely — no amount of traditional authority makes consuming raw geological exudate safe without modern analytical verification

**If a shilajit product cannot provide a current, batch-specific COA with heavy metal results, do not use it.** This is the single most important guidance for shilajit preparation selection.
