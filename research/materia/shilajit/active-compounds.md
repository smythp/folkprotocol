# Shilajit - Active Compounds

## Geological Origin

Shilajit is not a plant extract — it is a mineral-organic exudate formed over centuries to millennia from the slow decomposition of plant matter compressed between rock layers in high-altitude mountain ranges. Found primarily in the Himalayas (3,000-5,000m elevation), but also in the Altai, Caucasus, and other mountain ranges worldwide.

This geological process — plant matter subjected to immense pressure and microbial activity over geological timescales — produces a unique composition that cannot be synthesized or replicated from any single plant source.

## Primary Bioactive: Fulvic Acid

Fulvic acid is considered the primary bioactive component and the key quality marker for shilajit preparations.

### Properties
- Low molecular weight humic substance
- Extremely high oxygen content with abundant carboxyl and hydroxyl groups
- Naturally chelates minerals, making them more bioavailable
- Both water-soluble and lipid-compatible, giving it unusual tissue penetration
- Potent antioxidant and free radical scavenger

### Quality Standards
- High-quality purified shilajit extracts are typically standardized to 20-60% fulvic acid
- Excessively high fulvic acid claims in raw product may indicate adulteration with synthetic fulvic acid
- Fulvic acid content is the primary quality parameter used by reputable manufacturers

### Cognitive Relevance
- Inhibits aggregation of tau protein fibrils in vitro (Carrasco-Gallardo 2012)
- Can disassemble pre-formed tau fibrils
- Tau aggregation is a hallmark of Alzheimer's disease pathology

## Dibenzo-alpha-pyrones (DBPs)

The second major class of bioactive compounds in shilajit:

- Unique to shilajit among commonly used supplements
- Function as electron carriers in the mitochondrial electron transport chain
- May support mitochondrial energy production (ATP synthesis)
- Contribute to the antioxidant activity of shilajit
- Work synergistically with fulvic acid

## Trace Minerals

Shilajit contains a broad spectrum of trace minerals in naturally chelated (bioavailable) forms:

- **Iron (Fe)** — contributed by the mineral rock matrix; relevant to hemochromatosis contraindication
- **Zinc (Zn)** — essential for testosterone synthesis and immune function
- **Selenium (Se)** — antioxidant cofactor (glutathione peroxidase)
- **Copper (Cu)**
- **Manganese (Mn)**
- **Chromium (Cr)**
- **Magnesium (Mg)**
- Plus numerous other trace elements in smaller quantities

The mineral content varies depending on the geographic source and rock composition — this is why the classical Ayurvedic texts classified shilajit into types based on the associated rock (gold, silver, copper, iron, lead, tin).

## Bioavailability Enhancement Properties

One of shilajit's most distinctive pharmacological properties is its ability to enhance absorption of co-administered compounds:

- Fulvic acid acts as a natural bioavailability enhancer
- Has been shown to enhance absorption of CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10) when taken together
- The mineral chelation properties of fulvic acid may facilitate transport of other nutrients across intestinal membranes
- This property has implications for formulation — shilajit may potentiate other compounds in a stack

This bioenhancing effect is pharmacologically significant: it means shilajit's clinical effects may be partly indirect, improving the efficacy of endogenous compounds and co-administered substances rather than acting solely through its own bioactives.

## Composition Summary

| Component | Role | Significance |
|-----------|------|-------------|
| Fulvic acid (20-60%) | Primary bioactive | Antioxidant, mineral chelator, tau inhibitor, bioenhancer |
| Dibenzo-alpha-pyrones | Mitochondrial support | Electron carriers, ATP synthesis support |
| Trace minerals (Fe, Zn, Se, etc.) | Cofactors | Naturally chelated/bioavailable forms |
| Humic substances | Matrix | Contribute to overall activity |

## Key Distinction from Plant-Based Supplements

Shilajit's composition — the combination of organic acids (fulvic acid, DBPs) with naturally chelated minerals — is fundamentally different from plant-based supplements. It cannot be meaningfully compared to a single-compound extract. The geological formation process creates a matrix where the organic and mineral components are interlinked, which is why purification must preserve this matrix rather than isolating individual compounds.
