# Shilajit - Safety Profile

## Critical Safety Warning: Heavy Metal Contamination

**Heavy metal contamination is THE safety story with shilajit.** Unlike most herbal supplements where safety concerns are secondary, with shilajit the safety concern is primary and must be addressed before any discussion of efficacy matters.

**Raw, unpurified shilajit is unsafe for human consumption.**

### Contaminants Found in Raw Shilajit

- **Lead (Pb)** — neurotoxin, accumulates in bone
- **Arsenic (As)** — carcinogen, multi-organ toxicity
- **Cadmium (Cd)** — nephrotoxin, accumulates in kidneys
- **Mercury (Hg)** — neurotoxin, especially organic forms

These are not trace-level concerns. Shilajit is literally a geological exudate from rock — it concentrates whatever metals are present in the source rock. The potential for dangerous levels of heavy metals is inherent to the material's nature, not a rare manufacturing defect.

### Mitigation

- Only consume purified Shilajit from reputable brands that provide batch-specific, third-party Certificates of Analysis (COA) for heavy metals
- Testing must include at minimum: Lead (Pb), Arsenic (As), Cadmium (Cd), and Mercury (Hg)
- Traditional Ayurvedic Shodhana (purification) with Triphala decoction was developed precisely to address this issue — the ancients knew raw shilajit was dangerous
- Modern purified extracts (e.g., PrimaVie) use standardized purification processes with documented heavy metal testing

## Adulteration

The shilajit market is severely compromised by counterfeit and adulterated products. This is arguably worse than the heavy metal issue because adulteration can be invisible to the consumer.

### Common Adulterants

- **Ozokerite** — a mineral wax that visually resembles shilajit resin
- **Asphalt or coal tar** — cheap black sticky substances passed off as shilajit
- **Humic substances from soil** — contains fulvic acid but without shilajit's full compound matrix
- **Synthetic fulvic acid** — used to inflate fulvic acid content on lab tests, making adulterated product appear high-quality
- **Fillers** — charcoal, sand, or plant gums used to bulk up product

**Red flag**: Excessively high fulvic acid claims in raw product may indicate adulteration with synthetic fulvic acid rather than genuine high quality.

## Regulatory Status

**No pharmacopoeial monograph exists for shilajit.** This is significant:
- Not in the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India (API)
- Not in the WHO monographs on medicinal plants
- Not assessed by the European Medicines Agency (EMA)
- No USP (United States Pharmacopeia) monograph

This means there are no internationally recognized quality standards, no standardized identity tests, and no official purity criteria. The consumer is entirely dependent on manufacturer self-regulation and third-party testing. This regulatory gap makes adulteration easier and quality assurance harder.

## Drug Interactions

### Established Concerns

- **Blood sugar medications** (Insulin, Metformin): May enhance hypoglycemic effect — risk of dangerously low blood sugar
- **Anticoagulants** (Warfarin): May potentially interfere with blood clotting
- **Immunosuppressants**: May modulate the immune system, potentially counteracting immunosuppressive therapy
- **Thyroid medications**: Contains minerals (including iodine in some sources) that could affect thyroid function
- **Blood pressure medications**: May have an additive hypotensive effect

### Mechanism
Many of these interactions stem from shilajit's bioavailability-enhancing properties — fulvic acid may increase absorption of co-administered drugs, effectively raising their dose.

## Contraindications

### Who Should Avoid Shilajit

- **Pregnant or breastfeeding women** — lack of safety data; heavy metal risk is especially concerning for fetal development
- **Individuals with hemochromatosis** (iron overload) — shilajit contains iron in bioavailable forms, which would worsen iron accumulation
- **People with active gout** — may increase uric acid levels

## General Safety in Clinical Trials

When purified shilajit from reputable sources is used:
- Pandit 2016 (n=75, 500 mg/day, 90 days): No serious adverse events reported in healthy men
- Biswas 2010 (n=60, 200 mg/day, 90 days): No serious adverse events reported
- Keller 2019 (n=63, up to 500 mg/day, 8 weeks): No serious adverse events reported
- Carrasco-Gallardo 2012 review describes it as "a potent and very safe dietary supplement"

**The safety profile of purified, tested shilajit appears good. The danger is in the supply chain, not the substance itself.**

## Safety Summary

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Heavy metal contamination (raw) | **HIGH** | Only use purified product with COA |
| Adulteration | **HIGH** | Reputable brands, third-party testing |
| No pharmacopoeial standard | Moderate | Increases reliance on manufacturer QC |
| Drug interactions | Moderate | Screen medications, especially diabetes/anticoagulant |
| Hemochromatosis | Moderate | Absolute contraindication |
| Pregnancy/lactation | Moderate | Avoid entirely |
