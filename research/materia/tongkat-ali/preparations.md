# Tongkat Ali (Eurycoma longifolia) - Preparations & Dosing

## Traditional Preparation

### Water Decoction (Historical Method)
- **Method:** Sliced or chopped roots boiled in water
- **Solvent:** Water only — no evidence of traditional alcohol extraction
- **Character:** Extremely bitter due to quassinoid content; bitterness was considered part of the medicine
- **Plant part:** Root (primary), occasionally bark
- **Cultural context:** Prepared by traditional healers among the Orang Asli (indigenous Malaysians) and in Malay, Indonesian, and Vietnamese folk medicine

**Relevance to modern use:** The traditional water decoction is the historical basis for modern hot-water extraction methods. Standardized commercial extracts (Physta, LJ100) use hot-water extraction followed by freeze-drying — essentially an industrial-scale, quality-controlled version of the traditional decoction.

## Modern Standardized Extracts

### Physta (Biotropics Malaysia)

**Description:**
- Freeze-dried water extract of Eurycoma longifolia root
- Standardized to eurycomanone content
- Manufactured to comply with Malaysian Standard MS 2409:2011
- The most clinically studied form of tongkat ali

**Clinical Trial Usage:**

| Study | Dose | Duration | Population | Key Results |
|-------|------|----------|------------|-------------|
| Leitao 2021 | 100 mg/day vs 200 mg/day vs placebo | 12 weeks | 105 hypogonadal men (50–70 yrs, T < 300 ng/dL) | 200 mg: T from 9.30 → 11.23 nmol/L; 100 mg: T from 8.85 → 10.11 nmol/L |
| Talbott 2013 | 200 mg/day | 4 weeks | 63 moderately stressed adults (32M, 31F) | Cortisol −16%, testosterone +37% |
| Udani 2014 | 200 mg Physta + 100 mg Polygonum minus | 12 weeks | 26 men (40–65 yrs) | SHIM improved (p=0.005), erection hardness improved (p=0.038) |

**Manufacturer note:** Biotropics Malaysia manufactures Physta and has funded or been affiliated with most published clinical trials. This is a significant conflict of interest.

### LJ100 (HP Ingredients / Biotropics)

**Description:**
- Standardized hot-water extract
- "LJ" = longifolia Jack (the botanical authority name)
- Claims standardization to 22% eurycomanone and 40% glycosaponins (figures vary by source)
- Marketed primarily in North America and internationally
- Essentially the same extraction platform as Physta

## Dosing from Clinical Evidence

### Optimal Dose: 200 mg/day Standardized Extract

**Evidence basis:**
- Leitao 2021 directly compared 100 mg vs 200 mg vs placebo over 12 weeks:
  - 200 mg produced significantly greater testosterone increase than placebo at weeks 4, 8, and 12
  - 100 mg produced a smaller increase
  - 200 mg also significantly reduced cortisol
- Talbott 2013 used 200 mg/day with significant cortisol (−16%) and testosterone (+37%) effects at 4 weeks
- Udani 2014 used 200 mg/day Physta (in combination) with sexual function improvements

**Duration for effect:**
- Cortisol/stress effects: observed at 4 weeks (Talbott 2013)
- Testosterone effects: significant at 4 weeks in 200 mg group, continued through 12 weeks (Leitao 2021)
- Meta-analysis: significant testosterone increases only with supplementation > 8 weeks (Leisegang 2022)

**Dose range across all studies reviewed in meta-analysis:** 100–600 mg/day

### Who Benefits

Based on the Leisegang 2022 meta-analysis and individual trial data:
- **Hypogonadal men (low testosterone):** Significant benefit (MD 2.27 nmol/L; p = 0.02)
- **Eugonadal men (normal testosterone):** No significant benefit (p = 0.50)
- **Moderately stressed individuals:** Cortisol reduction and mood improvements (Talbott 2013)
- **Men with lower baseline erectile function:** Significant IIEF-5 improvement (Kotirum 2015 subgroup MD 3.32, p = 0.04)

## Quality Verification — Why It Matters More for This Herb

Tongkat ali has a worse adulteration problem than most herbs. The combination of sexual enhancement marketing, expensive raw material, and high consumer demand creates strong incentives for fraud.

### Minimum Quality Indicators
1. **Eurycomanone standardization** — a specific percentage (e.g., 0.8–1.5%), not a ratio claim
2. **Third-party Certificate of Analysis (CoA)** — from an independent laboratory, not the manufacturer's own
3. **Compliance with MS 2409:2011** — the Malaysian national standard for tongkat ali extract
4. **Testing for adulterants** — specifically sildenafil and tadalafil (PDE5 inhibitors commonly spiked into sexual enhancement supplements)
5. **Heavy metal testing** — mercury and lead contamination documented

### Red Flags
- **Ratio claims (100:1, 200:1)** — these are marketing, not analytical specifications; they are unverifiable and meaningless
- **Aggressive sexual enhancement claims** — products marketed primarily for sexual enhancement are at higher risk of PDE5 inhibitor spiking
- **No standardization information** — if the label doesn't specify eurycomanone content, the product quality is unknown
- **Unusually low price** — genuine tongkat ali root extract is expensive to produce; very cheap products likely contain little to no actual Eurycoma longifolia

## Forms NOT Supported by Clinical Evidence

### Raw Root Powder (Not Extracted)
- Not used in any clinical trial
- Lower concentration of active compounds vs extracted form
- Would require much higher doses to approximate clinical trial doses
- Bitter taste makes large doses impractical

### Alcohol/Ethanol Tinctures
- Not the traditional preparation method (water decoction was traditional)
- No clinical trials using alcohol-based extracts
- Different compound profile than water extraction — may extract different compounds
- Cannot assume equivalence to the water extracts used in clinical research

### Ratio Extracts Without Standardization
- No clinical validation
- Unverifiable claims
- See active-compounds.md for detailed explanation of why ratios are meaningless
