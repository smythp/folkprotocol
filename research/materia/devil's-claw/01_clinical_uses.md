# Devil's Claw - Clinical Uses

**Species:** *Harpagophytum procumbens* DC. (primary); *Harpagophytum zeyheri* Decne. (secondary)
**Plant part:** Dried secondary root tubers
**Active marker:** Harpagoside (iridoid glycoside)

Clinical evidence is strongest for musculoskeletal pain — low back pain and osteoarthritis. Most trials use one of two proprietary aqueous/aqueous-ethanolic extracts: WS 1531 (Schwabe) or Doloteffin (60 mg harpagoside/day). Note: 86% of clinical trials were published >20 years ago (Bargsten & Seifert 2025).

---

## Low Back Pain

### Chrubasik 1996 — First LBP RCT
- **Sample:** 118 enrolled; 109 completed (treatment n=51, placebo n=54)
- **Population:** Acute low back pain
- **Dose:** 6,000 mg crude preparation/day = 50 mg harpagoside/day, two tablets three times daily
- **Duration:** 4 weeks
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled
- **PMID:** 23194854

**Primary Outcome:**
- Pain-free status: **9/51 (17.6%) treatment vs. 1/54 (1.9%) placebo**
- Arhus index: greater improvement in treatment group (94% confidence level)
- Tramadol rescue medication: no significant difference between groups

**Safety:** No demonstrable adverse effects observed.

**Clinical Significance:** Established initial proof-of-concept; modest absolute pain-free rate but striking placebo difference (17.6% vs 1.9%).

---

### Chrubasik 1999 — Dose-Comparison RCT (WS 1531)
- **Sample:** 197 enrolled; 183 completed
- **Population:** Chronic LBP susceptibility with current exacerbation (median chronic pain duration 15 years; ~82–83% with exacerbation >3 months)
- **Dose:** Three arms — Placebo / WS 1531 600 mg/day (50 mg harpagoside) / WS 1531 1200 mg/day (100 mg harpagoside)
- **Duration:** 4 weeks
- **Design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind (3-arm)
- **PMID:** 10101629

**Primary Outcome — pain-free without rescue tramadol for ≥5 of last 7 days:**
- Placebo: 3 patients
- 600 mg (50 mg harpagoside): 6 patients
- 1200 mg (100 mg harpagoside): **10 patients**
- Dose-response trend: **p=0.027**

**Secondary Outcomes:**
- Higher dose superior especially for patients with pain <42 days duration and severe/radiating symptoms
- No evidence of Harpagophytum-related side effects except mild, infrequent GI symptoms

**Clinical Significance:** First dose-comparison RCT establishing harpagoside-dependent efficacy. 100 mg/day arm outperformed 50 mg/day. Critical result: preparation formulation and harpagoside dose are the primary determinants of efficacy.

---

### Chrubasik 2003 — Devil's Claw vs. Rofecoxib (Vioxx) Pilot
- **Sample:** 88 enrolled (44/arm); completers: 43 Doloteffin, 36 rofecoxib
- **Population:** Acutely exacerbated LBP (84% chronic pain >6 days; ~90% acute exacerbation >90 days)
- **Dose:** Doloteffin 60 mg harpagoside/day vs. Rofecoxib 12.5 mg/day
- **Duration:** 6 weeks
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, double-dummy pilot
- **Rescue:** Tramadol up to 400 mg/day permitted in both groups
- **PMID:** 12509627

**Primary Outcome:**
- Pain-free ≥5 days without rescue medication: **10 (Doloteffin) vs. 5 (rofecoxib)** — not statistically significant
- >50% pain reduction: 18 (Doloteffin) vs. 12 (rofecoxib)
- Mean Arhus Index pain reduction: 23% SD 52 (Doloteffin) vs. 26% SD 43 (rofecoxib)

**Adverse Events:**
- 14 patients per group experienced 39 total AEs
- Drop-outs due to AEs: **Doloteffin=1, rofecoxib=6** (plus 2 additional rofecoxib drop-outs from excessive back pain)
- GI complaints: 8 (Doloteffin) vs. 9 (rofecoxib), more severe in rofecoxib group

**Clinical Significance:** Pilot insufficient for equivalence claims; efficacy non-inferior to rofecoxib with substantially better tolerability (1 vs. 6 AE-related drop-outs).

---

### Chrubasik 2005 — 54-Week Long-Term Follow-Up
- **Sample:** 73 entered follow-up (38 ex-Doloteffin + 35 ex-rofecoxib); 43 completed 54 weeks
- **Total patient-days:** 21,761
- **Dose:** Doloteffin 60 mg harpagoside/day
- **Design:** Open-label follow-up of Chrubasik 2003 RCT
- **PMID:** 15693701

**Pain Distribution Across 21,761 Patient-Days:**
- **28% pain-free; 39% mild; 22% moderate; 8.5% severe; 1.5% excruciating**
- No meaningful difference between ex-Doloteffin and ex-rofecoxib groups
- Modest overall improvement in Arhus and HAQ scores vs. baseline

**Safety:** Three patients had minor drug reactions; long-term treatment well-tolerated.

**Clinical Significance:** First and only long-term (54-week) safety data for Harpagophytum; confirms tolerability over extended use. Primary limitation: open-label, no placebo arm.

## Osteoarthritis (Hip and Knee)

### Chantre 2000 — Devil's Claw vs. Diacerhein (Hip/Knee OA)
- **Sample:** 122 patients with knee and hip osteoarthritis
- **Dose:** Harpadol 6 capsules/day (6 × 435 mg cryoground powder = 2,610 mg/day) vs. Diacerhein 100 mg/day
- **Duration:** 4 months
- **Design:** Randomized, double-blind, multicenter
- **PMID:** 11185727

**Primary Outcomes:**
- 10 cm VAS pain: significant improvement in both groups; **no significant between-group difference**
- Lequesne functional index: significant reduction in both groups; **no significant between-group difference**

**Secondary Outcomes:**
- NSAID and analgesic use: **significantly lower in Harpadol group**
- Diarrhea rate: **8.1% (Harpadol) vs. 26.7% (diacerhein)**
- Total adverse events: **significantly lower frequency in Harpadol group**

**Clinical Significance:** Comparable efficacy to diacerhein (an established OA drug) with superior safety profile. The reduced need for rescue analgesics/NSAIDs is a clinically meaningful secondary endpoint.

---

### Leblan 2000 — Companion Paper to Chantre 2000
- **Sample:** 122 hip and/or knee OA patients (same population as Chantre 2000)
- **Dose:** Harpagophytum 2,610 mg/day vs. Diacerhein 100 mg/day
- **Duration:** 4 months
- **PMID:** 11143915

**Outcomes:** VAS pain, Lequesne score, functional disability — considerable improvement in both groups with no significant between-group differences. Analgesic/NSAID use significantly reduced in Harpagophytum group.

---

### Wegener 2003 — Aqueous Extract for Hip/Knee Arthrosis
- **Sample:** 75 patients with hip or knee arthrosis
- **Dose:** Aqueous extract 2,400 mg/day containing 50 mg harpagoside
- **Duration:** ~12 weeks
- **Design:** Uncontrolled multicenter surveillance (no placebo arm)
- **PMID:** 14669250

**WOMAC Outcomes:**
- Pain subscale: **reduced 23.8%**
- Stiffness subscale: **reduced 22.2%**
- Physical function: **reduced 23.1%**
- Overall WOMAC: **decreased 22.9%**

**VAS:** Actual pain decreased **25.8%**
**Clinical measures:** Pain on palpation −45.5%; Mobility limitation −35%

**Safety:** Two cases of dyspeptic complaints and sensation of fullness.

**Limitation:** No placebo arm; effect direction is clinically meaningful but requires controlled confirmation.

---

### Chrubasik 2002 — Doloteffin Postmarketing Surveillance (Multi-Site Pain)
- **Sample:** 250 enrolled; 227 completed (back n=104, knee n=85, hip n=61)
- **Dose:** Doloteffin 60 mg harpagoside/day
- **Duration:** 8 weeks
- **Design:** Comparative multicenter postmarketing surveillance (observational)
- **PMID:** 12046857

**Outcomes:**
- **50–70% of patients benefitted** from Doloteffin with few adverse effects
- Both generic and disease-specific measures improved by week 4, further gains by week 8
- Hip group showed more convincing improvement than back group
- ~10% experienced minor adverse events possibly attributable to Doloteffin
- Back pain patients required significantly more NSAIDs than other groups (though usage declined over time)

---

### Farpour 2021 — Teltonal vs. Meloxicam (Knee OA)
- **Sample:** 60 adults with mild knee OA grades 1–2, aged 40–60
- **Dose:** Teltonal 2 × 480 mg tablets/day (1 month) vs. Meloxicam 15 mg/day (10 days)
- **Duration:** 1-month treatment; 8-week follow-up
- **Design:** Randomized active-controlled
- **PMID:** 34712343

**Outcomes at 8 Weeks:**
- VAS pain: Teltonal **4.80 ± 1.80** vs. meloxicam **5.06 ± 1.43** (no significant between-group difference)
- Oxford Knee Scale: Teltonal 34.06 ± 4.38 vs. meloxicam 34.00 ± 7.87
- WOMAC total: Teltonal 25.73 ± 10.11 vs. meloxicam 26.20 ± 13.94
- Both groups showed significant improvement over time in all measures (p<0.05)

**Clinical Significance:** Most recent RCT (2021); confirms short-term efficacy comparable to meloxicam for mild KOA. Viable alternative for patients unable to tolerate NSAIDs. **Note:** 1-month herb treatment vs. 10-day NSAID course is not directly comparable in exposure duration.

## Systematic Review Findings

### Gagnier 2004 — 12-Trial Systematic Review
- **Scope:** 6 OA, 4 LBP, 2 mixed-pain conditions
- **PMID:** 15369596

**Evidence Hierarchy:**
- **Strong evidence:** Aqueous extract at 50 mg harpagoside/day for acute exacerbations of chronic non-specific LBP
- **Moderate evidence:** Aqueous extract 100 mg harpagoside/day for acute LBP exacerbations
- **Moderate evidence:** Powder at 60 mg harpagoside/day for spine, hip, knee OA
- **Limited evidence:** Ethanolic extract <30 mg harpagoside/day for knee/hip OA

Key conclusion: **Preparation formulation and harpagoside dosage critically determine therapeutic effectiveness.**

---

### Gagnier 2007 — Cochrane Review (LBP)
- Strong evidence for short-term pain improvement at 50–100 mg harpagoside/day
- One trial demonstrated **equivalence to 12.5 mg rofecoxib/day** for pain reduction
- Harpagophytum, Salix alba, and Capsicum frutescens all reduce pain more than placebo
- **PMID:** 17202897

---

## Overall Clinical Evidence Assessment

### Strengths
1. Multiple RCTs showing consistent efficacy signal for LBP and OA
2. Comparator trials vs. active drugs (rofecoxib, meloxicam, diacerhein) with non-inferior results
3. One 54-week open-label safety dataset (21,761 patient-days)
4. Two well-characterized proprietary extracts (WS 1531, Doloteffin) with reproducible dosing
5. Cochrane review endorsement for LBP at 50–100 mg harpagoside/day

### Limitations
1. **Majority of trials >20 years old** (86% pre-2007 per Bargsten 2025)
2. **No trials exceed 54 weeks** (only open-label)
3. **Methodological quality generally poor** in older trials
4. **Preparation heterogeneity** — results may not transfer between extract types
5. **Small sample sizes** in most individual trials

### Clinical Bottom Line

**Low back pain:** Best-supported indication. 50–100 mg harpagoside/day from aqueous extract for 4 weeks significantly reduces pain vs. placebo (strong Cochrane evidence). 60 mg harpagoside/day non-inferior to rofecoxib 12.5 mg/day with better tolerability.

**Osteoarthritis (hip/knee):** Moderate evidence for ≥50 mg harpagoside/day. Equivalent to diacerhein and meloxicam in direct comparators, with better adverse event profiles. At least 4 months required for OA benefit.

**Minimum effective dose:** 50 mg harpagoside/day; 100 mg/day shows dose-dependent superiority for LBP. Ethanolic extracts below 30 mg harpagoside/day have limited evidence.
