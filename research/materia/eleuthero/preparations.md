# Eleuthero (Eleutherococcus senticosus) - Preparations and Dosing

*Extracted from: bibliographies/eleuthero.yaml*
*Date: 2026-02-27*

## Regulatory Standard Dosing

All three major regulatory authorities (Commission E, WHO, EMA) converge on similar doses for dried root and liquid extracts.

### German Commission E (1991)
- **Dried root:** 2–3 g/day
- **Equivalent preparations:** Proportional
- **Duration:** Up to 3 months; course may be repeated

### WHO Monographs Vol. 2 (2002)
- **Dried root (decoction):** 2–3 g/day
- **Liquid extract (1:1, ethanol):** 2–3 mL/day
- **Tincture (1:5):** 10–15 mL/day

### EMA Community Herbal Monograph (2014)
- **Dry extract (4–8:1, 30% ethanol):** 100–200 mg, 1–3 times daily
- **Liquid extract (1:1, 30% ethanol):** 2–3 mL/day
- **Tincture (1:5, 40% ethanol):** 10–15 mL/day
- **Maximum duration:** Up to 2 months

## Preparation Types

### 1. Dried Root / Decoction (Tea)

**Method:**
- Simmer 2–3 g dried root material in 240–300 mL water for 20–30 minutes
- Strain and drink 1–2 cups per day

**Dose:** 2–3 g dried root/day (Commission E / WHO)
**Notes:**
- Aqueous extraction favors polysaccharide (Eleutheran) content over eleutheroside extraction
- Traditional TCM preparation method
- Eleutheroside B and E content lower than ethanolic preparations
- Bitter, slightly woody taste; often combined with other adaptogen roots

**Standardization:** No standardization possible with home decoction; Ph. Eur. quality standard (≥0.08% eleutherosides B + E) applies to dried root material — verify source quality

---

### 2. Liquid Extract / Fluid Extract (1:1)

**Preparation:** 1 part herb to 1 part solvent (30–40% ethanol), maceration or percolation

**Dose:** 2–3 mL/day (WHO and EMA)
**Notes:**
- Most common form used in Soviet clinical trials (the SHE extract was a 40% ethanolic fluid extract)
- Soviet trial standard dose: 2 mL/day (most common across 14 of 46 reviewed Soviet trials per Gerontakos 2021)
- Bohn 1987 used 10 mL × 3 daily = 30 mL/day (exceptionally high dose; immunostimulatory trial)
- Dowling 1996 used 3.4 mL/day; Gaffney 2001 used 8 mL/day (equivalent to 4 g/day dried root)

---

### 3. Tincture (1:5)

**Preparation:** 1 part herb to 5 parts solvent (40% ethanol)

**Dose:** 10–15 mL/day (WHO and EMA)
**Notes:**
- More dilute than fluid extract; larger volume needed for equivalent dose
- Shelf-stable; suitable for home use
- EtOH content: 10–15 mL of 40% tincture delivers approximately 4–6 mL pure ethanol per day (moderate alcohol content; relevant for patients avoiding alcohol)

---

### 4. Dry Standardized Extract (Capsules/Tablets)

**Preparation:** Concentrated dry extract, typically 4–8:1 ratio from 30% ethanol extraction

**Dose:** 100–200 mg, 1–3 times daily (EMA)
**Notes:**
- Most convenient commercial form
- **Standardization target:** 0.8–1% total eleutherosides (B + E) is typical commercial standard
- At 0.8% eleutherosides: 200 mg extract delivers ~1.6 mg eleutherosides B + E
- Schaffler 2013 used 120 mg/day root extract — lowest tested dose; null result
- Cicero 2004 used 300 mg/day dry extract — positive result at 4 weeks in elderly
- Hartz 2004 used 2000 mg/day (standardized extract) — null overall; largest Western RCT

**Important:** Clinical trial doses varied enormously (120 mg to 2000 mg/day) with no dose-response study conducted. Optimal dose unknown.

## Practical Dosing Guidance

### For General Fatigue and Adaptogenic Use

**First-line dose (regulatory consensus):**
- Dry extract (4–8:1): 100–200 mg, 1–3 times daily
- Or: dried root decoction 2–3 g/day
- Or: liquid extract (1:1): 2–3 mL/day

**Duration:** Commission E allows up to 3 months; EMA up to 2 months; repeat course permitted after break

**Timing:** Take in morning or early afternoon to minimize potential insomnia from stimulant effects

### For Endurance Performance (Based on Kuo 2010)

**Dose tested:** 800 mg/day (dry weight unspecified extract)
**Duration:** 8 weeks minimum (crossover study showed no benefit until full 8-week course)
**Population:** Recreationally trained individuals (not highly trained athletes)
**Note:** This dose/population context is the only positive performance RCT; null result in elite athletes (Dowling 1996)

### For Immune Support (Based on Bohn 1987)

**Dose tested:** 30 mL/day ethanolic extract (10 mL × 3 daily) — substantially higher than regulatory standard dose
**Duration:** 4 weeks
**Note:** This dose (30 mL/day) greatly exceeds the WHO/Commission E recommendation of 2–3 mL/day for a 1:1 liquid extract. The immunostimulatory effect observed may be dose-dependent; not replicated in modern trials at standard doses.

## Standardization Notes for Product Selection

### What to Look For

- **Eleutheroside B + E content stated in mg** (not just total extract mg)
- **Ph. Eur. grade** or **USP/NSF certified** products
- **Minimum:** ≥0.08% eleutherosides B + E (Ph. Eur. 10th ed. minimum; commercial products should meet or exceed this)
- **Preferred:** 0.8–1% eleutherosides in dry extract (10× the pharmacopoeia minimum)

### Red Flags

- Products labeled "Siberian Ginseng" without "Eleutherococcus senticosus" on label
- No eleutheroside content declared
- Unusually cheap products from non-verified sources
- Japanese products without third-party testing (~30% of Japanese market found to contain wrong species)

### Adulteration Risk

Historical adulterant *Periploca sepium* (silk vine) was linked to adverse effects in pediatric patients in the 1990s when mistakenly used as "Siberian ginseng." Modern supply chain issues focus on other Acanthopanax species substitution. Third-party testing (ConsumerLab, NSF, USP) is strongly advisable.

## Research Gaps in Dosing

- No dose-response study conducted in humans
- Range tested in trials: 120 mg to 2000 mg/day standardized extract — 17-fold variation with no systematic comparison
- Optimal duration not established; regulatory recommendations (2–3 months) are precautionary
- Bioavailability differences between preparation types not studied in humans
- Comparison of root vs leaf vs cortex preparations not conducted
