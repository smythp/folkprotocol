# Eleuthero (Eleutherococcus senticosus) - Clinical Uses

*Extracted from: bibliographies/eleuthero.yaml*
*Date: 2026-02-27*

## Overview

Eleuthero has a large body of Soviet-era research (1960s–1980s) largely inaccessible to English-language audiences, which is generally positive. Modern Western RCTs are mostly negative or equivocal, with small sample sizes and heterogeneous preparations. Commission E (1991), WHO (2002), and EMA (2014) all recognize traditional use for fatigue and debility.

---

## Regulatory-Supported Indication

### Fatigue, Debility, and Declining Work Capacity

**Regulatory Status:** German Commission E positive monograph (1991); WHO Monograph (2002); EMA Traditional Use (2014)

**Official Indication:** Tonic in times of fatigue and debility, declining capacity for work and concentration, and during convalescence.

**Commission E Dosing:** 2–3 g/day dried root or equivalent preparation; up to 3 months with possible repeat course.

## Well-Studied Clinical Areas

### 1. Chronic Fatigue

**Evidence Level:** RCTs — mixed/negative overall; possible benefit in moderate fatigue subgroup

**Key Studies:**

- **Hartz et al. 2004** (PMID 14971626) — Randomized controlled trial
  - **Sample Size:** 96 participants (76 completed 2-month follow-up)
  - **Population:** Community volunteers and chronic fatigue support group members
  - **Dose:** 2000 mg/day standardized extract (eleutheroside B + E content) — 4 capsules × 500 mg/day
  - **Duration:** 2 months
  - **Findings:** Overall — no statistically significant difference between eleuthero and placebo; fatigue substantially reduced in both groups
  - **Subgroup finding:** Less-severe fatigue subgroup (n=45): significant improvement vs placebo (P=0.04)
  - **Conclusion:** Overall efficacy not demonstrated; possible efficacy in patients with moderate (not severe) fatigue warrants further research
  - **Notes:** Largest Western RCT for fatigue indication to date; used standardized extract

- **Schaffler et al. 2013** (PMID 23740477) — Phase IV multicenter RCT, three-arm parallel-group
  - **Sample Size:** 144 participants
  - **Population:** Patients with asthenia (fatigue/weakness) and reduced working capacity from chronic stress
  - **Dose:** 120 mg/day E. senticosus root extract ± 2-day professional stress management training (SMT)
  - **Duration:** 8 weeks (assessments at baseline, 2 weeks, 8 weeks)
  - **Findings:** Almost all parameters improved significantly over time in all groups without group differences; combined treatment showed superiority over eleuthero alone for mental fatigue and restlessness; combined was not superior to SMT alone at week 8
  - **Conclusion:** Effects of adding eleuthero to stress management training are, if any, negligible; herb alone provided no benefit beyond time effect

**Effective Dosing Tested:** 120–2000 mg/day (standardized extract)
**Typical Duration:** 2 months

### 2. Endurance and Athletic Performance

**Evidence Level:** Small RCTs — conflicting results; positive in recreational athletes, null in elite athletes

**Key Studies:**

- **Kuo et al. 2010** (PMID 21793317) — Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled crossover trial
  - **Sample Size:** 9 recreationally trained college-age males
  - **Dose:** 800 mg/day
  - **Duration:** 8 weeks per condition (4-week washout between crossover conditions)
  - **Findings:**
    - VO2 peak increased by 12% (P<0.05)
    - Endurance time improved by 23% (P<0.05)
    - Maximum heart rate during exhaustion increased by 4% (P<0.05)
    - At 30 min exercise: plasma free fatty acid production significantly increased (P<0.05)
    - Plasma glucose levels significantly decreased (P<0.05), suggesting glycogen-sparing effect
  - **Conclusion:** 8 weeks supplementation enhances endurance capacity and alters metabolism toward fatty acid utilization
  - **Caveat:** Very small n=9; recreational athlete population

- **Dowling et al. 1996** (PMID 8778554) — Double-blind RCT
  - **Sample Size:** 20 highly trained distance runners
  - **Dose:** 3.4 mL liquid extract daily
  - **Duration:** 6 weeks supplementation + 2 weeks post-withdrawal
  - **Findings:** No significant differences on any measured parameter — no differences in heart rate, oxygen consumption, ventilatory measures, respiratory exchange ratio, perceived exertion, time to exhaustion, or blood lactate
  - **Conclusion:** No ergogenic benefit for aerobic exercise performance in trained athletes

- **Gaffney et al. 2001** (PMID 11798012) — Controlled clinical trial
  - **Population:** Competitive club-level endurance athletes
  - **Dose:** 33% ethanolic extract, 8 mL/day = equivalent to 4 g/day dried root
  - **Comparator:** Panax ginseng 8 mL/day; placebo
  - **Duration:** 6 weeks
  - **Findings:** E. senticosus group — testosterone-to-cortisol ratio (TCR) decreased 28.7% (0.0464 → 0.0331, P=0.03); no significant immune system changes; Panax ginseng group — no changes in testosterone, cortisol, or TCR
  - **Conclusion:** Contrary to expected adaptogenic effect, eleuthero did not reduce stress hormones; possible increase in stress hormone indices in trained athletes

**Pattern:** Positive results in recreationally trained individuals (Kuo 2010); null results in already highly trained athletes (Dowling 1996); potential pro-stress effect in competitive athletes (Gaffney 2001).

### 3. Immune Function

**Evidence Level:** One positive foundational RCT; not replicated in modern trials

- **Bohn et al. 1987** (PMID 2963645) — Double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
  - **Sample Size:** 36 healthy volunteers
  - **Dose:** 10 mL ethanolic E. senticosus extract × 3 daily = 30 mL/day total
  - **Duration:** 4 weeks
  - **Findings:** "Drastic increase" in absolute number of immunocompetent cells; particularly notable effects on T lymphocytes (helper/inducer types, cytotoxic cells) and NK cells; enhanced T lymphocyte activation
  - **Safety:** No adverse effects during trial or 6-month follow-up
  - **Conclusion:** Significant immunostimulatory effects on T-cell subsets and NK cells
  - **Notes:** Foundational immunology trial; quantitative flow cytometry methodology; commonly cited in regulatory monographs; not replicated in subsequent controlled trials

### 4. Elderly Quality of Life

**Evidence Level:** Small pilot RCT; short-term benefit only

- **Cicero et al. 2004** (PMID 15207399) — Double-blind RCT
  - **Sample Size:** 20 (n=10 per group)
  - **Population:** Elderly hypertensive volunteers aged ≥65 years
  - **Dose:** 300 mg/day dry extract
  - **Duration:** 8 weeks
  - **Findings:**
    - At 4 weeks: significantly higher scores in social functioning (SF-36) in treatment group (P=0.02)
    - Improvements did not persist at 8-week endpoint
    - 70% of treated subjects perceived receiving active therapy vs 20% placebo (P<0.05)
    - No differences in blood pressure control or digitalized heart medication levels
  - **Safety:** No adverse events in either group
  - **Conclusion:** 300 mg/day dry extract safely improves some aspects of mental health and social functioning at 4 weeks; effect attenuates with continued use
  - **Limitations:** Severely underpowered (n=10 per group); effect not sustained

### 5. Cognitive Function

**Evidence Level:** One positive RCT (combination extract); cannot isolate eleuthero contribution

- **Tohda et al. 2020** (PMID 31979283) — Placebo-controlled, randomized, double-blind trial
  - **Population:** Healthy adults
  - **Intervention:** Combined water extracts of E. senticosus leaf + Drynaria fortunei rhizome
  - **Duration:** 12 weeks
  - **Findings:**
    - Figure recall (RBANS): significantly increased (P=0.045)
    - Language domain improved (P=0.040)
    - Semantic fluency enhanced (P=0.021)
    - Anxiety/uncertainty scores improved (P=0.022)
  - **Safety:** No adverse effects
  - **Limitations:** Combination extract — cannot attribute to eleuthero alone; used leaf (not root) of E. senticosus (different phytochemical profile)

### 6. Psychiatric Adjunct: Bipolar Disorder in Adolescents

**Evidence Level:** Single RCT from China; requires independent replication

- **Weng et al. 2007** (PMID 24683218) — Double-blind RCT
  - **Sample Size:** 76 (45 females, 31 males; mean age 15.4 years; range 12–17)
  - **Population:** Chinese adolescents with bipolar disorder
  - **Intervention:** Acanthopanax senticosus 1.5 g/day + lithium
  - **Comparator:** Fluoxetine 20 mg/day + lithium
  - **Duration:** 6 weeks
  - **Efficacy Findings:**
    - Response rate: eleuthero+lithium 67.6% vs fluoxetine+lithium 71.8% (no significant difference)
    - Remission rate: 51.4% vs 48.7% (no significant difference)
  - **Safety Findings:**
    - Switching to mania: 0 (eleuthero) vs 3 patients (fluoxetine)
    - Total adverse events: 3 (eleuthero) vs 12 (fluoxetine)
  - **Conclusion:** Equivalent efficacy to fluoxetine+lithium with fewer adverse events; potential safer adjunct in adolescent bipolar disorder
  - **Caveats:** Single study; treatment doses not fully specified; Chinese origin; replication needed

## Soviet-Era Clinical Research (Gerontakos 2021 Synthesis)

**Source:** Gerontakos et al. 2021 (PMID 34087398) — first English-language synthesis of 46 Soviet-era clinical studies (1962–1986)

**Key Characteristics of Soviet Research:**
- 29 of 46 studies were placebo-controlled; 11 were controlled trials
- 21 studies examined healthy volunteers
- Most common dose across Soviet trials: 2 mL liquid extract daily (used in 14 trials)

**Primary Applications Studied:**
- Physical and mental stamina
- Cold/influenza prevention (5 studies)
- Vision effects (2 studies)
- Hearing loss prevention (3 studies)
- Work capacity and cognitive function

**Overall Soviet literature conclusion:** "Appears to exhibit benefits for cognitive function and physical and mental endurance"

**Caveat:** Soviet research not peer-reviewed by modern standards; largely not independently replicated.

## Evidence Assessment

**Commission E / WHO / EMA rating:** Recognized traditional use for fatigue and debility (not "well-established use" by modern EMA standards due to insufficient modern RCTs).

**Panossian & Wikman 2009 (PMID 19500070):** Rates eleuthero at "good scientific evidence" for boosting endurance and mental performance in mild fatigue/weakness (below Rhodiola rosea rated "strong scientific evidence").

**Overall pattern:** Soviet literature broadly positive; Western RCTs mostly negative or equivocal; possible benefit in recreational (not elite) athletes and moderate (not severe) fatigue.
