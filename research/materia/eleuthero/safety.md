# Eleuthero (Eleutherococcus senticosus) - Safety and Interactions

*Extracted from: bibliographies/eleuthero.yaml*
*Date: 2026-02-27*

## General Safety Profile

Eleuthero is well-tolerated in short-term use (up to 3 months) at standard doses. No serious adverse events were reported in any of the Western RCTs reviewed. The primary safety concerns are cardiovascular (blood pressure), pregnancy/lactation, and drug interactions.

---

## Contraindications

### Absolute / Strong Contraindications

| **Contraindication** | **Evidence Source** | **Notes** |
|----------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Hypertension** | Commission E (1991), WHO (2002), EMA (2014) — consensus across all three regulatory bodies | Consistent warning across all regulatory monographs; possible elevating effect on blood pressure |
| **Children under 12** | EMA Community Herbal Monograph (2014) | Lack of safety data in pediatric populations |
| **Pregnancy** | WHO (2002), EMA (2014), LactMed (2024) | Insufficient safety data; recommended against use |
| **Breastfeeding/Lactation** | LactMed (2024), EMA (2014) | No data on safety/efficacy in nursing mothers or infants; "most sources recommend against use" |

## Adverse Effects

### Reported Adverse Effects

| **Effect** | **Evidence Type** | **Notes** |
|------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Elevated blood pressure | LactMed review; regulatory monographs | Basis for hypertension contraindication; magnitude not well-characterized in clinical trials |
| Irritability | Postmarketing reports; regulatory monographs | CNS stimulant-type effect |
| Heart rhythm changes | Regulatory monographs | Mechanistic basis unclear; generally mild |
| Headache | Postmarketing reports | |
| Anxiety | Postmarketing reports; CNS stimulation class effect | |
| Insomnia | Postmarketing reports; CNS stimulation class effect | Take earlier in day to mitigate |
| GI discomfort | Postmarketing reports | Generally mild |
| Breast tenderness | LactMed review | Case reports |
| Elevated blood glucose | LactMed review | Paradoxical; also possible glucose-lowering in some contexts (see Kuo 2010) |

**Clinical trial data:** No serious adverse events were reported in any of the RCTs (Bohn 1987, Cicero 2004, Hartz 2004, Schaffler 2013, Weng 2007, Kuo 2010, Dowling 1996). Weng 2007 reported 3 total adverse events in the eleuthero group vs 12 in the fluoxetine group over 6 weeks.

## Drug Interactions

### Documented / Case Report Interactions

**Digoxin:**
- **Interaction type:** Interference with digoxin assay; possible pharmacodynamic interaction
- **Evidence:** Case report level (not formally studied)
- **Clinical significance:** Digoxin has a narrow therapeutic index; eleuthero may cause falsely elevated or altered digoxin plasma levels in assay
- **Recommendation:** Avoid concurrent use; monitor digoxin levels if used together

**Anticoagulants (Warfarin, Heparin, Aspirin):**
- **Interaction type:** Possible additive anticoagulant/antiplatelet effect (mechanism not fully established)
- **Evidence:** Theoretical/preclinical; regulatory caution
- **Recommendation:** Use with caution; monitor bleeding parameters

**Immunosuppressants:**
- **Interaction type:** Potential antagonism of immunosuppressive therapy due to immunostimulatory effects
- **Evidence:** Theoretical (based on immunostimulatory mechanism); not formally studied
- **Recommendation:** Caution in transplant patients or others on immunosuppressive regimens

### Potential Pharmacodynamic Interactions

| **Drug Class** | **Potential Interaction** | **Mechanism** |
|----------------|--------------------------|---------------|
| Antihypertensives | May antagonize blood pressure control | Possible blood pressure-elevating effect |
| Antidiabetics (insulin, metformin) | May enhance glucose-lowering (hypoglycemia risk) | Kuo 2010 showed decreased plasma glucose during exercise; glycogen-sparing effect |
| CNS stimulants | Additive stimulant effects | CNS-activating properties of eleuthero |
| CNS sedatives / anxiolytics | Possible antagonism | Stimulant properties |
| Lithium | Theoretical interaction (Weng 2007 used combination but no interaction noted) | Renal clearance effects possible |

### Cytochrome P450 Notes
- Formal CYP450 inhibition/induction studies not published
- WHO bibliography notes interaction concern with CYP-metabolized drugs; digoxin case report may involve P-glycoprotein or assay interference
- **Gap:** No comprehensive P450 interaction data available

## Pregnancy and Lactation

### Pregnancy
- **WHO (2002):** "Not recommended during pregnancy"
- **EMA (2014):** Contraindicated — "insufficient data"
- **Basis:** No controlled human studies; animal studies insufficient to fully characterize risk
- **Practical guidance:** Avoid throughout pregnancy

### Breastfeeding / Lactation
- **LactMed (2024, PMID 30000865):** "No data on safety or efficacy in nursing mothers or infants"
- **LactMed conclusion:** "Most sources recommend against use during breastfeeding due to lack of safety data"
- **Reported concerns:** May elevate blood pressure, increase bleeding risk, raise blood glucose, cause breast tenderness
- **Practical guidance:** Avoid during breastfeeding; if used, monitor infant for unusual symptoms

## Quality and Adulteration Safety Concerns

### Species Misidentification

**This is a clinically significant safety issue:**
- ~30% of Japanese commercial products found to contain wrong species (Patyra et al. 2025)
- Historical adulterant: *Periploca sepium* (silk vine) — contains different compounds (periplocoside A); linked to adverse cardiovascular effects in cases where mislabeled eleuthero was used
- Other adulterants: Siberian ginseng label sometimes applied to Panax ginseng or other Acanthopanax species

### Content Variability
- Eleutheroside content varies 43–200-fold across commercial products
- Actual eleutheroside amounts: 11.9–327.7% of labeled content in one survey
- Underdosed products may have no effect; overdosed products carry unknown risk

**Practical guidance:** Purchase from manufacturers with independent third-party testing (USP, NSF, ConsumerLab verification); prefer products that clearly state eleutheroside B + E content in mg rather than just total extract mg.

## Duration Limits

| **Authority** | **Maximum Duration Recommended** |
|---------------|----------------------------------|
| German Commission E (1991) | Up to 3 months; course may be repeated |
| WHO Monographs (2002) | Not specified |
| EMA (2014) | Up to 2 months |

**Rationale for duration limit:** Long-term safety data beyond 3 months is not available from controlled studies. The duration limit is precautionary, not based on documented harm with longer use.

## LactMed General Note on Efficacy

LactMed (2024) summarizes: "No good human evidence supports any of these uses" — this reflects the general state of Western RCT evidence and should be read in context of the stronger Soviet-era evidence base that Western regulatory bodies have acknowledged (Commission E positive monograph) while noting it doesn't meet modern evidentiary standards.
