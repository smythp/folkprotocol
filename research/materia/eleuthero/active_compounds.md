## Primary Standardization Markers

### Eleutheroside B (Syringin)

| **Property** | **Detail** |
|--------------|------------|
| Chemical class | Phenylpropanoid glycoside |
| Structure | 4-Hydroxy-3,5-dimethoxyphenyl acrylate glucoside |
| Role | Primary quality marker; adaptogenic activity in preclinical models |
| Pharmacopoeia standard | ≥0.08% combined with eleutheroside E (Ph. Eur. 10th ed.) |
| Brain bioavailability | Higher relative brain tissue accumulation vs systemic exposure (rat PK data) |
| Source | Root; also present in stem bark |

**Biological Activities (Preclinical):**
- Anti-inflammatory via inhibition of MAPK, Akt, and NF-κB activation pathways
- Antioxidant activity
- Immunostimulatory in vitro
- Neuroprotective: increases BDNF expression in preclinical models
- Antifatigue activity in forced swim test (rodent models)

- Anti-fatigue and endurance enhancement (rodent models)
- Neuroprotective; accumulates preferentially in brain tissue

## Full Eleutheroside Series

The plant contains eleutherosides A through G (and additional identified after the original Soviet classification):

| **Eleutheroside** | **Chemical Identity** | **Class** |
|-------------------|-----------------------|-----------|
| A | Daucosterol | Phytosterol glucoside |
| B (syringin) | Syringin | Phenylpropanoid glycoside |
| B1 | Isofraxidin | Coumarin glucoside |
| C | Ethyl galactose | Sugar derivative |
| D | Syringaresinol diglucoside isomer | Lignan |
| E (syringaresinol diglucoside) | Syringaresinol-4,4'-O-bis-β-D-glucoside | Lignan glycoside |
| F | Caffeic acid ethyl ester | Phenolic acid ester |
| G | (-)-Sesamin | Lignan |

**Note:** Eleutherosides B and E are the two compounds selected as quality markers by the European Pharmacopoeia. They are detectable by HPLC and are present at highest concentrations in root.

## Other Bioactive Constituents

### Polysaccharides (Eleutherans)
- Heterogeneous high-molecular-weight polysaccharides (Eleutherans A–G identified)
- Key immunostimulatory constituents; responsible for NK cell activation in some preclinical studies
- Not measured by current Ph. Eur. quality standard
- Water-soluble; present in aqueous extracts; absent or low in ethanolic extracts

### Phenolic Acids
- Caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, ferulic acid, sinapic acid
- Antioxidant activity

### Triterpene Saponins
- Acanthosides (including acanthoside D = eleutheroside E)
- Structurally distinct from Panax ginsenosides (which are also saponins but different scaffold)

### Phytosterols
- β-Sitosterol, daucosterol (eleutheroside A)
- Anti-inflammatory activity

### Lignans (beyond eleutherosides D, E, G)
- Sesamin (eleutheroside G), pinoresinol, medioresinol

## Standardization

### European Pharmacopoeia Standard (Ph. Eur. 10th Ed., 2019)
- **Minimum:** ≥0.08% sum of eleutheroside B + eleutheroside E in dried root (HPLC)
- **Botanical identification:** Standards defined (microscopy, TLC)
- **Limits:** Heavy metals, pesticide residues, microbial contamination specified
- **Also listed in:** British Pharmacopoeia

### Commercial Dry Extract Standardization
- Modern commercial dry extracts typically standardized to 0.8–1% total eleutherosides
- Standardization ratio: 4–8:1 concentrated extract from 30% ethanol extraction
- Commission E / WHO standard dose of 2–3 g/day dried root corresponds to 100–200 mg of a 4–8:1 dry extract

### Quality Problems in Commercial Products

**Critical issue — extreme content variability:**
- Eleutheroside content varies 43–200-fold across commercial products in published surveys
- Actual eleutheroside amounts: 11.9–327.7% of labeled content in one survey
- ~30% of Japanese market products found to contain wrong species
- Adulteration species include *Periploca sepium* (silk vine) — historically a common adulterant in the 1980s-90s

**Clinical implication:** Commercial product quality is severely variable. Regulatory monographs and clinical trials used specific preparations (Soviet SHE ethanolic extract; standardized dry extracts); these cannot be assumed equivalent to unlabeled commercial products.

## Bioavailability

### Human Pharmacokinetic Data
- **No published human PK studies** for eleutheroside B or E after oral administration (as of 2026)
- This is a major research gap acknowledged in the literature

### Animal (Rat) Pharmacokinetic Data
**Source:** Ma et al. 2013 (PMID 23369882) — HPLC-ESI-MS/MS method

Key findings:
- Linear calibration range: 1–2000 ng/mL for both eleutheroside B and E in plasma
- Extraction recovery: >80% from plasma
- **Oral absolute bioavailability:** Characterized as "relatively low" for both compounds
- **Brain tissue distribution:** Eleutheroside E and syringin (eleutheroside B) show higher relative brain tissue bioavailability compared to other constituents
- This preferential brain accumulation may explain observed neuroprotective and cognitive effects despite low systemic bioavailability

**Note:** Rat data cannot be directly extrapolated to humans; studies comparing isolated compounds vs whole aqueous extract showed different PK profiles (matrix effects).

## Mechanisms of Action (Preclinical Evidence)

### Adaptogenic / Stress-Protective
- Regulation of HPA axis (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal)
- Modulation of stress response via Hsp70 (heat shock protein 70), cortisol, and nitric oxide pathways
- Panossian & Wikman 2009: adaptogens regulate homeostasis via stress response pathways

### Anti-Inflammatory
- Inhibition of MAPK pathway
- Inhibition of Akt signaling
- Inhibition of NF-κB activation
- Downstream: reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6)

### Immunostimulatory
- NK cell enhancement (preclinical and Bohn 1987 human data)
- T-lymphocyte activation (helper/inducer subtypes, cytotoxic T cells)
- Polysaccharides (Eleutherans) primary immune-stimulating fraction

### Neuroprotective
- Increases BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) expression
- Antioxidant protection of neuronal tissue
- Neurotransmitter modulation (preclinical; specific receptors not fully characterized)
- Anti-apoptotic activity in neurons

### Antiviral (In Vitro Only)
- Strong activity against RNA viruses: human rhinovirus (HRV), RSV, influenza A
- No effect against DNA viruses: adenovirus, HSV-1
- Activity appears post-adsorption (does not prevent virus binding)
- **Caveat:** In vitro only; no human clinical trial data on antiviral effects

### Metabolic / Ergogenic
- Glycogen-sparing effect: increased free fatty acid oxidation, decreased plasma glucose during exercise (Kuo 2010)
- Possible effects on lipid metabolism: reduced total cholesterol, LDL, triglycerides vs baseline (Szołomicki 2000)

## Relationship of Chemistry to Preparation Type

| **Extraction Solvent** | **Primary Compounds Extracted** | **Notes** |
|------------------------|----------------------------------|-----------|
| Water (aqueous) | Polysaccharides (Eleutherans), phenolic acids, some eleutherosides | WHO/Soviet hot decoction; immune-relevant fraction |
| 30–40% ethanol | Eleutherosides B and E (primary markers), phenolics, lignans | Standard for WHO/Commission E/EMA; quality-marker compounds |
| 70–80% ethanol | Higher lipophilic content; higher eleutheroside yield | Less traditional; some research extracts |

**Practical implication:** The Ph. Eur. quality standard (eleutherosides B + E) measures the ethanolic extraction fraction. Aqueous tea preparations may have a different bioactive profile, particularly higher polysaccharide content.
