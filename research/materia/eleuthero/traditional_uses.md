# Eleuthero (Eleutherococcus senticosus) - Traditional Uses

*Extracted from: bibliographies/eleuthero.yaml*
*Date: 2026-02-27*

## Overview

Eleuthero has a primarily East Asian traditional history, with its most systematic development in 20th-century Soviet phytomedicine. It is not an Ayurvedic herb and has no significant European folk history prior to Soviet-era research. Its modern Western use derives almost entirely from Soviet research programs beginning in the 1960s.

---

## Traditional Use by Tradition

### 1. Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) — Ciwujia

**Common Chinese Name:** Ciwujia (刺五加)

**Plant Part Used:** Root and root bark; occasionally stem bark and leaves

**TCM Classification:**
- Category: Tonic herbs (補益藥 — bǔ yì yào)
- TCM actions: Tonifies qi and yang, strengthens the Spleen and Kidney, calms the mind (shen)

**Traditional Indications:**
- Weakness, debility, and lack of vitality
- Cold extremities (associated with yang deficiency)
- Kidney and Spleen qi deficiency patterns
- Poor appetite, low back pain, weak legs
- Insomnia and restlessness (calming the mind)
- Supporting recovery during illness and convalescence

**Traditional Preparations:**
- Decoction (water extract) of dried root, 9–27 g per dose in traditional texts
- Alcohol maceration / medicinal wine (药酒 — yào jiǔ)
- Combined with other tonic herbs (e.g., in formulas for kidney-spleen deficiency)

**Historical Period:** Use documented in classical Chinese medical texts; Shennong Bencao Jing (Divine Farmer's Classic of Materia Medica) mentions Acanthopanax species. Ciwujia differentiated from Wujia (A. gracilistylus) in later texts.

### 2. Russian / Soviet Ethnomedicine and Phytomedicine

**Regions:** Far East Russia, Siberia, Manchuria

**Historical Background:**
- Indigenous populations in the Russian Far East (including Nanai, Udege peoples) used the plant as a tonic and stimulant
- Soviet pharmacological research beginning in the 1950s–1960s under pharmacologist Nikolai Lazarev, and later Israel Brekhman, systematically investigated eleuthero as an alternative to Panax ginseng (which was scarce)
- Brekhman coined the term "adaptogen" to describe the non-specific resistance-enhancing properties observed in eleuthero and related plants

**Soviet-Era Applications (1960s–1980s):**
- Administered to Soviet athletes, cosmonauts, miners, and factory workers to enhance performance under stress
- Used in clinical settings for convalescence, fatigue, declining work capacity
- Prescribed for workers in extreme environments (cold, high altitude, radiation exposure after Chernobyl)
- Given to cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy to reduce side effects and improve immune function
- Doses used: predominantly 2 mL liquid ethanolic extract daily (most common dose in 46 reviewed Soviet trials per Gerontakos 2021)

**Soviet Standardized Preparation:**
- "SHE extract" (Soviet/Russian ethanolic extract): fluid extract prepared in 40% ethanol, served as standard preparation for Soviet clinical trials
- Widely distributed as a registered medicine in the USSR

### 3. Korean and Japanese Traditional Medicine

**Korean Traditional Medicine (Hanbang):**
- Known as 가시오가피 (Gasi-ogapi)
- Used similarly to TCM applications: tonic for qi deficiency, fatigue, kidney weakness
- Combined with other tonic herbs in formulas

**Japanese Kampo / Folk Use:**
- Known as エゾウコギ (Ezo-ukogi) — "Ezo" referring to Hokkaido region
- Less prominent in classical Kampo than in TCM; more used in modern Japanese phytomedicine influenced by Soviet research
- **Quality concern:** ~30% of Japanese commercial products found to contain wrong species (documented in Patyra et al. 2025)

## Traditional Preparations

| **Preparation** | **Method** | **Traditional Dose** | **Tradition** |
|-----------------|------------|----------------------|---------------|
| Decoction | Boil dried root 20–30 min | 9–27 g root per dose | TCM |
| Medicinal wine | Macerate root in rice wine/spirits | Variable | TCM, Russian folk |
| Fluid extract (1:1, 40% EtOH) | Soviet-standard extraction | 2 mL/day (most common Soviet trial dose) | Soviet phytomedicine |
| Tincture (1:5, 40% EtOH) | Maceration in 40% ethanol | 10–15 mL/day | WHO monograph standard |
| Powdered root | Dried and ground | 2–3 g/day | Commission E / WHO |

## Traditional Duration and Course

- **Commission E (1991):** Up to 3 months; course may be repeated
- **EMA (2014):** Up to 2 months
- **Soviet practice:** Variable; often 4–6 week courses repeated seasonally
- **WHO (2002):** Does not specify maximum duration

---

## Important Distinctions

**Eleuthero ≠ Asian/Panax Ginseng:**
- Eleuthero is commonly called "Siberian ginseng" but is not a *Panax* species
- Active compounds are eleutherosides (phenylpropanoids, lignans) vs ginsenosides (steroidal saponins) in Panax ginseng
- Soviet research deliberately positioned eleuthero as a cost-effective alternative to Panax ginseng
- US regulations (since 2002) prohibit labeling eleuthero as "ginseng"

**Leaf vs Root:**
- Traditional and regulatory use specifies the root (Radix Eleutherococci)
- Leaf preparations have been studied (Tohda 2020) but have different phytochemical profiles
- Quality standards apply to root only
