# Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica) - Active Compounds

## Overview

Urtica dioica contains distinct phytochemical profiles in the leaf/herb vs. root, with correspondingly different proposed mechanisms. No single universally accepted marker compound exists for standardization of either part.

---

## Root (Urticae radix) — Primary Compounds

### Lectins

**Urtica dioica Agglutinin (UDA)**
- Small chitin-binding lectin (~8.5 kDa)
- Proposed mechanisms:
  - Immunomodulatory: modulates T-lymphocyte proliferation
  - Anti-viral activity
  - Fungistatic activity
- Binds to SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin) — key proposed mechanism for BPH benefit
- Superagglutinin (dimeric UDA): also present; broader lectin binding profile

### Polysaccharides
- Immuno-modulating polysaccharide fractions (acidic polysaccharides)
- Anti-inflammatory activity demonstrated in vitro
- Stimulate cytokine activity
- Key fraction for immunological effects of root

### Sterols and Steryl Glycosides
- **Beta-sitosterol** (prominent phytosterol)
- **Steryl glycosides**: 3-O-glucoside and other conjugates
- Possible mechanisms:
  - Binding to sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG)
  - Inhibition of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in prostate membrane
  - Interference with prostate steroid metabolism

### Fatty Acids
- Including palmitic, stearic, oleic, and linoleic acids (minor)

### Phenolic Compounds (Root — minor fraction)
- Caffeic acid derivatives (present but less prominent than in leaf)

### Key Root Mechanism Summary (Chrubasik et al. 2007)
| Mechanism | Evidence Level |
|---|---|
| SHBG modulation | Supported (in vitro, proposed clinical) |
| Aromatase inhibition | Supported (in vitro) |
| EGF receptor inhibition (prostate) | Supported (in vitro) |
| Prostate steroid metabolism interference | Proposed |
| 5-alpha reductase inhibition | Unlikely (not supported) |
| Anti-inflammatory (polysaccharides) | Demonstrated (in vitro) |
| Cell proliferation inhibition (methanolic extract) | Demonstrated (in vitro) |

## Leaf/Herb (Urticae folium) — Primary Compounds

### Phenolic Acids and Derivatives
- **3-Caffeoylquinic acid (3-CQA / neochlorogenic acid)**: principal phenolic acid; antioxidant
- **Caffeic malic acid (CMA)**: prominent and relatively unique to nettle; antioxidant
- **Chlorogenic acid derivatives**: broad spectrum of caffeoylquinic acid isomers
- **Caffeic acid**: precursor and metabolite

### Flavonoids
- **Rutin (quercetin-3-O-rutinoside)**: principal flavonoid; anti-inflammatory, antioxidant
- **Quercetin** and quercetin glycosides
- **Kaempferol** glycosides (minor)
- **Isoquercitrin**

### Hydroxycinnamic Acids
- Sinapic acid, ferulic acid (minor components)

### Amine Compounds (Sting Components)
- **Histamine**: present in stinging trichomes (formic acid, silica needles)
- **Serotonin**: in trichomes
- **Formic acid**: in trichomes — causes immediate sting reaction
- These are destroyed by drying or heating; irrelevant to oral dried leaf preparations

### Minerals and Nutrients
- High iron content (significant for traditional anemia use)
- Calcium, magnesium, potassium
- Vitamins A, C, K (especially in fresh or lightly processed leaf)
- Silicon compounds

### Carotenoids
- Beta-carotene, lutein (in fresh leaf)

## Bioavailability (Bonetti et al. 2016 — PMID 27713992)

**Study Design**: In vitro — dynamic digestion simulation + Caco-2 transepithelial transport

| Compound | Bioaccessibility | Transepithelial Transport |
|---|---|---|
| 3-Caffeoylquinic acid (3-CQA) | Good release during duodenal phase | Low native form transport |
| Caffeic malic acid (CMA) | Good release during duodenal phase | Low native form transport |
| Rutin | Good release during duodenal phase | Low native form transport |

**Key Findings**:
- Food matrix type significantly influences dissolution rate and phenolic compound release
- Low transepithelial transport of native phenolic forms suggests limited intestinal absorption
- Colonic fermentation by gut microflora is likely the primary secondary route for systemic availability of phenolic metabolites
- Nettle-based food products create antioxidant environment against superoxide anion radicals throughout the GI tract

**Critical Gap**: No human pharmacokinetic studies for Urtica dioica whole herb or standardized extracts exist as of 2026. Bioavailability data is entirely from in vitro models. This is a significant research gap.

## Standardization

### Leaf/Herb Preparations
| Authority | Extraction Solvent | Extract Ratio | Marker/Standard |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMA/HMPC approved | 50% ethanol | 8–10:1 dry extract | No single marker specified; analytical markers include chlorogenic acid derivatives, rutin, CMA |
| Commission E | Not specified | 8–12 g dried herb equivalent | Whole herb dry weight |
| Clinical trials | Hydroalcoholic (variable) | Variable | Largely undefined in trial reports |

**Notes**:
- Analytical markers used in research: chlorogenic acid, caffeic malic acid, rutin (HPLC-DAD method)
- No internationally harmonized marker compound for leaf standardization
- Most commercial products standardize to "total phenolics" or "flavonoids" using Folin-Ciocalteu — not pharmacologically specific

### Root Preparations
| Authority | Extraction Solvent | Extract/Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Commission E | Aqueous | 360 mg/day (aqueous extract) |
| Commission E | Methanol | 600–1,200 mg/day (methanolic extract) |
| Clinical trials | Variable (methanol, aqueous, hydroalcoholic) | 450 mg/day (Karami 2020); dose variable (Safarinejad 2005) |

**Proposed Marker Compounds for Root (not harmonized)**:
- Polysaccharides (immunomodulating fraction)
- Lectins (UDA — Urtica dioica agglutinin)
- Beta-sitosterol
- Steryl glycosides (e.g., 3-O-glucoside)

**Critical Notes**:
- No single universally accepted marker compound for nettle root standardization exists
- Most clinical trials and commercial products use proprietary methanolic or aqueous extracts without explicit marker specifications
- Methanolic and aqueous extracts have differing compositions and potentially different mechanisms
- This makes cross-trial comparison and clinical dose translation difficult
