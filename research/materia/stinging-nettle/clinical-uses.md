# Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica) - Clinical Uses

## Evidence-Based Clinical Applications

---

### 1. Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

**Evidence Quality: Moderate-Strong — Multiple RCTs and Two Meta-Analyses**
**Part Used: Root (Urticae radix)**

#### Meta-Analysis Results

**Men et al. 2016 — 5 RCTs, 1,128 participants**
- IPSS: SMD = -10.47 (95% CI [-18.12, -2.82], p=0.007) — large effect favoring Urtica dioica
- Peak urinary flow rate (Qmax): significantly improved vs control
- Prostate volume: significantly reduced vs control
- PSA levels: unaffected (no safety concern for prostate cancer masking)
- All 5 studies reported no adverse effects

**2025 Systematic Review — 6 RCTs, 1,210 participants**
- All 6 studies consistently favored Urtica dioica vs controls
- Databases searched: MEDLINE, Embase, CINAHL, CENTRAL, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov (inception to Feb 2024)
- Conclusion: Promising evidence; methodological limitations (high/unclear risk of bias) prevent definitive conclusions

#### Key RCTs

**Safarinejad 2005 — 558 participants (620 enrolled; 90% completion), 6 months**
- Design: Prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, partial crossover
- Intervention: Urtica dioica root extract (dose not published in abstract) vs placebo
- IPSS: 19.8 → 11.8 (treatment) vs 19.2 → 17.7 (placebo)
- 81% of treatment patients reported symptom improvement vs 16% placebo
- Maximum urinary flow rate: +8.2 mL/s (treatment) vs +3.4 mL/s (placebo)
- Post-void residual volume: 73 → 36 mL (treatment)
- Prostate volume: 40.1 cc → 36.3 cc (treatment)
- Safety: No adverse effects in either group

**Karami et al. 2020 — 60 participants (30/30), 12 weeks**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind controlled trial
- Dose: **450 mg/day Urtica dioica root extract**
- IPSS: intermediate effect size
- Serum hs-CRP: small effect size (anti-inflammatory benefit)
- Malondialdehyde (MDA): intermediate to large effect size (antioxidant)
- Superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity: intermediate effect size
- Safety: No adverse effects observed

**Ghorbanibirgani et al. 2013 — 100 participants**
- Design: Randomized, double-blind controlled trial
- Demonstrated efficacy for BPH symptom management (full quantitative data unavailable from abstract)

#### Mechanisms (Chrubasik et al. 2007 — comprehensive review)
- SHBG modulation (primary proposed mechanism)
- Aromatase inhibition
- EGF receptor inhibition in prostate epithelium
- Prostate steroid interference (via steryl glycosides)
- 5-alpha reductase inhibition: unlikely mechanism
- Anti-inflammatory: polysaccharide fractions
- Cell proliferation inhibition: methanolic extract
- UDA lectin: immunomodulatory activity

#### Regulatory Endorsement
- German Commission E (1987): Approved for symptomatic treatment of micturition disorders in BPH stages I and II
- EMA/HMPC Assessment Report on Urtica dioica radix: Supports traditional use category for BPH
- Health Canada: Licensed for relief of LUTS associated with BPH (root)

### 2. Type 2 Diabetes — Glycemic Control

**Evidence Quality: Moderate — Meta-Analysis Support with Notable Effect Sizes**
**Part Used: Leaf (Urticae folium)**

#### Meta-Analysis Results

**Ziaei et al. 2020 (PMID 31802554) — 8 RCTs, 401 participants**
- Fasting Blood Sugar (FBS): **-18.01 mg/dL** (p<0.001) — statistically significant
- HbA1c: -0.77% (p=0.12) — not statistically significant
- Insulin: no significant change
- HOMA-IR: no significant change

**Tabrizi et al. 2022 (PMID 34587883) — 13 RCTs (1,485 initial citations)**
- Fasting Blood Glucose: **-17.17 mg/dL** (statistically significant)
- HbA1c: **-0.93 percentage points** (statistically significant)
- C-Reactive Protein: **-1.09 mg/dL** (statistically significant)
- Triglycerides: **-26.94 mg/dL** (statistically significant)
- Systolic Blood Pressure: **-5.03 mmHg** (statistically significant)
- No significant changes: insulin, total cholesterol, LDL-C, HDL-C, BMI, diastolic BP

#### Key RCTs

**Kianbakht et al. 2013 (PMID 24273930) — 92 participants (46/46), 3 months**
- Population: Advanced T2DM requiring insulin therapy
- Dose: **500 mg nettle leaf extract capsule × 3/day = 1,500 mg/day**, combined with conventional medications
- Comparator: Placebo + conventional medications
- Fasting blood glucose: significantly reduced (p<0.001)
- 2-hour postprandial glucose: significantly reduced (p=0.009)
- HbA1c: significantly reduced (p=0.006)
- Liver enzymes (SGOT/SGPT) and creatinine: no significant change (p>0.05)
- Well tolerated; no hepatic or renal adverse effects

**Namazi et al. 2011 (PMID 22303583) — 50 participants, 8 weeks**
- Population: Adults with T2DM (men and women)
- Dose: **100 mg/kg/day hydroalcoholic extract** in 3 divided doses
- Comparator: Placebo
- IL-6: significantly decreased (p<0.05) — anti-inflammatory effect
- hs-CRP: significantly decreased (p<0.05)
- Fasting blood sugar: no significant effect
- HOMA-IR: significant reduction (improved insulin sensitivity)

**Amiri Behzadi et al. 2016 (PMID 28078249) — 50 participants (women), 8 weeks**
- Population: Women with T2DM
- Dose: Hydroalcoholic extract (dose not published)
- Significant improvements: fasting plasma glucose ↓, triglycerides ↓, SGPT ↓, HDL-C ↑, nitric oxide ↑, SOD ↑
- Conclusion: May decrease cardiovascular risk factors in diabetic patients

### 3. Allergic Rhinitis

**Evidence Quality: Limited — Two Small RCTs with Mixed Outcomes**
**Part Used: Leaf (Mittman 1990) and Root (Bakhshaee 2017)**

#### Key RCTs

**Mittman 1990 (PMID 2192379) — 69 participants (98 enrolled), double-blind**
- Intervention: Freeze-dried Urtica dioica (dose not specified)
- Comparator: Placebo
- Global assessment: Urtica dioica rated higher than placebo
- Daily symptom diary: Urtica dioica rated only slightly higher than placebo
- Notes: Earliest published RCT; modest diary-recorded symptom effect despite positive global assessment

**Bakhshaee et al. 2017 (PMID 29844782) — 40 participants (74 enrolled; 46% dropout), 1 month**
- Intervention: Urtica dioica root extract **150 mg** (Urtidin® FC tablet)
- Comparator: Placebo
- SNOT-22 clinical symptom severity: significant improvement in both groups (p<0.001) — possible placebo effect
- Nasal smear eosinophil count: statistically significant reduction post-treatment (p<0.01)
- Interferon-γ: significant difference between nettle and placebo groups (p=0.017)
- Serum IgE, IL-4, IL-5: no significant changes (p>0.1)
- Limitations: High dropout rate, short duration, small final sample

#### Clinical Interpretation
Evidence insufficient for clinical recommendation as primary therapy. May have mechanistic basis via eosinophil modulation and IFN-γ response. Further adequately powered RCTs required.

### 4. Musculoskeletal Conditions / Arthritis

**Evidence Quality: Preliminary — Attribution Confounded by Multi-Ingredient Studies**
**Part Used: Leaf/Herb**

#### Osteoarthritis (OA)

**Phytalgic RCT — Jacquet et al. 2009 (PMID 20015358) — 81 participants, 3 months**
- Population: Adults with knee or hip OA using NSAIDs/analgesics regularly
- Intervention: Phytalgic (fish oil + vitamin E + Urtica dioica combination)
- Analgesic use: active 6.5 tablets/week vs placebo 16.5 tablets/week (p<0.001)
- NSAID use: active 0.4 DDD/day vs placebo 1.0 DDD/day (p=0.02)
- WOMAC pain score: active 86.5 vs placebo 235.3 (p<0.001)
- WOMAC function score: active 301.6 vs placebo 746.5 (p<0.001)
- **Critical limitation**: Multi-ingredient preparation — nettle's individual contribution cannot be isolated

#### Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)

**Jahan et al. 2022 — 90 participants (3 arms), 3 months**
- Population: Adults aged 24–82 with RA (mean disease duration 8.58 ± 6.77 years)
- Design: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (3 arms: Urtica dioica, EPO, placebo)
- Significant vs placebo: DAS28 improved, IL-17 reduced, TAC increased, RF reduced, CRP reduced
- Non-significant: VAS pain score, anti-CCP, ESR

#### Topical Sting Therapy

**Randall et al. 1999 (PMID 10581821) — 18 self-selected patients (uncontrolled)**
- All but one reported nettles "very helpful" for joint pain
- No serious adverse effects; transient urticarial rash only
- Notes: Uncontrolled pilot; mechanistic rationale involves counter-irritation and local anti-inflammatory peptides
- Requires RCT confirmation

#### Regulatory Position
German Commission E (1990): Nettle leaf approved for **rheumatic complaints (supportive/adjunctive)** — not stand-alone therapy.
