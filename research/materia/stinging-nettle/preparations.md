# Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica) - Preparations and Dosing

## Overview

Stinging nettle has distinct preparations for leaf/herb vs. root, with different indications and dose ranges. Commission E, EMA, and clinical trials provide the primary evidence base for dosing.

---

## Leaf / Herb (Urticae folium / Urticae herba)

### Indications
- Urinary tract irrigation (diuretic, anti-inflammatory, urinary gravel)
- Rheumatic/arthritic complaints (supportive/adjunctive)
- Allergic rhinitis (limited evidence)
- Glycemic control in T2DM (clinical trial evidence)
- Nutritional supplement (iron, calcium, vitamins)

### Tea / Infusion

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Dose per cup** | 2–4 g dried leaf/herb |
| **Frequency** | 3× daily |
| **Total daily dose** | 6–12 g dried herb (Commission E range) |
| **Preparation** | Pour boiling water over dried herb; steep 5–10 min; strain |
| **Fluid intake** | Minimum 2 L/day additional fluid for irrigation therapy |
| **Duration** | Commission E: no specific limit stated; EMA: max 2–4 weeks per episode without medical advice |

**EMA Approved Reference Dose (Traditional Use)**:
- Dry extract (8–10:1, 50% ethanol extraction): **536 mg** per dose, up to twice daily
- Equivalent to approximately 4.8 g herbal substance per dose
- Duration: Not to exceed 2–4 weeks without medical advice (per EMA/HMPC monograph)

### Fluid Extract

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Ratio** | 1:1, 25% ethanol |
| **Dose** | 2–4 mL |
| **Frequency** | 3× daily |

### Tincture

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Ratio** | 1:5, 45% ethanol |
| **Dose** | 2–6 mL |
| **Frequency** | 3× daily |

### Standardized Extract (Capsule/Tablet)

**Clinical Trial Protocols:**

| Study | Dose | Form | Duration | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kianbakht et al. 2013 | 500 mg × 3/day = 1,500 mg/day | Leaf extract capsule | 3 months | T2DM |
| Namazi et al. 2011 | 100 mg/kg/day in 3 doses | Hydroalcoholic extract | 8 weeks | T2DM, inflammation |
| Amiri Behzadi et al. 2016 | Not published (hydroalcoholic) | Hydroalcoholic extract | 8 weeks | T2DM lipids |
| Mittman 1990 | Not specified | Freeze-dried leaf | Not specified | Allergic rhinitis |

**Practical Standardized Dose (Leaf)**:
- For glycemic support: **500 mg leaf extract** (standardized), 3× daily = 1,500 mg/day
- Based on Kianbakht 2013 (n=92, 3 months, advanced T2DM) — best-characterized clinical trial dose

## Root (Urticae radix)

### Indications
- Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) stages I and II — primary evidence-based indication
- LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) in men
- Allergic rhinitis (Bakhshaee 2017; limited evidence)

### Decoction (Root Tea)

| Parameter | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Dose** | 1.5–2 g dried root |
| **Frequency** | 3× daily |
| **Total daily dose** | 4.5–6 g dried root |
| **Preparation** | Simmer in water 10–15 min; strain |

### Commission E Approved Doses (Root)

| Preparation | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Aqueous extract | 360 mg/day | Standard Commission E dose for BPH |
| Methanolic extract | 600–1,200 mg/day | Range used in clinical BPH trials; 6–9 weeks |

### Standardized Root Extract (Capsule/Tablet)

**Clinical Trial Protocols:**

| Study | Dose | Form | Duration | Indication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Karami et al. 2020 | **450 mg/day** | Root extract | 12 weeks | BPH |
| Safarinejad 2005 | Not published | Root extract | 6 months | BPH |
| Bakhshaee et al. 2017 | **150 mg** (Urtidin® FC) | Root extract tablet | 1 month | Allergic rhinitis |

**Best-Evidence Clinical Dose (Root for BPH)**:
- 450 mg/day root extract (Karami 2020, n=60) — most clearly specified recent trial
- Commission E: 360 mg/day aqueous extract; 600–1,200 mg/day methanolic extract
- Duration for BPH: minimum 6 months (Safarinejad 2005) for full assessment

## Topical Applications

### Urtication (Joint Pain)
- **Method**: Apply fresh stinging nettle plant directly to painful joint area
- **Mechanism**: Counter-irritation, local anti-inflammatory peptides, sting-mediated neurological response
- **Evidence**: Randall et al. 1999 (n=18, uncontrolled pilot); highly positive patient-reported outcomes; no serious adverse effects
- **Practical note**: Transient urticarial rash and burning expected; resolves within hours
- **Limitation**: No RCT confirmation; anecdotal evidence only beyond pilot study

### Leaf Poultice
- Traditional preparation: Fresh or lightly blanched leaf applied to skin for burns, eczema, wounds
- No clinical trial evidence for topical poultice use

## Preparation Considerations

### Fresh vs. Dried Leaf
- **Sting compounds** (formic acid, histamine, serotonin in trichomes) are deactivated by drying or heating
- **Nutritional content** (iron, vitamins, carotenoids) is higher in lightly processed or freeze-dried forms
- **Freeze-dried leaf** preserves more phytochemical content than heat-dried preparations; used in Mittman 1990 allergic rhinitis trial

### Extract Solvent Effects
- **50% ethanol (EMA standard)**: Optimizes extraction of both polar (phenolics, flavonoids) and semi-polar constituents; recommended for leaf
- **Aqueous (water) extract**: Used for polysaccharide and mineral extraction; Commission E root dose (360 mg/day)
- **Methanolic extract**: Higher steryl glycoside, beta-sitosterol extraction for root; Commission E: 600–1,200 mg/day
- **Hydroalcoholic extract**: Used in T2DM clinical trials (Namazi, Amiri Behzadi); ratio variable; most commercial tinctures

### Food Matrix Effects (Bonetti et al. 2016)
- Food matrix significantly influences phenolic compound dissolution and bioaccessibility
- Cooking (boiling) destroys sting compounds and dramatically changes phenolic profile
- Nettle soup, blanched nettle: phenolics released during duodenal digestion phase; low native-form transepithelial transport
