# Stinging Nettle (Urtica dioica) - Safety and Interactions

## Overall Safety Profile

**Hepatotoxicity Risk: LiverTox Likelihood Score E (Unlikely)**
- No published reports of serum enzyme elevations or clinically apparent liver injury from stinging nettle root or leaf extracts
- Systematic reviews of herb-induced liver injury (US, Spain, Latin America, Sweden databases) identified zero cases attributed to stinging nettle
- Multiple controlled trials across diverse indications reported no adverse effects
- This is an exceptionally clean hepatic safety record — notably distinct from other popular herbs (e.g., Scutellaria baicalensis, Cimicifuga racemosa, Kava)

**Source**: LiverTox (NIH/NCBI Bookshelf NBK589898)

## Common and Known Adverse Effects

### Oral Preparations (Leaf and Root Extracts)
| Adverse Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| GI discomfort (nausea, diarrhea, abdominal cramping) | Uncommon; usually mild | More common with leaf products; take with food |
| Headache | Uncommon | Reported in some trials; transient |
| Gastric irritation | Uncommon | More with leaf infusion vs. standardized extracts |
| Allergic reactions (oral) | Rare | Reported in individuals with plant allergies |

### Fresh Plant / Topical Contact
| Adverse Effect | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Urticarial rash and local inflammation | Expected (contact sting) | Caused by formic acid, histamine, silica needles in trichomes |
| Local burning, itching, wheals | Expected (contact sting) | Resolves within hours; mechanism is urtication |
| Serious allergic reaction | Very rare | Only in prior sensitized individuals |

**Note**: Sting reaction from fresh plant contact is the expected pharmacological effect of the trichomes, not an adverse effect of medicinal preparations. Dried, heated, or extracted preparations lack the sting compounds.

## Contraindications

### Established Contraindications

**1. Edema Due to Cardiac or Renal Insufficiency**
- **Basis**: German Commission E (1990) — nettle leaf used as irrigation therapy (diuretic/aquaretic)
- Using diuretic herbs in patients with cardiac- or renal-origin edema can worsen fluid imbalance and cardiac load
- Applies specifically to the irrigation therapy indication (leaf, high fluid intake context)
- Not categorically contraindicated for root preparations used for BPH at standard doses

**2. Pregnancy**
- **Basis**: Insufficient safety data; uterotonic effects theoretically possible
- German Commission E and EMA list as contraindicated or "not recommended" during pregnancy
- Traditional use of leaf as emmenagogue (period-inducing) supports theoretical concern for pregnancy risk
- Health Canada NHP monograph: use contraindicated in pregnancy
- No controlled human safety data available

**3. Lactation**
- **Basis**: Insufficient safety data; no human studies in breastfeeding populations
- German Commission E and EMA: not recommended during lactation
- Traditional use to promote lactation exists (folk use) but lacks clinical safety evidence; contradictory position warrants caution

## Drug Interactions

### Diuretics
- **Interaction**: Potential additive diuretic effect
- **Mechanism**: Nettle leaf has aquaretic/diuretic properties; may potentiate thiazides, loop diuretics, or other diuretic agents
- **Clinical concern**: Possible enhanced fluid/electrolyte loss; monitor if using nettle leaf alongside prescribed diuretics
- **Risk level**: Low-moderate; theoretical and based on pharmacological class overlap

### Antidiabetic Medications
- **Interaction**: Potential additive hypoglycemic effect
- **Mechanism**: Meta-analysis evidence (Tabrizi 2022; Ziaei 2020) demonstrates nettle leaf significantly reduces FBS (-17–18 mg/dL) and HbA1c (-0.93%); additive to insulin and oral hypoglycemics
- **Clinical note from Kianbakht 2013**: Nettle used as adjunct to conventional anti-hyperglycemic medications; glycemic improvements occurred on top of standard medications — no hypoglycemia events reported, but monitoring warranted
- **Risk level**: Low-moderate; clinically meaningful blood glucose lowering warrants monitoring; particularly relevant in insulin-treated patients

### Anticoagulants / Antiplatelet Agents
- **Interaction**: Theoretical; based on high vitamin K content in nettle leaf
- **Mechanism**: High dietary vitamin K may antagonize warfarin anticoagulation
- **Applies to**: Fresh/whole leaf (high K1 content); dry extracts have variable/lower vitamin K
- **Risk level**: Low for standardized extracts; potentially relevant for high-dose fresh leaf consumption
- **Recommendation**: Caution with warfarin; INR monitoring if consuming significant quantities of nettle leaf

### NSAIDs / Analgesics
- **Interaction**: Potentially complementary (reduced NSAID requirements in Phytalgic OA trial)
- **Not a safety concern**: Additive benefit observed; no negative interaction identified

### Antihypertensive Agents
- **Interaction**: Possible modest additive effect
- **Mechanism**: Tabrizi 2022 meta-analysis: SBP reduction of -5.03 mmHg with nettle supplementation
- **Risk level**: Low; effect size modest; unlikely to cause clinically significant hypotension at standard doses

## Special Populations

### Pregnancy
- **Recommendation**: Avoid
- Contraindicated per German Commission E and EMA
- Emmenagogue tradition and insufficient safety data

### Lactation
- **Recommendation**: Avoid (insufficient data) — despite folk use as galactagogue
- German Commission E and EMA: not recommended

### Pediatrics
- No clinical studies in children
- Not recommended without medical supervision

### Renal Impairment
- Use with caution for irrigation therapy (leaf diuretic use)
- Standard BPH root doses may be used; monitor fluid balance

### Cardiac Failure / Edema
- Contraindicated for irrigation therapy (leaf)
- Root preparations for BPH: exercise clinical judgment; not categorically contraindicated

---

## Allergy and Hypersensitivity

- True IgE-mediated allergy to oral nettle extracts: uncommon but possible, especially in individuals with known plant allergies (Urticaceae family)
- Cross-reactivity with other members of Urticaceae: possible but not well characterized
- Contact urticaria from fresh plant: expected pharmacological effect, not a contraindication to oral use
- Known allergy to nettle: contraindication to use
