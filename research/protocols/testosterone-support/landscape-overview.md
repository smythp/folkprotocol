# Herbal Testosterone Support — Landscape Overview

## The Reality of Herbal Testosterone Support

Normal testosterone ranges:
- Total T: 300–1000 ng/dL (10.4–34.7 nmol/L)
- Free T: 5–25 pg/mL
- Age-related decline: ~1–2% per year after 30

The realistic magnitude of herbal testosterone effects:
- Best-case increases: 15–25% or ~50–100 ng/dL in responders
- This is NOT the "300% testosterone boost" of supplement marketing
- Most effects are seen in men with LOW baseline T, not healthy eugonadal men
- The meta-analytic data (Leisegang 2022) shows the single most important finding: tongkat ali significantly increased T in hypogonadal men (MD 2.27 nmol/L) but had NO significant effect in healthy men (p = 0.50)

## The Hypogonadal vs Eugonadal Distinction

This is the single most important concept in herbal testosterone support:
- **Hypogonadal men** (T < 300 ng/dL or symptomatic low-normal): Herbs can help normalize
- **Eugonadal men** (T in normal range): Most herbs show no significant effect
- The "low-normal" range (300–500 ng/dL) is where herbs may have the most realistic benefit — not clinically hypogonadal but symptomatic

Pattern across herbs:
- Tongkat ali: Effect only in hypogonadal subgroup (Leisegang 2022)
- Tribulus: No T effect in any group (multiple studies)
- Shilajit: Notable exception — Pandit 2016 showed +20% in healthy men 45-55
- Ashwagandha: Works via cortisol reduction — most effective in stressed men
- Fenugreek: Modest meta-analytic effect (0.89 nmol/L) but small study pool

## What "Testosterone Support" Actually Means

Three different things get conflated:
1. **Raising total testosterone levels** — what people think they're buying
2. **Increasing free (bioavailable) testosterone** — via SHBG modulation
3. **Improving symptoms attributed to low T** (energy, libido, body comp) — which may happen through non-hormonal pathways

Maca improves libido without changing T. Tribulus improves ED without changing T. These aren't "testosterone boosters" but they address the symptoms people associate with low T.

## The Supplement Marketing Gap

The supplement industry sells "testosterone boosters" as a category. The clinical reality:
- Most popular T-booster ingredients have weak or no evidence for raising T
- Clemesha et al. (2020) systematically reviewed testosterone booster supplements and found claims were "not backed by the academic literature"
- The ingredients with actual evidence (ashwagandha, tongkat ali, shilajit) work modestly and in specific populations
- The gap between what's marketed and what's supported is enormous
