# Mechanism Categories — Herbal Testosterone Support

## 1. Herbs That Increase Total Testosterone

### Via HPG axis support (direct):
- **Tongkat Ali** — mechanism unclear but may involve LH signaling. Only effective in hypogonadal men (Leisegang 2022: MD 2.27 nmol/L hypogonadal, p=0.50 eugonadal). Possibly normalizes suppressed HPG axis rather than enhancing it.
- **Shilajit** — Pandit 2016: +20.45% total T, +19.14% free T in healthy men. Notable because it's one of the few herbs showing effect in healthy (non-hypogonadal) men. Mechanism unclear — possibly mineral-mediated (zinc, selenium) or mitochondrial support.
- **Fenugreek** — Mansoori 2020 meta-analysis: WMD 0.89 nmol/L increase. Mechanism may involve furostanol saponins acting as 5-alpha-reductase and/or aromatase inhibitors, but this is speculative.

### Via cortisol reduction (indirect):
- **Ashwagandha** — doesn't directly stimulate testosterone production. Reduces cortisol by 11-32% (multiple studies), which removes cortisol's suppressive effect on the HPG axis. Most relevant in stressed, high-cortisol populations. KSM-66 data shows 15-18% T increase in stressed men.

## 2. Herbs That Increase Free Testosterone (SHBG Modulation)

- **Stinging Nettle Root** — lignans bind to SHBG, displacing testosterone and increasing the free (biologically active) fraction. Works regardless of total T level. Theoretically synergistic with herbs that raise total T.

## 3. Herbs That Reduce Aromatization

- **No herb has strong human evidence for clinically meaningful aromatase inhibition.** Some in vitro data exists for various compounds but the clinical translation is weak. Weight loss is the most effective aromatase reduction strategy in obese men.

## 4. Herbs That Improve Sexual Function WITHOUT Changing Testosterone

- **Maca** — multiple studies show libido and sexual function improvement with NO change in testosterone (Gonzales 2002, Brooks 2008). Non-hormonal mechanism, probably CNS.
- **Tribulus** — debunked as T booster, but some evidence for ED improvement (IIEF-5 improvement in men with lower baseline function). Mechanism unclear, possibly nitric-oxide mediated.

## 5. Combination Logic

Rational stacking based on complementary mechanisms:
- **Total T + Free T**: Tongkat ali or shilajit + stinging nettle root
- **Cortisol-mediated + Direct**: Ashwagandha + tongkat ali
- **Hormonal + Non-hormonal libido**: Any T herb + maca (different pathways)
- **Avoid redundancy**: Don't stack multiple herbs with the same mechanism (e.g., tongkat ali + fenugreek both targeting total T through unclear pathways)
