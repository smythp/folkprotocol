# Review of Existing Materia Medica for Testosterone Support

This document reviews the relevance of herbs already present in the Folk Protocol materia medica to the domain of testosterone support, based on the current clinical evidence.

## Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*)

*   **Relevance:** Highly Relevant and Evidence-Based.
*   **Mechanism:** Primary mechanism is HPA axis modulation and cortisol reduction. By lowering stress-induced cortisol, it removes a significant inhibitor of testosterone production, allowing levels to return to a healthy baseline.
*   **Evidence:** Strong. Multiple RCTs confirm its ability to increase testosterone, particularly in men who are stressed or overweight. Reported increases are often in the 15-18% range, with one study on resistance-training men showing an absolute increase of 96.2 ng/dL.
*   **Use Case:** Ideal for individuals with documented or suspected high cortisol, chronic stress, and fatigue-related low testosterone.

## Tribulus (*Tribulus terrestris*)

*   **Relevance:** Relevant primarily for Debunking.
*   **Mechanism:** The long-held theory was that Tribulus stimulates Luteinizing Hormone (LH), thereby increasing testosterone.
*   **Evidence:** Largely Debunked. The vast majority of human clinical trials in healthy men show no increase in testosterone. While it may have some effect on libido (likely through non-hormonal pathways), it is ineffective as a testosterone booster.
*   **Use Case:** As a case study in the gap between supplement marketing and clinical reality. It is a libido enhancer, not a testosterone booster.

## Maca (*Lepidium meyenii*)

*   **Relevance:** Relevant for Libido, but Not Testosterone.
*   **Mechanism:** Maca enhances libido and sexual function through a non-hormonal mechanism.
*   **Evidence:** Clinical studies have repeatedly shown that Maca does not change serum testosterone levels in men. Its benefits for sexual desire appear to stem from other pathways.
*   **Use Case:** Clarifies that improving libido does not always mean increasing testosterone. Perfect example of an herb that benefits sexual function without altering hormone levels.

## Saw Palmetto (*Serenoa repens*)

*   **Relevance:** Relevant for Prostate Health & DHT, Not Testosterone.
*   **Mechanism:** Primary mechanism involves inhibition of 5-alpha-reductase (5-AR), the enzyme that converts testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT).
*   **Evidence:** Well-studied for BPH. By reducing testosterone-to-DHT conversion, may theoretically lead to slightly higher testosterone, but not a primary testosterone booster. Main role is in managing downstream androgen effects on the prostate.
*   **Use Case:** Explains the difference between modulating total/free testosterone and modulating its conversion to other androgens like DHT.

## Stinging Nettle (*Urtica dioica*)

*   **Relevance:** Highly Relevant for Free Testosterone.
*   **Mechanism:** Root is rich in lignans with high binding affinity for Sex Hormone-Binding Globulin (SHBG).
*   **Evidence:** By binding to SHBG, nettle lignans occupy sites that would normally bind testosterone, increasing the amount of free, unbound (biologically active) testosterone in circulation.
*   **Use Case:** Key herb for individuals with normal or high total testosterone but low free testosterone due to elevated SHBG. Works synergistically with herbs that increase total testosterone.
