# Tone Brief — Hangover Prevention and Recovery

## Report voice and source-method narration

**"different symptom scales do not even group the syndrome identically"** (opening paragraph). This talks about the measurement apparatus rather than what a hangover is. The reader does not need to know that researchers categorize symptoms differently. Rewrite: Either drop it or say directly that the symptoms don't cluster into one predictable set—headache without nausea, nausea without fatigue, and so on.

**"The mapped mechanism literature treats it as a convergence of..."** (opening paragraph). Pure report voice about what sources say rather than stating the mechanism. Rewrite: Say directly what happens—alcohol exposure irritates the gut, disrupts sleep, triggers inflammation, produces acetaldehyde, and disturbs electrolyte and glucose balance. No need to attribute this to "the literature."

**"electrolyte disturbance has not tracked overall hangover severity consistently"** (dehydration paragraph). This is evidence-status language that hides a useful observation. Rewrite: Say directly that electrolyte levels don't predict how bad someone feels, or that correcting electrolytes doesn't reliably improve the whole hangover.

**"forum participants themselves dispute whether dehydration is central or only one part of the event"** (dehydration paragraph). Source-method narration. Rewrite: Say that thirst and dry mouth are real, but dehydration doesn't explain the headache, nausea, or brain fog—or say that some people feel better after water and some don't.

**"a review found a substantial self-reported 'hangover-resistant' minority, although that estimate depends on how intoxication and resistance were defined"** (plurality paragraph). The qualification swamps the claim. Rewrite: Say that some people report rarely getting hangovers, even after heavy drinking, while others are consistently affected. Drop the methodological hedge or move it to a footnote.

**"national guidance notes that attention, decisions, and coordination may remain impaired during a hangover"** ("what worked means" paragraph). Source-method narration. Rewrite: Say directly that attention, decisions, and coordination can stay impaired even when the headache lifts or nausea fades.

**"The most broadly reconstructed practice is to begin after eating"** (food-first subsection). Pure report voice about the project's categorization. Rewrite: Say directly that eating before drinking, pacing drinks, and alternating with water is widely recommended and practiced.

**"Food-before-drinking, pacing, and water alongside alcohol recur in general and harm-reduction forums; current UK harm-reduction guidance also names..."** (food-first subsection). Source-method narration throughout. Rewrite: Say that people across contexts eat first, drink slower, and alternate with water, and that UK harm-reduction guidance recommends the same.

**"The direct experiment behind the food component was narrower"** (food-first subsection). Meta-commentary about evidence scope. Rewrite: Say that one trial tested a specific protein-and-fiber bar, not just any meal.

**"That result establishes that this particular food changed absorption under the study conditions. It does not establish that any meal halves peak BAC, that water caused the effect, or that the three-part practice prevents a hangover"** (food-first subsection). Pure evidence-status commentary. Rewrite: Say what the bar did (lowered peak BAC by about half with this dose) and what's not known (whether a different meal would do the same, whether it prevents next-day symptoms).

**"There is no sourced basis here for a fixed water quantity or minute-by-minute schedule"** (food-first subsection). Report voice about the project. Rewrite: Say that no specific water amount or timing is established—just drink water between drinks.

**"One randomized study gives a precise, limited version of 'some drinks hit worse.'"** (bourbon-vodka subsection). Source-method narration. Rewrite: Say that bourbon produced worse hangover symptoms than vodka at the same alcohol dose in one trial.

**"Blinding was imperfect: 36 of 43 bourbon participants identified what they had received"** (bourbon-vodka subsection). Study-quality critique that doesn't help the reader. Rewrite: Drop it or say that people could taste the difference, so some of the effect might be expectation.

**"The result therefore supports 'bourbon versus vodka as prepared here,' not a complete dark-versus-clear hierarchy, an IPA rule, or a license to consume more vodka"** (bourbon-vodka subsection). "The result therefore supports" is evidence-status language. Rewrite: Say that vodka was gentler than bourbon in this trial, but that doesn't mean all clear liquors are safe or that vodka prevents hangovers—it just seemed to reduce symptoms at the same dose.

**"This is a formulation dossier, not a kitchen tomato suggestion"** (alanine-fortified tomato subsection). Meta-commentary. Rewrite: Say directly that this was a freeze-dried tomato-and-alanine powder made in a lab, not something you can make at home.

**"The preparation may have changed gastric emptying or alcohol oxidation, but the unchanged acetaldehyde result resists a simple 'acetaldehyde clearing' summary"** (alanine-fortified tomato subsection). Hedged mechanism speculation. Rewrite: Say that it lowered blood alcohol but not acetaldehyde, so it probably slowed absorption rather than speeding breakdown.

**"the study does not support transferring 10.4 g alanine to a supplement, other tomato forms, women, or people with other ALDH2 genotypes"** (alanine-fortified tomato subsection). Evidence-status language. Rewrite: Say that the dose and formulation were specific to this trial, and the participants were all Japanese men with a particular enzyme type, so it's not clear if it works for others.

**"This remains a precisely described experimental direction rather than a ready protocol"** (alanine-fortified tomato subsection). Meta-commentary about protocol status. Rewrite: Say that the product isn't available and the recipe isn't reproducible at home.

**"The protocol reconstructed here is only the brine from naturally fermented Polish-style dill cucumbers, not vinegar pickle liquid, soup, raw egg, or alcohol"** (pickle brine subsection). Report voice about the project. Rewrite: Say directly what it is—the salty, sour brine from fermented dill pickles, not vinegar pickle juice or soup.

**"The hangover-use record is thin"** (pickle brine subsection). Report voice about the evidence base. Rewrite: Say that the evidence is limited to a few forum reports, or that it's not well documented.

**"One indexed forum post described five sips after waking and after two large cups of water; a later reply described half a cup followed by water. The direct thread was inaccessible during dossier work, and neither amount is a traditional standard or evidence of a dose–response effect"** (pickle brine subsection). This entire paragraph is source-method narration and project-status reporting. Rewrite: Say that people report drinking a few sips or up to half a cup alongside water, and that there's no standard dose.

**"The record gives no reason to escalate the amount if it increases thirst, nausea, or aversion"** (pickle brine subsection). "The record gives no reason" is report voice. Rewrite: Say directly that if it makes thirst or nausea worse, don't drink more.

**"Running, cycling, hard cardio, stair-stepper work, light exercise, and simply keeping moving recur across separate replies in one 353-comment AskReddit capture"** (movement subsection). Source-method narration. Rewrite: Say that people report trying running, cycling, hard cardio, or just staying in motion.

**"One reply claimed that sweating felt dreadful at first and that the person felt fine about 30 minutes later, but the thread supplied no shared intensity, duration, starting symptom, stopping rule, or later follow-up"** (movement subsection). Source-method narration. Rewrite: Say that one person reported feeling fine after about 30 minutes of sweating, but the reports don't specify how hard, how long, or what symptoms improved.

**"Heat, sauna, and hot or cold showers were often bundled with movement and are separate loads rather than part of this reconstruction"** (movement subsection). Project meta-commentary. Rewrite: Say that exercise is distinct from heat or sauna, which add their own stress.

**"'Sweating it out' is the forum explanation, not an established route of ethanol removal"** (movement subsection). Good critical point, but phrased as source-method commentary. Rewrite: Say that sweating doesn't remove alcohol, but movement might shift arousal, circulation, or attention.

**"A temporary change in arousal, circulation, breathing, attention, or perceived agency is a later hypothesis, while the gorge study also documents an exertional cost"** (movement subsection). "is a later hypothesis" is project meta-commentary. "the gorge study also documents" is source-method narration. Rewrite: Say that movement might change how you feel through circulation or attention, but it also costs energy—people finished the hike more exhausted.

**"Dihydromyricetin, or DHM, has strong market and forum recognition"** (DHM subsection). This describes discourse rather than the substance. Rewrite: Say that DHM is sold in many hangover supplements and talked about online.

**"Commercial dose rubrics and 'cheat code' testimonials sit beside an indexed forum rebuttal reporting no human safety or efficacy data. Those records establish interest and disagreement, not an isolated ingredient regimen"** (DHM subsection). Pure source-field description. Rewrite: Say that some people call it a cheat code and products recommend 300–600 mg, while others point out there's no human safety or efficacy data for isolated DHM.

**"The relevant human crossover did not test isolated DHM"** (DHM subsection). Evidence-status meta-commentary. Rewrite: Say that the trial used a whole *Hovenia* fruit extract, not pure DHM.

**"A 2026 systematic review found only two small clinical studies, both using Hovenia extracts rather than isolated DHM, with inconsistent preclinical findings on ethanol metabolism and neurobehavior"** (DHM subsection). Source-method narration. Rewrite: Say that a recent review found only two small trials, both using fruit extracts, and that animal studies gave inconsistent results.

**"A separate survey of 82 US hangover products found DHM or Hovenia in 39; most did not disclose an amount, disclosed amounts ranged from 200 to 1,200 mg, and no independent peer-reviewed human safety or efficacy evidence was found for any surveyed finished product"** (DHM subsection). Source-method narration. Rewrite: Say that DHM appears in nearly half of US hangover products, doses range from 200 to 1,200 mg, and none have independent human evidence.

**"No dose, purity threshold, or lead time for isolated DHM can be reconstructed from this record"** (DHM subsection). Report voice. Rewrite: Say that there's no established dose, purity, or timing for pure DHM.

**"A whole-fruit extract, quercetin standardization, vendor dose rubric, proprietary DHM–NAC product, and rodent-derived amount are not substitutes"** (DHM subsection). List of ruled-out sources. Rewrite: Say that vendor recommendations, multi-ingredient products, and animal doses don't tell you what pure DHM does in people.

## Assistant tics and structural problems

**"It also matters what 'worked' means"** (opening section). This is assistant meta-commentary about the analytical task rather than explaining the thing. Rewrite: Fold the point directly into the previous paragraph—say that practices target different parts of the problem, so a practice that eases nausea might not improve sleep or restore coordination.

**Laundry-list structure in the commercial products paragraph** (practices section, lines ~90-92). "ZBiotics claims... Cheers pairs... patches assert... IV clinics sell" is a flat enumeration with no synthesis. Rewrite: Group by mechanism claim (gut acetaldehyde, liver support, bypass digestion) or by delivery (capsules, patches, IV), and say what distinguishes each approach rather than listing them.

**Section title "Practices that remain practices, disputes, or leads"** (line 82). Pure project meta-commentary. Rewrite: Change to something like "Other directions" or "Traditional foods and disputed practices," or drop the section title and use subsection headers directly.

**"Specific restorative dishes deserve to remain culturally specific rather than being flattened into 'electrolytes'"** (practices section). Editorial meta-commentary about the project's treatment. Rewrite: Say directly that Korean *haejang-guk*, Russian pickle brine, Japanese umeboshi, and Mexican menudo each have their own ingredients and traditions, and they're not interchangeable electrolyte recipes.

**"Premodern medical systems also should not be mined as supplement lists"** (practices section). Editorial meta-commentary. Rewrite: Say directly that Chinese, Ayurvedic, and Persian traditions diagnose different patterns and prescribe accordingly—kudzu flower is part of a thirteen-herb formula for a specific pattern, not a standalone hangover supplement.

**"'Hair of the dog' is old and disputed rather than selected"** (practices section). Meta-commentary about the project's selection. Rewrite: Say that drinking again the next morning has a long history and current advocates, but also critics who say it delays symptoms or adds liver stress.

**"Symptom drugs belong under the symptom they target"** (practices section). Organizational meta-commentary. Rewrite: Fold this into the description—say that aspirin and caffeine (as in Blowfish) target headache and fatigue after the fact, not prevention.

**"Some leads are thin because the record is thin"** (practices section). Meta-commentary. Rewrite: Drop the meta-frame and say directly that the evidence for each of these is limited or comes from one source.

**"These are preserved as cross-substance experimentation and folk-magic lore, not translated into protocols"** (practices section). Project meta-commentary. Rewrite: Say what they are—one thread discussed psilocybin microdosing after drinking, and a Scottish herbalist recorded transferring the hangover to a slug or pinning a cork thirteen times—without framing them as archive categories.

**"Negative space is part of the record"** (practices section). Pure project meta-commentary. Rewrite: Drop the frame and say directly what wasn't found—no hangover passage in *Donguibogam*, no Aztec remedy for hangover despite modern claims about nopal, no hangover entry in an Ozark remedy archive.

**Symmetrical mini-verdicts throughout**. Many subsections end with "this is X, not Y" formulations that feel like repeated assistant hedging: "not one tested recipe or common mechanism," "not translated into protocols," "not a ready protocol," "not an established route," "not substitutes." These are often useful distinctions, but the repetition creates a tic. Rewrite: Vary the structure—sometimes state the positive claim first, sometimes omit the negative contrast when the positive claim is already scoped.

## Qualifications that swamp the observation

**"although that estimate depends on how intoxication and resistance were defined"** (plurality paragraph). The hedge buries the finding that some people rarely get hangovers. Rewrite: Lead with the observation, and if the definition matters, say briefly what varied (self-report, dose thresholds, next-day measures).

**"The formulation was proprietary, its ingredient weights were unpublished, and the low dose, short drinking window, and fasted setup differ from an ordinary evening"** (food-first subsection). Three distinct limits stacked together. Rewrite: Pick the most important one (probably that the formulation isn't available) and move the rest to a note or drop them.

**"Yet the exact product is not a reproducible retail or household preparation, and the study does not support transferring 10.4 g alanine to a supplement, other tomato forms, women, or people with other ALDH2 genotypes"** (alanine-fortified tomato subsection). Two sentences of limits for a finding already described as non-generalizable. Rewrite: Say once that it's not available and wasn't tested in other populations, and stop.

**"If someone independently follows the label of a legally marketed single-ingredient product, the claimed everyday outcome would have to be a plainly easier next morning without drinking more; nonresponse supplies no basis for escalating the amount"** (DHM subsection). Heavy hedging ("if," "would have to be," "supplies no basis"). Rewrite: Say that if someone tries a pure DHM product, they should notice a clearly easier next morning—and if it doesn't work, taking more won't help.

## Places where judgment or effect size is hidden

**"Mean peak BAC was 0.031% with the bar and 0.064% while fasting, and 90-minute alcohol bioavailability was 45% lower"** (food-first subsection). The effect size is buried in numbers. Rewrite: Say that the bar cut peak blood alcohol roughly in half.

**"At 7:00 a.m., the Acute Hangover Scale average was 1.88 after bourbon and 1.38 after vodka, about 36% higher after bourbon"** (bourbon-vodka subsection). Scale scores are meaningless to the reader. Rewrite: Say that bourbon produced worse symptoms than vodka by about a third.

**"At 6:00 a.m., heavy-headed, sluggish, and other sedative feelings were lower; the yes/no hangover count was 1 of 20 crossover occasions versus 6 of 20"** (alanine-fortified tomato subsection). The second finding is stark (1 versus 6 out of 20), but it's listed flatly after vague "feelings were lower." Rewrite: Lead with the 1-versus-6 finding, and say what "heavy-headed and sluggish" means in plain terms.

**"mean severity fell from 4.6/10 before walking to 3.4 during and 3.5 after"** (movement subsection). Scale scores again. Rewrite: Say that severity dropped noticeably during the walk but came back after, or say that the change was modest and came with exhaustion.

## Passages to preserve

**"Dehydration is one aggravating path, not a complete account"** (dehydration paragraph). Clean, direct claim that frames the problem without hedging.

**"A result large enough to matter would be a noticeably gentler rise in intoxication during the occasion, less urgent thirst, and possibly an easier next morning"** (food-first subsection). Concrete, vivid description of what someone would actually notice.

**"If feeling less drunk merely leads to faster drinking, the pacing component has failed; food and water do not erase the alcohol consumed"** (food-first subsection). Direct, practical warning without false warmth.

**"The substitution changed reported symptoms, not measured function"** (bourbon-vodka subsection). Clean, useful distinction in seven words.

**"The meaningful comparison is comfort, especially headache or global severity; it must not be interpreted as restored attention, coordination, or readiness for consequential tasks"** (bourbon-vodka subsection). Direct, consequential guidance about what the finding does and doesn't mean.

**"The modest target is thirst, dry mouth, or reluctance to eat—not alcohol clearance or the whole hangover"** (pickle brine subsection). Clear scoping of what the practice addresses.

**"The smallest faithful ordinary-use test is easy, familiar movement in a cool, low-consequence place, with immediate freedom to stop. A result large enough to matter is a clear improvement in alertness or bodily heaviness during or shortly after movement that persists after stopping"** (movement subsection). Concrete, specific protocol with a vivid outcome description.

**"Unsteadiness, faintness, weakness, unusual exhaustion, worsening symptoms, or only momentary distraction counts against the practice"** (movement subsection). Direct list of meaningful failure signals.

**"Re-dosing may change the felt moment by adding alcohol exposure, which is different from resolving the next-day state"** (practices section). Clean critical observation without hedging.

## Summary rewrite directions

1. Replace source-method narration ("recurs in forums," "the literature treats," "a review found") with direct statements of what happens, what people do, or what the evidence shows.

2. Replace evidence-status language ("establishes," "does not support," "the record gives no reason") with plain claims about what is known, what isn't, and what transfer limits exist.

3. Replace project meta-commentary ("the protocol reconstructed here," "this remains an experimental direction," "practices that remain practices") with direct description of what the thing is and what it does.

4. Reduce stacked qualifications to one clear limit per finding, and place it after the claim rather than burying the claim under hedges.

5. Translate scale scores and percentages into plain descriptions of what someone would notice—"cut peak blood alcohol roughly in half," "worse symptoms by about a third," "1 out of 20 occasions versus 6."

6. Vary the "this is X, not Y" structure to avoid assistant-verdict repetition—sometimes state the positive scope directly, sometimes omit the negative when the claim is already bounded.

7. Consolidate laundry-list commercial products into groups by mechanism or delivery, and say what distinguishes each rather than enumerating claims.

8. Drop organizational and editorial meta-commentary—let section headers and direct description do the work.

9. Preserve the concrete outcome descriptions, vivid failure signals, direct warnings, and clean distinctions like "changed symptoms, not function."

10. The voice should sound like someone who looked through the territory, can judge what's thin or disputed or worth trying, and doesn't need to show the research scaffolding to make the point.
