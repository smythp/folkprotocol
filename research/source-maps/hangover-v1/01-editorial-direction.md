# Editorial direction

## Initial impetus

Find practices that produce noticeable changes through ordinary use. Do not
reduce the subject to generic abstinence advice, a supplement ranking, or a
requirement for clinical-trial-level evidence before a direction may be
discussed.

## Later direction from the pilot

- Treat hangover as overlapping symptom paths rather than one uniform state.
  Headache, gastrointestinal distress, sleep disruption, thirst, and a flat or
  physically inert state can appear separately or together.
- Earlier drinking has sometimes produced the best next morning. The timing
  and sleep relationship deserves direct attention.
- A substantial whole-milk meal once changed the felt alcohol trajectory
  dramatically. Meal composition and absorption are more interesting than a
  generic instruction to eat.
- IPAs reliably produce worse headache and sometimes gastrointestinal symptoms
  for the commissioner. Beverage composition should not be reduced to total
  alcohol or a universal dark-versus-clear rule.
- Outdoor swimming can produce a large next-day improvement. Exercise, bright
  light, breathing, circulation, water temperature, and the change of setting
  remain entangled. Sauna, hot or cold showers, and Wim Hof-style breathing are
  adjacent state-change practices; “sweating out toxins” is not the proposed
  explanation.
- Aspirin has been useful for headache. Alka-Seltzer has been useful for
  gastrointestinal symptoms, usually near bedtime. They are different symptom
  interventions used at different times.
- Pedialyte is a common hangover practice; Liquid I.V. and other oral
  electrolyte products should be compared as formulations rather than treated
  as interchangeable branding.
- Korean pear juice and NAC capsules remain interesting consumable leads. NAC
  powder's taste and gastrointestinal effects make capsule form materially
  different in ordinary use.
- Prefer practices whose effect would become obvious over normal life. Do not
  turn the page into a controlled self-experiment or reward pills merely
  because they are easy to specify.

These are commissioner observations and priorities. They direct research and
editorial weight; they are not general evidence.
