# Protocol dossiers

These dossiers reconstruct the six directions selected in the assessment. A reconstruction describes the practice actually present in the record; it is not a claim that the practice prevents harm, makes a given alcohol dose safe, or has been clinically validated. Hangover is a bundle of possible outcomes, and feeling better is not proof that attention, coordination, or judgment have recovered [P12].

## 1. Pre-drinking food, slower drinking, and water alternation

**What the maps selected.** Eating substantially before drinking, pacing alcohol, and taking water alongside it recur in general forums, harm-reduction discussion, and community guidance [10:RF1][10:RF2][10:RF3][50:EX2][50:EX3]. A controlled snack study supplies an absorption result, but it tested food only—not the three-part bundle [30:PR10].

**The exact studied food intervention.** In a four-period, open-label randomized crossover, 21 healthy social drinkers (10 men and 11 women, ages 25–64) completed four conditions after an overnight water-only fast and a standardized dinner the previous evening. One condition used a 70 g, 210 kcal “reduced alcohol bioavailability bar” containing milk-protein hydrolysate, almonds, white chocolate, protein crisps, allulose syrup, tagatose, insoluble oat fiber, water, acacia gum, flavor, sea salt, and processing ingredients. Its declared macros were 20 g protein, 9 g fat, 30 g carbohydrate, and 5 g insoluble fiber. Ingredient-by-ingredient weights were not published, so this is a branded experimental formulation, not a reproducible household recipe [P1].

Participants ate the bar with 8 US fluid ounces of water over 10 minutes. Five minutes later they drank, over 10 minutes, a 20% ABV vodka-and-noncaloric-tonic drink providing 0.30 g ethanol/kg for women and 0.35 g/kg for men. Against fasting, mean peak BAC was 0.031% with the bar versus 0.064% without food; the 90-minute alcohol-bioavailability measure was 45% lower. The bar condition's mean peak occurred about 43 minutes after drinking. No next-day symptoms or function were measured [P1]. A 210 kcal savory snack and a 635 kcal mixed meal were comparators, but neither makes this a trial of “any meal.”

**Ordinary-use reconstruction.** The evidence-supported shape is: do not begin on an empty stomach; eat before the first alcohol; drink more slowly; and alternate alcoholic drinks with water or a soft drink. Current UK clinical harm-reduction guidance names those behaviors together but specifies neither water volume nor a clock interval [P2]. Thus “one nonalcoholic drink between alcoholic drinks” is the closest direct reconstruction of alternation; a fixed number of minutes, a fixed water volume, or an alcohol target would be invented. The 70 g experimental bar is a reference formulation only, not a required food.

**Claimed or observed result.** The controlled result is a lower and later BAC peak after one standardized food product [P1]. The community record claims less abrupt intoxication and a better next morning [10:RF1][10:RF2][10:RF3], but the trial does not verify a hangover effect. Water can relieve thirst without making dehydration the master explanation for every symptom [10:RF2][50:EX1].

**Likely pathway.** The study authors propose delayed gastric emptying plus greater gastric or hepatic first-pass metabolism, with protein and insoluble fiber as likely contributors [P1]. That explanation belongs to the food condition. Pacing separately lowers the rate at which alcohol arrives, while alternating water can replace some fluid and, in practice, occupies time that otherwise might contain another alcoholic drink [P2]. These are later behavioral interpretations, not three manifestations of one proven biochemical mechanism.

**Disagreements and boundaries.** The bar investigators had a commercial interest in the product, the study was open-label, and the formulation weights are proprietary [P1]. Its low laboratory alcohol dose, brief drinking window, and fasted setup do not resemble a long social evening. Food and water can lower or reshape exposure but do not cancel the alcohol consumed. The official guidance treats slower pace, lower strength, eating first, and alternation as harm reduction, not protection from intoxication [P2].

**Ordinary-use test.** A noticeable result would be a gentler rise in intoxication during an otherwise ordinary occasion, less urgent thirst, and possibly an easier next morning. If the only result is feeling less drunk while the same alcohol disappears faster, the pacing part has failed. Next-day alertness should not be inferred from comfort alone.

## 2. Lower-congener drink at the same alcohol dose

**What the maps selected.** The central record is a randomized comparison of bourbon and vodka, with a separate experimental-community “toxin ranking” as an unvalidated extension [30:PR3][50:EX6]. The defensible direction is a beverage substitution at the same ethanol amount, not a hierarchy of every drink.

**Exact comparison.** Ninety-five healthy heavy drinkers aged 21–33 were assigned to either 101-proof Wild Turkey bourbon or 100-proof Absolut vodka and completed an alcohol night and a matched-placebo night in randomized order. Each spirit was mixed 1:4 with caffeine-free cola. Men received 1.2 g ethanol/kg and women 1.1 g/kg, paced from 8:30 to 10:00 p.m.; redosing was allowed to reach the target. Mean peak breath alcohol was 0.11%, with a 0.09–0.15% range. Participants had eaten a standardized dinner between 4:15 and 5:00 p.m., received snacks after reaching target, and had an 11:00 p.m.–7:00 a.m. sleep opportunity [P3]. These doses describe the experiment and are not an ordinary-use target.

The paper says bourbon contained 37 times the congeners of the vodka, citing an earlier analysis rather than chemically assaying the served bottles; it supplies no absolute congener concentration [P3]. Consequently, “bourbon versus vodka as prepared here” is exact, while “37 times cleaner” would overstate what this trial measured.

**Outcome and timing.** At 7:00 a.m., the nine-item Acute Hangover Scale averaged 1.88 after bourbon and 1.38 after vodka on its 0–7 item scale, roughly 36% higher after bourbon. Placebo-night averages were 0.64 and 0.57, respectively [P3]. Testing ran about 8:00–9:30 a.m. and included a five-minute psychomotor vigilance task plus continuous performance, tapping, symbol substitution, sequence, pattern-memory, and digit-span tasks. Alcohol, compared with placebo, slowed sustained-attention reaction times, but bourbon and vodka did not differ on performance [P3].

**Likely pathway.** The authors interpret the subjective difference as a congener effect: fermentation and distillation by-products add biological burdens beyond ethanol. Their data support that interpretation for the bourbon-vodka contrast, not each specific congener or all dark-versus-clear drinks [P3]. Sleep disruption tracked hangover severity but did not statistically explain the performance deficit [P3].

**Disagreements and boundaries.** Beverage type changed reported severity without restoring measured function [P3]. Blinding was also imperfect: 36 of 43 bourbon participants identified their beverage, leaving expectancy as a plausible contributor [P3]. Total ethanol remains the dominant controllable exposure; the NIAAA notes that more alcohol generally means worse hangover regardless of drink order or beverage family [P12]. This substitution does not license a larger vodka dose.

**Ordinary-use test.** At an occasion where a person would otherwise drink a congener-rich brown spirit, the relevant observation is whether an ethanol-matched vodka choice leaves a noticeably milder overall next morning. A clearer head subjectively does not establish driving, work, or care-giving readiness; coordination and attention may still be impaired [P12].

## 3. Alanine-fortified tomato with the drinking meal

**What the maps selected.** One randomized crossover supplies the entire evidence base for this exact direction [30:PR11]. It is a manufactured tomato-paste preparation with added DL-alanine, not tomato juice, raw tomato, or an unspecified tomato food.

**Exact preparation and sequence.** Investigators mixed 2,000 g tomato paste, 250 g DL-alanine, and 2,750 g water, then freeze-dried the mixture to 880 g. The tested 40 g serving supplied 150 kcal and was described as equivalent to about 400 g raw tomato plus 10.4 g alanine. The control was 38 g sugar supplying 152 kcal [P4]. Both were consumed with a commercial micronutrient-fortified meal containing 716 kcal, 16.4 g protein, 39.4 g fat, 74 g carbohydrate, and 12 g fiber, plus 20% ABV shochu providing 1.2 g ethanol/kg. Participants completed the supper, alcohol, and test preparation within one hour beginning around 6:00 p.m.; water was available freely [P4]. The report does not establish a finer order within that hour.

The crossover enrolled 20 healthy Japanese men selected for ALDH2*1/*1. Fifteen were included in the main analysis: four could not finish the alcohol and one vomited during the control condition. Those 15 consumed a mean 73.6 g ethanol [P4]. The genotype and sex selection are part of the preparation's tested boundary, not demographic trivia.

**Outcome.** Breath alcohol was lower at most evening measurements with fortified tomato; breath-alcohol area under the curve was 2.1 versus 2.5 mg/L·hour, and the investigators estimated mean peak BAC near 0.10% versus 0.12% [P4]. At 6:00 a.m., total “sedative” feelings and the items down, heavy-headed, and sluggish were lower. The yes/no hangover count was 1 of 20 after fortified tomato and 6 of 20 after control—5% versus 30% of crossover occasions—not a 25-point change established in a 20-person parallel population [P4]. Blood acetaldehyde did not differ [P4].

**Pathway.** The authors propose that tomato insoluble fiber and alanine delayed gastric emptying; they also interpret higher acetate as compatible with alanine supporting NAD+-dependent alcohol oxidation [P4]. The unchanged acetaldehyde result works against a simple “clears acetaldehyde” summary. Their own preparation work reportedly found that heating diminished tomato fiber's ethanol-holding capacity, and the comparison meal already contained 12 g fiber [P4]. Those details make raw tomato, paste, juice, and freeze-dried fortified paste noninterchangeable.

**Disagreements and boundaries.** This was a small open-label study conducted by Asahi Group researchers, restricted to men with the fully active ALDH2 genotype [P4]. Four participants' inability to finish the high alcohol dose and the control-condition vomiting show that tolerability and analyzability shaped the apparent sample. The study gives no basis for transferring the 10.4 g alanine amount to isolated alanine, other tomato forms, women, or ALDH2*1/*2 and *2/*2 drinkers.

**Ordinary-use test.** A noticeable result, if the exact fortified food were available and appropriate, would be less heavy-headedness and sluggishness on waking after an otherwise ordinary occasion. Faster alcohol disappearance, absence of flushing, or lack of vomiting is not a substitute outcome. Because the experimental product is not a reproducible retail or kitchen preparation, this remains a formulation dossier rather than a ready household protocol.

## 4. Next-morning fermented cucumber brine

**What the maps selected.** Sour-and-salted morning foods appear in Russian pickle brine and rassolnik, Japanese umeboshi, German rollmops, Korean and Mexican soups, and several living-practice accounts [20:TR5][20:TR6][20:TR8][60:PF4][60:PF6][60:PF7]. They should not be collapsed into one remedy. This dossier chooses one named preparation: the brine from naturally fermented Polish-style dill cucumbers, *ogórki kiszone*.

**Exact preparation.** One current traditional-recipe account packs small pickling cucumbers in one-liter jars with dill, one or two garlic cloves, half a piece of horseradish, and optional blackcurrant leaf. Its batch brine is 3 liters water plus 6 tablespoons non-iodized rock salt—one tablespoon per 0.5 liter. Hot, not boiling, brine covers the cucumbers. Jars stand at room temperature for 3–5 days for initial fermentation and then move to a cool place; full sourness develops after 2–3 weeks [P5]. This is lactic-fermented cucumber brine without vinegar. Vinegar pickle liquor, rassolnik soup, a prairie oyster, and an alcoholic “hair of the dog” are materially different preparations.

**Amount and timing in living use.** A 2022 r/alcohol question describes five sips of generic “pickle juice” after waking with a severe hangover and after two large cups of water. A later reply in the same thread reports usually taking half a cup and then switching to water [P6]. The snippets do not identify a naturally fermented, Polish-style, or vinegar-free preparation, so neither amount can be transferred to the exact brine reconstructed above. They are two first-person amounts, not a traditional standard or dose-response study. Automated access to the thread was blocked at dossier time; the wording and amounts were available only in the search index, so the account remains partial [P6].

**Claim and pathway.** The larger mapped record treats brine as a sour, salty restorative and sometimes as an ingredient in a substantial meal [20:TR5][60:PF4]. The forum question asks how much to take rather than reporting a resolved outcome [P6]. A plausible modern interpretation is simply that a small, strongly flavored liquid supplies some fluid and salt and may make drinking water or eating feel easier [P5][P12]. That is this dossier's interpretation, not the recipe author's mechanism, and electrolyte replacement has not been shown to track overall hangover severity [P12].

**Disagreements and boundaries.** The evidence is strong for the existence of the preparation and weak for its effect. A German living-practice source explicitly rejects a neighboring pickled-herring cure [60:PF6], underscoring that longevity or sourness is not validation. The recipe says visible mold or a strong off-smell means discard and that opened jars should stay refrigerated with cucumbers covered by brine [P5]. Raw egg and added alcohol are excluded, because they change both exposure and risk rather than refining this preparation.

**Ordinary-use test.** No exact amount can be reconstructed for the fermented brine. If someone already consumes it as food, the modest result worth noticing is easier drinking or eating and less thirst or dry mouth; this is the dossier's narrow interpretation, not an effect reported by the recipe or indexed thread. It is not a cure test. If it is not tolerable or merely produces thirst for more water, the record offers no basis for escalating toward a larger serving.

## 5. Single-ingredient DHM before drinking

**What the maps selected.** DHM—dihydromyricetin, also called ampelopsin—has forum recognition, appears across capsule and drink products, and attracts striking “cheat code” claims [10:RF7][10:RF8][40:CW9][40:CW11][50:EX12]. The assessment correctly demanded isolation from NAC, vitamins, herbs, and engineered probiotics. Direct research shows that an evidence-grounded single-ingredient human protocol cannot presently be reconstructed.

**Preparation mismatch.** The relevant human crossover did not test isolated DHM. It tested 2,460 mg of a hot-water extract of *Hovenia dulcis* fruit, mixed with dextrin at 10:6 and standardized to 5.9–8.9 mg quercetin per gram. Twenty-six young men with heterozygous ALDH2 consumed the extract with 360 mL Korean soju containing 50 g ethanol; blood was sampled at 1, 4, and 12 hours [P7]. A whole fruit extract standardized to quercetin cannot establish the identity, purity, or dose of DHM.

That study found no group difference in blood alcohol, acetaldehyde, or total hangover score at the one-hour peak, although the extract group showed greater subsequent declines in selected symptoms [P7]. Its authors emphasized inflammatory regulation rather than demonstrated acceleration of alcohol clearance [P7]. A 2026 systematic review found only two small clinical studies, both using *Hovenia* extracts, and no clinical trial directly evaluating isolated DHM [P8]. Preclinical findings for oxidative stress, inflammation, liver injury, and signaling are mechanistic leads; the review found inconsistent ethanol-metabolism and neurobehavioral findings and did not convert animal doses into a human hangover regimen [P8].

**Commercial amount is not a validated dose.** A peer-reviewed survey of 82 US-marketed hangover products found DHM or *Hovenia* in 39. Of those, 23 did not disclose the amount; disclosed amounts ranged from 200 to 1,200 mg, often inside multi-ingredient products. The authors found no independent peer-reviewed human safety or efficacy evidence for any surveyed finished product [P9]. Those label amounts document market practice, not an effective isolated-DHM range.

**Protocol status.** No dose, purity threshold, or lead time is supportable for single-ingredient DHM from this record. Substituting the 2,460 mg fruit-extract dose, a vendor's milligram rubric, or a dose derived from rodents would manufacture a protocol. The selected direction therefore remains a well-specified research question: chemically characterized isolated DHM, taken before alcohol, compared with placebo, with alcohol exposure, BAC, acetaldehyde, named symptoms, function, and adverse events all reported.

**Boundaries.** “DHM,” powdered *Hovenia* plant material, quercetin-standardized fruit extract, proprietary DHM-plus-NAC formulas, and multi-ingredient recovery drinks are not interchangeable [P7][P8][P9]. Product repetition can reflect shared suppliers or copied formulas [40:CW9][40:CW11][40:CW12]. A product's biochemical vocabulary does not establish that it changes BAC or acetaldehyde in people.

**Ordinary-use test.** If someone independently uses a legally marketed, single-ingredient product exactly as its label directs, a noticeable claimed result would be a plainly easier next morning after an otherwise ordinary occasion—not a subjective license to drink more and not presumed faster sobriety. Because no isolated-DHM human expectation or safe/effective dose is established, nonresponse cannot rationally be answered by escalating the amount.

## 6. Deliberate next-day exercise as a state-change test

**What the maps selected.** Running, hard cardio, stair-stepper work, and continued movement recur across separate replies in a 353-comment AskReddit capture, often bundled with fluid, food, sauna, or showers [10:RF12]. The reconstructable core is movement alone. Heat exposure is not part of it.

**What people actually did and claimed.** The thread contains no stable common dose. Replies name running, cycling, hard cardio, a stair-stepper, light exercise, and simply keeping moving. One poster's concise claim was that sweating felt dreadful initially and that about 30 minutes later they felt fine [P10]. The page supplies neither heart-rate intensity nor distance, food sequence, baseline symptoms, stopping rule, or later follow-up. The direct Reddit page required login when reopened for this dossier; the complete page capture underlying [10:RF12] remains the fuller access record [P10]. Thirty minutes is therefore a claimed time-to-change, not a prescribed workout duration.

**Adjacent observational evidence.** In a naturalistic study of 299 people walking the 15.8 km Samaria Gorge, 176 reported a hangover after consuming a mean 3.0 ten-gram alcohol drinks; their starting severity averaged 4.6/10. All groups took about six hours to hike. Hungover participants' mean severity fell to 3.4 during and 3.5 after the walk, but they finished more exhausted than non-hungover participants. Severity was not correlated with walking time, breaks, or water consumption [P11]. Because everyone walked and symptoms were retrospectively rated during the walk, elapsed time, scenery, food, and movement cannot be separated. This is not a treatment trial.

**Pathway.** Posters commonly say they “sweat it out” [10:RF12][P10]. Ethanol is not thereby shown to leave through sweat. A later hypothesis is a temporary change in arousal, circulation, breathing, attention, or perceived agency; neither the forum nor the observational study establishes it [P10][P11]. The gorge study is compatible with a felt symptom shift during movement while also showing greater exertional cost [P11]; it does not identify which pathway caused the shift.

**Boundaries and stopping conditions.** A hangover can impair attention, decisions, and muscle coordination even when the person feels capable [P12]. Exercise in heat raises dehydration and heat-illness risk; CDC guidance says to start slowly and stop activity and get to a cool place if faint or weak [P13]. Those conditions matter directly here. Sauna, infrared heat, deliberate hot/cold exposure, driving to an exercise site, competition, and technical terrain are excluded from the first test because they add heat, coordination, or consequence beyond the mapped practice [P10][P12][P13].

**Ordinary-use test.** The smallest faithful test is easy, familiar movement in a cool, low-consequence setting, with the freedom to stop immediately. A noticeable result would be a clear shift in alertness or heaviness during or shortly after moving that persists once the person stops. Feeling worse, unsteady, faint, unusually exhausted, or only briefly distracted is a negative result. The record does not justify pushing through those signals to reach the forum's 30-minute anecdote.

## Research spillover

The direct search clarified rather than expanded the six directions. Human *Hovenia* trials belong beside DHM as a preparation mismatch, not as a seventh remedy. The Samaria Gorge study belongs beside exercise as a causal warning, not as proof of a hiking cure. The 2022 systematic review of 21 placebo-controlled hangover trials found no intervention tested twice and rated all efficacy evidence very low quality [P14]; that field-level result does not erase specific traditional or first-person observations, but it blocks converting singleton trials into general validation. No newly encountered remedy was added as a dossier.
