# Tone brief: hemorrhoids-v1 factual draft

## Opening: Strong start with evidence creep

**"A clinical review proposes several paths that can coexist"** — Report voice. The review's proposals are already the page's working model. Say what the paths are: straining raises pressure, blood volume expands, progesterone relaxes vein walls, the uterus compresses pelvic veins. Delete "proposes" and own the physiology.

**"although it does not show which path caused any one person's symptoms"** — Qualification swamps the observation. The preceding sentence already said the paths can coexist, so this adds no decision. If the point is that the paths are not mutually exclusive and a person's case might involve more than one, say that. Otherwise cut.

**"propose a systemic effect on venous tone, capillary permeability, or lymphatic drainage"** — Report voice. The review says the mechanism is not fully established, so the page has room to say what the claimed paths are. Rewrite as: "act systemically on venous tone, capillary permeability, or lymphatic drainage—the review says the mechanism is not fully established."

**Preserve:** "Hemorrhoids in pregnancy do not reduce to a constipation story." "The useful first distinction is what a practice is meant to change." These are clean judgments that orient the reader without filler.

## Position section: "Recoverable" and "establish" machinery

**"no usable dose or time course was recovered"** — Report voice. The draft is not a legal brief accounting for what it recovered. Say: "no source gave a dose or time course."

**"No source here establishes that position shrinks a hemorrhoid, shortens the flare, or prevents recurrence."** — Evidence-status tic. The page has already said position changes are about breaking up sitting and that one account could not isolate the effect. Rewrite as: "None of the sources showed that position shrinks a hemorrhoid, shortens the flare, or prevents recurrence."

**"That is enough to treat worsening while seated as a meaningful result, rather than assuming that any offloading cushion offloads the affected tissue."** — Good judgment, convoluted delivery. The point is that a ring can concentrate pressure rather than relieve it. Rewrite as: "That is enough to take worsening seriously: a ring can concentrate pressure rather than relieve it."

**Preserve:** "The most defensible positional practice is simple: break up prolonged sitting by standing or moving." "A positional change is large enough to matter if pressure or pain reliably eases while standing, moving, or lying on the side and returns during prolonged sitting." These sections are specific and functional.

## Sitz bath section: Source-method narration and caution accumulation

**"The clearest sitz-bath protocol uses a toilet-seat basin or shallow tub with enough water to cover the perineum; a full tub needs about three to four inches."** — Good opening, but the tub depth becomes fussy. If the protocol says "enough to cover the perineum," the three-to-four-inch detail belongs only if it changes the decision. Otherwise cut.

**"The study offers a possible explanation for temporary relaxation, but the practical protocol stays at about 40°C because hotter water can burn."** — Academic hedging. The study found a thermosphincteric reflex. The protocol uses 40°C and says hotter water can burn. Rewrite as: "The study found a thermosphincteric reflex that relaxed the sphincter temporarily. Hotter water can burn, so the protocol stays at about 40°C."

**"These sources do not establish durable shrinkage or healing over days."** — Evidence-status tic. The page already said the ordinary-use result is a clear reduction in pain or itching during the soak and for part of the hour after. Rewrite as: "None of the sources showed durable shrinkage or healing over days."

**"A randomized trial after hemorrhoid surgery found no benefit from twice-daily warm sitz baths for pain, analgesic use, or satisfaction, but that wound-care population does not answer the pregnancy home-care question."** — Qualification swamps the finding. The trial is null for a different population, and the page acknowledges the population mismatch. Either the trial belongs or it doesn't. If it stays, rewrite as: "A trial after hemorrhoid surgery found no benefit from twice-daily sitz baths for pain, analgesic use, or satisfaction. That wound-care population is not the same as pregnancy home care."

**"No source supplied a concentration tested against plain water."** — Report voice. Say: "No source tested a concentration against plain water."

**"Plain water is therefore the interpretable version of the practice; added salt earns its place only if it produces an obvious comfort gain without irritation."** — Good judgment voice. Preserve.

## Cold and witch hazel: Label repetition and precedent language

**"The reproducible witch-hazel preparation is an external pad containing 50% w/w witch hazel."** — Report voice. Say: "The external pad contains 50% w/w witch hazel."

**"A result large enough to matter is prompt, repeatable relief of one of those symptoms; the label does not establish shrinkage or recurrence prevention."** — The first clause is good practical threshold; the second is evidence-status tic. The label is for temporary relief and has a seven-day boundary. Shrinkage is not its job. Rewrite as: "A result large enough to matter is prompt, repeatable relief of one of those symptoms. The label does not claim shrinkage or recurrence prevention."

**"This supplies a bounded cold-contact precedent for injured postpartum tissue, not proof that a frozen witch-hazel pad shrinks hemorrhoids."** — Academic scaffolding. The page is comparing ice for perineal trauma to frozen pads for hemorrhoids and noting the populations differ. Rewrite as: "That is ice for perineal trauma, not hemorrhoids, and it does not show that frozen witch-hazel pads shrink hemorrhoids."

**Preserve:** "Cold and witch hazel often arrive together in a postpartum kit, but they are different interventions." "Immediate numbing from a plain cold pad is a cooling result. Relief from a room-temperature 50% pad is consistent with the labeled witch-hazel use. A frozen multi-ingredient pad cannot distinguish temperature, witch hazel, aloe, or oils." These are clean, specific distinctions.

## Topical actives: Laundry-list structure and cannot-be-transferred preamble

**"The following schedules belong to the cited finished products and cannot be transferred to another strength, combination, or route."** — Academic framing. The point is that the dosing is product-specific. Rewrite as: "These schedules are product-specific and do not transfer to other strengths, combinations, or routes."

**The five-bullet active list** — Laundry-list structure. Each bullet follows the same pattern: active, labeled use, frequency, boundary. The repetition makes the list skim-proof. Consider breaking it into two passes: one that names the actives and their jobs (anesthetic for pain, vasoconstrictor for swelling, steroid for itch), and a second that gives the schedules for readers who have already chosen. Or preserve the list but add a lead sentence that says the actives do different jobs and the choice follows the symptom.

**"Pregnancy language is attached to formulations, not ingredient families."** — Good observation. Preserve.

**"Routine short use rests on low expected fetal exposure rather than comparative pregnancy trials."** — Report voice and convoluted. Say: "Short use is considered routine because fetal exposure is expected to be low, not because pregnancy trials compared them."

**"The practical threshold follows the active: less itch with hydrocortisone, temporary numbness or less pain with lidocaine or pramoxine, temporary reduction in swollen tissue or burning with phenylephrine, and temporary relief of external itch or burning with witch hazel."** — This repeats the active/symptom pairs from the bullet list. Either the bullets should be tighter and this sentence is the summary, or this sentence is redundant. If it stays, it should come before the bullets.

**Preserve:** "Hemorrhoid products with different active ingredients do different jobs." "Bleeding, worsening, applicator pain, or persistence beyond the product's stated seven-day boundary is a stop point, not a reason to assemble a stronger stack." These are clear functional judgments.

## Bowel path: Convoluted qualifier and report voice

**"Iron belongs in this section only when bowel changes follow its use."** — Good conditional statement. Preserve.

**"one account cannot separate formulation, amount, pregnancy timing, and other causes."** — Evidence-status tic. The page is not defending why it did not draw a conclusion. Say: "One account does not separate formulation, amount, pregnancy timing, and other causes."

**"Pregnancy trials do show that iron formulation and elemental amount can change gastrointestinal tolerance."** — Report voice. Say: "Pregnancy trials found that iron formulation and elemental amount change gastrointestinal tolerance."

**"Neither study measured hemorrhoids."** — Evidence-status tic. This is the right observation, but it is phrased as a limitation catalog. Say: "Neither study tracked hemorrhoids."

**"That purpose makes the reason for supplementation and elemental amount part of any form or schedule decision; a lower-dose tolerance result does not establish equivalent treatment for diagnosed anemia."** — Convoluted academic voice. The point is that WHO recommends 30–60 mg for anemia prevention, so switching to a lower dose for tolerance must account for whether the person's iron need is met. Rewrite as: "WHO recommends 30 to 60 mg daily for anemia prevention, so a lower-dose switch for tolerance must account for whether the person's iron need is still met. A better-tolerated dose is not the same as adequate treatment for diagnosed anemia."

**"The meaningful ordinary-use result from an appropriate iron change would first be easier stool passage or less constipation while iron needs remain treated."** — Wordy. Say: "An appropriate iron change should first make stool passage easier or reduce constipation while still meeting iron needs."

**Preserve:** The trial summaries are specific and the outcomes are clearly stated.

## Oral venotonics: Heavy citation machinery and flattened verdicts

**"The oral options cannot be flattened into a 'vein support' category or a supplement stack."** — Good opening judgment. Preserve.

**The entire section is dominated by report voice:** "Its product information gives," "A separate open, uncontrolled report followed," "A double-blind placebo-controlled trial randomized," "A pregnancy review pooled," "A prospective single-arm study gave," "The EMA monograph concerns," "This is a traditional-use record, not a pregnancy trial."

This is the section where the draft most strongly resembles a literature summary. The citations are doing the talking, and the page is narrating what they said. The reader needs to know what the studies found, but the draft should digest the findings rather than report the study designs.

**Example rewrite for MPFF paragraph:**

Current: "Daflon 500 is a micronized purified flavonoid fraction containing 450 mg diosmin and 50 mg flavonoids expressed as hesperidin per tablet. Its product information gives an acute-attack schedule of six tablets daily for four days and four daily for three days, taken with meals; the same document prefers avoiding it during pregnancy because human data are absent or limited and says infant risk during breastfeeding cannot be excluded. [P21]"

Rewrite: "Daflon 500 combines 450 mg diosmin and 50 mg hesperidin per tablet. The product information schedules six tablets daily for four days, then four daily for three days, taken with meals. It advises against use during pregnancy because human data are limited and says breastfeeding risk cannot be excluded. [P21]"

The second version moves faster, says what the product is and what the schedule is, and states the pregnancy advice without "prefers avoiding" hedging.

**"the abstract does not expose the complete dose, so the commercial seven-day schedule cannot be substituted for the study regimen."** — Good warning, but "expose" is report voice. Say: "The abstract does not give the complete dose, so the commercial seven-day schedule cannot replace the study regimen."

**"with no established causal link"** — Evidence-status tic. Say: "without a clear causal link" or "and the study did not establish a causal link."

**"For ordinary life, the reported time windows are day-four symptom relief in the uncontrolled MPFF pregnancy report, change over two to four weeks in the controlled rutoside trial, and change after the two-week dual-route Euphorbia course."** — Report voice summary. The page is cataloging what it reported earlier. Either this is the practical takeaway and should be rewritten as direct advice, or it is redundant. If it stays, rewrite as: "The time windows for ordinary use are day-four relief with MPFF (uncontrolled pregnancy report), two to four weeks with rutosides (controlled trial), and two weeks with oral-plus-topical Euphorbia."

**Preserve:** "No faithful pregnancy or breastfeeding protocol emerged for horse chestnut, butcher's broom, unmicronized diosmin, or a self-built multi-compound stack." This is a clean summary judgment.

## Thin and unusual: "Preserves" and "is a warning"

**"The account preserves a specific folk maneuver, but it supplies no controlled comparison, standardized application, pregnancy safety assessment, or basis for turning sugar into routine home care."** — Report voice. The page is accounting for what the account preserves and supplies. Say: "The account describes a specific folk maneuver but gives no controlled comparison, standardized application, pregnancy safety check, or basis for making it routine home care."

**"The first is a historical lineage fragment rather than a recoverable present-day protocol; the second is a warning that repeated herbal lists can erase where a practice came from."** — The second clause is good meta-observation and should be preserved. The first clause uses "recoverable" (report voice). Rewrite as: "The first is a historical fragment, not a present-day protocol. The second is a warning that repeated herbal lists can erase where a practice came from."

## General assistant tics

**Repeated mini-verdicts:** The draft repeatedly follows a claim with "but" or "however" and then restates the limitation. This is useful when the limitation changes the decision, but it becomes tic when it repeats. Examples: "The study offers a possible explanation for temporary relaxation, but the practical protocol stays at about 40°C because hotter water can burn." "A household padsicle is a different preparation. … the optional ingredients make any result impossible to attribute to witch hazel alone."

Some of these are necessary ("A household padsicle is a different preparation" is a real distinction). Others are hedging or overqualification.

**Symmetrical lists:** The topical actives section and the oral venotonics section both follow symmetrical structures where each item gets the same treatment regardless of whether the detail changes the decision. This flattens the field and makes skimming harder.

**Canned transitions:** "For ordinary use," "For ordinary life," "The meaningful ordinary-use result," "A result large enough to matter," "The practical threshold." These are all variations on the same transition. The page uses them to signal when it is moving from evidence to judgment, which is good, but the repetition becomes formula. Vary the phrasing or cut some of the transitions where the shift is already clear.

## Missing warmth through specificity

The draft is thorough and careful, but it is not warm. Warmth in this voice comes from attention and specificity, not from chummy filler or reassurance. The commissioner observation paragraph is warm because it names the actual household kit and treats the starting case as a real person's question. The rest of the draft has moments of specificity (the 50% witch hazel pad, the frozen padsicle recipe, the toilet-seat basin protocol), but they are often wrapped in citation machinery or qualification.

**Example:** "A household padsicle is a different preparation. One labor-and-delivery nurse's recipe puts three to four tablespoons of witch hazel down the middle of an extra-large absorbent pad, optionally adds aloe or oils, reseals and freezes it, then thaws it for one or two minutes before use; another absorbent pad can serve as a barrier if it feels too cold."

This is specific and practical. It tells the reader how to make the thing. But then it says: "The author claims cooling, numbing, and less swelling but gives no hemorrhoid-specific contact time or follow-up, and the optional ingredients make any result impossible to attribute to witch hazel alone."

The second sentence is correct, but it undercuts the warmth of the first. The page is allowed to say the padsicle recipe is a household practice, name what it claims, and then say the optional ingredients prevent attribution. It does not need to also say the author "gives no hemorrhoid-specific contact time or follow-up" — that is report voice accounting for missing detail.

Rewrite: "One labor-and-delivery nurse's recipe puts three to four tablespoons of witch hazel down the middle of an extra-large absorbent pad, optionally adds aloe or oils, reseals and freezes it, then thaws it for one or two minutes before use; another absorbent pad can serve as a barrier if it feels too cold. The recipe claims cooling, numbing, and less swelling, but the optional ingredients prevent attributing the result to witch hazel alone."

**"Its label permits use up to six times a day or after bowel movements, or as a moist compress for up to 15 minutes, for temporary external itching, burning, irritation, and discomfort. The pad is not inserted into the rectum, and the label says to stop for bleeding or if the condition has not improved within seven days."** — The label detail is useful, but the rectal insertion warning is overexplaining unless there is a specific confusion to address.

## Evidence-status habits

**Treating PubMed as court of appeal:** The draft does not explicitly treat PubMed as the court of appeal, but it does repeatedly account for what studies measured versus what they did not measure. This is correct and necessary for a knowledge map, but the draft phrases these as limitations rather than as findings.

Examples: "Neither study measured hemorrhoids." "the abstract does not expose the complete dose." "the study cannot show whether the tablet, cream, time, or their combination produced the changes." "This supplies a bounded cold-contact precedent for injured postpartum tissue, not proof that a frozen witch-hazel pad shrinks hemorrhoids."

These are all correct observations, but they are phrased as what the evidence does not establish rather than what the practice does or does not do. The page is defending its reasoning rather than stating the finding.

**Repeating "limited evidence":** The draft does not use the phrase "limited evidence," but it uses structural equivalents: "no usable dose or time course was recovered," "No source here establishes," "These sources do not establish," "the label does not establish," "This supplies ... not proof."

The pattern is: state a practice, then immediately qualify what the evidence does or does not establish. This is useful when the qualification changes the decision, but it becomes tic when it repeats.

## What to preserve

- **Direct opening:** "Hemorrhoids in pregnancy do not reduce to a constipation story."
- **Commissioner observation paragraph:** Specific, grounded, treats the starting case as a real question.
- **Functional thresholds:** "A positional change is large enough to matter if..." "A result large enough to matter is..." "The practical threshold follows the active..."
- **Clear distinctions:** "Cold and witch hazel often arrive together in a postpartum kit, but they are different interventions." "Their preparations, outcomes, and pregnancy records differ."
- **Practical warnings:** "Bleeding, worsening, applicator pain, or persistence beyond the product's stated seven-day boundary is a stop point, not a reason to assemble a stronger stack."
- **Meta-observations:** "the second is a warning that repeated herbal lists can erase where a practice came from."
