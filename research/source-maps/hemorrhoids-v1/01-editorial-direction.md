# Editorial direction

## Initial impetus

Find practices whose effect becomes noticeable in ordinary use over days. Treat pregnancy and the postpartum weeks as the primary reader situation without making the page useless to other adults. Do not reduce the subject to "eat fiber and drink water," to a topical product ranking, or to a demand for trial-level evidence before a direction may be discussed.

The commissioner's observations, offered as leads rather than findings:

- Hemorrhoids can appear in pregnancy without constipation. A venous-pressure account (hormonally relaxed vein walls, expanded blood volume, uterine compression of pelvic veins) may fit such cases better than a straining account. The page should keep the two paths distinct rather than assuming one.
- Iron supplementation often begins around the same time hemorrhoids appear, which makes cause and coincidence hard to separate. Iron form and schedule are worth examining as a lever when the bowel does contribute.
- The warm sitz bath is the central home practice. Its mechanism, temperature, duration, and whether warm-then-cold contrast helps are open questions, as is whether Epsom salt adds anything to plain warm water.
- Cold applied directly (frozen witch hazel pads, padsicles, instant-cold perineal pads) is a widespread household practice with its own logic.
- Positional offloading (left side-lying, feet raised, less time seated on the toilet) is a distinct line of practice.
- Over-the-counter topicals should be examined by active ingredient (phenylephrine, hydrocortisone, pramoxine or lidocaine, witch hazel) and by how pregnancy advice treats each.
- Oral venotonics (diosmin or micronized purified flavonoid fraction, rutosides) and traditional venotonic plants (horse chestnut and others) are leads whose pregnancy and lactation status must be stated, not assumed.
- The postpartum weeks are when hemorrhoids commonly peak, so the page should carry across delivery and into breastfeeding.

These are commissioner observations and priorities. They direct research and editorial weight; they are not general evidence.
