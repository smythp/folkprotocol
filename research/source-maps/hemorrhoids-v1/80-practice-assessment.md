# Practice assessment: hemorrhoids, pregnancy and postpartum

## Working model of the problem

The mapped field supports several paths that can coexist without being made into one cause. First is pressure and load: pregnancy itself is associated with hemorrhoids, while late pregnancy, prolonged delivery straining, and higher birthweight concentrate mechanical load around the third trimester and birth [30:PR1][30:PR2]. The commissioner's current third-trimester case without constipation is a first-person observation about one known person, not a general finding. It gives the editor a reason to reconstruct the proposed venous-pressure path—relaxed vein walls, expanded blood volume, and uterine compression—and ask whether side-lying or less sitting changes symptoms. Pregnancy communities independently report hemorrhoids without constipation and side-lying relief, but do not establish that physiology [10:RF1][10:RF11][50:EX8][60:PF7].

Second is the bowel-and-straining path. A pregnancy trial combined fluid, fiber, exercise, and defecation technique and reported fewer postpartum hemorrhoids; forum and drug-use communities separately describe constipation from iron, opioids, or kratom as the preceding event [30:PR3][10:RF4][50:EX3]. Iron adjustment belongs only inside this path: the maps contain claims for chelated iron, changed dose timing, and alternate-day dosing, but no study connecting iron form or schedule to a hemorrhoid outcome [40:CW10][60:PF11][10:RF4][50:EX9].

Third is the symptomatic local episode. Warm water, cold contact, astringents, anesthetics, steroids, and vasoconstrictors address different claimed outcomes—comfort, itch, pain, irritation, or swelling—and should not be collapsed into a single idea of “relief.” The oral-phlebotonic review sharpens this distinction: it reports benefit for itch, bleeding, discharge, and overall symptoms, but not pain [30:PR6]. Prolapse or a suddenly hard painful mass also elicits practices aimed at reduction or escalation rather than routine soothing; the maps contain manual reduction, sugar applied to a prolapse, and one severe pregnancy case that resolved over about eleven days after prescription escalation [10:RF1][10:RF12].

The timing model must cross delivery rather than treat “pregnancy safe” as the end of the question. Perianal disease appeared in both late pregnancy and the first postpartum month in the mapped cohort; oral-product studies crossed into postpartum follow-up; and a breastfeeding thread reports recurrence after successive deliveries [30:PR2][30:PR4][30:PR5][10:RF10]. Ayurveda's twelve-week *Sutika Paricharya* similarly treats postpartum as its own restorative period, though the source does not name a hemorrhoid practice [20:TR3]. These are useful, revisable paths. Ayurveda, TCM, and Unani retain their own diagnostic logics rather than serving as translations of this syndrome model [20:TR1][20:TR4][20:TR6].

## Comparison across the field

Warm-water bathing has the widest practice footprint: pregnancy forums, hospital education, postpartum educators, herbalists, and Ayurvedic or TCM practitioners all use some form of local bath [10:RF2][40:CW6][60:PF2][60:PF6]. This is convergence on the move, not on its mechanism. Sources variously invoke blood flow, sphincter relaxation, cleansing, ritual, astringent herbs, or diagnosis-matched temperature [10:RF2][60:PF8][20:TR4]. TCM's cool bath for heat patterns contradicts the field's warm default [20:TR4]. The only controlled bath result is null for pain and analgesic use after hemorrhoid surgery, a wound-care population unlike conservative pregnancy care [30:PR9].

Epsom salt produces unusually clear disagreement. A seller says a topical soak relieves constipation, which does not supply a coherent path from bathwater to bowel function [40:CW5]. Hospital education says plain warm water is sufficient and additives may inflame tissue [40:CW6]. Forum users report momentary comfort, no healing, or irritation, while practitioner sources call salt optional or “spa vibes” [50:EX7][60:PF8][60:PF10]. No map found a warm-then-cold bathing practice or mechanism (10-reddit-forums §5; 40-commercial-web §4; 60-practitioner-folk-web §6).

Cold/astringent contact converges around witch hazel pads, wipes, foam, frozen pads, and padsicles [10:RF5][40:CW1][40:CW2][40:CW3][40:CW9]. Much of that repetition comes from related product formats and stable postpartum-kit vocabulary, so it is not independent confirmation of effect [40:CW1][40:CW2][40:CW3][50:EX10][50:EX11]. Strong first-person praise coexists with stinging, nonresponse, and abandonment in the same forum territory [10:RF7]. Commercial “safe because steroid-free” framing for a multi-herb salve is a marketing distinction, not a mechanism or reproductive-status account [40:CW4].

Named topical drugs offer a cleaner mechanism/practice split: anesthetics target pain, hydrocortisone inflammation or itch, and phenylephrine vasoconstriction. The commercial line divides pramoxine, lidocaine plus phenylephrine, and witch hazel, while pregnancy forums distinguish lidocaine-only from hydrocortisone combinations and describe prescription escalation [40:CW1][10:RF9][10:RF12]. Pregnancy-specific brand copy appears for witch hazel, not for the fetched anesthetic/vasoconstrictor lines; forum posters give about a week as the steroid-use boundary because of perianal skin thinning [40:CW1][10:RF9]. The mapped review for topical comparisons was withdrawn and not replaced [30:PR11].

Oral venotonics share “vein tone” language but not one preparation or reproductive-status record. MPFF is a defined 90% diosmin/10% hesperidin preparation with an acute commercial schedule and one uncontrolled pregnancy study; pooled phlebotonic trials mostly concern nonpregnant adults [40:CW11][30:PR4][30:PR6]. Euphorbia combines a 100 mg oral tablet with 1% topical cream, so its uncontrolled pregnancy report cannot identify which route contributed [30:PR5]. Horse chestnut has a general venous-insufficiency review, individual forum enthusiasm, self-combined stacks, and an explicit pregnancy/breastfeeding contraindication in the practitioner map, but no pregnancy hemorrhoid trial [30:PR7][10:RF7][50:EX5][60:PF4]. Repeating horse chestnut beside diosmin does not make them interchangeable. A modern review's placement of Balkan horse chestnut under TCM shows the cost of such flattening [20:TR12].

Bowel management and positional offloading are adjacent but distinct. The former has a pregnancy prevention bundle and several exposure-specific constipation stories [30:PR3][50:EX3]. The latter has repeated side-lying advice, toilet-time reduction, a negative squatty-potty report, and training substitutions for Valsalva-heavy lifts [10:RF8][50:EX10][50:EX12][60:PF7]. Ring cushions do not resolve the distinction: sellers generalize one product across unrelated seated-pain causes, while two forum threads say the ring can concentrate pressure and worsen external hemorrhoids [40:CW12][10:RF3][10:RF12].

Traditional systems contribute diagnosis-matched practice rather than extra votes for isolated ingredients. Ayurveda stages medicine/diet, caustic paste, cautery, and surgery; TCM matches formulas and bath temperature to four patterns; Unani begins with evacuation of melancholic humor [20:TR2][20:TR4][20:TR6]. Eight Ayurvedic answers on one Q&A page converge on a similar branded-and-herbal kit, but they remain responses collected by one site, including an undisclosed Pilex recommendation, rather than eight independent source lines [60:PF6]. Direct folk materials are sparse and heterogeneous: Talmudic site-specific remedies, a reader-remedy archive that also publishes failures, and granulated sugar for prolapse do not form a shared protocol [20:TR8][60:PF9][10:RF12]. Commercially, no verbatim claim block was found copied across unrelated owners; the clearer distortions are self-referral from a “guide” to its owner's product and repeated ingredient narratives within brand ecosystems [40:CW8].

## Commissioner impetus accounting

- **Pursue now — ordinary-use effects over days.** This remains the selection lens because the maps include day-four MPFF relief, a 14-day Euphorbia course, and an approximately eleven-day severe-case outcome, while many local practices report immediate comfort without follow-up [30:PR4][30:PR5][10:RF12]. Reconstruction must preserve timing and the claimed outcome rather than substitute a symptom-score hierarchy.
- **Pursue now — hemorrhoids without constipation and the proposed pregnancy pressure path.** The current case is the commissioner's first-person observation. Separate community reports keep the question open, and pregnancy/mechanical-load research supplies context without testing the proposed vein-wall, blood-volume, and uterine-compression chain [10:RF11][50:EX8][30:PR2].
- **Pursue now — the toilet-seat sitz basin, plain warm water, Epsom salt, and possible contrast.** Warm water is central across source kinds; exact temperature and duration appear in hospital and nonprofit protocols; Epsom salt is disputed; contrast is unmapped [40:CW6][60:PF10][50:EX7].
- **Pursue now — frozen witch-hazel pads, padsicles, and instant-cold pads.** These are stable household-kit practices with specific preparation formats and vivid positive claims, but also stinging and nonresponse; no published study of these cold formats was located [10:RF5][10:RF7][40:CW3][40:CW9] (30-published-research §6).
- **Research reserve — the angled perineal rinse bottle.** “Peri-rinse” appears as household vocabulary, but the map supplies no preparation, timing, hemorrhoid-specific outcome, or distinct operative claim to reconstruct (60-practitioner-folk-web §2).
- **Pursue now — hydrocortisone 1% and the active-ingredient comparison.** The kit's hydrocortisone belongs beside phenylephrine, pramoxine, lidocaine, and witch hazel because the field itself distinguishes their claimed jobs and gives a concrete duration concern for steroids [40:CW1][10:RF9].
- **Research reserve — docusate and stool management as a general page center.** Stool management has a strong prevention bundle, but the maps do not isolate docusate's amount, timing, or effect; it should support cases with a bowel contribution rather than displace the non-constipation question [30:PR3][10:RF12].
- **Research reserve — the ring cushion.** It is already in the kit, but the field supplies an undifferentiated sales claim and two mechanistically specific worsening reports, with no pregnancy-adapted use detail [40:CW12][10:RF3][10:RF12].
- **Pursue now — positional offloading.** Left side-lying, avoiding prolonged sitting, and limiting toilet time directly address the commissioner's pressure-path interest and recur across independent forum and doula sources [10:RF1][50:EX10][60:PF7][60:PF8]. Feet-raised positioning itself is not specified in the mapped sources.
- **Pursue now — iron form and schedule when the bowel contributes.** The timing coincidence in the current case remains a commissioner observation. Mapped practice offers dose-time changes, ferrous bisglycinate or chelate claims, and alternate-day self-titration, while the research map reports no hemorrhoid outcome tied to iron exposure [10:RF4][40:CW10][60:PF11][50:EX9] (30-published-research §5).
- **Pursue now — oral flavonoids separated from horse chestnut and other plants.** MPFF, general phlebotonics, Euphorbia, and horse chestnut have materially different preparations, outcomes, and pregnancy/lactation records; horse chestnut carries the map's explicit avoid-in-pregnancy-and-breastfeeding boundary [30:PR4][30:PR5][30:PR6][60:PF4].
- **Pursue now — postpartum and breastfeeding continuity.** The mapped record spans late pregnancy, delivery, the first postpartum month, and breastfeeding discussion, but reproductive-status language differs by practice and must be reconstructed individually [30:PR2][30:PR4][30:PR5][10:RF10].
- **Research reserve — existing Folkprotocol pages, bibliographies, and the yarrow entry.** Yarrow occurs in internal and topical herbal formulas, but the maps did not assess the existing Folkprotocol material; it remains prior material for a later provenance comparison rather than authority for selecting a direction [60:PF1][60:PF2][60:PF5].
- **Pursue now — pregnancy/postpartum as the primary reader situation without reducing the page to fiber, water, or product ranking.** The selected directions follow both pressure and bowel paths and separate product actives; the general-adult records remain useful where they actually supply the source footprint [30:PR3][40:CW1][50:EX5].

## Directions for targeted reconstruction

### 1. Non-constipation pregnancy pressure and positional offloading

**Commissioner impetus:** A current third-trimester case without constipation makes the causal distinction actionable now. **Source footprint:** Pregnancy forums and two doula sources repeat side-lying or avoiding prolonged sitting; the cohort identifies pregnancy stage and delivery load but does not test the proposed venous pathway [10:RF1][50:EX10][60:PF7][60:PF8][30:PR2]. **Operative plausibility:** Reducing time in positions said to load rectal veins coheres with a pressure account, while the maps do not establish that the reported relief comes through venous decompression [60:PF7]. **Practical signal:** The action is repeated and specific, but magnitude and duration are absent [10:RF1][50:EX10]. **Concrete downside:** Squatty-potty elevation has a worsening report, so “position” cannot be treated as one uniformly offloading family [10:RF8]. **Missing detail:** Separate left-side lying, standing, feet raised, ordinary chair sitting, and toilet sitting; recover duration, frequency, pregnancy stage, external versus internal symptoms, and pain versus swelling outcomes.

### 2. Iron form and schedule when constipation enters the chain

**Commissioner impetus:** Iron began shortly before the observed third-trimester case; the commission explicitly treats causation versus coincidence as open. **Source footprint:** One pregnancy poster traces morning iron to constipation and hemorrhoids; a prenatal seller and practitioner favor bisglycinate/chelate; a general anemia forum discusses alternate-day dosing; no mapped hemorrhoid study analyzes iron [10:RF4][40:CW10][60:PF11][50:EX9] (30-published-research §5). **Operative plausibility:** The coherent path is iron-related bowel change leading to straining, not a direct iron effect on hemorrhoidal tissue. **Practical signal:** The causal account is specific but singular; form and schedule claims repeat without a hemorrhoid outcome. **Concrete downside:** None is documented for a form or schedule switch in the mapped material. **Missing detail:** Identify the original formulation and elemental amount, timing relative to onset and meals, actual stool change, response after a single-variable switch, alternate-day pregnancy use, and hemorrhoid outcome distinct from gastrointestinal tolerance.

### 3. Sitz bathing: water parameters, Epsom salt, and contrast

**Commissioner impetus:** This is the commission's central home practice and the kit already contains a seat basin and Epsom salt. **Source footprint:** Warm bathing spans all source communities; protocols specify 104°F for 15–20 minutes or comfort-maintained warmth for 10–20 minutes; Epsom claims conflict; contrast does not appear [40:CW6][60:PF10][50:EX7] (10-reddit-forums §5). **Operative plausibility:** Warm water can provide local heat, cleansing, and immersion without requiring the field's untested blood-flow or sphincter stories. A topical salt soak has no coherent route to the seller's constipation claim [10:RF2][40:CW5]. **Practical signal:** Warm bathing is repeatedly described as soothing; Epsom salt yields momentary relief, no healing, and irritation reports [10:RF6][50:EX7]. **Concrete downside:** Additives may inflame tissue, and one user reports irritation [40:CW6][50:EX7]. **Missing detail:** Reconstruct basin depth, water temperature, session length, daily frequency, pregnancy/postpartum timing, plain-water comparator, exact salt concentration, skin response, and immediate comfort versus change over days; determine whether contrast is an actual practice before specifying it.

### 4. Cold and witch-hazel contact across pads, padsicles, foam, and ice

**Commissioner impetus:** Frozen witch-hazel pads, instant-cold pads, and padsicles are already central to the household kit. **Source footprint:** Forum, commercial, birth-worker, and older herbalist records describe witch hazel in pads, 50% foam, wipes, and frozen maxi pads, sometimes with aloe or arnica [10:RF5][40:CW2][40:CW3][40:CW9][60:PF5]. **Operative plausibility:** Cooling can plausibly change sensation and swelling temporarily; astringent contact is a separate proposed action and must be isolated from temperature. **Practical signal:** The family has high repetition and some emphatic first-person reports, alongside stinging, no effect, and quick abandonment [50:EX11][10:RF7]. **Concrete downside:** Stinging is reported; the commercial map found no tissue-cold caution for frozen formats (40-commercial-web §6). **Missing detail:** Separate room-temperature witch hazel, refrigerated product, frozen pad, plain cold pad, and multi-ingredient padsicle; recover concentration, freeze/thaw state, barrier fabric, contact duration, frequency, intact versus injured postpartum tissue, and pain versus itch versus swelling outcome.

### 5. OTC topical actives by ingredient and reproductive stage

**Commissioner impetus:** The commission asks what each active does and how pregnancy advice treats it; hydrocortisone 1% is already in the kit. **Source footprint:** Brand copy distinguishes pramoxine, lidocaine plus phenylephrine, and witch hazel; forums distinguish lidocaine-only products, hydrocortisone combinations, and prescription escalation [40:CW1][10:RF9][10:RF12]. **Operative plausibility:** Anesthesia, vasoconstriction, and steroid anti-inflammatory action propose different symptom targets, so a product-level “stronger” ladder obscures the choice. **Practical signal:** Posters give ingredient-specific choices and describe plateau/escalation, but follow-up after home treatment is uncommon [10:RF9][10:RF12]. **Concrete downside:** Continued steroid use is tied to perianal skin thinning, with about one week offered as the boundary [10:RF9]. **Missing detail:** For phenylephrine, hydrocortisone 1%, pramoxine, lidocaine, and witch hazel, recover formulation, amount, frequency, maximum duration, internal/external application, target symptom, pregnancy trimester, postpartum tissue status, lactation language, and what constituted plateau.

### 6. Oral venotonics without compound flattening

**Commissioner impetus:** Diosmin/MPFF, rutosides, horse chestnut, and other traditional venotonics were named leads, with pregnancy and lactation status required. **Source footprint:** A defined MPFF schedule and uncontrolled pregnancy report, a nonpregnant phlebotonic review, an oral-plus-topical Euphorbia pregnancy report, a general venous-insufficiency horse-chestnut review, traditional butcher's-broom use, and self-built stacks form separate records [40:CW11][30:PR4][30:PR5][30:PR6][30:PR7][20:TR10][50:EX5]. **Operative plausibility:** Venous tone, capillary permeability, and lymphatic drainage offer a coherent systemic path, though the review says the mechanism is not fully established [30:PR6]. **Practical signal:** MPFF reports day-four relief; pooled phlebotonics separate benefit for itch/bleeding/discharge from no pain effect; stack stories claim marked shrinkage without isolating ingredients [30:PR4][30:PR6][50:EX5]. **Concrete downside:** The practitioner source says horse chestnut should be avoided in pregnancy and breastfeeding [60:PF4]. **Missing detail:** Keep MPFF, unmicronized diosmin, rutosides, Euphorbia, horse chestnut, and butcher's broom separate; recover exact preparation, amount, taper, symptom outcome, onset, adverse-event collection, trimester, postpartum duration, breastfeeding status, and whether topical co-treatment prevents attribution.

## Research reserve

- **Bowel and stool management beyond iron (strain 5):** Preserve the pregnancy prevention bundle and exposure-specific constipation countermeasures for a later prevention reconstruction. The bundle combines several actions and cannot specify which changed the postpartum rate [30:PR3]; opioid/kratom practice belongs to its named exposure context [50:EX3].
- **Diagnosis-matched traditional systems (strain 8):** Preserve Ayurveda's six *Arshas* types and staged escalation, TCM's four patterns and temperature matching, and Unani's evacuation-first humoral account on their own terms [20:TR1][20:TR2][20:TR4][20:TR6]. They merit lineage-specific work, but the present maps do not connect their diagnostic preparations to the commission's pregnancy pressure question; the Ayurvedic Q&A does not address pregnancy [60:PF6].
- **Direct topical folk materials (strain 9):** Preserve Talmudic site-specific materials, banana peel/rutabaga/blackstrap-molasses submissions, and granulated sugar as separate leads [20:TR8][60:PF9][10:RF12]. Their materials, rationales, and claimed outcomes are too different to reconstruct as one remedy family.
- **Mechanical pressure-redistribution devices (strain 10):** Preserve the ring-cushion dispute because worsening reports propose a specific pressure-concentration mechanism against generic sales positioning [10:RF3][10:RF12][40:CW12].
- **Manual and osmotic reduction of protruding tissue (unclustered):** Preserve manual replacement after bathing and cream, and sugar applied to shrink a prolapse, as a focused future question about already-protruding tissue [10:RF1][10:RF12]. The map supplies no preparation boundary or corroboration beyond two forum accounts.
- **Rectal drug administration as a mechanical risk factor (unclustered):** Preserve the unanswered “boofing” question as route-specific harm-reduction material, not a treatment direction [50:EX4].
- **Personal tier-ranking as a decision practice (unclustered):** Preserve the explicit n=1 tradeoff comparison and prevention carve-out as an editorial form to examine, without importing its procedural ranking into this home-care commission [50:EX1].
- **Herbal bath blends:** Preserve the comfrey/calendula/yarrow/myrrh/witch-hazel/sea-salt infusion and the similar practitioner blend as exact preparations rather than evidence that every additive improves a plain bath [60:PF2][60:PF3].
- **Training modification:** Preserve substitution away from heavy squats and deadlifts for readers whose symptoms track Valsalva-heavy lifting; it is specific but outside the pregnancy/postpartum impetus of this run [50:EX12].
- **Reader remedies and self-built supplement stacks:** Preserve disclosed failures in the reader-remedy archive and the unisolated order-of-operations story in self-tracking forums [60:PF9][50:EX5]. They may generate later single-practice questions, but should not become a shopping list.

## Questions for targeted research

1. In pregnancy cases without constipation, which position is actually changed—left-side lying, standing, feet elevation, chair sitting, or toilet sitting—and what happens to pain, swelling, or prolapse during a stated interval?
2. When iron precedes a bowel change, what formulation, elemental amount, schedule, pregnancy stage, and stool change were present, and does changing one variable alter the hemorrhoid outcome as well as tolerance?
3. For a toilet-seat basin, what water temperature, depth, duration, and daily frequency distinguish immediate comfort from change over several days?
4. At what exact Epsom-salt concentration does a plain-water comparison change comfort, skin irritation, swelling, or bowel-independent outcomes?
5. Is warm-then-cold contrast a documented household protocol; if so, what sequence, temperatures, dwell times, claimed outcome, and concrete tissue boundary define it?
6. For witch hazel, which effect belongs to the ingredient and which to cooling when room-temperature liquid, refrigerated 50% product, frozen pad, and plain cold pad are separated?
7. How are frozen pads prepared and buffered from tissue, how long do they remain in contact, and are pregnancy hemorrhoids, intact postpartum tissue, tears, and stitches treated differently?
8. For each OTC active, what formulation, amount, internal/external use, frequency, maximum duration, target symptom, trimester, postpartum status, and breastfeeding language are actually stated?
9. What symptom and time boundary triggers movement from an OTC active to a prescription product in the reported escalation ladder?
10. For MPFF, diosmin, rutosides, Euphorbia, horse chestnut, and butcher's broom, what exact preparation and schedule was used, which symptom changed, and what pregnancy, postpartum, or breastfeeding population supplied that claim?
11. In oral-plus-topical or multi-compound venotonic reports, can the routes and ingredients be separated enough to attribute the claimed magnitude and onset?
