# Commission

## Slug

hemorrhoids-v1

## Question

What practices reduce the pain, swelling, itch, and recurrence of hemorrhoids, and what changes when the person is pregnant or recently postpartum?

## Boundaries

Adults managing hemorrhoids at home, with pregnancy and the postpartum period as the primary lens and general adult hemorrhoids in scope. Topical, bathing, positional, dietary, and over-the-counter practice; oral botanicals and drugs only with their pregnancy and lactation status stated. Excludes office procedures and surgery beyond naming when they are indicated, the workup of rectal bleeding, other anorectal conditions except to distinguish them, and generic diet moralizing.

## Seeds supplied by the commissioner

Existing Folkprotocol pages and bibliographies, including the yarrow materia entry that mentions hemorrhoids, are prior material, not authority.

The commissioner is interested in practices whose effects become noticeable through ordinary use over days, not a symptom-scoring regimen. Observations and leads offered as directions rather than established findings:

- In a current third-trimester case known to the commissioner, hemorrhoids appeared without constipation. Iron supplementation had started shortly before, which may be coincidence, since both commonly begin in the second or third trimester. The working model in that case is venous pressure (hormonally relaxed vein walls, expanded blood volume, uterine compression of pelvic veins) rather than straining, and a page should not assume the constipation story fits everyone.
- A home kit has already been assembled: a toilet-seat sitz basin, Epsom salt, witch hazel pads with some kept frozen, an angled perineal rinse bottle, instant-cold perineal pads, hydrocortisone 1% cream, docusate, and a ring cushion. Whether each item earns its place is an open question.
- Warm sitz baths are near-universal advice. Whether they act by relaxing the anal sphincter, by local blood flow, or mainly by cleansing, and whether water temperature or warm-then-cold contrast matters, is worth examining rather than assuming.
- Epsom salt in the sitz water is common folk practice with a thin record. Whether it adds anything to plain warm water is an open question.
- Frozen witch hazel pads and the postpartum "padsicle" (a maxi pad dressed with witch hazel and aloe, then frozen) are widespread household practice.
- Positional offloading in pregnancy (lying on the left side, feet raised, limiting time seated on the toilet) is a distinct line of practice from stool management.
- Over-the-counter topicals divide by active ingredient: phenylephrine, hydrocortisone, local anesthetics such as pramoxine and lidocaine, and witch hazel. How pregnancy advice treats each is worth mapping.
- Iron form and schedule (for example alternate-day dosing or chelated forms) is a lead for when the bowel does contribute.
- Oral flavonoid venotonics (diosmin and micronized purified flavonoid fraction, rutosides) are said to have a Cochrane review finding symptom benefit in pregnancy with safety left unsettled. Horse chestnut and other traditional venotonics reportedly lack pregnancy safety data. These are leads to check, not conclusions.
- Hemorrhoids commonly peak after delivery, so the same practices should be examined across the postpartum weeks, including breastfeeding.

## Working boundary

This commission covers what an adult, and in particular a pregnant or recently delivered person, might do at home to reduce hemorrhoid pain, swelling, itch, and recurrence, and how the pregnant and postpartum context changes what is used or avoided. Oral treatments belong only with their pregnancy and lactation status. It does not cover procedural or surgical treatment except to note when home practice stops being the right tool, and it does not cover the evaluation of rectal bleeding or the differentiation of other anorectal conditions beyond what is needed to keep the page honest. It is not a brief for moralizing about diet. Any existing Folkprotocol page or bibliography on this topic is prior material to be examined, not a starting authority or a fixed scope to preserve.
